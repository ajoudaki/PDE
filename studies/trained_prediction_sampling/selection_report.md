## Selection-level mathematical checks

I read the complete supplied arguments rather than adopting their status
labels. The following are the decisive compatibility checks.

1. **The finite analytic estimate has the right object and uniformities.**
   `route_source_response.md` proves fixed-order named-source tensor sums and
   the Gaussian expectation differentiation formulas, including singular
   covariance. `route_source_second_phase.md` then differentiates the full
   mass-dependent recursion, rather than only the frozen source coordinates.
   Its constants are claimed independent of mesh count, support size,
   minimum mass, and covariance rank. Both action orientations, changing
   residuals, and alpha/beta corrections remain present.

2. **The proposed closure addresses the dangerous feedback.** The key step is
   (C)/(J): a covariance term involving a new lower reverse slot annihilates
   the boundary expression, leaving an increment of source jets of order
   the block length. This is used also after summing the alpha row. It
   produces `E≤C_H+C ell E`, followed by finitely many blocks through time
   40, rather than an uncontrolled recursion in the number of mesh steps.
   The principal second-order system is the same linear one, with products
   of previously bounded first responses as forcing. The source-order
   accounting—five for the base, three for first explicit derivatives,
   one for second explicit derivatives—matches the Gaussian formulas.
   Repeated upper source indices are handled by full tensor sums; a false
   product of atom masses is not used.

3. **The actual-law passage is not an assumed ambient derivative.** P8–P12
   use the common quadratic remainder and actual value convergence to
   construct forward-quotient limits, then weak density and continuity to
   reach arbitrary Borel laws. Finite-sum centering and the integral
   representation are separately passed to the limit. Common quantization
   contracts TV and preserves probability rectangles. The limiting second
   derivative is neither needed nor asserted: the second finite-difference
   bound passes through the four actual values. P13 supplies a specified
   response procedure, not merely a name for an unknown derivative.

4. **The probabilistic normalization is correct.** The replacement proof
   controls bias by `O(1/m)`, a double replacement by `O(1/m²)`, and the
   squared higher-order Hoeffding remainder by `O(1/m²)`. Its first projection
   converges in `L²(mu;H)` to the centered influence. These give precisely
   `m E||r_m||²→0`, without a rate for empirical Wasserstein convergence.
   Centering is essential to the covariance formula and is provided. Zero
   covariance is allowed; the result does not promise a nondegenerate
   fluctuation for every law.

5. **Exceptional empirical laws and limits are treated honestly.** The
   finite-test cutoff has an exponentially small mismatch event, and both
   population extensions are bounded. This supports the mean-square
   transfer. P19–P20 condition on each fixed sample and use actual C.4.7
   finite-GF convergence inside its neighborhood. Bounded-Lipschitz tests
   cost at most two outside it, so no width theorem outside that neighborhood
   is smuggled into P6. The actual finite random readout is retained.
   Deterministic population expectations make the limiting covariance
   deterministic rather than an unaccounted random-environment mixture.
