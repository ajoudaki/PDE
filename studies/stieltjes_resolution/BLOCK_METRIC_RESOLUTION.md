# Resolution of the block-metric-uniform Stieltjes conjecture

Status: **disproved**, 18 August 2026; sharp six-moment transition added
19 August 2026.

This theorem settles the stronger extension in which the formal Stieltjes
property is required for every nonnegative parameter-block metric

\[
D_{\alpha,\beta}=D_a+\alpha D_u+\beta D_W,
\qquad \alpha,\beta\geq0.
\]

The degenerate origin $(0,0)$, where only the readout moves and the kernel is
constant, is excluded from the uniform claim.

The counterexample is the same one-input quadratic network with the same
Gaussian initialization, MFP normalization, MSE loss, and feature-to-loss
time change as in the canonical problem.  The simplest exact reduction occurs
at \((\alpha,\beta)=(0,1)\), where the first hidden variables are frozen.  A
second exact calculation now removes that degeneracy: the same negative
Hankel determinant persists for every
\[
0<\alpha\leq\frac1{100},\qquad \beta=1,
\]
so all three parameter blocks train at strictly positive rates.  This
disproves the uniform block-metric extension without relying on a frozen
layer.  It does **not** disprove the canonical \((1,1)\) sequence.

## Theorem

Fix \(\beta=1\), and for each \(\alpha\geq0\) let

\[
K_\alpha(y)=F_\alpha'(F_\alpha^{-1}(y)),\qquad
\frac{K_\alpha(\sqrt{x})-(63+48\alpha)}{x}
=\sum_{r\geq0}(-1)^r\mu_r(\alpha)x^r.
\]

The complete fixed-order width-limit jet through \(F_\alpha^{(13)}(0)\)
exists coefficientwise and is polynomial in \(\alpha\).  Its shifted
\(3\times3\) determinant is

\[
\Delta(\alpha)
:=\det(\mu_{i+j+1}(\alpha))_{i,j=0}^{2}
=\frac{55296\,P(\alpha)}
{2358125(63+48\alpha)^{33}},
\]

where \(P\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) is the exact primitive degree-36 polynomial
retained in ALPHA_INTERVAL_CERTIFICATE.json.  It satisfies

\[
P(0)=-4390263865367577686426705176678850125175774558883300<0,
\]

its linear coefficient is negative, and every coefficient of degree
\(2,\ldots,36\) is positive.  At the other endpoint,

\[
P(1/100)=-
\frac{4906843172971200580478870762709941495827987208484934793922629149362745126951838628156788861639108922079}
{1355252715606880542509316001087427139282226562500000}<0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\Delta(\alpha)<0\quad\text{for every}\quad
0\leq\alpha\leq1/100.}
\]

At the boundary this specializes to

\[
\Delta(0)
=-
\frac{86245462994269879146938487857152}
{200150589172828762588730609071155193161975}<0.
\]

For each \(\alpha\) in this interval, \((\mu_r(\alpha))\) is therefore not a
Stieltjes moment sequence.  In particular, every strictly positive metric
\((\alpha,1)\) with \(0<\alpha\leq1/100\) is a counterexample.  The
family-level uniform claim U1 is false, and its U2 and U3 strengthenings are
false as well because each contains U1.

## 1. Exact reduction of the finite-width feature flow

At width \(n\), write

\[
z_i=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^nW_{ij}u_j^2,
\qquad
f_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^na_i z_i^2,
\]

with all \(a_i,W_{ij},u_j\) independent standard Gaussians.  For the metric
\((0,1)\), feature time is generated by

\[
D_{0,1,n}
=n\left(\nabla_a f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla_a
       +\nabla_W f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla_W\right).
\]

Thus \(u\) is constant and the exact finite-width feature-flow equations are

\[
\dot a_i=z_i^2,
\qquad
\dot W_{ij}=2n^{-1/2}a_i z_i u_j^2.
\]

Set

\[
m_n=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nu_j^4.
\]

Differentiating \(z_i\) gives the closed row dynamics

\[
\dot z_i=2m_na_i z_i.
\]

There is no hidden approximation here: \(m_n\) is constant along this flow,
and different rows interact only through this frozen scalar.

Let

\[
X_m=z^2\partial_a+2maz\partial_z.
\]

The generator identity gives, exactly at finite \(n\),

\[
D_{0,1,n}^{\,k}f_n
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
  X_{m_n}^{\,k}(az^2)\big|_{(a_i,z_i)}.
\]

Conditionally on \(u\), the pairs \((a_i,z_i)\) are iid, with

\[
a_i\sim N(0,1),\qquad z_i\sim N(0,m_n),
\]

and the two coordinates are independent.  Put \(z=\sqrt m\,\xi\).  In the
coordinates \((a,\xi)\),

\[
X_m=m\left(\xi^2\partial_a+2a\xi\partial_\xi\right),
\qquad az^2=m a\xi^2.
\]

Hence, for a finite Gaussian constant \(c_k\),

\[
\mathbb E[D_{0,1,n}^{\,k}f_n\mid u]=c_km_n^{k+1}.
\]

The strong law gives \(m_n\to3\).  For every fixed \(k\), Jensen's inequality
also gives

\[
\sup_n\mathbb E[m_n^{2(k+1)}]
\leq \mathbb E[|u_1|^{8(k+1)}]<\infty.
\]

Thus \((m_n^{k+1})_n\) is uniformly integrable, and convergence of the
conditional expectations yields

\[
F^{(k)}(0)
:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E[D_{0,1,n}^{\,k}f_n]
=\mathbb E[X^k(az^2)],
\]

where

\[
X=z^2\partial_a+6az\partial_z,\qquad
a\sim N(0,1),\quad z\sim N(0,3),\quad a\perp z.
\]

This establishes the required fixed-order width limit directly, without a
trajectory-limit or derivative/limit interchange assumption.

The same metric governs MSE training.  For $\mathcal L=(1-f_n)^2$, metric
gradient descent has velocity $2(1-f_n)$ times the feature-ascent velocity,
so it traverses the same parameter path with the usual scalar time change
$\dot f=2(1-f)K(f)$ at the formal mean-field level.  The counterexample
therefore concerns the stated loss-evolution kernel, not an unrelated
auxiliary dynamics.

## 2. Exact formal jet

On monomials,

\[
X(a^pz^q)
=p\,a^{p-1}z^{q+2}+6q\,a^{p+1}z^q.
\]

The terminal Gaussian functional is

\[
\mathbb E[a^pz^q]
=\begin{cases}
(p-1)!!(q-1)!!\,3^{q/2},&p,q\text{ even},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

These two displayed formulas are a finite exact recurrence for any requested
coefficient.  Through the first order needed by the counterexample they give

\[
\begin{aligned}
F'(0)&=63,\\
F^{(3)}(0)&=77760,\\
F^{(5)}(0)&=274547232,\\
F^{(7)}(0)&=2141006515200,\\
F^{(9)}(0)&=31149221916487680,\\
F^{(11)}(0)&=759035131220036321280,\\
F^{(13)}(0)&=28719223368439752070594560,
\end{aligned}
\]

and every even derivative is zero.  The values through order nine agree
exactly with the independently retained Campaign-4 sector calculation at
\((0,1)\).

## 3. Exact inversion and the negative Hankel form

Write \(F(s)=s\psi(s^2)\), and let \(G=F^{-1}\).  Lagrange inversion gives

\[
G'(\sqrt{x})=\frac1{K(\sqrt{x})}
=\sum_{r\geq0}(-1)^rh_rx^r,
\qquad
h_r=(-1)^r[v^r]\psi(v)^{-(2r+1)}.
\]

Using the exact jet above, reciprocal-series extraction gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0&=\frac{480}{49},&
\mu_1&=\frac{43756}{151263},\\
\mu_2&=\frac{7214528}{200120949},&
\mu_3&=\frac{12545175968}{2402451992745},\\
\mu_4&=\frac{171752915595136}{200241971143303005},&
\mu_5&=\frac{2199776554157960896}{14570607030242443158825}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first four values independently reproduce the accepted Campaign-4
moments.  Direct exact elimination on the shifted Hankel matrix
\((\mu_{i+j+1})_{i,j=0}^2\) gives the negative determinant in the theorem.

For a determinant-free witness, define the moment functional
\(L(\lambda^r)=\mu_r\) and

\[
p(\lambda)=\lambda^2
-\frac{14165989123115588}{49896409440894219}\lambda
+\frac{40042013405871059816}{2310453239160606810795}.
\]

Then exact arithmetic gives

\[
L\!\left(\lambda p(\lambda)^2\right)
=-
\frac{673792679642733430835456936384}
{329714727520793070279653295504327135}<0.
\]

If a nonnegative representing measure \(\rho\) on \([0,\infty)\) existed,
the same expression would equal

\[
\int_0^\infty \lambda p(\lambda)^2\,\rho(d\lambda)\geq0,
\]

a contradiction.  This proves the theorem.

The equivalent inverse-coordinate sequence fails too:

\[
\det(h_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{3}
=-
\frac{86245462994269879146938487857152}
{788389295669640669091199792070276090944207633260074825}<0.
\]

That second failure is corroborative; the direct \(\mu\)-witness already
settles the claim.

## 4. Strictly positive extension and scope

For \(\beta=1\), exact integration of the rank-one \(W\)-update reduces every
fixed Taylor order to an interlaced finite Gaussian program.  The
detransposition lemma and coefficient recurrence are proved in
[POSITIVE_ALPHA_JET_DERIVATION.md](POSITIVE_ALPHA_JET_DERIVATION.md).  They
give every polynomial \(F_\alpha^{(k)}(0)\), \(0\leq k\leq13\); all even rows
vanish, and the complete odd coefficient rows are retained both in that note
and in
[BLOCK_METRIC_POSITIVE_ALPHA_JET.json](BLOCK_METRIC_POSITIVE_ALPHA_JET.json).
The rows through order nine exactly reproduce the frozen Campaign-4
\(\beta=1\) sector result, the \(\alpha=0\) row recurrence agrees through
order thirteen, and the \(\alpha=1\) value through order eleven reproduces
the accepted canonical certificate.

Exact formal inversion gives the determinant formula in the theorem.  Its
denominator is bounded below by \(2358125\cdot63^{33}>0\) on the interval.
Moreover, \(P''\) has strictly positive coefficients, so \(P\) is strictly
convex on \([0,\infty)\).  Both endpoint values on \([0,1/100]\) are
negative.  A convex function lies below its endpoint chord, proving
\(P(\alpha)<0\) throughout the interval.  Independently, all 37 exact
Bernstein coefficients of \(P\) on the same interval are strictly negative.

Thus the counterexample occupies a genuine segment of the strictly positive
metric quadrant; it does not depend on freezing a layer.  What is settled is
the deliberately stronger block-metric-uniform version U1 selected after the
exploratory proof search.  What remains open is the canonical
\((\alpha,\beta)=(1,1)\) V1 statement, and therefore its canonical V2 and V3
strengthenings.

### Sharp transition for the available six-moment prefix

The rational interval \(0\leq\alpha\leq1/100\) above is a convenient theorem
certificate, not the exact endpoint of the computed finite prefix.  A
separate exact sign audit of all quantities available from
\(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_5\) shows that the numerators of every moment, every leading
ordinary Hankel determinant through \(H_2\), and the two preceding shifted
determinants are coefficientwise strictly positive on \(\alpha\geq0\).  The
only sign-changing leading gate is \(\det H_2^+\), whose numerator is \(P\).

In ascending powers, \(P\) has two negative coefficients followed by 35
positive coefficients.  Descartes' rule of signs therefore permits at most
one positive root, while exact evaluations give

\[
\frac{17519225541486}{10^{15}}
<\alpha_*<
\frac{17519225541487}{10^{15}}.
\]

Consequently \(P\) has exactly one positive root,
\(\alpha_*=0.017519225541486\ldots\), and the complete available
six-moment classification on the \(\beta=1\) ray is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
0\leq\alpha<\alpha_*&H_2^+\text{ is indefinite},\\
\alpha=\alpha_*&H_2^+\text{ is positive semidefinite and singular},\\
\alpha>\alpha_*&H_2,H_2^+\text{ are both positive definite}.
\end{array}
\]

This is an exact phase boundary for the **six-moment truncation only**.  It is
not an all-order good-range theorem, and no comparable order-thirteen joint
\((\alpha,\beta)\) certificate currently exists.  The regenerating sign and
root-isolation record is
[ALPHA_TRANSITION_CERTIFICATE.json](ALPHA_TRANSITION_CERTIFICATE.json).

The same jet calculation has the useful but nondecisive canonical by-product

\[

and the corresponding next canonical shifted determinant is positive:

\[

{21875165394618842103069584785029512715749438929178771773425620184364845}>0.

That closes this one previously missing finite canonical gate; it is not an
