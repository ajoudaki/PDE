# Insertion-ready repairs for Sections 5--7

The following three blocks repair the time-realization, truncation, velocity-energy, and exact-GD path-law gaps.  All limits in width are in probability unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## A. Replace the action-law construction in Section 5

Fix \(T<\infty\), and let \(\mathbb T=\mathbb Q\cap[0,T]\).  For each \(m\geq1\), fix a countable algebra \(\mathscr C_m\) of bounded Lipschitz functions \(g:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R\) which contains the constants and generates the Borel sigma-field.  One may take rational finite linear combinations and products of rational tent functions.  For every Borel probability \(\lambda\) on \(\mathbb R^m\), the span of \(\mathscr C_m\) is dense in \(L^2(\lambda)\).

Enumerate the following countable, two-sorted, time-tagged grammar.  At every \(r\in\mathbb T\), it contains the right coordinate \(X_r\), the left coordinate \(C_r\), and the operator symbols \(B_0,B_0^*,B_r,B_r^*\).  It is closed under:

1. rational linear combinations of same-side probes, including probes carrying different time tags;
2. the structural coordinate maps in (2.2), so in particular
   \[
   A_r=J(X_r),\quad H_r=h(X_r),\quad S_r=B_rH_r,
   \quad G_r=\arctan S_r,\quad D_r=C_rq(S_r),
   \quad P_r=B_r^*D_r;
   \]
3. \(g(Z^1,\ldots,Z^m)\) whenever the \(Z^j\) are same-side probes and \(g\in\mathscr C_m\);
4. applications of \(B_0,B_0^*,B_r,B_r^*\) whenever the types agree; and
5. all same-side inner products.

In particular, for every positive integer \(M\), the grammar contains the clipping map
\[
\rho_M(p)=(-M)\vee(p\wedge M),
\qquad Q_{r,M}=q(A_r)^2\rho_M(P_r),
\qquad B_rQ_{r,M}.                                  \tag{5.a}
\]
If desired, \(Q_{r,M}\) can be obtained as an \(L^2\)-limit of members of the fixed countable algebras; adjoining these three displayed probes explicitly is equivalent and keeps the grammar countable.

For a fixed Euler mesh \(\delta\), evaluate every rational-time probe by polygonal interpolation of the Euler state.  Every finite subgrammar uses only finitely many time evaluations, coordinate maps, and calls to \(B_0,B_0^*\); hence it is still one fixed finite tensor program.  Record every finite joint coordinate law of same-side probes, now allowing different time tags in the same record, as well as every recorded inner product.

The same-width estimate (5.1) propagates through every fixed finite subgrammar.  For example,
\[
\|B_r^\delta v^\delta-B_r^{\delta'}v^{\delta'}\|_n
\leq \|B_r^\delta-B_r^{\delta'}\|_{\rm op}\|v^\delta\|_n
   +\|B_r^{\delta'}\|_{\rm op}\|v^\delta-v^{\delta'}\|_n, \tag{5.b}
\]
and every \(g\in\mathscr C_m\) is Lipschitz.  The structural product \(Cq(S)\) is covered by the uniform \(L^\infty\) bound on \(C\).  Thus, for each fixed finite record \(R\),
\[
\sup_{r\in\mathbb T}d_R(\mathfrak S^\delta(r),
                         \mathfrak S^{\delta'}(r))
\leq C_{T,R}(\delta+\delta').                       \tag{5.c}
\]
Here the supremum may first be taken over a finite rational grid and then over all rational times by the uniform time modulus.  Taking \(n\to\infty\) before \(\delta,\delta'\downarrow0\) is legitimate because the record is fixed.  Summing truncated record distances with weights \(2^{-j}\) gives a Cauchy family of complete, time-tagged action laws.  No tensor program is allowed to grow with \(n\).

For definiteness, perform the construction along the countable dyadic sequence \(\delta_\ell=2^{-\ell}\).  Estimate (5.c) shows that every other sequence of meshes tending to zero has the same limit, so this restriction changes neither the action law nor the continuous flow.

All finite limiting records are consistent because they are limits of consistent finite-dimensional laws.  Kolmogorov extension therefore gives canonical coordinate probability spaces \((\Omega_R,\mathcal F_R,\mathbb P_R)\) and \((\Omega_L,\mathcal F_L,\mathbb P_L)\) carrying all rational-time right and left probes jointly.  Let
\[
\mathcal H_R=L^2(\Omega_R,\mathcal F_R,\mathbb P_R),
\qquad
\mathcal H_L=L^2(\Omega_L,\mathcal F_L,\mathbb P_L). \tag{5.d}
\]
The bounded cylinder probes generated by the \(\mathscr C_m\)'s are dense in these spaces.  Since the finite-width operator bound implies
\[
\|B_0v\|_{\mathcal H_L}\leq M_0\|v\|_{\mathcal H_R}
\]
on this dense set, \(B_0\) extends uniquely to a bounded operator on all of \(\mathcal H_R\).  Passing to the limit in
\(\langle u,B_0v\rangle_n=\langle B_0^*u,v\rangle_n\)
identifies its adjoint.  The same argument applies to every rational-time \(B_r\).  In particular, multiplication by any bounded coordinate function, including \(q(A_r)^2\), acts on the full \(L^2\) space, and expressions such as \(B_0M_{q(A_0)^2}B_0^*u\) are now defined.

The finite-width estimates pass to rational times and give
\[
\|X_r-X_s\|_2+\|C_r-C_s\|_2
 +\|B_r-B_s\|_{\rm op}\leq C_T|r-s|.               \tag{5.e}
\]
Consequently the rational-time variables extend uniquely and strongly continuously to every \(t\in[0,T]\).  The derived variables \(H,S,G,D,P\) are strongly continuous as well.  Moreover, \(C(t)\in L^\infty\) with the limiting bound (2.11).  Therefore
\[
t\longmapsto e(t)D(t)\otimes H(t)
\]
is continuous in operator norm, because
\[
\|u\otimes v-u'\otimes v'\|_{\rm op}
\leq \|u-u'\|_2\|v\|_2+\|u'\|_2\|v-v'\|_2.       \tag{5.f}
\]

It remains to verify, rather than assume, the integral equation.  The polygonal Euler scheme satisfies its integral equations with the vector field frozen at the left endpoint of each mesh interval.  For any fixed rational-time probe, the \(L^2\) norm of the difference between this frozen-field integral and the integral using the polygonal state is at most \(C_T\delta\), by (2.10) and the uniform time modulus.  This squared norm is itself a recorded Gram quantity.  First take \(n\to\infty\) at fixed \(\delta\), and then \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Strong continuity turns the limiting Riemann sums into Bochner integrals and yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
X(t)&=X(0)-2\int_0^t e(s)B(s)^*D(s)\,ds,\\
C(t)&=C(0)-2\int_0^t e(s)G(s)\,ds,\\
B(t)&=B_0-2\int_0^t e(s)D(s)\otimes H(s)\,ds
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{5.g}
\]
in \(\mathcal H_R,\mathcal H_L\), and operator norm, respectively.  Equality for \(B(t)\) may equivalently be checked first on the dense cylinder probes and then extended using the common operator bound.

Thus all times live on fixed spaces, so (2.10) can be integrated on those spaces and Gronwall gives uniqueness.  At a restart time \(t_0\), (5.g) rewritten on \([t_0,t_0+t]\) involves only \((X(t_0),B(t_0),C(t_0))\).  Existence for the restarted equation and uniqueness on the fixed spaces give \(\Phi_{t+s}=\Phi_t\circ\Phi_s\).  This establishes the claimed autonomous, restartable action flow without identifying unrelated one-time GNS spaces.

## B. Insert after the dominated-pushforward lemma in Section 6

Write
\[
\mu_{n,t}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{(A_i(t),P_i(t))},
\qquad \mu_t=\mathcal L(A(t),P(t)).
\]
By (5.5), (6.4), and the time-modulus following from (6.5),
\[
\varepsilon_n:=\sup_{t\leq T}W_2(\mu_{n,t},\mu_t)
\longrightarrow0,                                  \tag{6.a}
\]
and \(K=\{\mu_t:0\leq t\leq T\}\) is compact in \(W_2(\mathbb R^2)\).

We need the following quantitative form of uniform integrability.  If \(\gamma,\mu\in\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^2)\), and \(p_\gamma,p_\mu\) denote the second coordinates in any optimal coupling, then
\[
\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M}\,d\gamma
\leq4W_2(\gamma,\mu)^2
   +2\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M/2}\,d\mu.             \tag{6.b}
\]
Indeed, on \(\{|p_\gamma|>M,|p_\mu|\leq M/2\}\) one has
\(|p_\gamma-p_\mu|>M/2\geq|p_\mu|\), and
\(|p_\gamma|^2\leq2|p_\gamma-p_\mu|^2+2|p_\mu|^2\).
Compactness of \(K\) implies
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_{t\leq T}
\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M}\,d\mu_t=0;               \tag{6.c}
\]
this follows by covering \(K\) by finitely many small \(W_2\)-balls and applying (6.b) to their centers.  Combining (6.a)--(6.c) gives, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb P\!\left(
 \sup_{t\leq T}\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M}\,d\mu_{n,t}
 >\epsilon\right)=0.                               \tag{6.d}
\]
This is the required uniform-in-time square-tail estimate; it is stronger than pointwise uniform integrability.

Let \(\rho_M(p)=(-M)\vee(p\wedge M)\), and define
\[
Q=q(A)^2P,\qquad Q_M=q(A)^2\rho_M(P).               \tag{6.e}
\]
Then
\[
\|Q-Q_M\|_2^2
\leq\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M}\,d\mu_t,             \tag{6.f}
\]
with the identical empirical inequality.  For fixed \(M\), the map
\((a,p)\mapsto q(a)^2\rho_M(p)\) is bounded and globally Lipschitz.  Hence \(Q_M\), \(BQ_M\), and all their joint Gram quantities with existing probes are part of the enlarged fixed grammar from Section 5 and converge uniformly on \([0,T]\).

Put
\[
\alpha_n=\|H_n\|_n^2,\qquad
Y_n=\alpha_nD_n+B_nQ_n,qquad
Y_{n,M}=\alpha_nD_n+B_nQ_{n,M},                    \tag{6.g}
\]
and use the analogous notation without \(n\) for the limit.  On the common high-probability bounded set,
\[
\|Y_n-Y_{n,M}\|_n\leq M_B\|Q_n-Q_{n,M}\|_n,
\qquad
\|Y-Y_M\|_2\leq M_B\|Q-Q_M\|_2.                 \tag{6.h}
\]
All four families \(Y_n,Y_{n,M},Y,Y_M\) are uniformly bounded in \(L^2\).  Therefore
\[
|\|Y_n\|_n^2-\|Y_{n,M}\|_n^2|
\leq(\|Y_n\|_n+\|Y_{n,M}\|_n)\|Y_n-Y_{n,M}\|_n, \tag{6.i}
\]
and similarly in the limit.  For fixed \(M\), convergence of the recorded truncated Gram quantities gives
\[
\sup_{t\leq T}|\|Y_{n,M}(t)\|_n^2-\|Y_M(t)\|_2^2|
\longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{6.j}
\]
Equations (6.d), (6.f)--(6.j), in the order
\[
\text{first }n\to\infty\text{ at fixed }M,
\qquad\text{then }M\to\infty,                     \tag{6.k}
\]
give
\[
\sup_{t\leq T}|\|Y_n(t)\|_n^2-\|Y(t)\|_2^2|
\longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{6.l}
\]
The same approximation, now applied to finite joint laws rather than only Gram quantities, gives uniform-in-time \(W_2\) convergence of the coordinate laws of \(Y_n\) to those of \(Y\).

Since \(\dot S_n=-2e_nY_n\), while \(e_n\to e\) uniformly, (6.l) yields
\[
\sup_{t\leq T}
|\|\dot S_n(t)\|_n^2-\|\dot S(t)\|_2^2|
\longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{6.m}
\]
For \(\dot A=-2e\,q(A)P\), use the same tail estimate with
\(|q(a)p|\leq|p|\); this gives
\[
\sup_{t\leq T}
|\|\dot A_n(t)\|_n^2-\|\dot A(t)\|_2^2|
\longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{6.n}
\]
Consequently both squared velocities converge in \(L^1([0,T])\), and
\[
\int_0^T\|\dot Z_n(t)\|_n^2dt
\longrightarrow
\int_0^T\|\dot Z(t)\|_2^2dt,
\qquad Z=A,S.                                      \tag{6.o}
\]
No width-dependent truncation is used: (6.k) is the complete order of limits.

For exact GD, let \(\widehat\mu_{n,k}\) be the empirical law of
\((A_k^{\rm GD},P_k^{\rm GD})\).  Equations (7.5) and (6.4), followed by the flow convergence above, imply
\[
\max_{k\eta_n\leq T}W_2(\widehat\mu_{n,k},\mu_{k\eta_n})
\longrightarrow0.                                  \tag{6.p}
\]
Thus (6.d) also holds uniformly over all GD nodes.  Let
\[
E_{n,k}=
\frac{\arctan(A_k+\eta_nV_{A,k})-\arctan A_k}{\eta_n}
-q(A_k)V_{A,k}.
\]
Because \(\arctan''\) is bounded and \(|V_{A,k}|\leq C_T|P_k|\),
\[
\|E_{n,k}\|_n^2
\leq C_T\eta_n^2M^4
 +C_T\int p^2\mathbf1_{|p|>M}\,d\widehat\mu_{n,k}. \tag{6.q}
\]
The first term vanishes as \(n\to\infty\) for every fixed \(M\), and the second vanishes in the iterated order (6.k), uniformly in \(k\).  Also
\[
\|V_{B,k}(H_{k+1}-H_k)\|_n
\leq C_T\eta_n\|V_{A,k}\|_n=o_{\mathbb P}(1)       \tag{6.r}
\]
uniformly over \(k\eta_n\leq T\).  Hence (7.6) gives
\[
\max_{k\eta_n\leq T}
\left\|
\frac{S_{k+1}-S_k}{\eta_n}
+2e_k\{\|H_k\|_n^2D_k+B_kq(A_k)^2P_k\}
\right\|_n\longrightarrow0.                       \tag{6.s}
\]
Apply the fixed-\(M\) truncated-probe convergence, then (6.d), exactly as in (6.g)--(6.l).  The limiting squared velocities are continuous in time: for example, strong continuity of \(A,P\), boundedness of \(q\), and uniform square-integrability of \(P\) imply strong continuity of \(q(A)^2P\), after which boundedness and operator-norm continuity of \(B\) apply.  Hence their Riemann sums converge.  It follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k\eta_n<T}\eta_n\|V_{A,k}\|_n^2
&\longrightarrow\int_0^T\|\dot A(t)\|_2^2dt,\\
\sum_{k\eta_n<T}\eta_n
\left\|\frac{S_{k+1}-S_k}{\eta_n}\right\|_n^2
&\longrightarrow\int_0^T\|\dot S(t)\|_2^2dt.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{6.t}
\]
For a final partial interval, replace its weight by its actual length.  In particular, both polygonal GD slope energies are bounded in probability uniformly in \(n\).

## C. Insert at the end of Section 7: exact-GD path laws

For \(Z=A,S\), let \(\widehat Z_n\) be the polygonal interpolation of the iterate values \(Z_k\), and let
\[
\widehat\Lambda_n^Z=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\delta_{\widehat Z_{n,i}(\cdot)}
\in\mathcal P_2(C([0,T])).                         \tag{7.a}
\]
Let \(\pi=\{0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=T\}\) be a deterministic coarse partition with mesh at most \(\Delta\), and let \(I_\pi z\) be the piecewise-linear interpolation of the values \(z(t_j)\).  For every absolutely continuous scalar path,
\[
\|z-I_\pi z\|_\infty^2
\leq4\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
\sup_{s,t\in[t_j,t_{j+1}]}|z(t)-z(s)|^2.           \tag{7.b}
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz on each interval and then averaging over coordinates gives
\[
W_2\!\left(\widehat\Lambda_n^Z,
            (I_\pi)_\#\widehat\Lambda_n^Z\right)^2
\leq4\Delta\int_0^T
\|\partial_t\widehat Z_n(t)\|_n^2dt.              \tag{7.c}
\]
By (6.t), the right side is \(O_{\mathbb P}(\Delta)\), uniformly in \(n\).

At the fixed coarse grid, (7.5) gives
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m
\|A_n^{\rm GD}(t_j)-A_n(t_j)\|_n^2\longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(J\) is Lipschitz, and
\[
\sum_{j=0}^m
\|S_n^{\rm GD}(t_j)-S_n(t_j)\|_n^2\longrightarrow0, \tag{7.d}
\]
because
\[
\|S-\widetilde S\|_n
\leq a_0\|B-\widetilde B\|_{\rm op}
   +M_B\|X-\widetilde X\|_n.
\]
The number of coarse-grid times is fixed before \(n\to\infty\).  Combining (7.d) with the finite-width flow path convergence (6.3) proves \(W_2\) convergence of the GD grid-vector empirical law to the grid marginal of \(\mu_Z\).  Since interpolation from \(\mathbb R^{m+1}\) to \(C([0,T])\) is Lipschitz, for each fixed \(\pi\),
\[
(I_\pi)_\#\widehat\Lambda_n^Z
\longrightarrow (I_\pi)_\#\mu_Z
\quad\text{in }W_2(C([0,T])).                     \tag{7.e}
\]

The flow estimate (6.2), passed to the limit, gives
\[
W_2(\mu_Z,(I_\pi)_\#\mu_Z)\leq C_T\sqrt\Delta.   \tag{7.f}
\]
The triangle inequality using (7.c), (7.e), and (7.f) now yields, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\lim_{\Delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb P\!\left(
W_2(\widehat\Lambda_n^Z,\mu_Z)>\epsilon\right)=0. \tag{7.g}
\]
Thus the exact-GD empirical path laws of both \(A\) and \(S\) converge in \(W_2(C([0,T]))\).  The order is: choose the coarse grid, take width to infinity, and only then send its mesh to zero.

This argument interprets “polygonal interpolation of \(S\)” as interpolation of the iterate values \(S_k=B_k\arctan A_k\), which is also the convention used in (7.6).  If instead \(S(t)\) is recomputed from polygonally interpolated \(A(t),B(t)\), its derivative is
\[
\partial_t S(t)=V_{B,k}\arctan A(t)+B(t)q(A(t))V_{A,k}
\]
inside the \(k\)-th step and has the same uniform integrated bound.  Its sup-path distance from the endpoint interpolation tends to zero by the same Taylor/truncation estimate (6.q), so (7.g) is unchanged.
