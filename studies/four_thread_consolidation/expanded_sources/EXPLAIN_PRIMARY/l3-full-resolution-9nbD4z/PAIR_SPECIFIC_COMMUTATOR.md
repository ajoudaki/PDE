# Exact middle-layer cancellation and its remaining causal commutator

## Result and limit

The coordinate \(F(x)=x+x^3/3\) cancels the middle-layer gate contribution
from that layer's own row updates, including for two actual trajectories.
The motion of the first layer leaves an exact commutator. Its skew
symmetry cancels the pairing against the current backward vector, but
not a general difference generated by the rank-one training history.
The formulas below isolate the remaining mixed-product estimate.

This is an exact finite-width calculation. It neither supplies a
width-uniform estimate for the commutator on Gaussian-initialized pairs
nor gives a counterexample to such an estimate. No ambient spike
counterexample is used.

## Notation and the actual middle-layer velocity

Use the finite-width feature flow and normalization of
\( \texttt{READOUT_COERCIVITY.md} \). All vector norms are ordinary
Euclidean norms. For this note only write
\[
 z=z^{(2)},\quad
 q=(W^{(3)})^{\mathsf T}\delta^{(3)},\quad
 \delta=\delta^{(2)},\quad
 D=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac1{1+z_i^2}\right).
\]
Thus \(\delta=Dq\). Set
\[
 a_2=\frac{\|h^{(1)}\|_2^2}{n},\qquad
 B=W^{(2)}D_1^2(W^{(2)})^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 A=a_2I+B.
\]
Both \(A\) and \(B\) are symmetric positive semidefinite. Directly from
the trained lower blocks,
\[
 z'=A\delta.                                             \tag{1}
\]
The term \(a_2\delta\) comes from \((W^{(2)})'h^{(1)}\), while
\(B\delta\) comes from \(W^{(2)}(h^{(1)})'\). The positive lower
second moment gives a lower bound on \(a_2\), but does not make \(B\)
diagonal.

For a coordinatewise vector \(c\), write \(M_c=\operatorname{diag}(c)\)
and \([B,M_c]=BM_c-M_cB\). Define
\[
 b_i=\frac{\phi''(z_i)}{\phi'(z_i)}
     =-\frac{2z_i}{1+z_i^2},\qquad |b_i|\le1.             \tag{2}
\]

## Exact tangent cancellation

Consider a differentiable family of finite trajectories or forced
finite trajectories, whose base trajectory solves (1). Let
\[
 e=\partial_\lambda z,\qquad
 \dot q=\partial_\lambda q,\qquad
 \dot A=\partial_\lambda A.
\]
Dots in this section mean variation in the family parameter; primes
mean feature-time derivatives. A parameter forcing induces a forward
tangent forcing \(\rho\). Differentiation of (1) gives
\[
 e'=A\dot\delta+\dot A\,\delta+\rho,\qquad
 \dot\delta=D\dot q+M_{b\odot e}\delta.                  \tag{3}
\]
For instance, forcing the first-layer and matrix tangent equations by
\(\xi_1\) and \(\Xi_2\) contributes
\(\rho=\Xi_2h^{(1)}+W^{(2)}D_1\xi_1\).

Because \(F'=1/\phi'\), the tangent in the natural middle coordinate is
\[
 j=\partial_\lambda F(z)=D^{-1}e.
\]
Using \(D'=\operatorname{diag}(\phi''(z_i)z_i')\) and (1)--(3),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 j'
 &=D^{-1}AD\dot q+D^{-1}\dot A\,\delta+D^{-1}\rho\\
 &\quad+D^{-1}A M_{b\odot e}\delta
          -D^{-1}M_{b\odot e}A\delta\\
 &=D^{-1}AD\dot q+D^{-1}\dot A\,\delta+D^{-1}\rho
          +D^{-1}[B,M_{b\odot e}]\delta.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{4}
\]
The scalar part \(a_2I\) cancels exactly in the last line. If the first
layer were frozen, \(B\) would be absent from (1), and this commutator
would vanish. In the prescribed model the first layer is trained.

Equation (4) keeps the other state-variation term \(\dot A\,\delta\)
explicit. No estimate or cancellation of that term is being assumed.
The coordinate change is exact for every finite \(n\); the uniform
control of \(D^{-1}\), or of the stronger difference \(F(z)-F(\widetilde z)\),
is a separate issue and is not supplied by the action estimate.

## Exact identity for a finite pair

No interpolation through arbitrary states is needed to see the same
residual for two trajectories. Allow defects in their actual middle
velocities:
\[
 z'=ADq+\rho,\qquad
 \widetilde z'=\widetilde A\widetilde D\widetilde q
                          +\widetilde\rho.
\]
For exact flows the defects vanish. A continuous Euler or oracle
comparison can be written in this form by defining its defect as the
difference between its actual parameter velocity and the current
feature vector field, followed by the forward chain rule.

Set
\[
 R=D\widetilde D^{-1},\qquad
 \widetilde\delta=\widetilde D\widetilde q.
\]
Then the exact finite-pair identity is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [F(z)-F(\widetilde z)]'
 ={}&D^{-1}AD(q-\widetilde q)\\
 &+D^{-1}(A-\widetilde A)D\widetilde q\\
 &+D^{-1}[\widetilde B,R]\,\widetilde\delta\\
 &+D^{-1}\rho-\widetilde D^{-1}\widetilde\rho.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{5}
\]
To check the only nontrivial algebra, use \(D=R\widetilde D\):
\[
 D^{-1}\widetilde A D
       -\widetilde D^{-1}\widetilde A\widetilde D
 =D^{-1}[\widetilde A,R]\widetilde D
 =D^{-1}[\widetilde B,R]\widetilde D.
\]
Thus the diagonal gate difference has been converted into a
commutator on the particular pair itself. Formula (5) does not estimate
the transformed defect; amplification by \(D^{-1}\) must also be
controlled in any proposed natural-coordinate metric.

In particular, this gives an exact relative-energy identity for the
natural middle-coordinate distance. Put
\[
 V=F(z)-F(\widetilde z),\qquad
 \mathcal E_F=\frac{\|V\|_2^2}{2n},\qquad v=D^{-1}V.
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_F'
 ={}&\frac1n v^{\mathsf T}AD(q-\widetilde q)
     +\frac1n v^{\mathsf T}(A-\widetilde A)D\widetilde q\\
 &+\frac1n v^{\mathsf T}[\widetilde B,R]\widetilde\delta
     +\frac1nV^{\mathsf T}
                  (D^{-1}\rho-\widetilde D^{-1}\widetilde\rho).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{5a}
\]
Since \(F'\ge1\), this distance controls
\(\|z-\widetilde z\|_2^2/(2n)\), but it is not uniformly equivalent to
that distance under the current action bounds. Equation (5a) also
specifies the actual test vector in its commutator term; it is
\(D^{-1}V\), not the current backward vector.

## What the rank-one history actually says

For a finite pair let \(\Delta\) denote differences and an overbar
arithmetic averages. Products satisfy the exact midpoint identity, so
\[
 \Delta z^{(2)}
   =\Delta W^{(2)}\,\overline h^{(1)}
       +\overline W^{(2)}\Delta h^{(1)},                  \tag{6}
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta W^{(2)}(s)
 ={}&\Delta W^{(2)}(0)\\
 &+\frac1n\int_0^s
  \left[
   \Delta\delta^{(2)}(u)(\overline h^{(1)}(u))^{\mathsf T}
   +\overline\delta^{(2)}(u)(\Delta h^{(1)}(u))^{\mathsf T}
  \right]du\\
 &+\int_0^s\Delta\Xi_2(u)\,du.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{7}
\]
Here \(\Xi_2\) is the matrix-velocity defect and vanishes for exact
flows. Consequently the learned-matrix part of (6) is indeed an
integral of rowwise backward differences and rowwise backward fields,
with scalar feature correlations as coefficients. For example, its
first integral is
\[
 \int_0^s
 \frac{(\overline h^{(1)}(u))^{\mathsf T}
                   \overline h^{(1)}(s)}n\,
                   \Delta\delta^{(2)}(u)\,du.             \tag{8}
\]
The scalar coefficient in (8) has absolute value at most \(a^2\),
where \(a=\pi/2\). The other integral coefficient has absolute value
at most \(a\|\Delta h^{(1)}(u)\|_2/\sqrt n\).

These identities establish the causal rank structure. They do not
force its value at time \(s\) to be parallel to \(\delta^{(2)}(s)\).
They also retain the second term
\(\overline W^{(2)}\Delta h^{(1)}\) in (6), which is precisely the
shared lower-layer motion represented by \(B\) in (1).

## The signed cancellation and the remaining mixed products

Since \(B\) and \(M_c\) are symmetric, their commutator is
skew-symmetric. Hence
\[
 \delta^{\mathsf T}[B,M_c]\delta=0.                       \tag{9}
\]
For a general test vector \(v\), however,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1n v^{\mathsf T}[B,M_c]\delta
 &=\frac1n(Bv)^{\mathsf T}(c\odot\delta)
       -\frac1n(c\odot v)^{\mathsf T}B\delta\\
 &=\frac1{2n}\sum_{i,j}B_{ij}(c_j-c_i)
                      (v_i\delta_j-v_j\delta_i).
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{10}
\]
In particular (9) is a real cancellation, but its hypothesis concerns
the test vector, not merely the rank of a matrix update.

For \(c=b\odot e\), Cauchy--Schwarz in (10) gives
\[
 \left|\frac1n v^{\mathsf T}[B,M_{b\odot e}]\delta\right|
 \le
 \frac{\|Bv\|_2}{\sqrt n}
       \frac{\|e\odot\delta\|_2}{\sqrt n}
 +\frac{\|e\odot v\|_2}{\sqrt n}
       \frac{\|B\delta\|_2}{\sqrt n}.                    \tag{11}
\]
The finite action and lower hidden second moments bound the time
integrals of \(\|\delta\|_2^2/n\) and
\(\|B\delta\|_2^2/n\). They do not yet bound either mixed product in
(11).

One can see the unresolved quantity entirely within the causal
rank-history representation. In the tangent version of (7), one
component of \(e(s)\) is
\[
 E(s)=\int_0^s k(s,u)\,\eta(u)\,du,\qquad
 k(s,u)=\frac{(h^{(1)}(u))^{\mathsf T}h^{(1)}(s)}n,
 \quad \eta=\partial_\lambda\delta^{(2)}.
\]
For this component alone the signed commutator pairing expands exactly as
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\frac1n E(s)^{\mathsf T}
       [B(s),M_{b(s)\odot E(s)}]\delta(s)\\
 &\quad=\int_0^s\int_0^s k(s,u)k(s,v)\,\Bigg[
 \frac1n(B(s)\eta(u))^{\mathsf T}
                    (b(s)\odot\eta(v)\odot\delta(s))\\
 &\hspace{49mm}
 -\frac1n(b(s)\odot\eta(u)\odot\eta(v))^{\mathsf T}
                    B(s)\delta(s)
 \Bigg]\,du\,dv.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{12}
\]
Thus restricting differences to rank-one histories changes the
obligation into signed mixed correlations at three times. It does
not turn (12) into an expression involving only pairwise \(L^2\)
norms or the known scalar action. No assertion is made that these
correlations are large on the prescribed Gaussian trajectories.

The currently missing bridge for this route is a bound on the
time-integrated signed commutator (10), with the actual pair-generated
test and error vectors, together with control of the transformed
coefficient/defect terms in (5). The natural coordinate supplies an
exact partial cancellation; the existing action estimates alone do
not complete that bridge.
