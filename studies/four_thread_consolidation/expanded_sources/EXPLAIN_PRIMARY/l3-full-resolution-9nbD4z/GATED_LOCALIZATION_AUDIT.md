# Gated-field localization: exact identity and its limitation

This note proves an exact \(O(R^{-1})\) signed localization estimate. It
does not yield uniform integrability of the gated middle backward field.
The remaining cross term can cancel the coercive term even when the full
two lower training equations and forward relation hold. The counterexample
is a deterministically forced lower subsystem, not the prescribed
Gaussian-initialized three-hidden-layer network.

## The signed estimate

Use normalized inner products throughout. Write
\[
 z=z^{(2)},\quad h=\arctan z,\quad q=q^{(2)},\quad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(1+z^2)^{-1},\quad \delta=Dq.
\]
The exact lower equations give
\[
 z'=A\delta,\qquad h'=DA\delta,\qquad
 A=m_1I+W^{(2)}D_1^2(W^{(2)})^*,\qquad
 m_1=\|h^{(1)}\|_2^2.
\]
Assume on \([0,S]\) that
\[
 \sup_s\|q(s)\|_2\le M,\qquad
 \int_0^S\|q'(s)\|_2\,ds\le V,\qquad m_1(s)\ge m_*>0.
\]
The established uniform \(L^2\) bound on the exact middle-field derivative
implies this variation bound on a fixed finite interval.

Let
\[
 T_R(v)=v-\max(-R,\min(v,R)),\qquad
 q_R=T_R(q),\quad \delta_R=Dq_R,\quad
 \delta_{\le R}=D(q-q_R).
\]
The absolutely continuous chain rule gives
\(q_R'=\mathbf1_{\{|q|>R\}}q'\) almost everywhere. Therefore
\[
 \begin{split}
 \int_0^S\langle\delta_R,A\delta\rangle\,ds
 &=\int_0^S\langle q_R,h'\rangle\,ds\\
 &=[\langle q_R,h\rangle]_0^S
   -\int_0^S\langle\mathbf1_{\{|q|>R\}}q',h\rangle\,ds
 =:\mathcal E_R.
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Because \(|h|\le a=\pi/2\),
\[
 \|q_R\|_1\le\frac{\|q\|_2^2}{R},
 \qquad
 \|\mathbf1_{\{|q|>R\}}q'\|_1
 \le\frac{\|q\|_2\|q'\|_2}{R}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\mathcal E_R|
 \le\frac{a}{R}
       \big(\|q(S)\|_2^2+\|q(0)\|_2^2+MV\big).                 \tag{2}
\]
Zero readout makes \(q(0)=0\). Thus the signed localized power is small
without a tail hypothesis on \(q\).

If one could deduce
\(\int_0^S\|\delta_R\|_2^2\,ds\to0\) uniformly over width from (1), then
space-time uniform integrability of \(\delta^2\) would follow: on
\(\{|\delta|>2R\}\), one has \(|q|>2R\) and
\(|\delta_R|\ge|\delta|/2\).

## Exact lower-training decomposition of the cross term

Define the actual first-layer velocities generated by the two parts:
\[
 v_R=D_1(W^{(2)})^*\delta_R,\qquad
 v_{\le R}=D_1(W^{(2)})^*\delta_{\le R}.
\]
Then \(z^{(1)\prime}=v_R+v_{\le R}\), and (1) is exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal E_R=\int_0^S\big[
 &m_1\|\delta_R\|_2^2+\|v_R\|_2^2\\
 &+m_1\langle\delta_R,\delta_{\le R}\rangle
       +\langle v_R,v_{\le R}\rangle\big]\,ds.                \tag{3}
 \end{split}
\]
The third term is nonnegative because the two truncation pieces have the
same coordinatewise sign. The last term has no such sign.

This decomposition already includes the rank-one matrix updates:
the localized matrix velocities are
\(\delta_R\otimes h^{(1)}\) and
\(\delta_{\le R}\otimes h^{(1)}\). Their Hilbert--Schmidt inner product is
the nonnegative third term. The only unfavorable term is the interaction
between their shared first-layer velocities.

Integrating that term by parts introduces
\[
 \left((W^{(2)})^*\delta_R\right)'
 =h^{(1)}\langle\delta,\delta_R\rangle
  +(W^{(2)})^*
       \left[Dq_R'
        +\operatorname{diag}(\phi''(z)q_R)A\delta\right].      \tag{4}
\]
The first summand comes from the actual rank update and has no missing
normalization. The last summand contains the mixed localized factor
\(\phi''(z)q_R\,A\delta\). Formula (4) is exact; the present action and
\(q'\) bounds do not bound its pairing with \(h^{(1)}\) by a vanishing
quantity. Treating the rank update as absent would not be a valid
integration-by-parts cancellation.

## A lower-training counterexample, not a full-network counterexample

Here is a family indexed by \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), all defined on one
common nonzero time interval. It satisfies the lower training equations,
the exact forward relation, \(q(0)=0\), positive \(m_1,m_2\), bounded
operators and primal \(L^2\) norms, bounded \(q,q'\) in \(L^2\), and bounded
lower-layer action. Nevertheless \(\delta^2\) is not uniformly integrable,
even after time integration.

Take a first-layer space with two atoms of probability \(1/2\).
In its orthonormal indicator basis, let \(x\in\mathbb R^2\) represent
\(z^{(1)}\). Thus its physical atom values are \(\sqrt2x_j\). Write
\[
 p_j(x)=\frac{\arctan(\sqrt2x_j)}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 D_1(x)=\operatorname{diag}
       \left(\frac1{1+2x_j^2}\right).
\]
The middle space has a rare atom of probability \(\varepsilon\) and a bulk
atom of probability \(1-\varepsilon\), with orthonormal indicators
\[
 u_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{\rm rare},\qquad
 v_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{\rm bulk}.
\]
Represent \(W^{(2)}\) between these orthonormal bases by a matrix \(L\).
For a middle backward coefficient vector \(d\), the lower feature flow is
exactly
\[
 x'=D_1L^\top d,\qquad L'=dp^\top,\qquad
 (Lp)'=Ad,\qquad A=\|p\|^2I+LD_1^2L^\top.                    \tag{5}
\]
Choose
\[
 x(0)=(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2),\qquad
 L(0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
 p(0)=\left(\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt2},\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt2}\right),
 \quad (L(0)p(0))_1=0,\quad A_{12}(0)=\frac14,
 \quad m_1(0)=\frac{\pi^2}{16}>0.
\]
Set \(\alpha(s)=-s\) and close (5) with the deterministic control
\[
 d(s)=\alpha(s)\left(1,-\frac{A_{11}(s)}{A_{12}(s)}\right).
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
This is a smooth finite-dimensional ODE near the initial state. On a
common sufficiently small interval \([0,S_*]\), it has a unique smooth
solution with \(A_{12}\ge1/8\), positive \(m_1\), and bounded \(x,L,d,d'\).
Crucially, neither the ODE nor that interval depends on \(\varepsilon\).
By (5)--(6),
\[
 (Lp)_1'=A_{11}d_1+A_{12}d_2=0,
 \qquad (Lp)_1=0.                                           \tag{7}
\]
Hence the physical rare preactivation is identically zero.

Put \(y=(Lp)_2\), and define the middle backward field by its orthonormal
coefficients:
\[
 q(s)=d_1(s)u_\varepsilon
      +d_2(s)\left(1+\frac{y(s)^2}{1-\varepsilon}\right)
           v_\varepsilon.                                 \tag{8}
\]
The actual middle forward preactivation is
\[
 z^{(2)}(s)=\frac{y(s)}{\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}}
                         \mathbf1_{\rm bulk}.
\]
Equations (7)--(8) imply exactly
\[
 D_2q=d_1u_\varepsilon+d_2v_\varepsilon=\delta.
\]
Thus (5) really is the prescribed lower training flow driven by this
\(q\), with \(z^{(2)}=W^{(2)}h^{(1)}\); it is not merely a factorization
of an arbitrary positive matrix.

Since \(1-\varepsilon\ge1/2\), equation (8) gives uniform bounds on
\(\|q\|_2\) and \(\|q'\|_2\) on \([0,S_*]\). Also \(q(0)=0\).
The primal norms and \(\|W^{(2)}\|_{\rm op}=\|L\|_{\rm op}\) are bounded.
The middle moment stays positive uniformly after shrinking \(S_*\),
because \(y(0)=\pi/(4\sqrt2)>0\).
The full lower action is
\[
 \int_0^{S_*}\big(\|x'\|^2+\|L'\|_F^2\big)\,ds
 =\int_0^{S_*}d^\top A d\,ds=O(S_*^3);
\]
it can be made at most one.

Nevertheless,
\[
 \int_{\rm rare}|\delta(s)|^2\,d\mathbb P=s^2,
 \qquad
 \int_0^{S_*}\int_{\rm rare}|\delta(s)|^2\,d\mathbb P\,ds
       =\frac{S_*^3}{3}.                                    \tag{9}
\]
The exceptional set has probability \(\varepsilon\to0\), so (9) disproves
both fixed-positive-time and space-time uniform integrability for this
class of deterministic lower subsystems.

The choice of control can instead be \(\alpha(s)=-s^k\), or the smooth
function \(\alpha(s)=-e^{-1/s^2}\) for \(s>0\), extended by zero at zero.
The same proof then gives rare energy \(\alpha(s)^2\). In the latter
case every time derivative of \(q\) initially vanishes, but uniform
integrability still fails at every positive time. Thus initial
flatness alone cannot repair this deterministic implication.

The cancellation is explicit. Pairing the rare-only backward contribution
with the full middle velocity gives zero by (7):
\[
 d_1(A_{11}d_1+A_{12}d_2)=0.
\]
The positive rare self-energy is canceled by the shared-layer cross term,
not by a failure of coercivity. For thresholds above the uniformly bounded
bulk \(q\), the same zero pairing holds for \(\delta_R\).

For ordinary equal-width finite vectors, take even \(n\), represent the
first-layer atoms by two groups of \(n/2\) neurons, and take the middle rare
atom to be one neuron, so \(\varepsilon=1/n\). The normalized formulas
then reproduce \(W^{(2)\prime}=\delta(h^{(1)})^\top/n\).

## Precise consequence for the research route

Equations (1)--(3) are useful exact localization information, but no
deterministic implication from those estimates and the full lower rank
updates to gated-field uniform integrability can hold in the stated
generality. The example does not satisfy the independent Gaussian matrix
initialization, and its \(q\) is externally selected rather than generated
by the top-layer training equations. It therefore says nothing negative
about the target network's global continuation.

A successful localization proof must exploit an additional restriction
from that canonical initialization and top-layer feedback. The remaining
specific quantity is the signed first-layer cross term in (3), or an
equivalent estimate that uses this additional structure. Bounds on the
frozen \(q\)-generator, lower action, and \(A\ge m_*I\), by themselves,
cannot supply it.
