**The extension works at every fixed finite depth, including unbounded activations such as softplus, GELU, and SiLU.** The proof gives a positive physical-time interval independent of width and learning rate, and has passed three independent adversarial reviews.

The activation condition is
\[
\phi^{(\ell)}\in C^1(\mathbb R),\qquad
\sup_s|\phi^{(\ell)\prime}(s)|<\infty,\qquad
|\phi^{(\ell)\prime}(s)-\phi^{(\ell)\prime}(t)|
\le D_2|s-t|.
\]
Thus the derivative must be bounded and globally Lipschitz; the activation itself may be unbounded. Different layers may use different activations. Oddness, analyticity, monotonicity, and derivatives bounded away from zero are unnecessary.

The theorem covers the following setting:

- **Architecture:** any fixed number \(L\ge2\) of hidden layers, with
  \[
  z_a^{(1)}=\frac{W^{(1)}x_a}{\sqrt d},\qquad
  z_a^{(\ell)}=W^{(\ell)}h_a^{(\ell-1)},\qquad
  h_a^{(\ell)}=\phi^{(\ell)}(z_a^{(\ell)}),
  \]
  and stored readout
  \[
  f_{n,a}=\frac{(W^{(L+1)})^\top h_a^{(L)}}n.
  \]
- **Data:** any fixed finite dataset with bounded input RMS norms and labels. Inputs may coincide, be parallel, or have a singular Gram matrix.
- **Initialization:** iid centered subGaussian first-weight entries; independent Gaussian middle matrices with entries \(N(0,\sigma_\ell^2/n)\); and any independent iid subGaussian stored readout. This includes zero, vanishing Gaussian, and nonvanishing order-one Gaussian readout.
- **Training:** full-batch GD with stored learning-rate multipliers
  \[
  (n\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\ldots,\kappa_L,n\kappa_{L+1}),
  \]
  and physical time \(t=k\eta_n\).

For weighted squared loss
\[
\mathcal L_n=\sum_a\omega_a(f_{n,a}-y_a)^2,
\qquad \omega_a>0,\qquad \sum_a\omega_a=1,
\]
there is a constant \(T_*>0\), depending only on depth and the stated activation, initialization, input, label, and training bounds, such that
\[
\text{for every }\eta_n>0\text{ with }\eta_n\to0,
\qquad
\sup_{0\le t\le T_*}|f_{n,a}(t)-f_a(t)|
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

There is **no additional restriction relating \(\eta_n\) to width**, such as \(\eta_n\sqrt n\to0\).

Loss and kernel entries also converge uniformly. Joint hidden path distributions converge with second moments in the uniform path norm, and integrated squared hidden speeds converge. The population flow is unique and autonomous, retaining each middle operator and its actual adjoint. Its fields and operators still contain infinitely many scalar degrees of freedom.

With normalized loss weights, the existence time does not depend on the number of examples or a smallest Gram eigenvalue. The dataset remains fixed during convergence; this does not establish convergence for datasets growing with width. For the original summed loss, the corresponding interval is rescaled by the number of examples.

The loss can also be generalized to
\[
\mathcal L_n=\sum_a\omega_a\ell_a(f_{n,a}),
\]
provided the derivatives \(\ell_a'\) have common bounds and Lipschitz constants on bounded prediction intervals. Every \(2r_a\) in the squared-loss updates becomes \(\ell_a'(f_a)\). Convexity is unnecessary for this local theorem.

The crucial proof improvement is the control of accumulated matrix-reuse responses. A perturbation in one backward Gaussian slot contributes a factor
\[
\Delta\omega_b,
\]
and the proof preserves that factor through every later propagation. Consequently, the random growth terms involve weighted time averages rather than maxima over an increasingly fine Gaussian history. This establishes marginal subGaussian bounds uniformly over the Euler mesh.

Those bounds permit localization of the backward products. Each layer adds a cutoff error, while previously accumulated errors are multiplied only by bounded derivatives and operators. The resulting comparison has the form
\[
C e^{C(1+R)T_*}
\left[
(1+R)(\eta_n+\Delta)
+e^{-cR^2}
+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
\right].
\]
Take width to infinity at fixed cutoff \(R\) and coarse mesh \(\Delta\), then send \(\Delta\to0\), and finally \(R\to\infty\). The Gaussian tail defeats the Gronwall factor. This proves the joint limit on a fixed positive interval. The fixed-computation Gaussian matrix/transpose rules used here come from [Tensor Programs III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10685); the mesh-uniform estimates and continuous-time comparison are supplied in the proof.

**Genuine nonlinear feature movement is also established under the broader activation assumptions for two hidden layers.** With Gaussian first initialization, zero limiting readout, positive scales, pairwise nonparallel normalized inputs, nonzero labels, and both activations nonaffine, every input satisfies, on a possibly smaller fixed interval,
\[
\|Z_a^{(\ell)}(t)-Z_a^{(\ell)}(0)\|_{L^2}\asymp t^2,
\qquad
\|H_a^{(\ell)}(t)-H_a^{(\ell)}(0)\|_{L^2}\asymp t^2,
\quad \ell=1,2.
\]
The RMS speeds are proportional to \(t\), the middle matrix changes on every initial feature, the kernel changes, loss strictly decreases, and best-affine-fit errors remain positive.

The existence theorem extends to arbitrary fixed depth; that stronger per-layer activity conclusion has only been proved here for two layers. ReLU, non-Gaussian middle-matrix universality, and all-finite-horizon control remain outside this proof.

**Yes. There are precise conditions guaranteeing exactly this: for every fixed \(T>0\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), a finite Padé order gives uniform error below \(\varepsilon\), even when the original Taylor series diverges for every \(t>0\).** There are also guarantees for Padé applied to the Borel transform.

The important distinction is between:

- **Direct Padé:** a rational approximation to the original function, computed from its initialization coefficients.
- **Borel–Padé:** a rational approximation to its Borel transform, followed by a reconstruction integral.

These procedures can both converge, but their guarantees require different assumptions.

Write
\[
F(t)\sim\sum_{p\ge0}a_p t^p,
\qquad a_p=\frac{F^{(p)}(0)}{p!}
\]
when these derivatives exist. A Padé approximant \([L/M]_F=P_L/Q_M\) satisfies
\[
Q_M(t)\sum_{p\ge0}a_pt^p-P_L(t)
=O(t^{L+M+1}),
\qquad Q_M(0)=1,
\]
with numerator and denominator degrees at most \(L,M\). Thus it uses finitely many coefficients at initialization.

By “not Taylor approximable,” I mean that these Taylor partial sums fail to approximate \(F\). Arbitrary polynomial approximation is a different question: every continuous function on \([0,T]\) admits that, but the approximating polynomials need not be obtainable from its initialization derivatives.

**A concrete genuine Padé guarantee is available for positive Stieltjes integrals.** Suppose the actual function has the representation
\[
F(t)=\int_{[0,R]}\frac{d\mu(x)}{1+tx},
\qquad \mu\ge0,\qquad m_0=\mu([0,R])<\infty.
\]

Then its \([M-1/M]\) Padé approximant has the form
\[
R_M(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{w_j}{1+tx_j},
\qquad w_j>0,\quad x_j\in[0,R].
\]

The nodes and weights are determined by the first \(2M\) initialization coefficients. All poles are negative, and
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}|F(t)-R_M(t)|
\le
2m_0\left(\frac{RT}{2+RT}\right)^{2M}.
\tag{1}
\]

Because \(RT/(2+RT)<1\) for every finite \(T\), this gives your requested guarantee. For \(0<\varepsilon<2m_0\), it suffices to choose
\[
M\ge
\frac{\log(2m_0/\varepsilon)}
{2\log\!\bigl((2+RT)/(RT)\bigr)}.
\]

The mechanism is concrete. The Padé approximant is Gaussian quadrature for the displayed integral, exact on polynomials in \(x\) through degree \(2M-1\). Expand
\[
\frac1{1+tx}
=
\frac1{1+tR/2}
\frac1{1+\dfrac{t(x-R/2)}{1+tR/2}}.
\]
The inner ratio has absolute value at most \(RT/(2+RT)\). Truncating its geometric expansion gives a uniformly small polynomial error; integration and quadrature agree on the polynomial. Their two remaining errors give (1). The Padé–quadrature correspondence is classical. [Allen–Chui–Madych–Narcowich–Smith](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972700043641)

For example,
\[
F(t)=\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+tx}
=\frac{\log(1+t)}{t},\qquad F(0)=1.
\]
Its Taylor series fails for \(t>1\), while (1) guarantees uniform Padé approximation on every finite \([0,T]\).

**Even zero Taylor radius is compatible with uniform Padé convergence.** Consider
\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-x}}{1+tx}\,dx.
\]
Its initialization expansion is
\[
F(t)\sim\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^p p!\,t^p,
\]
which diverges for every \(t>0\). Nevertheless,
\[
[M-1/M]_F\longrightarrow F
\quad\text{uniformly on every }[0,T].
\]

Here the positive measure has unbounded support, but its moments \(m_p=p!\) determine it uniquely. More generally, positive Stieltjes measures with all moments finite and a **determinate moment problem** have this Padé convergence property. Uniformity includes \(t=0\): both the integral and its quadrature approximants have the common Lipschitz bound \(m_1|t-s|\), which upgrades pointwise convergence to uniform convergence on the compact interval. [Simon, Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/9906008)

This example also makes the Borel connection especially transparent:
\[
B(\xi)=\sum_{p\ge0}\frac{(-1)^pp!}{p!}\xi^p
=\frac1{1+\xi},
\qquad
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}B(tu)\,du.
\]
The \([0/1]\) Padé approximant of \(B\) is already exact. Thus both direct Padé and Borel–Padé recover a function with a divergent Taylor series. [Borghi–Weniger](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2474)

**The general link is a uniform approximation theorem for the Borel reconstruction integral.** For some \(\sigma>0\), suppose
\[
B(\xi)=
\sum_{p\ge0}\frac{a_p}{\Gamma(1+\sigma p)}\xi^p
\]
converges near zero, continues along the positive ray, and the **actual target** satisfies
\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}B(tu^\sigma)\,du.
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose Padé approximants \(B_M\), computed from finitely many transformed coefficients, converge uniformly to \(B\) on every finite positive-ray segment. Also suppose the true integral has a uniformly small tail:
\[
E(U):=
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\int_U^\infty e^{-u}|B(tu^\sigma)|\,du
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Then the finite-cutoff reconstruction satisfies the elementary bound
\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\left|
F(t)-\int_0^U e^{-u}B_M(tu^\sigma)\,du
\right|
\le
E(U)
+
\sup_{0\le\xi\le TU^\sigma}|B(\xi)-B_M(\xi)|.
\tag{3}
\]

This proves the desired quantifiers:

1. Choose a finite \(U\) to make the tail small.
2. Choose a finite \(M\) to approximate \(B\) on \([0,TU^\sigma]\).
3. Approximate the remaining integral by finite quadrature.

The resulting expression
\[
\widetilde F(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}w_jB_M(tu_j^\sigma)
\]
is a finite rational function of \(t\), with coefficients obtained from initialization data and the declared quadrature rule. It generally is **not** the conventional Padé approximant of the original series: Padé matching was imposed on \(B\).

For an explicit tail condition, it suffices that
\[
|B(\xi)|\le C e^{b\xi^{1/\sigma}},
\qquad b\ge0,\qquad bT^{1/\sigma}<1.
\]
Then \(E(U)\le C e^{-\lambda U}/\lambda\), where \(\lambda=1-bT^{1/\sigma}>0\).

**There are explicit coefficient conditions that make this Borel–Padé argument work.** One sufficient condition is that
\[
B(\xi)=\int_{[0,R]}\frac{d\nu(x)}{1+\xi x},
\qquad \nu\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]

Now the earlier Padé theorem applies to \(B\), and both \(B\) and its approximants are bounded by \(\nu_0=\nu([0,R])\) on the entire positive ray. Assuming (2), their full Laplace reconstructions \(F_M\) satisfy
\[
\sup_{t\le T}|F(t)-F_M(t)|
\le
2\nu_0
\left[
\left(\frac{RTU^\sigma}{2+RTU^\sigma}\right)^{2M}
+e^{-U}
\right].
\tag{5}
\]
Again, choose \(U\), then \(M\). This works for every finite \(T\).

Condition (4) can be expressed directly in the coefficients. Define
\[
s_p=\frac{(-1)^p a_p}
{\Gamma(1+\sigma p)R^p}.
\]
A sufficient—and equivalent compact-moment—condition is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}s_{p+j}\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }p,k\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
These inequalities say that \(s_p\) are moments of a finite positive measure on \([0,1]\); rescaling gives (4). They are **all-order conditions**, so finitely many successful coefficient tests do not establish the theorem. [Liu–Pego on Hausdorff moment sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.8052)

There is an additional direct connection: under (2)–(4), when \(0<\sigma\le2\), genuine \([M-1/M]\) Padé approximants of \(F\) itself also converge uniformly. Indeed, the double integral represents \(F\) as a Stieltjes integral with moments bounded by
\[
\nu_0R^p\Gamma(1+\sigma p).
\]
The Stieltjes Carleman criterion applies because its terms are bounded below by a constant times \(p^{-\sigma/2}\), whose sum diverges for \(\sigma\le2\). Thus the measure is determinate. For larger \(\sigma\), this particular argument stops; the Borel–Padé guarantee (5) still holds.

**Positivity is sufficient, but it is not necessary.** Two other useful routes are:

- **Meromorphic continuation:** if \(F\) is analytic near zero and extends to \(|t|<r\) with exactly \(q\) poles, then the fixed-denominator Padé sequence \([L/q]\), \(L\to\infty\), converges uniformly on compact subsets avoiding those poles. Hence it covers \([0,T]\), \(T<r\), even beyond the Taylor radius. This is the de Montessus theorem. [Precise statement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.2774)
- **A known analytic domain for \(B\):** a conformal change of variable can give a guaranteed finite-jet rational construction. For example, if \(B\) is analytic in \(\operatorname{Re}\xi>-a\), put
  \[
  w=\frac{\xi}{\xi+2a},
  \qquad
  B\!\left(\frac{2aw}{1-w}\right)=\sum_{p\ge0}g_pw^p.
  \]
  Then \(\sum_{p=0}^{M}g_p[\xi/(\xi+2a)]^p\) converges uniformly on every finite positive-ray segment. Each \(g_p\) uses only the first \(p+1\) Borel coefficients. Combining this with (3) gives the desired finite rational approximation without Stieltjes positivity. This is a conformal rational construction, rather than conventional diagonal Padé. [Costin–Dunne, Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.01962)

For our population GF problem, these results provide **specific sufficient assumptions that could close the reconstruction conjecture**. We would need to establish the relevant conditions for the actual kernel or prediction observable. Existence of the GF, smoothness, or a factorial coefficient bound alone does not establish them. In particular, equality with the Borel sum in (2) remains essential: initialization derivatives cannot detect an added nonzero flat function.

and

\[
v_t(x)=\frac{t(x-R/2)}{A_t}.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{1}{1+tx}
=
\frac{1}{A_t}\frac{1}{1+v_t(x)},
\]

with

\[
|v_t(x)|\le\frac{tR}{2+tR}<1.
\]

Truncating the geometric expansion in \(v_t(x)\) gives a uniformly accurate polynomial in \(x\). Integration and Gaussian quadrature agree on that polynomial; their remaining errors give the bound above. The Padé–quadrature correspondence is classical. [Allen–Chui–Madych–Narcowich–Smith](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972700043641)

For example,

\[
F(t)=\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+tx}
=\frac{\log(1+t)}{t},
\]

with \(F(0)=1\). Its Taylor series fails for \(t>1\), while these Padé approximants converge uniformly on every finite interval \([0,T]\).

**Even a Taylor series with zero convergence radius can admit uniform Padé approximation.**

Consider

\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-x}}{1+tx}\,dx.
\]

Its initialization expansion is

\[
F(t)\sim
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^p p!\,t^p.
\]

This series diverges for every \(t>0\). Nevertheless,

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left|[M-1/M]_F(t)-F(t)\right|
\longrightarrow0
\]

for every finite \(T\).

Here the measure has unbounded support, but its moments \(m_p=p!\) determine it uniquely. More generally, positive Stieltjes measures with every moment finite and a **determinate moment problem** have this Padé convergence property.

The endpoint \(t=0\) is included: the integral and its quadrature approximants share a Lipschitz bound controlled by the first moment. Pointwise convergence plus this common bound gives uniform convergence on the compact interval. [Simon, Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/9906008)

This example also shows the Borel connection. Its ordinary Borel transform is

\[
B(\xi)
=
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^pp!}{p!}\xi^p
=
\frac{1}{1+\xi}.
\]

The original function is recovered by

\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}B(tu)\,du.
\]

The \([0/1]\) Padé approximant of \(B\) is already exact. Thus both direct Padé and Borel–Padé recover a function whose Taylor series diverges everywhere except at zero. [Borghi–Weniger](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2474)

**The general Borel connection is a uniform approximation theorem for the reconstruction integral.**

For some \(\sigma>0\), define

\[
B(\xi)=
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}
\frac{a_p}{\Gamma(1+\sigma p)}\xi^p.
\]

Suppose this series converges near zero, admits continuation along the positive ray, and the **actual target function** satisfies

\[
F(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-u}B(tu^\sigma)\,du.
\]

Suppose further that Padé approximants \(B_M\), computed from finitely many transformed coefficients, converge uniformly to \(B\) on every finite positive-ray segment.

We also need a uniformly small tail. Define

\[
E(U)=
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\int_U^\infty e^{-u}|B(tu^\sigma)|\,du,
\]

and assume

\[
E(U)\longrightarrow0.
\]

For a finite cutoff \(U\), define

\[
F_{M,U}(t)=
\int_0^U e^{-u}B_M(tu^\sigma)\,du.
\]

Subtracting the actual reconstruction integral gives

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}|F(t)-F_{M,U}(t)|
\le E(U)+D_M(TU^\sigma),
\]

where

\[
D_M(A)=
\sup_{0\le\xi\le A}|B(\xi)-B_M(\xi)|.
\]

This establishes the desired finite-order guarantee:

1. Choose a finite \(U\) to make the tail small.
2. Choose a finite \(M\) to approximate \(B\) on the required finite segment.
3. Approximate the remaining integral by finite quadrature.

The resulting expression is

\[
\widetilde F(t)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{J}w_jB_M(tu_j^\sigma).
\]

It is a finite rational function of \(t\), constructed from initialization coefficients and the declared quadrature rule.

It generally is **not** the conventional Padé approximant of the original series: the Padé matching conditions were imposed on \(B\).

An explicit sufficient tail bound is

\[
|B(\xi)|\le C e^{b\xi^{1/\sigma}},
\qquad b\ge0.
\]

For any \(T\) satisfying

\[
bT^{1/\sigma}<1,
\]

put

\[
\lambda=1-bT^{1/\sigma}>0.
\]

Then

\[
E(U)\le\frac{C}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda U}.
\]

**Explicit conditions on the Borel coefficients can guarantee that this procedure works.**

One sufficient condition is that the Borel transform itself has a positive Stieltjes representation:

\[
B(\xi)=
\int_{[0,R]}\frac{d\nu(x)}{1+\xi x}.
\]

Write

\[
\nu_0=\nu([0,R]).
\]

The earlier Padé theorem now applies to \(B\). Both \(B\) and its Padé approximants are bounded by \(\nu_0\) on the entire positive ray.

Let

\[
F_M(t)=
\int_0^\infty e^{-u}B_M(tu^\sigma)\,du.
\]

For a chosen cutoff \(U>0\), define

\[
q_U=\frac{RTU^\sigma}{2+RTU^\sigma}.
\]

Then

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}|F(t)-F_M(t)|
\le
2\nu_0\left(q_U^{2M}+e^{-U}\right).
\]

Choose \(U\) to control the second term, then \(M\) to control the first. This works for every finite \(T\).

The positive-measure condition can be expressed directly in the coefficients. Define

\[
s_p=
\frac{(-1)^p a_p}
{\Gamma(1+\sigma p)R^p}.
\]

A sufficient—and equivalent compact-moment—condition is

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{k}
(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}s_{p+j}
\ge0
\]

for every pair of integers \(p,k\ge0\).

These inequalities say that \(s_p\) are the moments of a finite positive measure on \([0,1]\). Rescaling gives the required representation of \(B\). They are **all-order conditions**: checking finitely many of them does not establish the theorem. [Liu–Pego on Hausdorff moment sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.8052)

There is an additional direct connection. Under these assumptions, when

\[
0<\sigma\le2,
\]

genuine \([M-1/M]\) Padé approximants of \(F\) itself also converge uniformly on every finite \([0,T]\).

To see why, the reconstruction formula becomes

\[
F(t)=
\int_{[0,R]}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-u}}{1+tu^\sigma x}\,du\,d\nu(x).
\]

This is another positive Stieltjes integral, with moments satisfying

\[
\widetilde m_p
\le
\nu_0R^p\Gamma(1+\sigma p).
\]

The terms in the Stieltjes Carleman criterion obey, for large \(p\),

\[
\widetilde m_p^{-1/(2p)}
\ge c\,p^{-\sigma/2}.
\]

Their sum diverges when \(\sigma\le2\). Therefore the measure is determinate, giving direct Padé convergence. For larger \(\sigma\), this particular argument stops; the Borel–Padé guarantee above remains valid.

**Positivity is sufficient, but it is not necessary.**

One alternative is **meromorphic continuation**. If \(F\) is analytic near zero and extends to the disk \(|t|<r\) with exactly \(q\) poles, then the fixed-denominator sequence

\[
[L/q]_F,\qquad L\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

converges uniformly on compact subsets avoiding those poles.

Hence it covers \([0,T]\), with \(T<r\), even when nearby complex poles make the Taylor radius smaller than \(T\). This is the de Montessus theorem. [Precise statement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.2774)

Another alternative is a **known analytic domain for the Borel transform**. Suppose \(B\) is analytic in

\[
\operatorname{Re}\xi>-a,
\qquad a>0.
\]

Use the change of variable

\[
w=\frac{\xi}{\xi+2a}.
\]

The transformed function is analytic in the unit disk:

\[
B\!\left(\frac{2aw}{1-w}\right)
=
\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}g_pw^p.
\]

Consequently, the rational functions

\[
R_M(\xi)=
\sum_{p=0}^{M}
g_p\left(\frac{\xi}{\xi+2a}\right)^p
\]

converge uniformly on every finite positive-ray segment. Each \(g_p\) uses only the first \(p+1\) Borel coefficients.

Combining this construction with the finite-cutoff argument gives uniform approximation of \(F\) from finitely many initialization coefficients, without Stieltjes positivity. This is a **conformal rational construction**, rather than conventional diagonal Padé. [Costin–Dunne, Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.01962)

For our population GF problem, these theorems provide specific sufficient assumptions that could establish the reconstruction conjecture. We would need to prove the relevant properties for the actual kernel or prediction observable.

GF existence, smoothness, or a factorial coefficient bound alone does not establish those properties. In particular, **identifying the actual population observable with its Borel sum remains essential**: initialization derivatives cannot detect an added nonzero flat function.

The complete statements and proofs are in [Uniform Padé and Borel approximation from initialization jets](/tmp/pde-gf-supervisor-worktree/PADE_BOREL_UNIFORM_APPROXIMATION.md).
