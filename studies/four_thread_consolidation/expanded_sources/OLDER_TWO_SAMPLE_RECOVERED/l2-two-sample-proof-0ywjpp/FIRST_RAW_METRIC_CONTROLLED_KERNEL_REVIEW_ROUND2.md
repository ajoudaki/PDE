## 1. Minimal interpretation and exact ordinary-norm scaling

Let d be the fixed input dimension, T a fixed finite time horizon, and n the number of first-layer rows. Write

    X = [x_1,x_2],       X^T X = d C,
    C = [[1,rho],[rho,1]].

To avoid confusing the dimension d with the vector d_{n,i}, write q_i = d_{n,i} temporarily. All vector norms in this review are Euclidean; matrix norms are Frobenius unless an operator norm is specified. The prescribed row evolution and evaluation are

    dot W_i = X q_i / d,       z_i = X^T W_i.

Since X is constant and W_i is absolutely continuous, differentiation gives, at least almost everywhere,

    v_i = dot z_i = X^T dot W_i = C q_i.

The ordinary row speed satisfies

    |dot W_i|^2
      = q_i^T X^T X q_i / d^2
      = q_i^T C q_i / d.

Consequently

    (d/n) ||dot W_n^(1)||_F^2
      = (1/n) sum_i q_i^T C q_i.                 (R1)

These verify source (1) and its matrix normalization exactly. In particular, the ordinary, unnormalized matrix speed is n/d times the right-hand side of (R1). The phrase “raw first-matrix kinetic energy” in source (3) refers to the displayed d/n-weighted ordinary Frobenius speed; there is no additional normalized norm hidden inside the Frobenius notation.

When -1 < rho < 1,

    D = C^(-1)
      = 1/(1-rho^2) [[1,-rho],[-rho,1]],
    q_i = D v_i,
    dot W_i = A v_i,       A := X D / d.

Thus

    d |dot W_i|^2 = v_i^T D v_i
      = (v_{i,1}^2 - 2 rho v_{i,1}v_{i,2} + v_{i,2}^2)
        /(1-rho^2).                                (R2)

The least eigenvalue of C is 1-|rho|. Hence

    ||D||_op = 1/(1-|rho|),
    A^T A = D/d,
    ||A||_op^2 = 1/[d(1-|rho|)].

The constants are finite for each fixed interior angle. They do not give uniform bounds as an endpoint is approached. Source lines 33–34 are correct; no limit in rho is being taken in the lemma.

## 2. Antiparallel reconstruction and the factor of two

Accept precisely the source's antiparallel premise, including equality of deltas and opposition of controls at every relevant raw state or GD node. Odd activations alone are not being used here as an independent theorem about an unspecified architecture.

At rho = -1,

    x_2 = -x_1,
    C = [[1,-1],[-1,1]],
    C^2 = 2C,
    D = C^+ = C/4.

The range of C is E_- = span{(1,-1)}, its nonzero eigenvalue is 2, and its nullspace is span{(1,1)}. The accepted invariance gives

    q_i = (b_i,-b_i),
    v_i = C q_i = (2b_i,-2b_i),
    D v_i = q_i.

In particular, all three equations of source (2), including recovery of q_i itself, remain valid with D = C^+. Directly,

    dot W_i = (2b_i/d) x_1,
    d |dot W_i|^2 = 4b_i^2,
    |v_i|^2 = 8b_i^2,
    v_i^T D v_i = |v_i|^2/2 = 4b_i^2.           (R3)

This is the speed of the one actual row driven by both samples. There is no second row energy to add and no extra factor of two.

Also,

    D C D = D,        A^T A = D/d,
    ||D||_op = 1/2,   ||A||_op^2 = 1/(2d).

The anti-invariance is particularly important for the full controlled kernel. Without it, q_i could have an undetected nullspace component: C(q_i+s(1,1)) = Cq_i. Row velocity and row energy would still be reconstructible by the pseudoinverse, but the individual products q_{i,a}q_{i,b} would not be determined by v_i. For example, q=(1,1) has v=0 while C_ab q_a q_b forms the nonzero matrix C; its entry sum is zero. This example is excluded by the accepted anti-invariance and explains why that premise is sufficient and substantive.

Under the actual accepted premise, the contribution of each antiparallel row to the controlled kernel is

    C_ab q_{i,a}q_{i,b} = b_i^2 [[1,1],[1,1]]_ab.

The sum of its four entries is 4b_i^2, agreeing with (R3). The source makes no claim at rho=1, and none is needed for this verdict.

## 3. The W2 premise and what it preserves

Use the source's state space

    E = C([0,T];R^2) x L2([0,T];R^2),
    dist((z,v),(z',v'))^2
      = ||z-z'||_infty^2 + ||v-v'||_L2^2.

Interpret W2 in its usual domain P2(E), the probability measures with finite second moment for this metric. Thus the limiting law mu is a P2 law. L2 elements are equivalence classes, not functions supplied with fixed-time evaluation maps.

From W2(mu_n,mu) -> 0, choose couplings pi_n of mu_n and mu with

    epsilon_n^2
      := E_pi_n[||z_n-z||_infty^2 + ||v_n-v||_L2^2]
      -> 0.

There is no need for an optimal coupling, a common coupling for every n, or a labeling of limiting particles. Near-minimizers of the defining infimum suffice.

Put

    M_n = (E_mu_n ||v||_L2^2)^(1/2),
    M   = (E_mu   ||v||_L2^2)^(1/2).

The triangle inequality in the coupling's L2 space gives

    |M_n-M| <= (E_pi_n ||v_n-v||_L2^2)^(1/2)
             <= epsilon_n.

Therefore M_n -> M and the uniformly bounded integrated mean-square velocity asserted in lines 54–56 indeed follows from W2 convergence. It is not an independent compactness mechanism.

The evolution relation also survives this convergence. Define

    R(z,v)(t) = z(t)-z(0)-integral_0^t v(s) ds.

The integral is well-defined since L2 is contained in L1 on a finite time interval, and

    ||R(z,v)-R(z',v')||_infty
      <= 2||z-z'||_infty + sqrt(T)||v-v'||_L2.

Every empirical atom has R=0, by the given integral evolution. Consequently

    E_mu ||R(z,v)||_infty^2
      <= (4+T) epsilon_n^2 -> 0.

Thus mu-almost every pair satisfies z(t)=z(0)+integral_0^t v(s)ds for every t. Its position path is absolutely continuous with weak derivative v, and that derivative agrees with the ordinary derivative almost everywhere. This does not assert differentiability at every time.

At rho=-1, the same coupling argument applied to projection onto E_- perpendicular shows that limiting z paths take values in E_- at every time and limiting v paths lie in L2([0,T];E_-). The first assertion uses the supremum norm for continuous positions; the second uses the L2 norm. Thus the limit preserves the range constraint needed for the antiparallel interpretation.

## 4. Reconstruction in the product topology

In both accepted cases q_i=Dv_i almost everywhere, so

    W_i(t)-W_i(0)
      = integral_0^t A v_i(s) ds
      = A[z_i(t)-z_i(0)]                         (R4)

for every t. Equality of the velocities is an L2 equality, hence an a.e. equality for representatives. Define

    F(z,v) = (A[z(.)-z(0)], Av(.))

with values in C([0,T];R^d) x L2([0,T];R^d). For two input pairs,

    ||A[(z-z')-(z(0)-z'(0))]||_infty
      <= 2||A||_op ||z-z'||_infty,
    ||A(v-v')||_L2 <= ||A||_op ||v-v'||_L2.

Therefore F is bounded linear and Lipschitz, with a valid squared Lipschitz constant 4||A||_op^2 for the stated product metrics. Pushing pi_n through F in both coordinates gives

    W2(F_#mu_n,F_#mu)^2
      <= 4||A||_op^2 epsilon_n^2 -> 0.            (R5)

The empirical law F_#mu_n is exactly the empirical law of actual raw row increments and velocities, by (R4). The limiting law remains supported on compatible increment/velocity pairs. This proves lines 59–71 in the claimed topology, and in fact explicitly in its quadratic-Wasserstein metric.

The limitation concerning full rows is correct. With

    P = X D X^T/d,

P is the orthogonal projection onto span{x_1,x_2}; this follows from symmetry, DCD=D, and its action on the columns of X. Since dot W_i belongs to this span,

    W_i(t) = A z_i(t) + (I-P)W_i(0).

The second term is constant and invisible to z and v. Full-row convergence requires suitable *joint* convergence including this initial orthogonal component, with the necessary second moments. Knowing its separate marginal law would not generally supply the missing dependence. The source correctly requests its inclusion in the joint law.

## 5. No first-matrix energy defect

For an L2 velocity path define the scalar L1 function

    Q(v)(t) = v(t)^T D v(t).

D is symmetric and positive semidefinite in both cases. In the antiparallel case it is positive definite on the supported range E_-. For any v,w,

    ||Q(v)-Q(w)||_L1
      <= ||D||_op ||v-w||_L2 (||v||_L2+||w||_L2),
    ||Q(v)||_L1 <= ||D||_op ||v||_L2^2.          (R6)

These follow by expanding the difference of quadratic forms and applying Cauchy--Schwarz in time. The quadratic growth bound makes the L1-valued expectations below integrable. By (R1)–(R3), the finite-width speed density is

    g_n(t) := (d/n)||dot W_n^(1)(t)||_F^2
            = E_mu_n Q(v)(t)

almost everywhere. Let g=E_mu Q(v), understood first as an L1-valued expectation. Coupling, the norm bound for an expectation, and Cauchy--Schwarz give

    ||g_n-g||_L1
      <= E_pi_n ||Q(v_n)-Q(v)||_L1
      <= ||D||_op epsilon_n (M_n+M)
      -> 0.                                      (R7)

Thus the source's total-energy identity (3) holds, with no defect. More strongly, the speed densities converge in L1. For every Lebesgue-measurable A contained in [0,T],

    |integral_A g_n - integral_A g|
      <= ||g_n-g||_L1 -> 0.

This verifies lines 80–81 and shows that no exceptional choice of a fixed measurable time subset is needed.

The full W2 premise matters. For an analytic illustration, take a fixed nonzero L2 path u in an admissible range, set Z(t)=integral_0^t u(s)ds, and define empirical laws with n atoms by

    nu_n = (1-1/n) delta_(0,0)
           + (1/n) delta_(sqrt(n)Z,sqrt(n)u).

These laws converge weakly to delta_(0,0) and have bounded, nonvanishing mean velocity energy. Their mean value of integral Q is the positive constant integral u^T D u. They do not converge in W2 to delta_(0,0). This is a direct mathematical example, not an experiment or a purported training trajectory. It demonstrates why bounded mean energy alone does not prove the no-defect conclusion.

## 6. Full-matrix node-controlled kernel in L1

Controls are sample-dependent and common to all rows at a given width and time. Therefore their factors can be moved inside the finite sum, giving exactly

    c_{n,a}c_{n,b} K^(1)_{n,ab}
      = C_ab (1/n) sum_i d_{n,i,a}d_{n,i,b}.

In both accepted cases d_{n,i}=Dv_{n,i} almost everywhere. Define

    H(v)(t) = C Hadamard [(Dv(t))(Dv(t))^T],

where Hadamard denotes entrywise matrix multiplication. Its entries are C_ab(Dv)_a(Dv)_b. Then

    J_n = E_mu_n H(v)

as matrix-valued L1 functions. This uses actual instantaneous controls/deltas for GF and the held node controls/deltas for raw affine GD.

For any two vectors e,f in R^2,

    ee^T-ff^T = (e-f)e^T + f(e-f)^T,
    ||ee^T-ff^T||_F <= |e-f|(|e|+|f|).

Since |C_ab|<=1, entrywise multiplication by C does not increase the Frobenius norm. Applying the preceding bound with e=Dv(t), f=Dw(t), then integrating, proves

    ||H(v)-H(w)||_L1(F)
      <= ||D||_op^2 ||v-w||_L2 (||v||_L2+||w||_L2),
    ||H(v)||_L1(F) <= ||D||_op^2 ||v||_L2^2.      (R8)

Here L1(F) means integration of the matrix Frobenius norm. Thus H is continuous from L2 into matrix-valued L1 and is integrable against the velocity marginal of every P2 law. Define J=E_mu H(v) in that Banach space. The same coupling yields the direct full-matrix estimate

    ||J_n-J||_L1(F)
      <= E_pi_n ||H(v_n)-H(v)||_L1(F)
      <= ||D||_op^2 epsilon_n (M_n+M)
      -> 0.                                      (R9)

This proves convergence of all four entries simultaneously, including both off-diagonal entries. No convergence of c_n, of delta_n separately, or of an unweighted reverse field is needed.

The source's entrywise bound in lines 103–109 is also correct as written: expand

    e_{n,a}e_{n,b}-e_a e_b
      = (e_{n,a}-e_a)e_{n,b} + e_a(e_{n,b}-e_b),

move the absolute value inside the coupling expectation, and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to each term on time times coupling. Bounding each coordinate norm by its vector norm gives exactly the displayed right-hand side. Multiplication by |C_ab|<=1 completes its application to J.

Summing every entry, not just the diagonal, gives

    sum_ab H(v)_ab
      = (Dv)^T C(Dv)
      = v^T D C D v
      = v^T D v.                                (R10)

Taking expectations gives sum_ab J_n,ab=g_n and sum_ab J_ab=g. The identity DCD=D is valid both for the inverse and for this pseudoinverse. In general the trace alone would omit cross terms and would not be the claimed speed. The source correctly says to sum the entries.

As an additional algebraic check, H(v) is positive semidefinite: for any y in R^2,

    y^T H(v)y = (y Hadamard Dv)^T C(y Hadamard Dv) >= 0.

Hence J_n and an a.e. representative of J are positive semidefinite. This does not replace the full-matrix proof and does not give separate control of the unweighted deltas.

## 7. L2 representatives, almost-everywhere time statements, and nodes

The definition J=E_mu H(v) is mathematically sufficient on its own. The continuous map H takes values in the separable space L1([0,T];R^(2x2)), and (R8) gives an integrable norm bound. Thus its Bochner expectation exists. Changing the time representative of any L2 path leaves H(v) unchanged as an L1 element.

To interpret formula (5) pointwise, one may choose a jointly measurable representative of the random L2 velocity. One construction uses piecewise time averages on successively finer partitions: each averaged path depends measurably on its L2 input through bounded linear averaging functionals; the averages converge in L2 for each input and in L2(mu times dt), using the finite mean squared L2 norm. Their limit supplies the required representative, whose sections represent the original paths for mu-almost every input. Then

    integral_0^T E_mu |v(t)|^2 dt = E_mu ||v||_L2^2 < infinity.

Consequently E_mu|v(t)|^2 is finite for almost every t, and for those times the product in (5) is integrable. Interchanging expectation and time integration for these integrable products identifies its a.e. representative with the Bochner expectation. Different such representative choices give the same L1 element and the same formula outside a time-null set.

This establishes all the evaluation claims actually needed in lines 94–112. It does not make evaluation v -> v(t_0) a well-defined continuous functional on L2 at any prescribed t_0. It also does not transfer every-time regularity of finite-width classical GF velocities to the limit.

L1 convergence need not give a.e. convergence of the full sequence. To see the precise issue using only an analytic construction, take T=1 and C=I. For n=2^k+j with 0<=j<2^k, set

    I_n = [j/2^k,(j+1)/2^k),
    v_n(t) = (1_{I_n}(t),0),
    z_n(t) = integral_0^t v_n(s)ds,
    mu_n = delta_(z_n,v_n).

Each mu_n can be written as an empirical law with n identical atoms. We have

    ||z_n||_infty <= 2^(-k),
    ||v_n||_L2^2 = 2^(-k),

so mu_n -> delta_(0,0) in the exact joint W2 topology. Yet H(v_n)_11=1_{I_n}. For every t in [0,1), this entry equals 1 once in each block of level k and equals 0 at many other indices. It does not converge pointwise, although its L1 norm tends to zero. These paths satisfy the integral relation and admit the prescribed raw row reconstruction. This example concerns the topology used by the lemma; it is not asserted to arise from an independently specified neural-network loss.

A subsequence with a.e. kernel convergence can be obtained: choose indices n_k for which ||J_n_k-J||_L1(F)<=2^(-k). The integral of sum_k ||J_n_k(t)-J(t)||_F is finite, so that sum is finite for a.e. t, and in particular the summands tend to zero there. This is a subsequence conclusion, not a property of the original full sequence.

For GD, affine-cell derivatives are constant inside each cell and may jump at its endpoints. Assigning arbitrary values at the finite or countable set of nodes does not affect any L2 velocity class, L1 kernel class, or integrated energy identity here. The source correctly uses held node deltas and controls throughout each cell. A kernel recomputed from the current nonlinear forward/reverse fields inside that cell is a different observable and is not covered by (R9).

## 8. Deterministic, subsequential, and random scopes

**Deterministic full-sequence premise.** If the deterministic empirical measures mu_n converge in W2 to mu, the limit mu is deterministic and the full sequence of each displayed observable converges to the corresponding deterministic functional of mu. A deterministic probability measure need not be a Dirac measure: it can encode a distribution of neuron paths without being a random measure. Source lines 121–123 correctly distinguish these notions.

**Only subsequential W2 compactness.** For any selected subsequence along which mu_n -> mu in W2, (R5), (R7), and (R9) hold along that same subsequence. Different subsequences can have different joint laws and kernels. Compactness itself gives neither full-sequence convergence nor uniqueness of a population equation. The sentence about different subsequences in the source is valid in this compactness-only branch; under its earlier full-sequence convergence premise the W2 limit is already unique as a metric limit.

**Explicitly random measures.** Suppose mu_n and mu are random P2(E)-valued variables on a common probability space and

    W2(mu_n,mu) -> 0 in probability.

The relevant maps of measures are continuous in W2. Indeed the pushforward map is Lipschitz by (R5), and taking arbitrarily close couplings in (R7) and (R9) gives deterministic bounds in terms of W2 and the two velocity second-moment roots. The moment-root inequality above gives

    M(mu_n) <= M(mu) + W2(mu_n,mu).

Since M(mu) is finite almost surely, these roots are bounded in probability. Thus the right-hand sides for the energy and kernel are a factor tending to zero in probability times a factor bounded in probability. They tend to zero in probability. Concretely, the conclusions are

    W2(F_#mu_n,F_#mu) -> 0 in probability,
    ||J_n-J||_L1(F) -> 0 in probability,
    ||g_n-g||_L1 -> 0 in probability,

and hence convergence in probability of the integrated energies, including on fixed measurable time subsets. This verifies the continuous-map claim in lines 124–126 without adding an a.s. convergence or an expectation-convergence claim.

If mu is random, J can be random. If the supplied probabilistic premise has a deterministic mu, J is deterministic. Determinism of the limit cannot be inferred merely because the observable is defined by an expectation with respect to a random empirical measure.

Bare convergence in probability supplies no convergence of the expectations over external randomness. For example, make the random law equal to delta_(sqrt(n)Z,sqrt(n)u) with probability 1/n and delta_(0,0) otherwise, with fixed nonzero u and Z as above. Its W2 distance to delta_(0,0) tends to zero in probability; its expected integrated Q-energy stays equal to integral u^T D u. Additional integrability assumptions could rule this out, but are not consequences of the probability mode alone.

Compact containment also cannot be promoted to convergence in probability or uniqueness. Even a deterministic sequence alternating between two different point-mass path laws is contained in a finite compact set and fails to converge. No claim in the source supplies a mechanism identifying all possible limits. Source lines 127–129 correctly retain that obligation.

## 9. Gradient flow versus gradient descent loss

The source proves the algebraic identity

    sum_ab J_n,ab(t) = (d/n)||dot W_n^(1)(t)||_F^2

almost everywhere. Its validity uses the raw first-row update and the chosen controls. It does not require the derivative of a loss along an interpolation.

For a differentiable loss L and a full classical gradient flow

    dot theta(t) = -G_n^(-1) grad L(theta(t))

with a specified fixed positive definite metric matrix G_n, the chain rule gives

    d/dt L(theta(t))
      = grad L(theta(t)) dot dot theta(t)
      = -dot theta(t)^T G_n dot theta(t).

Identifying the first-layer contribution with the displayed d/n-weighted speed would require the first metric block to be (d/n)I and the stated loss-gradient normalization to agree with that convention. The isolated source does not specify all parameter blocks, the complete loss, or the normalization relating its deltas to prediction derivatives. Accordingly, this review certifies its raw-speed normalization from the given update, not a new total-loss identity for an unspecified model.

For raw affine GD, let theta_k be the node and let the held velocity be

    u_k = -G_n^(-1) grad L(theta_k),
    theta(t) = theta_k + (t-t_k)u_k

inside the cell. At differentiability times the chain rule instead gives

    d/dt L(theta(t)) = grad L(theta(t)) dot u_k,

whereas its squared metric speed is u_k^T G_n u_k. Equality with negative squared speed would require

    [grad L(theta(t))-grad L(theta_k)] dot u_k = 0,

which generally fails. For example, for L(theta)=theta^2/2, G_n=1, theta_k=1 and u_k=-1, the cell path theta(t)=1-t has loss derivative -(1-t) and squared speed 1. They agree with the negative-speed identity at t=0 and disagree at interior positive times.

Thus source lines 113–117 make the correct distinction. The note also correctly avoids identifying the held-node controlled kernel with a recomputed interpolation kernel or asserting that their difference vanishes. Such assertions need additional regularity and discretization estimates absent from the premises.

## 10. Unweighted and mean-field exclusions

The proof recovers d_{n,i}=c_n Hadamard delta_{n,i} and its second products. It never divides by a control. If c_{n,a}=0, information about delta_{n,a,i} can disappear entirely from the controlled observable. Even at nonzero limiting controls, recovering an unweighted L1 kernel from a sequence would need suitable control of the reciprocal factors and their convergence; these assumptions are not supplied. Lines 131–132 correctly deny the conclusion where a control vanishes.

Nothing in the given law of the two first-layer evaluations and their velocities identifies a population force equation, determines second-layer dynamics, proves a higher reverse-field moment estimate, establishes a uniform estimate over angles, identifies node and recomputed GD kernels, or proves population uniqueness. The reconstructed law and its moments are conditional observables of the supplied W2 limit. They do not establish that actual random network trajectories supply this premise, nor that a putative limit solves a mean-field equation.

The bounded lemma therefore passes within its explicit fixed-input, first-layer, controlled-observable scope. No actual GF/GD/MF convergence theorem is certified.
