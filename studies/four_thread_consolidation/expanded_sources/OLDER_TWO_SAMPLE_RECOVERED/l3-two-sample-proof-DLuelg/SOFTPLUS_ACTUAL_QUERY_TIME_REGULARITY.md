# Actual-query time regularity for the uncut softplus references

Analytical sidecar, 2026-09-06. No experiments or auxiliary agents.

The only mathematical project inputs used here are
`SOFTPLUS_ENERGY_PRESERVING_OPERATOR_REFERENCES.md` and
`MIDDLE_CURVATURE_MODE_ACTION.md`, in this directory. Their explicit
common-space/local premise is accepted conditionally. In particular,
this note works on the symmetric, globally existing, bounded-kernel
reference paths constructed in the first input. It does not construct
or assert a global canonical path.

The conclusions are: closed equations for both actual reverse queries;
uniform spatial L1 estimates for their curvature sources, including an
action estimate for the top source generated by the third-matrix update;
and explicit L2 time-increment estimates with the unbounded-field tails
left visible. An algebraic example shows that a uniform pointwise L2
query-derivative estimate cannot follow just from the displayed current
primal, operator, speed, and curvature-action densities, even with both
samples, symmetry, and both exact matrix updates. The example is not
claimed to be reached by the prescribed zero-readout reference paths.

The proof first differentiates at one bounded reference, then separates
the terms with uniform L2 bounds from the curvature products. It proves
the available action estimate before testing the stronger proposed L2
estimate against an explicit symmetric state.

## 1. Setup and uniform constants

Write e=1/10 and

    phi(z)=1+e log(1+exp(z)),
    p(z)=phi'(z),             c(z)=phi''(z).

Thus 0<p<e, 0<c<=e/4, c=p-p^2/e, and phi has linear growth.
The two labels are y=(1,sigma), sigma in {+1,-1}, and
lambda_b=y_b/2. All sums below include b=1,2. Inner products and norms
are on the indicated layer's probability space; a tensor acts by
(u tensor v)h=u<v,h>. Put B=W^(2), A=W^(3), and w=W^(4).

For sample a define the actual fields

    H^ell_a=phi(Z^ell_a),   p^ell_a=p(Z^ell_a),
    c^ell_a=c(Z^ell_a),
    Z^2_a=B H^1_a,         Z^3_a=A H^2_a,
    delta^3_a=p^3_a w,     q^2_a=A* delta^3_a,
    delta^2_a=p^2_a q^2_a, q^1_a=B* delta^2_a,
    delta^1_a=p^1_a q^1_a.

Products of fields are pointwise on their own population. No product
identifies two different populations, and no independence is assumed.

Use feature time s on [0,s_N], up to and including the reference's
fitting endpoint. A prime denotes this time derivative. We use the
following conclusions of the first input, uniformly for sufficiently
large N:

* s_N<=S_max<infinity, with S_max depending on the fixed input Gram and
  label choice through k_0>0.
* The first raw norm, the forward/backward field L2 norms, the readout
  L2 norm, and both current L2 operator norms are bounded by M>=1.
* If a(s)=||theta'(s)||_raw, then

      g'=a^2,    g(0)=0,    0<=g<=1,
      integral_0^s_N a(s)^2 ds=1.

Here the first raw metric is the C^{-1} metric stated in the inputs,
or its one-field version at rho=-1; the matrix tangent norms are HS.
The constant M depends only on the uniform initial raw/operator
bounds, S_max, and the fixed input data. It does not depend on a
reference kernel supremum or an L2-to-L-infinity operator norm.

All differentiations below are classical at fixed N in the bounded
reference's function spaces. We do not assert an L2 Frechet chain rule
for the nonlinear state map. No initial operator is assumed bounded on
an Lp space other than L2, uniformly in N.

## 2. Closed equations, with both current matrices and both memories

The exact feature-gradient equations are

    Z^1_a' = sum_b lambda_b C_ab delta^1_b,
    B' = sum_b lambda_b delta^2_b tensor H^1_b,
    A' = sum_b lambda_b delta^3_b tensor H^2_b,
    w' = F := sum_b lambda_b H^3_b.                       (1)

Define the actual forward velocities v^ell_a=Z^ell_a'. The chain rule
gives the triangular formulas

    v^1_a = sum_b lambda_b C_ab p^1_b q^1_b,

    v^2_a = sum_b lambda_b delta^2_b <H^1_b,H^1_a>
                         + B(p^1_a v^1_a),

    v^3_a = sum_b lambda_b delta^3_b <H^2_b,H^2_a>
                         + A(p^2_a v^2_a).                (2)

The terms on the first lines of v^2 and v^3 are respectively B'H^1_a
and A'H^2_a. Neither can be discarded as a fixed-operator correction.
Introduce

    R^2_a = sum_b lambda_b H^2_b <delta^3_b,delta^3_a>,
    R^1_a = sum_b lambda_b H^1_b <delta^2_b,delta^2_a>,
    P^3_a = c^3_a w v^3_a,
    P^2_a = c^2_a q^2_a v^2_a.                            (3)

Differentiating the actual backward definitions gives

    delta^3_a' = p^3_a F + P^3_a,
    q^2_a' = R^2_a + A*(p^3_a F + P^3_a),
    delta^2_a' = p^2_a q^2_a' + P^2_a,
    q^1_a' = R^1_a + B*(p^2_a q^2_a' + P^2_a).           (4)

In particular, the two query equations can be written without an
undetermined derivative on their right-hand sides:

    F^2_a = R^2_a + A*(p^3_a F),
    F^1_a = R^1_a + B*(p^2_a F^2_a),

    q^2_a' = F^2_a + A*P^3_a,
    q^1_a' = F^1_a + B*(p^2_a A*P^3_a + P^2_a).          (5)

Together with (1)-(2) and the forward definitions, this is a closed
evolution for the actual queries along the reference state. It is not
an autonomous equation in q^1,q^2 alone: current forward fields,
readout, and operators remain necessary state variables.

For completeness, integrating both matrix updates yields the exact
rank memories

    B(s)=B(0)+integral_0^s sum_b lambda_b
                          delta^2_b(u) tensor H^1_b(u) du,
    A(s)=A(0)+integral_0^s sum_b lambda_b
                          delta^3_b(u) tensor H^2_b(u) du. (6)

For ell=2,3 these imply, in both orientations,

    Z^ell_a(s)=W^ell(0) H^(ell-1)_a(s)
       + integral_0^s sum_b lambda_b delta^ell_b(u)
                    <H^(ell-1)_b(u),H^(ell-1)_a(s)> du,

    q^(ell-1)_a(s)=(W^ell(0))* delta^ell_a(s)
       + integral_0^s sum_b lambda_b H^(ell-1)_b(u)
                    <delta^ell_b(u),delta^ell_a(s)> du.   (7)

Differentiating (7) gives exactly (4). In particular, differentiation
of the second argument in a memory covariance retains
<delta^ell_b(u),delta^ell_a'(s)>; it does not remove the curvature
source in (4). Treating that covariance as constant would change the
actual query equation.

## 3. Uniform estimates that do follow on the actual references

For every sample,

    ||v^1_a||_2<=a,
    ||v^2_a||_2<=L_2 a,       L_2=M(1+e),
    ||v^3_a||_2<=L_3 a,       L_3=M+Me L_2.              (8)

For the first inequality, evaluation of coordinate a has norm
sqrt(C_aa)=1 in the first raw metric; at rho=-1 use the one-field
metric. For the next two inequalities apply (2) in the form
W'H+W(pv), using ||W'||_HS<=a. Also ||w'||_2<=a and
||A'||_HS,||B'||_HS<=a.

The vector field itself has a uniform raw speed bound. Indeed, each
matrix gradient in (1) has HS norm at most M^2, the readout gradient
has norm at most M, and the first raw gradient has norm at most M.
For |rho|<1 the last assertion follows from

    ||Z^1'||_raw^2=E[d^T C d]<=2 sum_b lambda_b^2
                                      ||delta^1_b||_2^2<=M^2,

where d_b=lambda_b delta^1_b; the rank-one endpoint has the same
bound directly. Thus a<=K:=2M^2 suffices.

For an interval I=[s,t] in the reference's feature lifetime write

    E_I=integral_s^t a(u)^2 du,
    D_I=sqrt((t-s) E_I).

Every raw state increment is at most D_I, and the Z^ell increments
are at most L_ell D_I, where L_1=1. Also D_I<=K(t-s).

The noncurvature terms in (5) obey

    ||F^2_a||_2 <= M(1+e)a,
    ||F^1_a||_2 <= [M+M^2 e(1+e)]a.                     (9)

For example R^2_a=A'^*delta^3_a has norm at most Ma; the other
terms follow by bounded gates and current L2 operator norms.

The curvature sources have the following uniform, actual estimates:

    ||P^3_a||_1 <= (eM/4)L_3 a,
    ||P^2_a||_1 <= (eM/4)L_2 a,

    integral_I ||P^ell_a||_1 du <= (eM/4)L_ell D_I,
                                                   ell=2,3. (10)

These are spatial Cauchy--Schwarz followed by time Cauchy--Schwarz.
They concern the actual forward velocities in (2), including both
matrix updates. They yield, in particular,

    ||delta^3_a(t)-delta^3_a(s)||_1
                      <= [e+(eM/4)L_3]D_I.             (11)

Consequently the actual top backward fields have a uniform Lipschitz
bound in spatial L1 in feature time. Equations (10)-(11) do not assert
that A* extends uniformly from L1 to L2.

### A sharper action estimate for the top matrix contribution

This estimate uses the two-sample structure beyond (10). For a sample
pair X set X_+=(X_1+X_2)/2 and X_-=(X_1-X_2)/2. Write

    U=H^2_+,   V=H^2_-,   u^2=||U||_2^2,   kappa=||V||_2^2,
    m_a=c^3_a w,          delta_a=delta^3_a.

Sample symmetry gives <U,V>=0. The softplus identity implies

    |m_-|<=|delta_-|,       |m_+|<=|delta_+|.             (12)

Indeed c_1-c_2=[1-(p_1+p_2)/e](p_1-p_2), with the bracket of
absolute value at most one, while 0<=c_a<=p_a for the sum.

Let P^3,mat_a=m_a(A'H^2_a), the part of P^3_a generated by the
third-matrix update. For opposite labels, the full modal update is

    A'=delta_- tensor U + delta_+ tensor V,
    A'H^2_a=u^2 delta_- + epsilon_a kappa delta_+,
    epsilon_1=1, epsilon_2=-1.

Therefore

    P^3,mat_- = u^2 m_- delta_- + kappa m_+ delta_+,

    ||P^3,mat_-||_1
       <=u^2||delta_-||_2^2+kappa||delta_+||_2^2
       =||A'||_HS^2.                                    (13)

The equality is the orthogonal two-term rank-update norm, not a
factorization of correlated fields. For equal labels,

    A'=delta_+ tensor U + delta_- tensor V,
    P^3,mat_+ = u^2 m_+ delta_+ + kappa m_- delta_-,
    ||P^3,mat_+||_1<=||A'||_HS^2.                        (14)

Thus the label-selected mode satisfies the new actual estimate

    integral_I ||P^3,mat_selected||_1 du
                           <=integral_I ||A'||_HS^2 du<=E_I. (15)

It holds on every reference subinterval with constant one, for both
label configurations, without division by a contrast Gram. The other
mode has not been omitted from the update: it appears explicitly in
(13) or (14).

This is an L1 action estimate, not an L2 estimate or a signed bound on
the whole query derivative. For example, for opposite labels the
remaining part of the difference-mode source is

    (P^3-P^3,mat)_-
       =m_- A(H^2_+') + m_+ A(H^2_-').                   (16)

The action bound on sqrt(kappa)m_+ does not bound its second product
in L2, nor does it supply a bound on H^2_-'/sqrt(kappa). Formula (2)
retains the lower matrix and first-field motion inside H^2_-' exactly.
The middle curvature-mode estimates from the second input likewise
bound curvature fields themselves, with their stated weights, not
their multiplication operators on actual forward velocities.

### Actual L2 time increments with the missing tails exposed

For an actual L2 field X define its tail

    T_X(s,R)=||X(s) 1_{|X(s)|>R}||_2,   R>=0.

Using |p(z)-p(z')|<=min(e,(e/4)|z-z'|), split w(s) at height R_3
in the exact difference

    delta^3_a(t)-delta^3_a(s)
       =p^3_a(t)[w(t)-w(s)]
                    +[p^3_a(t)-p^3_a(s)]w(s).

It follows that

    ||delta^3_a(t)-delta^3_a(s)||_2
       <=[e+(e/4)R_3 L_3]D_I + e T_w(s,R_3).

Now split the actual operator differences as well. Equations (6)
bound both matrix increments in HS norm by D_I, so

    ||q^2_a(t)-q^2_a(s)||_2
       <=M[1+e+(e/4)R_3 L_3]D_I + Me T_w(s,R_3),        (17)

    ||q^1_a(t)-q^1_a(s)||_2
       <=M D_I+Me||q^2_a(t)-q^2_a(s)||_2
             +(Me/4)R_2 L_2 D_I+Me T_(q^2_a)(s,R_2).   (18)

In (18) the cutoff is on the actual q^2_a(s). It is not a cutoff of
the dynamics. These inequalities are valid for every R_2,R_3>=0,
with constants independent of N. Endpoints can also be exchanged.

At each fixed reference the fields are bounded on its finite feature
interval, so finite cutoffs remove the tails and recover classical L2
Lipschitz regularity. Uniform L2 bounds alone do not make the two
displayed tails small uniformly in N. Thus (17)-(18) are not a proof
of uniform L2 equicontinuity on the whole reference family.

The zero initial readout does give a uniform estimate at the initial
endpoint, where neither query has a tail:

    ||q^2_a(s)||_2 <= eM min(Ks,sqrt(s g(s))),
    ||q^1_a(s)||_2 <= e^2 M^2 min(Ks,sqrt(s g(s))).       (19)

Here w(s)=integral_0^s F, ||F||_2<=a, and both initial queries vanish.
This is a basepoint estimate, not a bound for arbitrary time increments.

## 4. Explicit algebraic obstruction to a pointwise L2 estimate

We now test the stronger proposed estimate

    sum_a (||q^2_a'||_2+||q^1_a'||_2)
       <= finite function of uniform current raw/primal/operator
          bounds, raw speed, and the curvature-action densities. (20)

The example below makes its left side unbounded while all those
quantities stay bounded. It uses opposite labels, rho=0, nonzero
updates of both matrices, the actual forward/backward definitions,
and exact sample symmetry. All kernels and fields are bounded for
each example. It also shows that the offending top product survives
the complete lower-motion term in (2), rather than merely one summand
considered in isolation.

### The states and their bounds

Let Omega_1 and Omega_2 each carry independent symmetric signs x,h.
Use separate copies on the two populations. Let J flip x and fix h.
Set epsilon_1=1, epsilon_2=-1, and

    Z^1_a=epsilon_a x,
    B=x_2 tensor x_1 + h_2 tensor h_1.

Define the fixed constants

    p_0=e/2,
    U_1=[phi(1)+phi(-1)]/2,    V_1=e/2,
    t_2=V_1,
    u=[phi(t_2)+phi(-t_2)]/2,  v=e t_2/2=e^2/4,
    d_1=[p(1)-p(-1)]/2,
    d_2=[p(t_2)-p(-t_2)]/2.

The identity phi(z)-phi(-z)=ez gives exactly

    H^1_a=U_1+epsilon_a V_1 x,
    Z^2_a=epsilon_a t_2 x,
    H^2_a=u+epsilon_a v x,
    p^1_a=p_0+epsilon_a d_1 x,
    p^2_a=p_0+epsilon_a d_2 x.                           (21)

Thus B has operator norm one. Neither feature contrast vanishes.

On a fixed third probability space take disjoint sets E_+,E_- of
mass m/2 each, with J exchanging them. Put E=E_+ union E_- and

    b=m^(-1/2)1_E,
    o=m^(-1/2)(1_(E_+)-1_(E_-)).

These are orthonormal even/odd fields. Their supremum norms diverge
as m decreases. Let

    L=log(3),    a_0=L/(2u),    a_1=L/(2v),
    A=b tensor h + sqrt(m)[a_0 b tensor 1-a_1 o tensor x],
    w=-o.                                                   (22)

Then A intertwines the involutions, ||w||_2=1, and

    ||A||_op=max(sqrt(1+m a_0^2),sqrt(m)a_1)<=2

for all sufficiently small m. Its forward actions are

    Z^3_1=0 on E_+,  L on E_-,  0 outside E,
    Z^3_2=L on E_+,  0 on E_-,  0 outside E.               (23)

Every forward field is uniformly bounded even pointwise. In particular,
the forward fields here are not arbitrary unbounded test inputs to A.
The nonuniform pointwise field is the readout. The symmetry is exactly
J_3 w=-w and J_ell H^ell_a=H^ell_(3-a). Also

    g=<w,(H^3_1-H^3_2)/2>
                            =(e log(2)/2)sqrt(m) in (0,1). (24)

All actual q and delta L2 norms are uniformly bounded by bounded
gates and the two operator norms. The raw speed obtained by substituting
this state in (1) is uniformly bounded. All modal action densities in
the inputs, including the middle and top curvature densities, are
uniformly bounded: their constituent L2 field norms and feature-Gram
weights are uniformly bounded. No Lp bound for A independent of m
has been used.

### Full leading-order calculation, including B' and A'

The top gate and curvature values on E are constants. Write

    d=[p(0)-p(L)]/2=-e/8,
    p_bar=[p(0)+p(L)]/2=5e/8,
    c_minus=[c(0)-c(L)]/2=e/32,
    c_plus=[c(0)+c(L)]/2=7e/32.

The exact top backward modes and middle queries are

    delta^3_-=-d b,        delta^3_+=-p_bar o,

    q^2_a=-epsilon_a d h
              +sqrt(m)[a_1 p_bar x-epsilon_a a_0 d].     (25)

All asymptotic constants below depend only on the fixed constants in
(21)-(22), not on m. A remainder on Omega_1 or Omega_2 is uniformly
bounded in L-infinity unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Multiplication of (25) by p^2_a and application of B* give

    delta^2_a=-epsilon_a d p^2_a h+sqrt(m)r_a(x),
    q^1_a=-epsilon_a d p_0 h+sqrt(m)b_a x,                (26)

where r_a is a function of x alone and b_a is a bounded scalar.
Because rho=0, v^1_a=epsilon_a p^1_a q^1_a/2. Consequently the
h-component of B(p^1_a v^1_a) is

    -d (p_0/2)(p_0^2+d_1^2)h.

The other contribution to v^2_a is B'H^1_a. Using the complete
two-sample update and
<H^1_b,H^1_a>=U_1^2+epsilon_b epsilon_a V_1^2, its h-dependent
part is

    -d[p_0 U_1^2+epsilon_a d_2 V_1^2 x]h.

It follows that

    A_0=p_0[U_1^2+(p_0^2+d_1^2)/2]>0,
    A_1=d_2 V_1^2,
    v^2_a=-d[A_0+epsilon_a A_1 x]h+sqrt(m)r^v_a(x).

In particular, for B_0=p_0 A_0+d_2 A_1>0,

    <h,p^2_a v^2_a>=-d B_0,
    A(p^2_a v^2_a)=-d B_0 b+O(m)b+O(m)o.                (27)

The two O(m) coefficients are bounded scalars times m. This order
follows because the remaining input is sqrt(m) times a function of x,
and A acts on span{1,x} with its displayed sqrt(m) coefficients.
There is no omitted h component in that remaining input.

Meanwhile the other forward contribution, from the exact update of A,
is

    A'H^2_a=-d u^2 b-epsilon_a p_bar v^2 o.

Combining this with (27), rather than holding B or the lower features
fixed, gives

    T=u^2+B_0>=u^2>=1,
    v^3_a=-d T b-epsilon_a p_bar v^2 o+O(m)b+O(m)o.       (28)

Both matrix gradients are nonzero. For example the two leading modal
terms in B' are

    -d p_0 h tensor U_1,
    -d d_2 xh tensor (V_1 x),

and those in A' are -d b tensor u and
-p_bar o tensor (v x). Thus neither matrix update was removed to
create (28).

### The divergent derivatives and the absence of cancellation

On E, let sign_E=+1 on E_+ and -1 on E_-. Then
c^3_a=c_plus+epsilon_a c_minus sign_E and w=-o.
Using (28), integration against b gives

    <b,P^3_a>
       =epsilon_a K_*/sqrt(m)+O(sqrt(m)),

    K_*=c_minus d T+c_plus p_bar v^2
                         =(e^2/256)[-T+35v^2].         (29)

Since e=1/10 and v=e^2/4, 35v^2<1<=T. In particular

    K_*<=-(e^2/256)(1-35v^2)<0,

uniformly in m. The lower-motion contribution B_0 strengthens the
negative term; it does not cancel it.

Now A h=b. The term R^2_a lies in span{1,x}, so its pairing with h
vanishes. The readout term obeys <b,p^3_a F>=O(sqrt(m)), because F
is uniformly bounded and supported on E. Equation (4) therefore gives

    <h,q^2_a'>=epsilon_a K_*/sqrt(m)+O(sqrt(m)).           (30)

More explicitly q^2_a' has this h coefficient, while its coefficients
in span{1,x} stay bounded: the potentially large b/o pairings in
A*(delta^3_a') acquire the sqrt(m) factors in (22).

To check the first query as well, note B h_1=h_2. Its R^1_a term
lies in span{1,x} and has zero h_1 pairing. Hence (4) gives

    <h_1,q^1_a'>=<h_2,p^2_a q^2_a'+P^2_a>.

The first term is p_0 times (30), since p^2_a depends on x and
E p^2_a=p_0. For the second term, c^2_a=c(t_2) is constant:
c is even and Z^2_a takes the two values +/-t_2. The leading terms
of q^2_a and v^2_a in (25),(27) each contain one factor h, so their
product is a function of x and pairs to zero with h. The cross terms
are O(sqrt(m)). We conclude

    <h_1,q^1_a'>=p_0 epsilon_a K_*/sqrt(m)+O(sqrt(m)).     (31)

Both tests have L2 norm one. Thus both actual query derivative norms
at these states diverge. In fact their difference modes have the
same divergence. The middle curvature source itself is uniformly
bounded here; it does not cancel the inherited top source in q^1'.

This calculation also pinpoints the spatial issue more sharply than
an abstract L2-times-L2 warning. The correlated factors w and v^3_a
have size m^(-1/2) on the same sets. The top curvature stays positive
at Z^3_a=0 or L, and the current adjoint sends the resulting order
m^(-1) source to an order m^(-1/2) coefficient in the fixed bounded
test direction h. All two-sample coefficients and lower motions have
been computed; the nonzero coefficient is (29). The source's L1 norm
remains bounded, consistently with (10) and (15).

## 5. Exact scope and remaining question

Equations (1)-(19), including the constant-one action estimate (15),
are statements on the actual prescribed reference paths, conditional
on the two inputs' explicit premises. The dependence on reference
supremum norms has been excluded from every constant in those
estimates. The tail terms in (17)-(18) are quantities to be controlled,
not extra uniform assumptions asserted here.

The obstruction (21)-(31) has a narrower but precise logical scope.
It disproves (20) as a universal estimate on symmetric bounded states
with the exact softplus forward/backward algebra and exact raw
gradient vector field. A fixed finite bound on the named current
quantities cannot imply such an L2 derivative bound. It also excludes
a universal algebraic cancellation among the terms of (5) as a proof
of that bound. The softplus modal identities and the action densities
are all compatible with the divergence.

The example's readout is nonzero. Its states have not been obtained
at positive times from zero readout and the particular projected
Gaussian initialization of the first input. In particular, it is not
a counterexample to uniform time regularity of that specific sequence.
At each state the bounded-kernel equations define a classical local
trajectory and the raw energy chain rule applies; that observation
does not establish the required zero-readout history. Nor does this
pointwise example disprove a bound on a whole-interval time integral
that might exploit such a history in a new way. A conclusion about
those stronger history-dependent questions would need an additional
argument.

The exact rank memories (6)-(7) retain that history but do not, by
their L2 bounds alone, regularize the curvature sources when
differentiated. Strong approximation of initial operators is also
only available on fixed or compact L2 input sets; none of the
calculations above establishes compactness of these moving source
families. Additional use of the prescribed initialization/history,
or a uniform tail estimate strong enough for (17)-(18), remains a
possible route. It is neither assumed nor proved here.

Finally, on the symmetric physical paths

    ds/dt=4(1-g),      0<=4(1-g)<=4.

Thus (4)-(5) convert to physical derivatives by this factor; the
proved L1 variation bounds pull back to physical subintervals, and
the increment estimates use their corresponding feature endpoints.
This change of clock supplies no missing spatial L2 product bound.
No global canonical convergence, uniqueness, finite-width
identification, or global canonical theorem is claimed.
