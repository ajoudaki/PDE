## Independent checks

1. **Exact model and scaling.** The stored readout convention, the identity `delta = n partial f / partial z`, and all three kinds of updates agree, including the middle factor `1/n` and the end-layer learning factors `n` (population proof, lines 61–91). The first-layer block has the input Gram factor, and the middle blocks are products of two contractions. Differentiating the prediction gives the kernel and weighted loss identities at lines 404–418. No initialization variance belongs on a trained outer product. Summed unweighted loss is obtained by taking the uniform weights and making the stated time rescaling.

2. **External finite-program theorem.** I checked the primary [Tensor Programs III source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10685), Setup 2.2, Box 1, Theorem 2.10, and Remarks 2.11–2.12. It supplies the fixed-program empirical limits used here, with formal opposite-direction derivatives and degenerate covariances allowed. The written programs satisfy its polynomial-growth conditions. Independent orientation innovation families, while retaining the response terms, agree with Box 1. The proof does not apply the theorem directly to a number of steps that grows with width. The operator claims also agree with [Tensor Programs IVb](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01814), Definition 2.6.7 and Propositions 2.6.8–2.6.9; the present argument supplies its own norm-bound passage.

3. **SubGaussian roots.** The quantile construction at population-proof lines 170–182 is valid for atomic, asymmetric, and unbounded scalar subGaussian laws. Gaussian tail lower bounds and the subGaussian tail upper bound give a linearly bounded quantile transform on finite Gaussian arguments. The transform is used as a measurable coordinate operation. Matrix-slot derivatives hold its independent input fixed, so no differentiability of the quantile map is being assumed. Centering and independence of the first-weight entries provide the dimension-independent subGaussian projection bound. The arbitrary fixed readout law need not be centered. This argument does not assert non-Gaussian middle-matrix universality.

4. **Shared operator spaces.** In the countable program construction at population-proof lines 184–203, finite unions must use the same initial arrays, as the proof does. Joint finite-program laws then provide consistency and preserve linear identities. For every generated source and every finite rational linear combination, the finite operator inequality gives the limiting inequality with the same deterministic constant. In particular a zero-norm source has a zero-norm image, making the induced operator well defined on equivalence classes. Extension to the closure is unique. Passing both sides of finite adjunction for arbitrary generated source/target pairs identifies the transpose extension with the actual Hilbert adjoint. The coordinate operations and their clipped approximations supply the closure needed for the vector field. There is no comparison of a finite matrix with a population operator in operator norm.

5. **Preliminary interval.** The forward, backward, residual, and state-speed bounds at lines 214–240 use only RMS field norms and operator norms. The first-exit argument has a fixed margin and applies to finite Euler trajectories as well as population ones. It does not require a coordinate maximum. The input averages use `sum omega = 1` and `|G_ab| <= X^2`; no inverse Gram matrix or smallest eigenvalue appears. The concentration assertion is only over a fixed finite dataset. The resulting time bound does not grow worse with the number of examples when the displayed marginal bounds are fixed.

6. **Response representation and causal derivative estimates.** Response-proof equations (6)–(11), lines 96–178, agree with the expanded trained-matrix actions and the literal finite-program response rule. The forward memory is strictly in earlier times; the current backward response is allowed to use current forward slots. These are different causal roles. The full forward-slot row derivative obeys (26): taking the prefix maximum adds no maximum of random backward fields because the memory coefficient bound retains `Delta omega_b`. The single backward-slot calculation (30)–(31) injects exactly one pulse of order `Delta omega_b`. Subsequent growth remains in weighted sums over time and examples.

7. **No tail bootstrap circularity.** Jensen's bound (14) controls those weighted sums using marginal subGaussian bounds and requires no temporal independence. The response prefactors at zero time have the triangular dependence displayed in (33)–(34): choose the `c` caps upwards, then the `a` caps downwards, and only then choose time. All remaining exponents tend to zero with time after those choices. In the actual construction order at lines 459–487, computing a current forward response uses past backward tails, and computing a current backward response uses the already constructed higher-layer backward response. The top starts with the readout root. This also covers a nonzero readout mean. The induction therefore closes without a bound on a Gaussian-history maximum, an inverse covariance, or a mesh-dependent constant. The variable-step extension at lines 503–514 follows from the same weighted pulse and Jensen calculation.

8. **Continuity, existence, and uniqueness.** The decomposition at population-proof lines 274–300 is the appropriate one-sided localization: only the reference multiplier needs a tail estimate. Recursing down the backward pass multiplies old errors by bounded derivatives and operators; new cutoff terms are added. Thus its stability constant grows linearly in the cutoff, not as a depth-dependent power of that cutoff. Consequently `exp(C R T - c R^2)` tends to zero for the comparison and uniqueness arguments. The product-continuity argument at lines 317–325 is valid in L2: split the varying unbounded field from the bounded multiplier acting on a fixed L2 field. Euler paths converge in the complete field/operator product space, their integral equations pass to the limit, and the reference tail bound transfers at fixed times by Fatou. The competing solution in lines 327–330 needs no independent tail hypothesis. The current-state action-law formulation at lines 332–338 preserves the generated coordinate operations and operators, which is enough for the stated autonomy and restartability.

9. **Actual GD versus a fixed oracle, including unbounded readout.** For fixed coarse mesh, the finite oracle is a legitimate finite program with deterministic coefficients. Its proxy parameter matrices use empirical outer products. Equation (11) exhibits the exact discrepancy between their action and the oracle action. At fixed mesh there are only finitely many such discrepancies, and their RMS errors vanish. Forward consistency follows directly. Backward consistency must use the oracle `P` fields as reference in (7); this is explicitly done at lines 366–371 and handles the unbounded readout. The elementary tail-transfer inequality at lines 373–378 is valid. The proxy assigned velocity differs from its recomputed vector field by a vanishing fixed-mesh error, so the actual fine-grid/proxy coarse-grid comparison gives (12). Its initial distance is zero for the sampled reference arrays. Sending width to infinity with cutoff and coarse mesh fixed, then removing the coarse mesh and finally the cutoff, proves the result for an arbitrary sequence `eta_n -> 0`. There is no hidden width/step coupling and no empirical-tail claim over the growing fine grid.

10. **Velocities and path topology.** The derivative recursion (14) is a continuous map of the state and assigned parameter velocity into L2. For its multiplier term, the same fixed-reference split is valid with the reference derived velocity in place of `P`. After state stability, population Euler velocities converge uniformly, and the limiting velocity path is a compact L2 family. Compactness supplies uniform square-tail integrability. Finite coarse-grid oracle velocities have convergent cutoff measurements. Thus cutoffs for these derived fields can be removed after the state comparison; they never enter the Gronwall exponent. This proves integrated squared hidden-speed convergence without additional actual-GD moments. Independently, the RMS/operator ball bounds all hidden RMS speeds by the derivative recursion. The cell inequality at lines 438–447 therefore gives a vanishing mean-square uniform-path reconstruction error. Finite-grid joint empirical convergence plus second moments supplies Wasserstein-2 convergence of the grid laws; the reconstruction estimate transfers it to the uniform hidden-path norm. The population paths admit the required coordinatewise absolutely continuous versions by their L2 integral representation. Lipschitz activations preserve this hidden-path conclusion.

11. **Finite probes and quadratic measurements.** The stated class at lines 107–117 and 449–452 stays within the L2 topology. A bounded operator propagates an L2 error, a Lipschitz coordinate map preserves convergence, and a product `b(X) U`, with bounded continuous `b`, is continuous under joint L2 convergence. To verify the last claim, first split off the error in `U`, then truncate the reference `U`, and use continuity of `b` on bounded argument sets together with tightness. Compact reference families make this uniform in the time parameter. Finite oracle programs give the corresponding empirical limits. Joint L2 approximation and reference square-tail integrability then give continuous quadratic-growth measurements. The proof only asserts uniform-path Wasserstein convergence for the hidden paths; it does not need an extension of an arbitrary bounded operator to an L2 space of arbitrary probe paths with a supremum norm. Arbitrary products of two unbounded probe fields are correctly excluded.

12. **C1,1, general losses, and perturbations.** The mollification bounds at lines 456–474 are correct; a uniform value-at-zero bound follows at once from the first bound, with smoothing scales bounded by one. The response constants are uniform in the smoothing scale. Comparing different activations adds the stated cutoff-dependent small error and preserves the ordered limit. For the general losses at lines 477–488, local Lipschitz continuity of the loss derivative on the preliminary prediction ball supplies the feedback estimate, and frozen oracle coefficients require only their bound in the response lemma. Convexity and global loss growth are unnecessary on this local interval. Small initialization perturbations enter the initial-distance term in the same one-sided comparison; even concentrated readout perturbations do not require their own coordinate tails. The vanishing Gaussian readout statement for every positive exponent follows from its normalized L2 norm.

## Scope of the sign-off

The sign-off is for a positive time fixed before width and step size tend to their limits, for each fixed finite depth and dataset. The proof does not provide an all-time result, a growing-depth or growing-dataset convergence theorem, a rate uniform in such growth, arbitrary unbounded-product probe limits, ReLU, or replacement of the Gaussian middle matrices by arbitrary order-one non-Gaussian matrices.

The two-hidden-layer strict-activity corollary has separate hypotheses. I checked the weighted onset formulas and positivity arguments reproduced in `ACTIVITY_SOURCE_AUDIT.md` against the stated corollary, rather than treating its prior verdict as evidence. They are consistent with the proved strong flow, Gaussian first roots and middle matrix, zero limiting readout, pairwise nonparallel normalized inputs, every label nonzero, and nonaffine activations. Positivity of the first two kernel blocks is a strictly positive-time assertion: those blocks vanish at time zero. No arbitrary-depth or nonzero-readout strict-activity claim is being certified.

**Numbered blocking gaps: none. No repair is required for the reviewed theorem.**
