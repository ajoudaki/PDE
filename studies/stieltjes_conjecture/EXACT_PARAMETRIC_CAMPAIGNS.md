# Exact parametric Stieltjes campaigns

## Current conclusion

Five bounded parameter-extension campaigns are now recorded here.  The first
four found **no falsification** of the output-kernel Stieltjes conjecture and
replace several isolated checks at one canonical network by algebraically
certified Hankel inequalities over four nontrivial continuous families:

1. a full relative-metric ray, together with the first- and second-hidden
   preactivation norms;
2. two-input equal-label and opposite-label squared-loss channels over every
   admissible input correlation; and
3. a centered-to-overcentered family of first-hidden quadratic activations;
   and
4. the full independent two-block metric quadrant, rather than only its
   diagonal ray.

The fifth campaign established a faithful and genuinely non-scaling
three-input equicorrelation extension through derivative order five.  It also
proves the two available necessary moment signs, $\mu_0,\mu_1>0$, on the full
PSD interval.  Its order-seven resource gate failed, however, so it produced
neither $\mu_2$ nor the first ordinary Hankel determinant.  It is therefore a
feasibility/partial-moment result, **not a fifth Hankel pass**.

Every accepted sign statement is exact: it follows from checked-integer MFP
jets, exact formal series reversion, and either coefficientwise-positive
polynomials or exact Sturm root counts.  None is a floating-point grid test.

This is materially stronger finite-order evidence than the original
single-configuration PSD tests.  It is **not** an all-order Hankel-positivity
theorem, does not construct a representing measure, and does not identify the
formal resolvent with an actual global mean-field training trajectory.  Those
claim levels remain open.

The broader conjecture, its earlier one-point order-eleven certificates, and
its unresolved global obligations are described in
[CURRENT_RESEARCH_STATE.md](CURRENT_RESEARCH_STATE.md).  The present report is
the authoritative synthesis of these five bounded parametric campaigns.  The
separately bounded canonical order-thirteen probe is not a parametric pass and
is recorded in [`NEXT_CAMPAIGN_OUTCOMES.md`](NEXT_CAMPAIGN_OUTCOMES.md).

## 1. Common formal object and claim level

### 1.1 Feature ascent and the output-coordinate kernel

For the canonical one-input, two-hidden-layer quadratic model, write

$$
z_i=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_j W_{ij}\phi(u_j),
\qquad
f_n=\frac1n\sum_i a_i z_i^2.
$$

The canonical activation is $\phi(u)=u^2$.  Campaign 3 replaces only this
first-hidden activation.  Campaigns 2 and 5 evaluate the same network on two
and three inputs respectively.  Campaigns 1 and 4 change the relative
parameter-block metric.

Let $g_n$ denote the scalar output channel being followed: $g_n=f_n$ in the
one-input campaigns, $g_n=(f_1+\sigma f_2)/2$ in a two-input symmetry
channel, and $g_n=(f_1+f_2+f_3)/3$ in the equal-label three-input channel.
Feature ascent is generated by the appropriate metric gradient
operator $D$; in the canonical metric, $D=n\nabla g_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla$.
At every fixed derivative order, the formal mean-field feature jet is

$$
F^{(k)}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E[D^k g_n].
$$

The fixed-order limit is the object computed by MFP.  It is not, merely by
definition, a positive-time limit or a convergent Taylor series.

In every nondegenerate channel considered below, parity makes $F$ formally
odd and $a:=F'(0)>0$.  Hence $F$ has a unique odd formal inverse.  Define

$$
K(y)=F'\!\left(F^{-1}(y)\right).
$$

Thus $F'(s)=K(F(s))$ as an exact identity of formal jets.  We write

$$
K(y)=a+\mu_0y^2-\mu_1y^4+\mu_2y^6-\mu_3y^8+\cdots
$$

and

$$
R(x)=\frac{K(\sqrt x)-a}{x}
=\sum_{r\ge0}(-1)^r\mu_r x^r.
$$

The formal Stieltjes conjecture is that $(\mu_r)_{r\ge0}$ is a Stieltjes
moment sequence: there is a nonnegative measure $\rho$ on $[0,\infty)$ such
that

$$
\mu_r=\int_0^\infty \lambda^r\,\rho(d\lambda).
$$

Equivalently, at the purely formal moment level, every ordinary and shifted
Hankel matrix must be positive semidefinite:

$$
H_m=(\mu_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{m}\succeq0,
\qquad
H_m^+=(\mu_{i+j+1})_{i,j=0}^{m}\succeq0.
$$

If such a measure exists, define its positive-axis resolvent by

$$
\widehat R_\rho(x)
=\int_0^\infty\frac{\rho(d\lambda)}{1+\lambda x}.
$$

Its full formal right jet at zero is $R$.  This does not by itself identify
$\widehat R_\rho$ with an already established global output-kernel function.
The campaigns test finite principal minors of the two Hankel families; they
do not establish all of them.

For reference, if

$$
a=F'(0),\quad b=F^{(3)}(0),\quad c=F^{(5)}(0),
\quad d=F^{(7)}(0),
$$

exact reversion gives

$$
\mu_0=\frac{b}{2a^2},
$$

$$
\mu_1=\frac{4b^2-ac}{24a^5},
$$

$$
\mu_2=\frac{a^2d-26abc+70b^3}{720a^8},
$$

and therefore

$$
\det H_1
=\mu_0\mu_2-\mu_1^2
=\frac{2a^2bd-5a^2c^2-12ab^2c+60b^4}{2880a^{10}}.
$$

These universal identities explain why an exact jet through order seven is
the first useful common gate: it supplies $\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2$ and the
ordinary $2\times2$ determinant.  Order nine supplies $\mu_3$ and the first
shifted determinant $\mu_1\mu_3-\mu_2^2$.

### 1.2 Exact squared-loss reduction

These feature calculations remain tied to the natural squared-loss gradient
training problem; they are not arbitrary ascent directions.  For one sample
with label $1$ and squared loss

$$
L=(1-f)^2,
$$

gradient descent in the same parameter metric is a scalar time change of
feature ascent:

$$
\dot\vartheta=2(1-f)\,\operatorname{grad}f.
$$

Consequently the output $y=f$ obeys, formally and locally whenever the
actual feature curve is invertible,

$$
\dot y=2(1-y)K(y),
\qquad
L=(1-y)^2,
\qquad
\dot L=-4K(y)L.
$$

A master learning-rate factor merely multiplies the right-hand sides.  Thus
$K$ determines both the feature-ascent output and the one-sample squared-loss
output after the appropriate clock change.

For Campaign 2, take labels $(1,\sigma)$ with $\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}$ and
average squared loss

$$
L_\sigma=\frac12\big[(f_1-1)^2+(f_2-\sigma)^2\big].
$$

On the input-exchange symmetry channel

$$
(f_1,f_2)=(g_\sigma,\sigma g_\sigma),
\qquad
g_\sigma=\frac{f_1+\sigma f_2}{2},
$$

one has exactly

$$
L_\sigma=(1-g_\sigma)^2,
\qquad
\dot g_\sigma=2(1-g_\sigma)K_\sigma(g_\sigma;\theta).
$$

This supplies the missing natural-loss bridge for both the same-label and
opposite-label experiments.

For Campaign 5, the equicorrelation Gram matrix and equal labels preserve the
permutation-invariant channel

$$
g_3=\frac{f_1+f_2+f_3}{3}.
$$

On $f_1=f_2=f_3=g_3$, the natural average squared loss is
$L=(1-g_3)^2$ and its full parameter gradient is
$2(g_3-1)\nabla g_3$.  Hence

$$
\dot g_3=2(1-g_3)K_3(g_3;\rho).
$$

The same input Gram matrix is used in both the initialization covariance and
the first-layer gradient metric.  This makes Campaign 5 a faithful reduction
of natural loss training rather than an auxiliary feature objective.

### 1.3 What “exact” means here

The accepted finite-order claims are exact under the leading-width MFP
recursion.  Scalarized derivative monomials are represented as decorated
bipartite forests; local parameter hits rewrite the decorations; bridge hits
split a tree; exact Leibniz convolution distributes remaining derivatives;
and the surviving leading-width Wick contractions are evaluated with checked
integer arithmetic.  Independent transparent Wick enumerators, endpoint
identities, parity, canonical one-input values, and source/result hashes are
used as validity gates.

The result therefore sits on the following claim ladder:

1. the displayed fixed-order jets and rational identities are accepted exact
   computational certificates;
2. their finite Hankel signs are exact on the displayed parameter domains;
3. all-order Hankel positivity remains open;
4. existence and determinacy of a representing measure remain open;
5. identification with a global deterministic mean-field trajectory remains
   open.

## 2. Campaign 1: relative metric and hidden norms

### 2.1 Nontrivial parameter and observables

Campaign 1 keeps the one-input quadratic network but changes the relative
metric of the three parameter blocks:

$$
D_\lambda=D_a+\lambda(D_u+D_W),
\qquad \lambda\ge0.
$$

Initialization is unchanged.  In particular, this is a training-metric
family, not the previously studied middle-weight initialization-variance
homotopy.

This is not removable even by allowing separate positive output and time
rescalings.  Under $\widetilde F(s)=A F(Bs)$, the dimensionless jet ratio

$$
\mathcal I(F)=\frac{F'(0)F^{(5)}(0)}{F^{(3)}(0)^2}
$$

is invariant.  In Campaign 1 its two endpoint limits are

$$
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\mathcal I(F_\lambda)
=\frac{44519}{14440},
\qquad
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal I(F_\lambda)
=\frac{59242799}{20187049},
$$

which are unequal.  Thus the parameter changes the normalized curve shape,
not only its units.  At $\lambda=0$ only the readout moves and

$$
F_0(s)=27s,
\qquad K_0(y)=27.
$$

The two hidden squared-RMS observables are

$$
Q_1=\frac1n\sum_j u_j^2,
\qquad
Q_2=\frac1n\sum_i z_i^2,
$$

with initial values $Q_1(0)=1$ and $Q_2(0)=3$.  They are the first- and
second-hidden preactivation RMS norms squared.

The first norm needs no independent companion kernel.  The exact Ward/Euler
identity is

$$
D_\lambda Q_1=8\lambda f,
\qquad
D_\lambda^kQ_1=8\lambda D_\lambda^{k-1}f.
$$

If $y=F_\lambda(s)$, then

$$
\frac{dQ_1}{dy}=\frac{8\lambda y}{K_\lambda(y)},
$$

so that

$$
Q_1(y)=1+8\lambda\int_0^y\frac{r}{K_\lambda(r)}\,dr.
$$

Thus $K_\lambda$ determines $Q_1$ exactly.  No analogous scalar identity
reduces $Q_2$ to $K_\lambda$.  The natural additional output-coordinate
observable is

$$
N_{2,\lambda}(y)
=Q_{2,\lambda}\!\left(F_\lambda^{-1}(y)\right),
$$

and its Stieltjes candidate is

$$
T_{2,\lambda}(x)
=\frac{N_{2,\lambda}(\sqrt x)-3}{x}
=\nu_0-\nu_1x+\nu_2x^2-\nu_3x^3+\cdots.
$$

The hidden extension conjectures separately that $(\nu_r)$ is a Stieltjes
moment sequence.  This does not follow merely from the output conjecture.
If both objects existed globally, $K_\lambda$ would determine the training
clock and $N_{2,\lambda}$ would read off the second-hidden norm on that same
trajectory.

### 2.2 Exact MFP extension

Each canonical forest carries a three-root exact polynomial amplitude

$$
(A_f(\lambda),A_{Q_1}(\lambda),A_{Q_2}(\lambda)).

A readout hit leaves the polynomial degree unchanged; a first-feature or
middle-weight hit multiplies its contribution by $\lambda$.  Descendants of
different roots share canonical connected-component and Wick caches.  The
production route uses checked unsigned 512-bit arithmetic.  A transparent
whole-forest oracle and a separately graded sector route audit the new
polynomial bookkeeping.

### 2.5 Resources, failed audit route, and stopping decision

The accepted dense order-nine/order-eight run used a 4 GiB address-space cap
and a 20-minute wall cap.  The output root took $717.631$ seconds and the
second-hidden root $260.327$ seconds.  A fresh rebuild produced a
byte-identical binary and reproduced every coefficient and cache count.

The independent double-graded audit completed all 55 output-order-nine
sectors and recovered the canonical $F^{(9)}(0)$.  It completed only 24 of 45
second-hidden order-eight sectors before its global timeout, and those
partials were not checkpointed.  That incomplete branch is **inconclusive**
for $Q_2^{(8)}$ and is not used to support it; the successful dense rerun is
the primary certificate.

No $Q_2^{(10)}$ or higher parametric branch was launched.  The authorized
branch had already reached the first shifted hidden test, while the dense
calculation was close enough to its cap that the next order requires a new
bounded optimization or sectorization decision.  This is a cost-benefit stop,
not a failed sign test.

## 3. Campaign 2: two inputs and natural-loss label channels

### 3.1 Nontrivial input geometry and exact loss reduction

Take two unit-RMS inputs with cosine similarity $\theta\in[-1,1]$ and put
$t=\theta^2\in[0,1]$.  In a fixed first-hidden neuron,

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(u^1,u^2)
=Q(\theta)
=\begin{pmatrix}1&\theta\\\theta&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The same matrix must appear both in the initialization law and in the
first-layer gradient metric.  Using independent $u^1,u^2$ coordinates in the
gradient rewrite would silently define a different training problem.

For labels $(1,\sigma)$, $\sigma=+1$ is the equal-label channel and
$\sigma=-1$ the opposite-label channel.  The exact squared-loss reduction in
Section 1.2 applies to

$$
g_\sigma=\frac{f_1+\sigma f_2}{2}.
$$

Thus each experiment has its own natural scalar kernel $K_\sigma$ and is
directly relevant to training.

The correlation is not a removable scale.  Already at initialization,

$$
K_+(0;t)=63+20t+28t^2,
$$

whereas

$$
K_-(0;t)=48-20t-28t^2=4(1-t)(7t+12).
$$

The scale-invariant jet ratio $\mathcal I$ above also changes exactly:

$$
\mathcal I(F_+)|_{t=0}
=\frac{7401965697}{2444403200},
\qquad
\mathcal I(F_+)|_{t=1}
=\frac{29831494015}{9860573312}.
$$

Hence even arbitrary separate positive output and time rescalings cannot
remove $t$.

At $t=1$, identical inputs make the opposite-label feature direction zero.
The exact endpoint factorization is

$$
F_-^{(2r+1)}(0;t)
=(1-t)^{r+1}F_{h_-}^{(2r+1)}(0;t).
$$

For $t<1$ this is a positive rescaling of output and time, so the raw and
normalized Hankel tests have the same signs.  The normalized $h_-$ family is
the nondegenerate algebraic continuation to $t=1$; the raw channel itself has
no locally invertible output coordinate there.

### 3.4 Failed route, accepted route, and stopping decision

The original dense quotient-Wick plus-channel order-seven run reached its
frozen 20-minute timeout.  It is classified **inconclusive** and contributes
no accepted coefficient.

An exact vertex-partition terminal evaluator was then frozen only after it
matched the quotient-Wick evaluator on all 11,236 terminal trees reachable
through order five and matched the transparent labelled-Wick oracle through
order three.  Under separate 4 GiB and 20-minute caps, the amended route
completed each order-seven channel in about $6$ minutes $43$ seconds, with
about $1.50$ GiB peak RSS.  Both channels used the same 711,818 connected
value states and 617,838 terminal trees.

Order nine was postponed.  A monolithic extrapolation predicts roughly
38--53 million value states, 34--46 million terminal trees, and 80--112 GiB
per channel.  A viable attempt therefore needs a new sectorized two-color
compiler.  Since order nine adds only the first shifted $2\times2$ gate, its
current information gain does not justify that implementation cost.  This is
a planned postponement, not a timeout or a negative sign result.

## 8. What the campaigns establish—and what they do not

### Established at fixed order

- All five experimental axes are genuine: none collapses to a common output
  or time scaling that would preserve signs trivially.
- Every displayed MFP jet satisfies its independent parity, endpoint,
  canonical-value, and low-order Wick gates.
- Campaign 1 proves both ordinary and shifted $2\times2$ output and hidden
  Hankel positivity over an unbounded relative-metric ray.
- Campaign 2 proves ordinary $2\times2$ positivity for two distinct
  natural-loss label channels over the entire admissible two-input
  correlation interval.
- Campaign 3 proves ordinary $2\times2$ positivity across uncentered,
  centered, and overcentered first-hidden activations.
- Campaign 4 proves both ordinary and shifted $2\times2$ output positivity on
  the full independent two-block metric quadrant.
- Campaign 5 proves a faithful exact three-input grammar through order five
  and the two lower necessary moment signs.  It is not counted among the four
  completed Hankel campaigns because it never reached $H_1$.
- Failed or incomplete computational routes were not reinterpreted as
  evidence.  Each accepted conclusion has a separate completed exact route.

### Stronger evidence, but not a theorem of the conjecture

The earlier evidence could have been an accidental sign pattern at one
initialization.  The new results require the same sign structure to survive
exactly over:

- an unbounded metric ray;
- two different label symmetries and every two-input correlation; and
- a signed activation-centering interval whose negative half cannot be
  certified by positive coefficients; and
- a two-dimensional unbounded block-metric quadrant, including independent
  boundary axes.

The three-input campaign adds a fifth, structurally novel continuum with a
signed triangle invariant, but only at the lower-jet and partial-moment claim
level.

That conjunction is substantially harder to attribute to one isolated
numerical accident.  It supports the possibility of an architecture-level
positivity mechanism.  It still does not reveal such an all-order mechanism.

### Open obligations

The following remain unresolved:

1. the first three-input ordinary Hankel determinant, higher parametric
   moments, and larger ordinary and shifted Hankel minors;
2. an all-order positive operator, Gram construction, or recurrence proving
   both Hankel families PSD;
3. existence and determinacy of the putative Stieltjes measures;
4. the corresponding multivariate/companion-measure theorem for hidden
   observables;
5. passage from every fixed-order mean-field derivative to an actual
   positive-time deterministic curve; and
6. identification of any resulting resolvent with the global mean-field
   feature-learning and loss trajectories.

Accordingly, the precise final verdict is

$$
\boxed{\text{no falsification; much stronger exact finite-order continuum
evidence; no all-order or global theorem.}}
$$
