## Canonical object and limit order

For every width $n$, let

\[
z_i=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^n W_{ij}u_j^2,
\qquad
f_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n a_i z_i^2,
\]

where all $a_i,W_{ij},u_j$ are independent standard Gaussians.  Canonical
feature time is generated by

\[
D_n=n\nabla f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla .
\]

For each fixed integer $k\geq0$, take $n\to\infty$ first and define

\[
F^{(k)}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E[D_n^k f_n].
\]

Readout sign reversal makes $F$ formally odd.  Since $F'(0)=111$, its formal
inverse $G=F^{-1}$ exists.  Define

\[
K(y)=F'(G(y)),
\qquad
R(x)=\frac{K(\sqrt{x})-111}{x}
     =\sum_{r\geq0}(-1)^r\mu_r x^r.
\]

These symbols denote formal series.  No positive-time curve, convergence
radius, concentration statement, or interchange of $k$ and $n$ is part of
the primary formal theorem.

## Canonical claim ladder

### V1: moment/existence conjecture

There is a finite nonnegative Borel measure $\rho$ on $[0,\infty)$, with
moments of every order, such that

\[
\mu_r=\int_0^\infty \lambda^r\,\rho(d\lambda)
\quad\text{for every }r\geq0.
\]

Equivalently, for every $d\geq0$,

\[
H_d=(\mu_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^d\succeq0,
\qquad
H_d^+=(\mu_{i+j+1})_{i,j=0}^d\succeq0.
\]

Also equivalently, the inverse-coordinate coefficients in

\[
G'(\sqrt{x})=\frac1{K(\sqrt{x})}
             =\sum_{r\geq0}(-1)^r h_r x^r
\]

form a Stieltjes moment sequence, or there are a Hilbert space, $A\geq0$,
and a vector $v$ in the domain of all powers of $A$ with

\[
\mu_r=\langle v,A^rv\rangle.
\]

These are three equivalent formulations of V1, not three independent
conjectures.

### V2: determinacy/uniqueness

V1 holds and the representing measure is unique.  A proof must establish
determinacy, for example through a verified Carleman condition, essential
self-adjointness of the Jacobi operator, or another complete uniqueness
criterion.  Constructing one measure is not enough.

### V3: global neural identification

V2 holds; an independently defined deterministic ordinary-Gaussian,
width-first mean-field response exists on the declared output domain; and
that response equals the unique Stieltjes resolvent selected by the formal
moments.  Equality of all derivatives at zero is insufficient because the
formal jet has radius zero and flat perturbations are possible.

## Resolution criteria

A complete affirmative resolution of V1 or U1 must do at least one of the
following without assuming an equivalent form of the conclusion:

1. prove both Hankel families PSD at every order in the claimed scope;
2. construct a nonnegative representing measure and prove all of its moments
   are exactly the target moments; or
3. construct a nonnegative self-adjoint operator and cyclic vector with the
   exact target moments.

A complete negative resolution is one exact target instance, degree, and
polynomial giving a negative ordinary or shifted Hankel quadratic form.  For
canonical V1 specifically, the next shifted determinant is negative exactly

## Permitted inputs and forbidden substitutions

Permitted inputs are the stated network syntax, proved fixed-order
decorated-forest/Wick reductions, exact Gaussian identities, and fully stated
external theorems whose hypotheses are verified.  Checked finite
coefficients may be used for regression and falsification, not induction by
pattern.

The following do not resolve canonical V1:

- any finite list of positive moments or determinants;
- empirical proxy accuracy, width extrapolation, or strict adjacent-order
  error improvement;
- replacing the canonical architecture by the solvable variance boundary,
  a two-input family, or a generic Gaussian polynomial;
- assuming a positive operator, representing measure, or S-fraction and then
  recovering Hankel positivity;
- hiding the full trajectory or infinite coefficient list in a finite oracle;
- proving positivity of raw feature coefficients, local transition profiles,
  aggregate sectors, or row-root polynomials without a proved implication
  through series inversion; or
- matching the complete formal jet to a global function without a
  nonperturbative identification theorem.

total positivity, analytic positive-resolvent geometry, probabilistic/Fock
representations, and exact counterexample certification.  Every route had to
return a proved lemma or obstruction rather than more positive finite data.
