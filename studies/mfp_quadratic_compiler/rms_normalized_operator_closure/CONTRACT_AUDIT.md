# Adversarial audit of the terminal contract

## Positive branch

The positive branch is mathematically testable as written: a candidate must
name its source, state spaces, topologies, vector field and readouts, after
which existence, uniqueness, continuity and compact-time convergence have
ordinary precise meanings.

## Negative branch

The negative branch contains an unavoidable formalization issue.  “A finite
number of fields” is not an information bound.  Every separable Hilbert space
embeds isometrically into a single scalar `L2(0,1)` field, and a probability
measure on a fixed Polish space can encode countably many coordinates.  If
`S_t` is any continuous flow on a separable state space `E` and
`J:E -> L2(0,1)` is an injective homeomorphic embedding onto its image, then

\[
\Phi_t=J S_tJ^{-1}
\]

is formally an autonomous one-field flow.  This construction is vacuous for
the scientific question, but field count and domain dimension alone do not
exclude it.

The bans on an oracle and “disguised hierarchy storage” express the correct
scientific intent, yet they are not invariant mathematical predicates.  A
change of coordinates can turn a visibly indexed hierarchy into one function
and back.  Therefore a theorem quantifying over *all* prose-admissible fields
is currently either false (if injective encodings are allowed) or undefined
(if they are disallowed without a formal membership test).

This means that a failed traffic, moment, marginal-law, ordinary-kernel, or
Fock ansatz cannot be promoted to the terminal universal impossibility
alternative.  The Markov-factor test is rigorous only after a particular
encoder and topology are fixed: two microscopic states in the same encoder
fiber must have identical future readouts under any exact restartable factor.

## Possible formal negative subclasses

A genuine no-go program could freeze one of the following stricter syntactic
classes:

- natural permutation-equivariant encoders generated by graph operations of
  uniformly bounded arity and depth;
- fields on fixed finite-dimensional mark spaces with uniformly bounded
  local differential order and a prescribed contraction grammar;
- operator systems generated by a fixed finite algebra under a specified
  finite list of continuous operations and a named Banach norm.

Each choice would support a theorem, but none has yet been proved to exhaust
the finite PDE/IDE/operator descriptions intended by the user.  Narrowing to
one silently would weaken the requested terminal alternative.

## Frozen decision

The study therefore continues primarily along the positive branch.  Negative
witnesses are retained as topology- or grammar-specific eliminations.  They
become terminal only if an independently audited exhaustion theorem is added.
