**Bounded primary-literature follow-up, 2026-10-02.** This note supplements this study's LITERATURE_AUDIT.md. Four targeted search queries and three newly inspected primary papers were used; no other study, training result, or frozen proof was changed. The search was limited to approximately five minutes. It is not a priority search or an independent audit of these papers' proofs.

**Verdict.** No inspected new source directly asserts that fast signed/permuted Hadamard matrices with the Gaussian limiting singular spectrum reproduce full finite-step nonlinear neural-network feature-learning dynamics. This absence of a hit is not evidence of priority. The previously inspected Wang–Zhong–Fan construction and universality theorem remain the decisive overlap: the matrix construction, prescribed spectrum, and substantial nonlinear adaptive reuse guarantee are already published. The potentially useful contribution here is a carefully scoped architecture-transfer corollary and its response-memory implementation, subject to the separate proof audit. Do not advertise a new spectrum prescription, a new general universality principle, or a breakthrough.

The target claim distinguishes four ingredients: a fast structured matrix; Gaussian limiting singular values; adaptive forward and transpose reuse during learning; and nonlinear hidden features that actually move. Finding some of these ingredients in separate papers does not establish the exact combination, but it sharply limits defensible novelty language.

**New primary sources inspected.**

1. Garrod, Keating, Thrampoulidis, [Diagonalizing the Softmax: Hadamard Initialization for Tractable Cross-Entropy Dynamics, arXiv:2512.04006v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04006v1). Read the abstract, Section 2 model setup, and complete Section 3, including Definitions 2 and Theorems 1–2. Theorem 1 identifies a Sylvester-Hadamard basis preserved by the columnwise softmax. Theorem 2 initializes both factors of an unconstrained features model in aligned singular bases; their gradient flow then evolves through singular values while the singular vectors remain fixed. The represented network is two-layer linear, although the cross-entropy dynamics are nonlinear and features are optimized. This is direct prior on Hadamard initialization plus feature optimization, but the inspected result neither prescribes the Gaussian singular spectrum nor proves replacement of Gaussian-initialized nonlinear hidden-layer learning. Its mechanism uses special invariant bases rather than a universality comparison. Proofs were not audited.

2. [Global Convergence and Rich Feature Learning in L-Layer Infinite-Width Neural Networks under μ Parametrization, arXiv:2503.09565v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.09565v2). Read complete Sections 3–4: the forward/backward history-dependent limiting recursions, Assumptions 4.1 and 4.3, Definition 4.2, Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 4.6. These statements explicitly concern evolving nonlinear features, with the limits inherited from the tensor-program framework. Theorem 4.5 asserts feature nondegeneracy under input geometry and activation assumptions; Corollary 4.6 is conditional on training becoming stationary. None of these inspected statements introduces a signed/permuted Hadamard sampler or proves initializer replacement. This source defeats any suggestion that finite training histories, transpose reuse, or rich feature-learning limits themselves are new. Its inherited tensor-program theorem and initialization dependency were not independently audited, so this note does not attribute an additional initializer-universality theorem to it.

3. Agazzi, Mosig García, Trevisan, [Quantitative Gaussian-Process limits of Tensor Programs, arXiv:2607.06290v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06290v1). Read the abstract and complete setup, execution rules, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.3, scope discussion and related-work discussion in Section 1. Finite executions explicitly initialize matrix entries independently with Gaussian variance inverse to input width. Theorem 1.2 gives quantitative Wasserstein approximation for finite Netsor programs with Lipschitz nonlinearities; Corollary 1.3 controls empirical kernels. Its displayed instruction set contains matrix multiplication and coordinatewise maps, not a tied-transpose rule. The authors explicitly distinguish feature learning and place this work in the GP/NTK setting. This is not a theorem for the structured replacement considered here. Section 2's scalar-kernel extension and the theorem proofs were not read; no claim about their exact scope is made.

**Consequence for positioning and the decisive experiment.** Retain the narrower statement that the study applies a published structured-matrix universality mechanism to a specific finite-step learning program, with the actual reduction and activation treatment supplied locally and separately reviewed. The same eligible finite-history calculation can apply to unrestricted dense feature learning; closure is not the source of an exclusive universality effect. Its proposed value is bounded representation of learned responses as history length grows.

The strongest experiment remains a width trend with substantial measured feature motion, comparing dense Gaussian, dense Haar bases with exactly the candidate's finite singular values, the fast quarter-circle sampler, and a spectrum matching only the second and fourth moments. Compare prediction and feature/response observables across independent ensemble replicates, not same-seed matrix distances. Include fixed-feature and established adaptive structured-layer baselines when making a practical training claim. This separates full adaptive spectrum transfer from an initial fourth-moment repair and from ordinary fast structured learning. It does not establish a width-uniform horizon, an ODE limit, or an all-time memory-approximation theorem.
