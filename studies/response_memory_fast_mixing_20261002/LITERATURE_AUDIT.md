**Frozen primary-literature audit, 2026-10-02.** Scope: this study's README.md and REUSE_THEORY.md; subsequently authorized TRAINING_PROTOCOL.md and fast_training.py; required research/notation/proof skills; nine primary papers listed below. No other study, training output, or GPU experiment was read or used. This is a bounded novelty and applicability audit, not a complete literature review or an independent review of the cited proofs.

**Recommendation.** Do not claim that prescribing a Gaussian singular spectrum inside a fast Hadamard mixer is new. An especially close construction and nonlinear reuse guarantee already appear in Wang–Zhong–Fan. Retain the candidate as a potentially useful implementation of response-memory learning, with finite-step transfer as a concrete theorem project. Its value must come from that application, a completed transfer proof, and measured learning/storage/runtime tradeoffs. Matching the initial return-energy diagnostic alone is inadequate evidence. The actual tanh training algorithm is not certified by a literal invocation of the published AMP theorem.

The audit separates three levels: published universality for a specified class of matrix iterations; the elementary polynomial-program consequence proved below using that published result; and the still-unproved extension to the actual tanh/Heun program. None supplies a width-uniform training horizon, a growing memory order, or the manuscript's all-time approximation theorem for the changed initializer.

**Exact operator correspondence, derived for this candidate.** Let n be the square hidden width and H the normalized real Hadamard matrix, so H^T H=I. Let s=(s_1,...,s_n) be nonnegative singular values with n^{-1} sum_j s_j^2=1. An immediately implementable instance of the cited D.1(b1) ensemble is

    W = Pi_U H Pi_E diag(s) Pi_F^T H^T Pi_V^T,

where the four Pi matrices are mutually independent uniformly random signed permutations, independent of s. Apply the factors from right to left; implement W^T by reversing the factor order and transposing every factor. Both use two Hadamard transforms and O(n) additional arithmetic/storage.

There is a simpler equivalent sampling recipe. Write Pi_E=diag(e) P_E and Pi_F=diag(f) P_F. Then

    Pi_E diag(s) Pi_F^T = diag(epsilon * s_shuffled) P,

where P=P_E P_F^T, s_shuffled is the permutation of s induced by P_E, and epsilon=e*(P f). Conditional on s, s_shuffled, P, and epsilon are independent, with uniform shuffle, uniform permutation, and independent Rademacher signs respectively. This follows by the bijection (P_E,P_F) <-> (P_E,P_E P_F^T), and independence of the original sign arrays. Thus two independent external signed permutations, one independent shuffled singular array, one middle permutation, and one middle sign array are enough. Independent seeds alone are not a mathematical substitute for the stipulated independent draws.

In fast_training.py the external unsigned permutations combine with the existing external sign arrays. Its written formula matches this recipe provided FastMixer's singular shuffle is uniform and independent of its middle permutation and signs. The imported reuse_check.py implementation was not an authorized input for this audit, so that last implementation-level independence check remains with the author. Quarter-circle quantiles are bounded and converge in all moments, satisfying the spectral input to this specialization. Gaussian-diagonal singular values have unbounded maximal singular value as n grows: D.1/D.9's all-moment framework still applies, but Theorem 2.22's bounded-operator-norm hypothesis does not apply to that baseline unchanged.

The source's illustrative prose after equation (2.19) calls its diagonal distribution Marcenko–Pastur. Use an unambiguous convention in this study: square Gaussian matrices with entry variance 1/n have quarter-circle **singular values**, and Marchenko–Pastur **squared singular values**. Do not implement MP samples as singular values by following that wording mechanically.

**Two exact diagnostic limitations, proved here.** Define the independent-probe statistic

    E(W) = E_h ||W^T psi(W h)||_2^2 / n,   h ~ N(0,I_n).

For every deterministic orthogonal V, E(WV)=E(W). Indeed Vh has the same law as h and (WV)^T psi(WVh)=V^T W^T psi(W(Vh)); V^T preserves Euclidean norms. This uses no asymptotic approximation and holds for any measurable psi for which the expectation exists. In particular W=U diag(s) V^T and W_bad=U diag(s) pass exactly the same test. Subsequent coordinatewise nonlinear processing of their returned vectors can distinguish them. The probe cannot establish right-basis mixing or adaptive universality.

Second, outer permutations do not change the distribution of the present initialized training observables. For permutation matrices P_L,P_R, transform the full q=1 state by

    W0' = P_L W0 P_R^T,     A' = P_R A,     w' = P_L w,
    H_a' = P_R H_a,         D_a' = P_L D_a, tau' = tau.

Coordinatewise tanh commutes with permutations. Substitution into TRAINING_PROTOCOL.md gives h_a'=P_R h_a, z_a'=P_L z_a, g_a'=P_L g_a and identical f_a,r_a,rho. Inner products are unchanged; every displayed derivative transforms as its associated state. Addition and scalar multiplication preserve this relation, so Euler and Heun steps do too. Initially A has independent identically distributed Gaussian rows, w=D=0, and H_a is determined by A. Its distribution is invariant under P_R. Therefore predictions, losses and within-layer feature Gram trajectories have exactly the same law with or without additional independent outer permutations. This is an ensemble statement, not equality under the experiment's shared A realization. General orthogonal right rotations do not enjoy this equivariance because tanh does not commute with them.

**A precise polynomial-program consequence.** This is a proved reduction conditional on the published Lemma D.9, not an independently established replacement for that lemma or a theorem about tanh.

Take a sequence of square W0 matrices satisfying D.9 with the same limiting diagonal distribution as square Gaussian matrices. On each of its two coordinate spaces provide finitely many initial vectors, independent of W0, whose joint empirical laws converge almost surely in all moments to deterministic laws with finite moments of every order. Consider a fixed finite computation using:

1. Applications of W0 and W0^T to existing vectors.
2. Finite linear combinations and coordinatewise polynomial functions of vectors on the same coordinate space.
3. Normalized empirical averages n^{-1} sum_i p(v_1(i),...,v_k(i)).
4. Scalar coefficients obtained by continuous functions of previously computed scalar averages and coefficients, defined at their finite deterministic limits; multiplication of vectors by these coefficients.

Then all scalar averages produced by the computation have deterministic almost-sure limits identical to those of the Gaussian-reference computation. This includes any polynomial empirical test of finitely many computed vector channels. It does not assert convergence of an unbounded-width number of channels or a number of instructions growing with n.

Proof: initially each vector coordinate is a polynomial label at a rooted vertex. Multiplication by W0 or W0^T adds an edge and changes the side of the root. A coordinatewise product glues the roots of fresh copies of the participating rooted trees; their other summation indices remain separate, so the result is still an alternating tree. Addition makes finite linear combinations. Taking a normalized empirical average sums the root and produces exactly an alternating tree contraction of Definition D.3. Products of such averages produce finite products of tree contractions, not a new non-tree connected contraction. Lemma D.9 supplies a deterministic common limit for each of the finitely many tree values. For scalar feedback, keep every current vector as a finite sum of rooted trees with scalar coefficients. At each averaging instruction the unweighted tree values have common limits; the coefficients already have common finite limits by induction. The continuous mapping theorem supplies the next scalar limit. This induction proves the claim. Initial vectors may themselves contain bounded nonpolynomial functions of independent Gaussian roots: D.9 only needs their all-moment empirical laws. Polynomial density, nondegenerate AMP covariance, and Onsager coefficients are not needed for this tree statement.

Applied to a version of the protocol with polynomial neuronal functions, this reduction accommodates fixed input dimension d, fixed number m of training and test examples, fixed memory order q, fixed positive step size, and a fixed finite number J of Euler/Heun steps. There are finitely many channels for A's columns, sample features, residual-weighted backward features, and memory moments. The residual norm is a continuous scalar function of finitely many predictions. The inverse clock is continuous because tau starts at 1 and increases under the implemented positive-step Heun updates. Thus these scalar operations need not themselves be replaced by polynomials. The reduction covers predictions and empirical feature/response products. The q>1 extension requires its actual update equations to be compiled; only q=1 equations were inspected here.

**What prevents claiming transfer for the actual tanh code.** The model is a plausible application of the tree result, but three bridges remain to be proved, rather than assumed from the name “tensor program.”

First, tanh and 1-tanh^2 are not polynomial. One needs an approximation/localization argument controlling the whole finite computation under both ensembles, including repeated products of adaptive channels, not pointwise approximation on an unverified compact set. Bounded tanh helps: for fixed J and finite labels, w,H,D and residuals admit width-independent coordinate bounds by induction, tau>=1, and bounded ||W0|| controls normalized second moments of A and preactivations. These are useful starting bounds, not by themselves an all-moment polynomial approximation proof.

Second, Theorem 2.22 describes AMP with specified Onsager corrections and nonsingular state covariances. Arbitrary residual/norm feedback and the zero readout/zero backward initialization are not literal matches. A cancellation-and-history embedding, or a direct extension of the tree argument, must handle redundant or initially zero channels. One cannot simply assert that its nonsingularity hypotheses hold. The polynomial reduction avoids this issue only at the polynomial claim level.

Third, constants can depend on d,m,q,J and step size. A theorem for every fixed J does not justify exchanging n→infinity with J→infinity, taking dt→0 at fixed physical horizon without further estimates, or transferring all-time q-error rates. Numerical discretization accuracy remains separate from width universality.

There is no evident closure-exclusive universality advantage. In the same two-hidden-layer scaling, unrestricted dense hidden-layer gradient descent has increments that are sums of m outer products divided by n. At fixed J, explicitly unrolling its updates gives

    W_k = W0 + (1/n) sum_{ell=1}^{R_k} c_ell u_ell v_ell^T,

with R_k<=mJ for Euler and R_k<=2mJ for Heun, up to indexing of stages. Applying this matrix or its transpose uses W0 plus finitely many empirical inner products and vector operations. It therefore fits the same polynomial-program reduction and faces the same nonpolynomial extension obligation. The response-memory distinction is bounded representation size as history grows, not exclusive access to finite-step universality.

**Strongest competing explanations and baselines.** The existing initial identity separates singular fourth moments. A positive trained result could still be explained by that first correction, by nearly frozen features, by finite-width sampling, or by generic fast structured learning. Necessary controls are:

| Comparator | Question it resolves |
|---|---|
| Dense iid Gaussian plus independent dense-Gaussian repeats | Ensemble sampling floor; no same-seed matrix-closeness interpretation. |
| Dense independent Haar left/right bases with the candidate's exact finite singular array | Does discrepancy arise from singular-value discretization or structured singular vectors? |
| The theorem-aligned fast quarter-circle construction | Does the intended complete spectrum survive adaptive reuse at useful feature motion? |
| Flat spectrum/SORF and Gaussian-diagonal core | Are differences consistent with the return-law calculation? These are mechanism controls, not sufficient state-of-the-art competitors. |
| Published radial-row-rescaled Fastfood | Does the claimed gap survive the actual Fastfood construction? Core-only comparisons cannot support a blanket claim about Fastfood. |
| Half zero and half sqrt(2) singular values, shuffled | This has second moment 1 and fourth moment 2 like quarter-circle, but sixth moment 4 rather than 5. It separates fourth-moment repair from full-spectrum matching. |
| W_bad=U diag(s), retaining the same left factor | Passes the independent Gaussian return-energy test exactly; exposes whether that test misses harmful right-basis structure. Treat as a diagnostic, not a competitive model. |
| Fixed-feature readout, trained Adaptive Fastfood/ACDC, and unrestricted dense training | Separate useful feature learning from kernel behavior and practical benefit from already available structured learning. |

For the standard square Fastfood radial normalization, write its normalized version as diag(r_i/sqrt(n)) times a row-normalized Gaussian-diagonal core, with r_i distributed as chi_n. The radial factors concentrate near 1. Thus there is no reason to expect this row correction alone to repair the core's asymptotic singular fourth moment; establishing the precise comparison under the chosen Fastfood convention, or simply measuring it, is preferable to labeling all variants as having the core spectrum. The original S changes finite-dimensional singular values and must not be omitted silently.

**Decisive next experiment, without launching it here.** Preserve the already frozen training gate. Its three seeds and two widths can reject a gross mismatch or indicate a direction; they cannot establish universality. Before an expanded campaign, compare full prediction/feature/response trajectories with a dense–dense ensemble discrepancy floor, report initialization variation, and require the existing feature-motion and fixed-feature improvement gates. Equivalence needs an explicit error tolerance and uncertainty bound, not failure to find a statistically significant difference.

If the gate survives, the most informative small addition is a basis/spectrum factorial: Haar versus fast bases, crossed with quarter-circle versus the fourth-moment-matched two-point spectrum, plus Gaussian reference and the bad-right-basis diagnostic. Use the same training data and physical dynamics, with independent matrix draws; shared Gaussian A may reduce noise but does not couple matrices closely. Add 2–4 nonlinear forward/transpose reuses of the same W to locate divergence before training, then inspect the trained trajectory. Agreement only at the first reuse supports the known moment calculation; quarter-circle versus two-point separation later supports sensitivity to more than the fourth moment; fast versus Haar separation with the same exact singular array diagnoses hidden structure. A width trend is needed to distinguish finite-width error from a persistent effect.

No additional budget is spent by this audit. Recommend at most one preregistered follow-up of 24 trajectories, two widths, three seeds, and four strategically selected extra conditions, only after the current gate is interpreted and with a fixed GPU-time cap. Full strong-baseline benchmarking is a later task. Do not expand by tuning until a desired ordering appears. A practical claim ultimately requires measured end-to-end speed and storage at matched learning quality, including transform implementation overhead and memory-state costs.

**Frozen claim status.** Exact: the independent-probe right-basis invariance; q=1 permutation equivariance and ensemble-law equivalence; algebraic correspondence of the fast sampler to the four-signed-permutation construction; the polynomial-program reduction assuming published D.9. Published precedent: fast prescribed-spectrum matrices with nonlinear AMP/tree universality. Open here: full tanh-Heun transfer, growing-time/memory limits, practical accuracy/speed advantage, and novelty beyond applying established structured universality to this particular compressed learning dynamics. No breakthrough or priority claim is supported.
