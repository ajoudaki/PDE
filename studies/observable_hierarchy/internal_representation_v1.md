## 3. The cutoff limit stays at fixed level

The crucial construction is not an added unbounded response field. It is a family of bounded-operand observation graphs, indexed by a scalar \(R\ge1\), used to evaluate a scalar derivative.

For every fixed \(R\), \(T_R(v)=R\tanh(v/R)\) is an allowed three-node graph. Replacing an action on a covector \(p\) with an action on \(T_R(p)\), and a bounded multiplication \(bp\) with \(bT_R(p)\), produces correctly typed observation nodes. Sums and scalar multiples remain valid \(L\) nodes. No unrestricted \(L\times L\) product is fed into an action.

The number of nodes is independent of \(R\). Letting \(R\) vary only varies finitely many real coefficients of this fixed graph family. Shared use of one cutoff parameter is a one-dimensional subfamily of the already permitted finite-dimensional mark space.

The convergence proof is valid:
\[
\|T_R(v_R)-v\|_2
\le \|v_R-v\|_2+\|T_R(v)-v\|_2.
\]
Starting from bounded terminal covectors, finite reverse induction gives \(p^R\to p\) in \(L^2\). Its norm bound is uniform in \(R\), because saturation contracts the \(L^2\) norm and the remaining multipliers/operators have fixed finite bounds. Equation (8) then pairs a covector error with a uniformly \(L^2\)-bounded current field. The original test graph and its covectors are fixed independently of the newly integrated training input, so the claimed uniformity in that input follows.

Only the joint laws of the fields at each fixed \(R\), together with the fields appearing in the same contraction, are needed to evaluate the derivative limit. One does not need an infinite joint tuple containing all cutoff values. The common realization is used to prove convergence, but the limit's scalar values are read entirely from the fixed-level marked-law interface.

Local uniformity in physical time follows from compactness of each exact continuous \(L^2\) covector curve and the saturation finite-net argument stated in the candidate. No derivative of a covector is taken. Consequently the earlier obstruction involving differentiation of \(W(bu)\) into an undefined \(W(\dot b\,u)\) does not invalidate this compiler: its covectors are a one-time reverse-chain-rule proof device, not coordinates whose own differential equations are asserted.

This is an exact limit interface, not an effective finite procedure. That limitation is already disclosed and belongs to C-H2/C-H3 under the permitted milestone wording.

* The retained result is a finite-dimensional population law, not an element of a named full Hilbert space with a raw operator application interface.

* The dependence on scalar marks is prescribed by ordinary finite affine/nonlinear operations. No bit-decoding or arbitrary real-number code is present.

Density of a probe set in a Hilbert space means a concrete bounded operator is determined if one knows its actual output vectors on that set. The fixed-level interface here gives laws of bounded-size tuples of outputs, not actual named output vectors or all joint laws of arbitrary size. Those are different data. Recovering a probability-algebra isomorphism that respects every observable product requires the complete finite-joint system used in Section 5.

In particular, the density observation alone does not prove that some fixed \(H_n\) equals a full parameter distribution or determines the raw dense operator. Pairwise laws generally do not determine higher joint laws; elementary independent-sign versus parity-constrained-sign examples illustrate that logical distinction. This illustration is not claimed to construct two canonical networks with equal \(H_n\), and no such counterexample is needed to reject the unsupported density-to-full-state implication.

Such sufficiency would still not automatically be a prohibited raw-coordinate substitution. The present audit found neither a proof of prohibited encoding nor an arbitrary-query back door.

The first row \(w\) and readout \(c\) are scalar neuronal observables recoverable from allowed passive/output responses; retaining their finite-dimensional joint populations is not retaining a distribution of the full dense middle layer.
