### 3.3 Positive filters, density and strong approximation

Let `S a=psi.T a`, `G=S* S`, and `eta=2^-N`. Then

`Q=S(G+eta I)^-1 S*`, while `U*U=G(G+eta I)^-1<=I`.

Thus Q is a positive contraction, generally not a projection. For `v=S a`,

`(I-Q)v=eta S(G+eta I)^-1 a`.

The scalar multiplier is `eta sqrt(lambda)/(lambda+eta)`, whose maximum for `lambda>=0` is `sqrt(eta)/2`. Consequently `||(I-Q)v||2<=sqrt(eta)||a||/2`. An earlier coefficient vector can be zero-padded into every later raw list, without changing its norm. Density and `||I-Q||<=1` then prove strong convergence on the whole initialized observable space. A vanishing smallest nonzero Gram eigenvalue causes no gap in this proof.

I explicitly tested the tempting stronger statement and rejected it: finite-rank Q cannot converge in operator norm to identity on an infinite-dimensional space, since its finite-rank complement contains a unit vector. The proposal does not need or assert that convergence. Instead, on the reducing observable spaces,

`B_N=Q2 A0 Q1`, `||B_N||<=2`, and

`||(B_N-A0)v||<=2||(Q1-I)v||+||(Q2-I)A0 v||`.

The identical argument applies to the actual adjoint. A finite epsilon net upgrades uniformly bounded strong convergence to uniform convergence on any compact set of L2 vectors, not on a whole bounded ball.

Invariance is also essential: the relevant spaces reduce both action orientations, and canonical Euler steps starting from initialization remain in them. A learned increment is a rank between those spaces. Convergence of restarted Euler to the existing tail-bearing canonical path, using the explicit one-reference Osgood argument in C-H1, transfers this invariant property to the flow. This avoids assuming that invariance of an arbitrary Euler sequence alone establishes invariance of a nonlinear flow.
