\]

Here `D` is injective but not bounded below. The first-row clock cancels the differentiated own gate at the two reference atoms. That cancellation is valid for the base-law homogeneous differential; it must not be reused for a general nonlinear changed-law field.

The kernel identity `ker Gamma=ker S=ker E*` is the decisive zero-mode compatibility. Positivity of `ES` alone would not rule out a nilpotent part in `SE`. With the compatibility, the finite-dimensional pseudoinverse gives the exact bounded endpoint semigroup and the projection

\[
 P=I-S_\infty\Gamma_\infty^+E_\infty.
\]

The proof then controls the actual nonautonomous generator by an integrable operator-norm perturbation of that endpoint generator. This requires both residual-curvature integrability and convergence of the finitely many evaluation columns. The latter uses the endpoint active `Q` fourth moment; raw L² convergence alone would not suffice for its gate-times-query product. These hypotheses and the reference endpoint must therefore remain part of the proof dependency, even though a full-rank Gram is unnecessary.

Strategic deduction (A) is a sharper consequence of the supplied estimates. Conditional on their validity, they imply

\[
 v_\sigma(t)=tP b_{\sigma,\infty}+v_{\sigma,0}+o_{\mathcal V}(1).
 \tag{A}

Here `v_sigma,0` denotes some fixed vector, not the initial value. To justify (A), set `L_infty=-2S_infty E_infty` and `B(t)=L(t)-L_infty`. Equations C.4.6.P21–22 give exponential decay of `||B(t)||`; C.4.6.S47 and its subsequent factor estimates give a uniform Hölder modulus in feature time for the source. Since `s_dagger-s(t)<=10 exp(-t/5)`, the source difference from its endpoint is integrable and exponentially decaying. The theorem gives `||v_sigma(t)||=O(1+t)`. Therefore

\[
 g(t)=B(t)v_\sigma(t)+b_\sigma(t)-b_{\sigma,\infty}
 \quad\hbox{belongs to }L^1([0,\infty);\mathcal V).
\]

The endpoint semigroup is identity on `ran P` and exponentially decaying on its finite-dimensional complementary range; positive eigenvalues, if present, have a positive minimum for this fixed reference. Variation of constants gives

\[
 v_\sigma(t)=\int_0^t e^{(t-s)L_\infty}b_{\sigma,\infty}\,ds
             +\int_0^t e^{(t-s)L_\infty}g(s)\,ds.
\]

Subtract `t P b_sigma,infty`. The stable part of the first integral converges; boundedness and strong convergence of the semigroup let the second integral converge to `P int_0^infty g(s) ds`, by dominated convergence. This proves (A), including the zero-Gram case. This is a deduction conditional on P1, not a separately established repository result.

Strategic deduction (B) gives a precise possible falsifier of bounded endpoint sensitivity. Let `g_u` be the raw predictor gradient at the reference endpoint, and let `Pi` be the raw orthogonal projector onto the two weighted training gradients. For `nu=delta_(sqrt2u,y)`, reference interpolation removes the reference part of `b_sigma,infty`. The exact conversion `R q_infty(u)=g_u` and C.4.6.P31 give

\[

 =-2r_\infty(u,y)\|(I-\Pi)g_u\|_{\rm raw}^2.
 \tag{B}

Thus a passive gradient outside the training-gradient span and a nonzero new residual force a nonzero secular prediction slope. P1 does not prove that such an input exists; merely displaying a large kernel of `E_infty` does not prove it. Conversely, vanishing of (B) for a selected input would not establish bounded response for every law. For a general direction, `P b_sigma,infty=0` is the exact condition eliminating state secular growth in (A); cancellation in a single observed prediction is weaker.

This matters strategically. If a nonzero direction is verified, a fixed-reference first-order approximation is naturally nonuniform when `epsilon t` is order one. It does not imply that the actual nonlinear predictor diverges. It means the perturbed optimizer may continue selecting among the reference interpolants on a slower time scale. A uniform-in-time `O(epsilon)` tube or endpoint derivative cannot be inferred from P1 and should not be the next universal claim.

**The topology does not control the nonlinear remainder.** The clock norm is stronger than the pulled-back raw norm, since `delta w_a=phi'(w_a) xi_a`. Its inverse need not be bounded. Both are still L²-based norms for fields; convergence in them is strong Hilbert convergence, but weak in the regularity needed for multiplying changing unbounded fields. They are not Banach-algebra norms.

For example, on an atomless probability space, take a fixed `a!=0` and `h_k=a 1_(E_k)` with `P(E_k)->0`. At the scalar background zero,

\[
 \frac{\|\tanh(h_k)-h_k\|_2}{\|h_k\|_2}
 =\frac{|\tanh(a)-a|}{|a|}>0.
\]

Bounded scalar derivatives therefore do not make this Nemytskii map Fréchet differentiable from L² to L². Products of two arbitrary L² increments have only an L¹ guarantee. The obstruction is relevant to the off-support inverse-gate/reverse-query products, readout-times-hidden variations, and activation remainders of the nonlinear field. Smoothness of each finite-dimensional network does not make the needed remainder estimate uniform in width.

The appropriate resolver is control of **reachable normalized nonlinear deviations**, with sufficient weighted uniform integrability to make their integrated remainder `o(epsilon)`. An ambient assertion for every L² direction is unnecessarily strong and can be false. Simply changing the norm to L4 is not a completed repair: the initialized action is known to be bounded on L², and bounded action in the proposed stronger space must itself be proved. Likewise, requiring every changed nonlinear state to have a clock-L² representation would add a hypothesis absent from the natural raw-state problem. Raw-state and prediction validity should be the theorem's contract; any stronger chart used in its proof needs its own reached-state justification.

**Total variation and transport have different uses.** With total-variation mass, a moved unit atom has distance two from its starting atom however small the move; the reference law with only one half-mass atom moved has distance one from the original reference. Empirical laws of a nonatomic distribution have distance two from it almost surely. A bound proportional to total variation alone cannot quantify small geometry changes or sampling consistency.

Strategic deduction (C) is an immediate linear transport consequence in the supplied proof that the summary's exclusion obscures. C.4.6.3, §7 proves a deterministic uniform `1/3` Hölder bound for the Hilbert-valued integrand `b(t,u,y)` on the compact feature/data set. For probability laws `nu,rho`, integrate its difference against any coupling and use concavity of `z^(1/3)`:

\[
 \sup_{t\ge0}\|b_{\nu-\nu_*}(t)-b_{\rho-\nu_*}(t)\|_{\mathcal V}
 \le C_Y\mathcal W_1(\nu,\rho)^{1/3}.
 \tag{C}
\]

The transport cost is the stated normalized-input-plus-label cost. Duhamel then yields a `C_U C_Y T W1(nu,rho)^(1/3)` bound for the response difference and the analogous whole-circle prediction bound. No optimal-coupling existence theorem is needed: take the infimum of coupling costs after applying the inequality. This is a Hölder modulus, not a Lipschitz bound on the Wasserstein signed-measure norm. It does not establish a transport derivative with respect to moving atoms, or any nonlinear law stability. The theorem summary should distinguish those missing claims from the linear modulus already implied by its source proof.

**Finite derivative capture is not nonlinear law differentiability.** The exact finite derivative is meaningful and survives the stated width limit. Neither a nonlinear population flow for fixed contamination nor its derivative follows. The elementary family

\[
 a_n(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon}{1+n\epsilon},\qquad\epsilon\ge0,
\]

has `a_n'(0)=1` for every `n`, whereas its pointwise limiting function is zero. This is a logical counterexample to the interchange, not a claim about these neural networks. A width-independent difference-quotient remainder or an equivalent compactness argument is indispensable.

The existing reference comparison in C.4.5 also does not already construct changed-law flows. It places approximations to a fixed nearby law within a small positive distance of the reference. Two such approximations can remain twice that distance apart as their meshes tend to zero. Proximity to the reference is not the Cauchy property at that changed law. To obtain existence and uniqueness there, one needs estimates for changing-law reachable trajectories or a nonlinear comparison whose error vanishes between their approximations. P1's bounded propagator is a promising tool for that task; it is not that estimate.

**The single recommended next theorem.** Fix the trained physical interval `[0,40]`, the same two-hidden-tanh architecture, Gaussian initialization and GF metric, and bounded labels. Prove that there is a radius `delta_40>0`, independent of sample count and width, such that every law in

\[
 \mathcal U_{40}=\{\mu:\mathcal W_1(\mu,\nu_*)<\delta_{40}\}
\]

has an autonomous strong population GF through time 40 on the common action realization, uniquely restartable on its specified reached-state class. Require continuity between laws in this neighborhood with a stated modulus tending to zero, and actual finite-GF capture for empirical approximations to each fixed law, including whole-circle predictions and paired hidden observables. As part of this same theorem, require that for contamination paths contained in the neighborhood the derivative is the P1 response, with the following proposed quantitative raw-state remainder target (D):

\[
 \sup_{t\le40}
 \|\theta_{\mu_\epsilon}(t)-\theta_*(t)
            -\epsilon R(t)v_\sigma(t)\|_{\rm raw}
 \le \epsilon\rho_{40}(\epsilon),
 \qquad \rho_{40}(\epsilon)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{D}
\]

The constants and modulus should be uniform over perturbing laws on the stated compact observation space; at minimum any weaker uniformity must be stated explicitly and must still permit the empirical-law approximation. The corresponding same-width finite remainder must be controlled in the ordered `limsup_n` sense, so (D) does not hide another derivative/width interchange. Raw-state differences in (D) are on one population carrier; finite-to-population identification continues to use the proxy topology, never subtraction across carriers. A superlinear modulus is the essential requirement; a quadratic remainder should not be imposed without sufficient moment control. Raw-GD differentiation is not needed for this next theorem.

This is one theorem bridging the missing claim levels: nonlinear existence, identification from finite training, and validity of the trained response. The transport-open domain is essential because fixed reference atoms retained with weight `1-epsilon` do not form a neighborhood that contains empirical samples from a nonatomic law. It also ensures that genuinely correlated and nonatomic training are present at nonzero perturbation size. The theorem must prove its reachable tail and remainder estimates, rather than assume an abstract differentiable law-to-flow map or the desired bound (D).

Its decisive dependencies and falsifiers are concrete. First, control the products generated by normalized nonlinear deviations along actual finite approximations; failure of their weighted uniform integrability invalidates an L²-chart remainder proof. Second, make approximations Cauchy at each fixed changed law and establish uniqueness there; a fixed positive reference-comparison error does not do this. Third, preserve the true initialized action and transpose through that limit. A nonvanishing normalized prediction remainder along `epsilon_j->0`, `n_j->infinity` falsifies the uniform finite-response contract. Failure only of clock integrability may defeat that chart while leaving a raw-state theorem possible. None of these outcomes can be repaired by shrinking the neighborhood with width or by proving a result for frozen features.

The minimal subsequent roadmap has two logical transitions. Once this trained-interval law map exists, use its two-law stability for empirical replacement and sampling, and combine actual achieved risk with a specified target distribution and an attainable-risk benchmark. That step connects learning under nonzero correlated laws to out-of-sample prediction. Then address fixed-law long-time selection, with the neutral drift in (A) made explicit rather than absorbed into a bounded-error assertion. Nonzero (B) requires following or rebasing that drift; no endpoint result follows from repeatedly choosing smaller contamination as the target accuracy improves. Extending the argument to additional trained hidden layers becomes meaningful after these two obligations are understood in this minimal nonlinear matrix-reuse model.

There is an intrinsic limit to any universal risk claim on the present admissible class. Every no-bias tanh predictor is odd. For the equally weighted observations `(u,+1)` and `(-u,+1)`, its mean-square risk is `1+f(u)^2>=1`. Noise and representational constraints therefore require an attainable target; arbitrary bounded-label laws cannot all enjoy the reference's fitting conclusion. Small expected train-test gap, or infinitesimal influence, would not address this obstruction. The requested scientific objective is advanced when the new theorem supplies an actual trained law map and its selection properties on a fixed correlated distribution, rather than only another isolated positive response.
