## Corrections, simplifications and exact scope preserved

1. The earlier zero-top-feature witness in
   `ACTUAL_SCHWARZIAN_MATERIAL_HESSIAN.md` has output zero and nonzero readout.
   It cannot be reached at positive feature time from zero readout, because
   `f'=||b||²/n` would force a stationary path. The candidate instead imports
   the later positive-bulk family, whose common top feature remains positive
   while integrating backward to exactly zero readout. It does not reclassify
   the earlier witness as reachable.
2. The full Hessian retains the two mixed trained-matrix terms. All derivatives
   of reused transposes and trained readout are present in the material
   derivative. The special tangent makes some terms vanish by actual algebra;
   none of those blocks is frozen or removed from the field.
3. The scalar `m=(pi/4)²` of the source is renamed `mu`, preserving canonical
   `m` for sample count. The source's hidden coordinate dimension `d` is renamed
   `N_h`, preserving canonical input dimension `d=1`. The potential is named
   `mathscr F`, distinguishing it from the arctangent primitive used elsewhere.
   Matrix scales and ordinary finite RMS factors remain explicit.
4. The candidate derives the complete Hessian quadratic form directly by two
   straight-line variations, then differentiates that form for a fixed tangent.
   Polarization supplies the full symmetric operator. This is the same material
   derivative as the source's operator formula. Sections 14.6–14.7 then prove the
   graph-volume and projection statements independently from those identities.
5. The only historical graph-volume correction concerns the distinction between
   an intrinsic-volume bound and a projected-volume LOWER bound. The candidate
   retains this distinction: the full volume gives an upper projected-volume
   bound, while the explicit angle factor has no proved lower bound here. The complete material-Hessian and reachable-Hessian review entries
   record no required mathematical corrections at their certified hashes.
6. The backward bootstrap bounds only primal quantities. The operator bound on
   the Hessian is evaluated at the terminal state. A bound on the time integral
   of the signed material expression, or a failure under extra Hessian-history
   conditions, is not inferred from the endpoint calculation. The separate
   positive nuclear-volume theorem gives a width-normalized integrated full
   Hessian bound, which is a different statement.
7. Physical time is differentiated independently: the Jacobian is
   `C=alpha B-2bb^T/n`, and its derivative includes all rank-one and residual
   terms. The endpoint bounds control these corrections. Multiplying the
   feature Hessian by a clock value alone would be insufficient.
8. Terminal feature/physical times can depend on width. They occupy a common
   positive compact interval for fixed epsilon, not a single common time.
   The theorem does not assert failure of convergence at a fixed time.

## Positive-volume proof assembly

The global primal estimates are explicit nonnegative polynomials obtained by
integrating the bounded readout field and the two rank-one updates. The
first-layer RMS bound is supplied as well, closing finite-dimensional
continuation in both feature directions. Smooth flow dependence and full
variational invertibility are justified through the integral equation and its
inverse linear ODE.

The candidate supplies nuclear duality, triangle/rank bounds, diagonal
sandwich bounds and projector trace bounds from singular-value decompositions.
The complete trained Hessian therefore has nuclear norm at most `n P_S(M,R0)`.
Jacobi differentiation gives the log-volume derivative on every transported
full-rank plane; its trace is bounded by that nuclear norm, independently of
plane dimension and initial plane choice.

For expectations, a sphere net of size at most `9^n` and scalar Gaussian
exponential moments give an explicit operator tail. Integration proves every
fixed operator-norm moment uniformly in width. Jensen proves readout RMS
moments of order `n^(-p)` and uniform first-layer RMS moments. Hölder then
controls every polynomial envelope without conditioning on a good event.

The forward physical argument keeps both signs of the initial residual and
uses its exact scalar decay equation. The total absolute feature clock is
bounded by `2T(1+P R0)`. The rank-one Jacobian correction has nuclear norm
`2||b||²/n`; retaining it gives a polynomial physical envelope and the same
expected `O_T(n)` intrinsic-volume conclusion. Backward physical completeness
is not assumed.

The projection formula is proved by orthonormalizing the transported hidden
plane and comparing the two Gram spectra. It includes singular projected
Jacobians. The slope formula and Riccati equation are restricted to intervals
where the hidden block is invertible. The physical block version is displayed
separately. No analytic critical-set, inverse-branch-density or covariance
result is required.

The Gaussian-divergence source has two historical wording corrections about
full-state mutual singularity and a fixed positive-time smoothing scope. Neither
statement is used here: only its complete global primal estimates, clock bound
and Gaussian-moment arguments enter this positive addition.
