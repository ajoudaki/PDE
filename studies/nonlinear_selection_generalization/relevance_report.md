    q(alpha) = cos(alpha)^3 - sin(alpha)^3 + sin(2 alpha)^2 v(alpha),
    v(alpha) = sum_{k>=0} [a_k cos((2k+1)alpha) + b_k sin((2k+1)alpha)],
    sum_{k>=0} (2k+1)^s (|a_k|+|b_k|) <= R <= 1/8,  s >= 1.

The input density p is normalized arc-density with 1/2 <= p <= 2 and a fixed
Lipschitz bound D. The candidates include declared finite degrees and weighted
infinite series. Labels are bounded, have conditional mean q, and have
conditionally centered noise of variance at most sigma^2. These restrictions
are substantive: targets are odd and interpolate the anchors, and the input
dimension and architecture remain fixed. They must stay visible in the theorem.

The family is independent of the network's eventual answer. The occurrence of
the established reference F_* in the analysis space E_N does not redefine q or
its coefficients, and no changed-law trajectory enters those definitions. The
uniform minimum over the permitted density class can determine a stop without

reference constant, not a demonstrated efficient computation. The chapter
must not describe this as milestone C or as a usable numerical stopping
implementation without additional work.

This is not a thin continuation of C.4.9's single-atom witness. Every admitted
input density has mass across the whole circle, the target coefficient family

## Quantitative scope and milestone B assessment

The finite-mode candidate gives, on a specified common episode [0,T],

    E(tau) <= exp(-lambda_N tau/2) E(0)
              + A_N(T) [1-exp(-lambda_N tau/2)],
    A_N(T) = 2(1+2 L0^2/lambda_N) (a_N + C_f V T)^2,
    a_N = R/(2N+3)^s,

with a_N=0 for a target of declared degree N. The horizon satisfies a stated
degree-dependent restriction, including C_d^2 V T <= lambda_N/8, as well as
the continuation bounds. It is not a tail defined to equal the unknown trained
risk. On the robust finite-degree subclass the supplied lower bound on E(0)
allows a fixed positive T at which the floor is below the initial error and
the displayed upper bound decreases with training duration.

The separate statistical argument evaluates the fluctuations at the
deterministic population path and then controls empirical feedback by the
proved one-reference Osgood comparison. Its input and noise terms scale as
1/sqrt(m), followed by the explicit Osgood modulus, and yield an error tending
to zero at fixed episode and failure probabilities. This is more than a
fluctuation statement around an unidentified target predictor because the
population approximation bound and the robust risk margin are supplied
separately. Combining those bounds makes a finite sample threshold possible;
the assembled theorem should display that threshold or its explicit inverse
modulus condition and a positive remaining risk margin.

The physical training law is the unchanged mixture with exact anchor weights
and the empirical added law, starting from the original Gaussian arrays. The
continuation route proposes capture through t=T/epsilon, with width first for
each fixed positive epsilon and fixed empirical law, epsilon second, and sample
size last when taking a sampling limit. This preserves an actual finite noisy
training interpretation. It does not provide a quantitative width bound or a
simultaneous width/contamination/sample theorem. Zero slow time is excluded
from capture because the fitted reference is an initial-layer limit.

Together these ingredients satisfy B's stated aim in a local finite-episode
regime. The improvement is positive independently of width and contamination
weight after the permitted choices of degree, class, time, and sample count.
Its practical size is not established. Increasing N reduces the target tail
but may reduce lambda_N and the usable horizon; the result is not universal
consistency as N or time increases. It proves neither a final changed-law
endpoint nor an advantage over frozen, linear, or shallow models, and it does
not establish that feature motion causes the risk improvement. Those stronger
claims are not needed for B as currently written.
