The strongest structural ingredient is continuum separation: every nonzero
odd residual gives a nonzero projected force, even in its middle-layer block.
This is much stronger than detecting one added atom. The proof uses protected
large Gaussian first rows, the nonzero odd Fourier coefficients of the sign
kernel, weighted tensor separation, and the impossibility of cancelling a
continuous residual measure with anchor atoms. It does not assume an invertible
training Gram matrix or an injective trained middle action.

That operator is nevertheless compact. The proof therefore correctly uses
positive conditioning only on each fixed finite-dimensional target space.
This distinction is central to the result's value and its limitations.

The robust strict-gain class has nonempty relative interior at a fixed Fourier
cap, not a single target chosen from the network's answer. Its input support
is the entire circle. The learning margin does not vanish just because the
added law has mixture weight `epsilon`: the theorem measures the added law's
unseen risk itself. Sampling is from that added law; the two known anchors
retain exact supplied weights. This is not iid sampling of the full mixture.

## What approval must preserve

1. The exact two-hidden-layer bias-free tanh model, initialization, mobilities,
   unhalved square loss, circle input normalization and GF clock.
2. A finite positive slow-time episode, with a population approximation floor.
   More samples reduce statistical error; they do not automatically remove
   that floor. The full-family upper bound need not be informative everywhere;
   the strict-gain corollary uses the declared robust finite-cap subfamily.
3. Positive but unevaluated conditioning constants. The resulting horizon,
   risk margin, hidden margin and sample threshold need not be practical.
   No useful uniform mode-count dependence has been proved. Compactness rules
   out a positive lower bound on the whole infinite-dimensional odd space.
4. The hidden observable is the combined uniform-circle-plus-two-anchor
   second-layer displacement. Its positive lower bound is not a separately
   established circle-only displacement bound.
5. Width tends to infinity at fixed sample and positive contamination; then
   contamination tends to zero; sample count grows afterwards. There is no
   simultaneous rate, explicit width requirement, raw-GD theorem or global
   changed-law endpoint in this addition.
6. Learning occurs with changing hidden representations. Superiority to frozen
   features, initialized NTK, deep linear training or trained shallow networks
   has not been proved, nor has a causal benefit of hidden movement itself.

In particular, increasing Fourier cap in the present estimate is not an
accuracy hierarchy. Conditioning can deteriorate while the allowed episode
shrinks. Solving that longer-training problem would require additional
mathematics, not a relabeling of B's statistical convergence.

## Recalibrated next target

B supplies the dynamics, a genuine task family, statistical control and
finite-network transfer. However, its risk proof treats movement away from
the fitted reference geometry as an error. The proof is therefore compatible
with the explanation that an appropriate fixed kernel would learn equally
well. The next high-value question is whether the nonlinear evolution does
something beneficial that this fixed geometry cannot do under a fair budget.

Use the **full tangent kernel frozen at the fitted reference** as the first
control. Give it the same initial whole-circle predictor, all parameter tangent
directions, anchor constraints, raw metric, data and canonical training clock.
Training only a frozen readout is a weaker control: B already nearly supplies
an initial risk-rate advantage over that restricted model. The full tangent
control has exactly the same initial risk derivative as nonlinear training.

The research target should explain favorable changes in the relative learning
of independently variable task components and prove their finite-episode
population-risk consequence on a robust, structurally described Fourier
family. A useful core inequality is

\[
\mathcal E_\nu(Z_\nu(T))-\mathcal E_\nu(P_\nu(T))\ge a>0,
\]

uniformly over the declared target/density class. Here `P` is B's actual
selected nonlinear prediction and `Z` is the matched frozen full-tangent
evolution. The target includes a controlled finite-horizon remainder and
sampling/finite-GF transfer of the surviving margin.

That inequality alone is not the full scientific success criterion. The
study should distinguish useful redistribution across task components from
an overall scalar acceleration. Any scalar-rate control must use an explicit,
fairly matched gain or optimization budget; an arbitrarily accelerated frozen
learner is a different comparison. The target family must be motivated by its
harmonic structure, not defined retrospectively as wherever a correction has
positive sign. A tiny favorable patch with no structural explanation would
not deliver the intended mechanism insight.

The comparison initially isolates a benefit of **nonlinear constrained
evolution**. It changes the anchor projector as well as the features and
readout. Attributing a gain specifically to middle-layer representation
learning requires a further decomposition or appropriate control; the
full-tangent comparison alone must not be advertised as that attribution.
It also compares a tangent kernel at a *trained reference*, not the NTK at
original initialization, and therefore does not complete E's wider claim.

There is a substantive mathematical starting point: the exact difference
between the two prediction equations is forced by the evolving kernel minus
the reference kernel. B controls that evolution and reached derivatives.
The new obligation is a favorable signed effect tied to task structure,
with a remainder that preserves it. B's injectivity, compactness and hidden
motion do not imply this sign. A finite-order diagnostic can help assess it,
but a formal time series or an uncontrolled initial coefficient is not a
solution, and no positive Taylor convergence radius is assumed.

This is narrower than full E without simply extending B's breadth: it tests
the missing causal explanation using already justified dynamics. Full E would
add unknown directions or interactions, growing task complexity, explicit
sample/time/width bounds and competitive lower bounds all at once. D is also
valuable, but adding a second activation entails another justified dynamical
scope and a nontrivial risk crossover before the benefit of the present
feature evolution has been isolated.

The recommendation is conditional and falsifiable. First determine whether
the signed kernel change has a structural interpretation and a robust useful
effect on a fixed family. If only generic curvature or scalar acceleration
appears, do not shrink or search the family indefinitely until a favorable
witness remains. Record the obstruction to this mechanism and recalibrate
toward D, where activation-dependent modal structure may supply the missing
explanation. A failed comparison route is not a theorem that feature learning
never helps. There is currently no identified GF obstacle that motivates a
switch to GD; the roadmap's conditional fallback remains unchanged.

The eventual obligations are still activation-dependent bias, efficient
unknown-structure discovery against linear and initialized/frozen alternatives,
trained-shallow separation, and compatibility with C's independently proved
solver. This proposed next task addresses one central missing mechanism; it
does not silently declare any of those larger obligations complete.
