# Independent assessment of the next strategic milestone after candidate B

Frozen assessment date: 2026-09-12. This is a strategic assessment, not an independent correctness verdict or a new proof campaign. C is already running and is excluded as the next target.

**Recommendation.** Insert one focused mechanism milestone before attempting full D or E: determine whether the nonlinear added-data evolution improves unseen risk beyond the frozen *full tangent model at the fitted reference*, on a robust full-circle Fourier family already admitted by B. Require an actual finite-episode inequality, sampling transfer and the existing width-first finite-GF bridge. Comparing only with a frozen readout is a useful preliminary calculation, but would leave a stronger fixed-kernel explanation intact. This target tests the scientific value of the very feature evolution B has succeeded in retaining, with much less scope expansion than changing activations or introducing high-dimensional unknown structure.

The positive outcome is not guaranteed. The leading comparison coefficient has a sign that B does not control. A rigorously identified failure of that sign criterion would be useful evidence for choosing the subsequent direction; it would not invalidate B or establish that feature learning never helps.

**What B actually contributes.** Taking its statements as valid, B connects original Gaussian-initialized, jointly trained two-hidden-layer tanh GF to noisy-sample unseen risk on a network-independent target family. The input distributions have full-circle support and uniformly positive density. Targets have independently varying odd Fourier coefficients, rather than being defined by the trained answer. Candidate C.4.10.2 constructs a common nonlinear episode for every bounded added law, including every empirical law, and captures it from original fixed-mixture GF at physical times of order (1/\varepsilon). C.4.10.3 proves continuum injectivity of the endpoint force, including its middle block. C.4.10.4 combines finite-mode conditioning, an approximation floor, separated input/noise errors, a deterministic positive stop, and robust risk-improvement and paired second-hidden-displacement margins.

That is a substantial useful-learning result. It is stronger than observing feature motion together with low training loss: the risk is relative to the regression target on fresh inputs, and the paired motion survives both width and contamination limits. It remains a result about useful learning **with** evolving features. The theorem does not establish that the evolving features cause better risk than a competent frozen alternative. Its risk proof uses the positive geometry at the fitted reference, then controls changes away from that geometry as errors; see NGL5–NGL7. Thus a fixed-kernel explanation is still viable. The hidden-motion lower bound NGL19 closes the “only the readout moves” description of the actual network, but does not close the “the same benefit is already available to a fixed model” explanation.

The scope also matters. Inputs lie in dimension two, the anchor task is fixed and supplied exactly, targets share its oddness and anchor labels, and the strict-margin subfamily has a declared finite coefficient cap. The lower hidden observation is the sum of circle and anchor observations in NGL17, not a proved circle-only margin. B neither discovers a previously unknown low-dimensional structure nor gives a sample-complexity advantage over models using the prescribed Fourier structure.

**The quantitative and infinite-dimensional bottlenecks.** Three are decisive for planning.

1. The operators (T_p,T_{H,p}) are compact and injective. Injectivity supplies positive conditioning on each fixed (E_N); it supplies no coercivity on the infinite-dimensional odd space. The constants \(\lambda_N,\lambda_{H,N}\) are defined through finite matrices and a compact density minimization, but have no evaluated or asymptotic lower bounds. Their dependence on the implicit trained endpoint is substantive. Positivity proved using rare protected Gaussian rows can be compatible with extremely weak conditioning.
2. Approximation, conditioning and continuation do not yet combine into an accuracy hierarchy. The target-tail bound is \(a_N\le R/(2N+3)^s\), whereas NGL2 requires \(T\le\lambda_N/(8C_d^2V)\) and has floor \(2(1+2L_0^2/\lambda_N)(a_N+LVT)^2\). Neither \(a_N^2/\lambda_N\to0\) nor a useful increasing-mode horizon is established. In fact the nested spaces generated by \(h\) times odd trigonometric polynomials are dense in odd (L^2): truncate away from the finitely many zeros of (h), divide there by (h), and approximate in (L^2) by odd trigonometric polynomials. Compactness therefore forces the decreasing finite-space minima to tend to zero, already at (p=1). The current allowed horizons then tend to zero as (N\to\infty), and \(\|P_\nu(T)-F_*\|_\infty\le LVT\) tends to zero. Taking more modes in this particular estimate cannot produce arbitrary-accuracy learning of a fixed nontrivial target. This diagnoses the estimate and its local episode, not an impossibility for longer nonlinear training.
3. The statistical term decreases almost as (m^{-1/2}) at fixed confidence and episode, but only around the selected population predictor. More observations do not remove its population approximation error. NGL20 provides a formal finite sample threshold through several unevaluated constants and possibly very small margins. The finite-network result is qualitative at each fixed positive contamination and sample, followed by contamination tending to zero. It supplies neither explicit width requirements nor a joint width/contamination/sample rate, raw GD, or continuation for repeated episodes. The source-radius budget and its integrated-control hypothesis remain separate from having favorable query tails.

These points prevent claiming efficient unknown-structure discovery or practical comparison margins. They do not make the present finite-episode result vacuous: its robust positive margins are independent of width and contamination. They do mean the next theorem should report the size and origin of its comparison effect rather than treating “positive” as evidence of resource efficiency.

Full D and E should remain roadmap objectives. The proposed intervening target is a recalibration using B's actual proof, not a claim that either is impossible or should be abandoned. It also avoids treating a routine extension of B's hidden-motion argument as an entire new milestone.

**A precise proposed contract.** Retain B's architecture, Gaussian initialization, raw metric, anchor task, noise assumptions and width-first limit order. Work first at one fixed nontrivial coefficient cap, for example (N=1), with all four coefficient directions available on an open set. Use the existing functions (q_0,h) and ordinary Fourier coordinates to specify the target family. Retain densities in a fixed neighborhood of (p=1) within \(\mathcal P_D\), or the whole class if the sign argument actually permits it. This keeps full support and robust changes in inputs and targets. The task patch must be characterized by its harmonic structure and coefficient inequalities, not by an eigenfunction selected from a trained trajectory, a single favorable atom, or a target copied from the learned predictor.

Let
\[
 d_\dagger(u)=\Pi_\dagger g_\dagger(u),\qquad
 k_\dagger(u,v)=\langle d_\dagger(u),d_\dagger(v)\rangle_{\rm raw}.
\]
Define the population frozen-tangent comparator (Z_\nu\) by
\[
 \partial_\tau Z_\nu(\tau,u)
 =-2\int k_\dagger(u,v)
       (Z_\nu(\tau,v)-q(v))p(v)\,d\rho(v),\qquad
 Z_\nu(0,u)=F_*(u).
\]
Its empirical version replaces the integral by the same sample average with labels (Y_i). The comparator has the complete fitted anchor representation, all full-parameter tangent directions, the same initial prediction, the same unhalved-loss clock, and the same data. Its kernel is determined solely by the known anchor reference, before the new target or sample is observed. It preserves both anchors because (d_\dagger(e_a)=0). It is a bounded linear evolution and uses no future trajectory information.

This is deliberately a stronger control than freezing hidden activations and training only the readout. It is a frozen tangent model at the *trained reference*, not the initialized NTK. A positive result against it would isolate a benefit of subsequent nonlinear representation change; it would not by itself complete E's comparison from original initialization against all initialized-kernel methods.

The central positive target is: identify one explicitly specified coefficient patch \(\mathcal Q\) of nonempty relative interior and constants \(T\in(0,T_c]\), \(a>0\), determined from that patch and the reference, such that
\[
 \inf_{q\in\mathcal Q,\,p\in\mathcal P}
 \bigl[\mathcal E_\nu(Z_\nu(T))-
       \mathcal E_\nu(P_\nu(T))\bigr]\ge a.
\]
Here \(\mathcal P\) is the declared full-support density class. Quantify the margin through an explicit signed mechanism coefficient and its finite-horizon remainder. Extend it to the paired empirical predictors with failure probability at most \(\delta\), an explicit sample threshold, and a remaining positive margin, then transfer the network side by B's actual finite-GF bridge. If a finite network realization of the comparator is claimed, supply its own approximation argument; it is not part of B's existing conclusion.

Use the same stopping budget (0\le\tau\le T). In the noiseless population comparison the fixed positive-kernel flow has nonincreasing excess risk, so granting it its best stopping time in that interval gives its value at (T). Do not create a win by slowing its scalar learning rate, excluding hidden tangent directions, or denying it the fitted reference. A comparison against unrestricted learning-rate tuning or arbitrarily longer training is a stronger resource statement and is not this target. Empirical stopping/tuning opportunities must be fixed and matched before a statistical comparison is claimed.

**Why this bridge is feasible, and where it can fail.** The exact population prediction equation is
\[
 \partial_\tau P_\nu=-2\mathscr K_{\theta_\nu,p}(P_\nu-q),
 \quad
 (\mathscr K_{\theta,p}a)(u)
   =\int\langle d_\theta(u),d_\theta(v)\rangle a(v)p(v)\,d\rho(v).
\]
B already controls this moving kernel in a common source neighborhood. A comparison with the frozen tangent model can therefore use the exact variation-of-constants identity
\[
 P_\nu(T)-Z_\nu(T)
 =-2\int_0^T e^{-2(T-s)\mathscr K_{\dagger,p}}
   (\mathscr K_{\theta_\nu(s),p}-\mathscr K_{\dagger,p})
   (P_\nu(s)-q)\,ds.
\]
The boundedness of the kernels makes this identity meaningful. It isolates the new source of prediction change instead of folding it into the approximation floor. Bounding its norm alone will not prove an advantage: its pairing with the test residual must have the correct sign.

For orientation, put \(r_0=F_*-q\). When the required along-flow derivative and uniform remainder are justified, the risk difference \(\Delta(T)=\mathcal E_\nu(Z_\nu(T))-\mathcal E_\nu(P_\nu(T))\) satisfies
\[
 \Delta(0)=\Delta'(0)=0,\qquad
 \Delta''(0)=4\langle r_0,
       \dot{\mathscr K}_{0,p}r_0\rangle_p.
\]
Indeed (E'=-4\langle r,\mathscr K r\rangle_p) and (r'=-2\mathscr K r), so (E''=16\|\mathscr K r\|_p^2-4\langle r,\dot{\mathscr K}r\rangle_p); the first term is common to the two models at zero. Thus the leading possible gain is
\[
 \Delta(T)=2T^2\langle r_0,
       \dot{\mathscr K}_{0,p}r_0\rangle_p+o(T^2).
\]
This is a diagnostic formula and proposed proof entry, not an asserted sign theorem. Candidate C.4.10.2 §5 provides strong derivatives of reached gradient fields and the anchor matrices. Those are a much better starting point than assuming an ambient bounded Hessian. A proof must retain the derivative of the anchor projector, the actual adjoint, and every hidden/readout term. It must then prove a remainder uniform over the declared patch and integrate to a fixed positive (T); an initial coefficient alone does not complete the target.

There is a useful warning from the easier comparator. Let \(S_R\) be the closed readout-only tangent subspace whose variations leave the anchors fixed, and (P_R) its orthogonal projector in the raw space. Since \(S_R\subset\ker G_\dagger^*\), for \(u_0=\int r_0g_\dagger p\,d\rho\),
\[
 \|\Pi_\dagger u_0\|^2-\|P_Ru_0\|^2
 =\|(I-P_R)\Pi_\dagger u_0\|^2
 \ge\|(\Pi_\dagger u_0)_H\|^2.
\]
B lower-bounds the last term on its robust class. Thus an initial risk-rate advantage over a frozen readout is already very close to its existing argument. The full tangent comparator has exactly the same first risk derivative as the nonlinear model, however. Neither this projection inequality nor B's positive hidden-motion bound controls the sign of \(\langle r_0,\dot{\mathscr K}_{0,p}r_0\rangle_p\). That is the new obligation with explanatory value.

The principal risks are concrete. The sign may be nonpositive on the natural B patch, may cancel by symmetry, or may vary with density. The certified neighborhood may be too short to make a second-order gain a useful fraction of total improvement. A positive population margin may demand very large sample sizes once it is smaller than B's first-order learning margin. A favorable local comparison may remain reproducible by a more broadly tuned fixed kernel. These are reasons to keep the claim narrow, not to weaken the control or search through unrelated task witnesses. First decide the signed mechanism on one fixed Fourier family; do not launch activation comparisons, a dimension sweep, new training experiments, or arbitrary mode growth in parallel with that decision.
