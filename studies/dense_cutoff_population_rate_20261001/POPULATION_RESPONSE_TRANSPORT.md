# Transport of represented Gaussian responses through rank changes

coordinator. This is collaborative research, not an independent review.
The source-gradient and causal-program construction were reread in the
current manuscript before deriving the identities below. No experiments,
external studies, or manuscript edits are involved.

**Conclusion.** For a consistent common coupling of causal population
programs, their represented Gaussian response is the adjoint of one
fixed isometry. It is consequently an \(L^2\) contraction, including
through rank changes. Both orientations can be constructed compatibly
by interleaving the manuscript's finite causal programs and then taking
the same countable completion used there. This gives an exact
creation/adjoint decomposition of each initialized population operator
and a coefficient-free stability estimate for the actual population
flow. It does not construct a strong coupling of finite trained neurons
to independent population samples. That finite-width bridge remains
unproved.

## 1. A fixed-space response lemma

Let \(H\) be a separable real Hilbert space. An isonormal map
\(I:H\to L^2(\Omega)\) is a linear map for which the variables
\(Ih\) are centered jointly Gaussian and
\[
 \mathbb E[(Ih)(Iv)]=\langle h,v\rangle_H.
 \tag{1}
\]
It is an isometry. Its Hilbert adjoint
\(I^*:L^2(\Omega)\to H\) has operator norm one.

Let \(h_1,\ldots,h_q\in H\), set \(\xi_r=Ih_r\), and let
\(u=F(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_q,Z)\), where the additional random
variables \(Z\) are independent of the entire isonormal family.
Assume initially that the source derivatives of \(F\) are integrable
and justify Gaussian integration by parts; bounded smooth maps suffice.
Then
\[
 I^*u=\sum_{r=1}^q h_r\,\mathbb E\partial_{\xi_r}u.
 \tag{2}
\]
There is no rank assumption. To prove (2), pair its two sides with any
\(v\in H\). Joint Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
 \langle v,I^*u\rangle_H
 =\mathbb E[(Iv)u]
 =\sum_r\langle v,h_r\rangle_H
                      \mathbb E\partial_{\xi_r}u.
 \tag{3}
\]
Equality against every \(v\) proves the identity. The polynomial
source-derivative envelopes in the manuscript's fixed programs justify
the same calculation for their products \(\phi'(Z)P\), by smooth
cutoffs and uniform integrability. The left side itself is defined and
continuous for every \(u\in L^2\), without a derivative hypothesis.

Equivalently, let \(E=\operatorname{span}\{h_r\}\) and let
\(\Pi_E\) denote projection onto the Gaussian linear span \(I(E)\)
in \(L^2(\Omega)\). The response in (2) equals
\(I^*\Pi_Eu\). But, crucially,
\[
 I^*\Pi_Eu=I^*u.
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(v\perp E\), the Gaussian variable \(Iv\) is independent of
the queried sources and of \(Z\), and has mean zero. Therefore
\(\mathbb E[(Iv)u]=0\). This proves (4), including when \(E\)
is a proper or rank-deficient subspace.

Now let another field \(u'\) depend on queries \(Ih'_r\) and
other variables jointly independent of the same entire isonormal family.
The joint law of those other variables may couple the two worlds in any
prescribed way. Their represented responses satisfy
\[
 \left\|\sum_r h_r\mathbb E\partial_{\xi_r}u
          -\sum_r h'_r\mathbb E\partial_{\xi'_r}u'\right\|_H
       =\|I^*(u-u')\|_H\le\|u-u'\|_{L^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Also \(\|Ih-Ih'\|_{L^2}=\|h-h'\|_H\). No moving-projector
bound is needed. The responses must use all source slots that the field
actually depends on; discarding a previously used slot would invalidate
(4).

## 2. Why two orientations require a causal construction

The preceding lemma alone does not authorize choosing independent
isonormal maps on two already completed layer spaces. Those spaces
contain fields generated by both orientations, so such a declaration
would be circular. Instead use the manuscript's existing finite-program
construction.

For one initialized hidden matrix, call its lower and upper generated
layer spaces \(H_-\) and \(H_+\). At any finite stage, a new matrix
query has a fully computed input field. The manuscript's Gaussian source
rule assigns the new forward source \(\xi_h\) covariances
\(\mathbb E\xi_h\xi_v=\mathbb E hv\) with previous forward
sources. These form a positive semidefinite matrix because the queried
inputs already lie in the lower layer's \(L^2\) space. The reverse
source is constructed from the corresponding upper-input Gram. Fresh
innovations are appended within the appropriate matrix/orientation source
group. Different groups and root tuples have the independence specified
by the manuscript's fixed-program lemma.

To couple two programs, begin with their prescribed joint root tuples
and interleave their finite instruction lists, using the same initialized
matrix name in both lists. At each new call, use inner products of the
already constructed **jointly realized** inputs, including cross-world
inner products, for its source covariance. A common finite-program
interpretation is to use the same finite initialized Gaussian matrices
and jointly sampled root tuples for the two finite computations. The
manuscript's lemma explicitly gives their joint limiting law. Its
source construction is chronological and does not require a pre-existing
completed \(H_-\) or \(H_+\).

Each program retains its original marginal law. Deleting unused
instructions preserves its root laws, queried-source marginal
covariances, and scalar expressions. Extra source coordinates on which
an expression does not depend have zero derivative in its source rule.
Thus the marginal consistency includes the Gaussian source bookkeeping,
not just the final matrix-query values.

Take a countable collection of the required paired finite programs,
their finite unions, rational linear combinations, and bounded smooth
functions of finite tuples used as additional queries. Their consistent
joint laws are realized as in the manuscript, and their generated spans
are completed in \(L^2\). Closing under the indicated bounded smooth
functions makes these the \(L^2\) spaces of the generated layer sigma
fields. Every finite set of sources used below belongs to one such
finite program. Consequently the stipulated Gaussian-group independence
holds on each finite set and then on the generated sigma fields.

Linearity and the Gram identity give compatible isometries
\[
 I:H_-\longrightarrow H_+,
       \qquad Ih=\xi_h,
 \qquad
 J:H_+\longrightarrow H_-,
       \qquad Ju=\zeta_u.
 \tag{6}
\]
For example \(\xi_{ah+bv}-a\xi_h-b\xi_v\) has zero variance
whenever these queries are included in a finite union. Thus the map is
well-defined on the generated span and extends by its norm identity.
The same argument applies to the reverse map. These are deterministic
operators between the completed probability spaces. They are **not**
independent random operators assumed on circular pre-existing domains.
Independence refers to their typed primitive Gaussian source groups in
the finite causal construction.

## 3. Exact decomposition of the population initialized operator

The manuscript's source rule, on a finite program, is
\[
 W_0h=\xi_h+\sum_su_s\mathbb E\partial_{\zeta_s}h,
 \qquad
 W_0^*u=\zeta_u+\sum_rh_r\mathbb E\partial_{\xi_r}u.
 \tag{7}
\]
At that instruction \(h\) depends only on its previously queried
reverse sources and on root/other source groups independent of the whole
reverse group. To apply (3) to a future reverse source \(Jv\), include
its query in an extension of that finite program. The joint Gaussian
rule still holds, so
\(\mathbb E[(Jv)h]=\sum_s\mathbb E[v u_s]
                                      \mathbb E\partial_{\zeta_s}h\).
This proves that the first response term is \(J^*h\) against every
generated upper field \(v\), not only against previously queried
fields. The corresponding argument gives the second response term
\(I^*u\).

It follows on the generated dense spans, and then by continuity, that
\[
             W_0=I+J^*,\qquad W_0^*=I^*+J,
             \qquad\|W_0\|_{\rm op}\le2.
 \tag{8}
\]
This is compatible with the true-adjoint identity already proved in
the manuscript. It supplies the same two orientations on one pair of
spaces, rather than separate forward and reverse laws. The bound applies
to the canonical equal-width initialized hidden matrices and their
generated population spaces. It does not describe a finite realized
Gaussian matrix as a sum of two isometries.

For a coupled pair of causal programs built as above, both worlds use
this same operator. Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|W_0h-W_0h'\|_{H_+}&\le2\|h-h'\|_{H_-},\\
 \|W_0^*u-W_0^*u'\|_{H_-}&\le2\|u-u'\|_{H_+},\\
 \|J^*h-J^*h'\|_{H_+}&\le\|h-h'\|_{H_-},\\
 \|I^*u-I^*u'\|_{H_-}&\le\|u-u'\|_{H_+}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
These bounds survive rank changes and increasing numbers of source
queries. They do not depend on a minimum Gram eigenvalue or on individual
response coefficients. In particular the possible fourth-derivative
loss from differentiating those coefficients is absent from (9).

## 4. Consequence for the actual population training flow

This section gives the deterministic stability consequence, explicitly
conditional on the common realization just constructed. It does not
assert that a finite trained network has such a realization with a
near-root error.

Use the canonical network equations, fixed data and small-label regime.
On the common generated layer spaces, write each trained hidden operator
as \(W_0^{(\ell)}+B^{(\ell)}(t)\), where the learned increment is
Hilbert--Schmidt. Couple the first-layer root rows and readouts on these
spaces as well. For two parameter states define
\[
 D(t)=\|W^{(1)}-W^{(1)\prime}\|_{L^2}
       +\sum_{\ell=2}^L\|B^{(\ell)}-B^{(\ell)\prime}\|_{\rm HS}
       +\|w-w'\|_{L^2}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Both paths use the same \(W_0^{(\ell)}\) from (8). Consider an exact
reference flow and a second path with an additive parameter forcing of
integrated norm at most \(\epsilon\). Work on the common physical
tube and positive tangent-gap region; a first-exit argument is available
when the reference has the established strict tube margins and the final
bound below is small. The reference has total residual activity at most
\(S\) and the manuscript's uniform Gaussian carrier tails.

Forward subtraction uses the bounded operators in (9). For the
unbounded-carrier backward product, split only the reference carrier
\(P\) at cutoff \(R\):
\[
 \|[\phi'(Z')-\phi'(Z)]P\|_2
 \le\|\phi''\|_\infty R\|Z'-Z\|_2
       +2\|\phi'\|_\infty\|P\mathbf1_{|P|>R}\|_2.
 \tag{11}
\]
The Gaussian tail makes the last term \(Ce^{-cR^2}\). Descending
the fixed number of layers gives a single factor \(1+R\), because
the changed-gate terms add and existing differences propagate through
bounded operators. The rank-one update identity then bounds the
prediction Jacobian difference by
\(C[(1+R)D+e^{-cR^2}]\).

Let \(e=r'-r\) be the training residual difference. The positive
tangent gap in the second path and the exact residual equation imply
\[
 \int_0^t\|e(s)\|\,ds
 \le C\|e(0)\|+
       C\int_0^t\rho(s)[(1+R)D(s)+e^{-cR^2}]\,ds+C\epsilon.
 \tag{12}
\]
The additive parameter forcing enters this residual equation through a
bounded prediction Jacobian, so its integrated contribution is at most
\(C\epsilon\). Subtracting the parameter integral equations, using
(12), and applying Gronwall with the reference activity measure gives
\[
 \sup_{t\ge0}D(t)
 \le C e^{CS(1+R)}
          [D(0)+\|e(0)\|+\epsilon+S e^{-cR^2}].
 \tag{13}
\]
For common zero readouts and identical labels, \(e(0)=0\). If
\(a=D(0)+\epsilon\) is small, choose
\(R\) proportional to \(\sqrt{\log(e+1/a)}\), obtaining
\[
 \sup_{t\ge0}D(t)
       \le C a\exp\{C\sqrt{\log(e+1/a)}\}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Only reference time-marginal carrier tails are used; no time supremum
inside their exponential moment is assumed. The same argument applies
first to paired finite Euler programs and then to their common strong
limit, using the manuscript's Euler construction. Thus (14) concerns the
actual population operator/scalar-response flow, rather than an arbitrary
\(L^2\) observable unrelated to its causal equations.

On this common realization, forward query subtraction also gives
\[
 \sup_t|f(t,x)-f'(t,x)|
       \le C(1+\|x\|/\sqrt d)\sup_t D(t).
 \tag{15}
\]
Consequently a second query moment suffices for the requested integrated
query norm **once the common parameter/source realization and its defect
have been supplied**. No new query moment is required by this stability
step.

## 5. What this proves and what it does not transport

There are three distinct statements here.

1. Given common feature realizations and common causal isonormal source
   maps, the represented response contracts \(L^2\) field differences
   exactly, by (5). Rank changes cause no loss.
2. The manuscript's paired finite-program construction and countable
   completion realize both orientations compatibly, leading to (8)--(9).
   The small-label population flow on that common environment has the
   near-Lipschitz stability (14).
3. A finite adaptive network has not been coupled to an approximate
   population parameter path on that common environment with integrated
   defect \(n^{-1/2}e^{C\sqrt{\log n}}\). This third statement is
   the missing finite-width theorem.

In particular, if a proposed Gaussian source has covariance \(Q\)
but its proposed physical query fields have a different Gram matrix,
there is no isometry satisfying (1) for that proposal. A covariance
coupling can repair the discrepancy only by constructing compatible
joint feature/source fields and accounting for the repair error. Equation
(5) does not manufacture them from closeness of covariance entries.

The independent-path covariance theorem supplies a strong coupling of
Gaussian processes when the covariance is an independent empirical
second moment with its required Sobolev bound. A finite reused matrix
query is conditional on previous forward and reverse matrix answers.
Couplings of its marginal source covariance do not automatically preserve
those history correlations. The finite conditional mean contains the
empirical response and projection terms, and their joint transport must
agree with the covariance coupling already chosen for earlier calls.

A simple exact example exposes this consistency requirement without
being a counterexample to (5). For a unit feature \(v\), query first
\(h_1=\eta v\), then \(h_2=v\). The source pair is
\((\eta g,g)\), with covariance
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\eta^2&\eta\\\eta&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Replacing the first query by zero changes its marginal source in
\(L^2\) by only \(|\eta|\). Nevertheless the next exact answer
in the original program is determined by the old answer divided by
\(\eta\); a coupling which treats the two future answers as fresh
independent Gaussians has order-one error. A common isonormal coupling
instead uses the same \(g\) and is stable. Thus the problem is not
the rank change itself; it is preserving the latent coupling across the
history. Controlling each marginal covariance or projecting after each
call separately is insufficient.

For a growing Picard or Euler program, one would need a finite-array
coupling that preserves this joint consistency for both orientations,
with an error bound uniform in its number of instructions. The local C1
insertion and averaged pulse identities supply response information;
the independent covariance lemma supplies another component. No argument
here combines them into that finite-array joint coupling, and no
exponential-in-program-length error estimate is asserted. Factorial
Picard truncation cannot be used to select a growing program until that
quantitative finite-program estimate is proved.

The positive result is therefore a deterministic population response
transport mechanism through rank changes, with its causal bookkeeping
made explicit. The requested dense-to-population width rate remains open.
