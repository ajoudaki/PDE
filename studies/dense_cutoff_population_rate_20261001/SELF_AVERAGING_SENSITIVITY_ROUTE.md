## 1. The exact Gaussian-root derivative

Use Euclidean mobility coordinates
\[
 \Theta=(W^{(1)},H^{(2)},\ldots,H^{(L)},w),\qquad
 H^{(\ell)}=\sqrt nW^{(\ell)}.
\]
Let \(G\) collect the entries of the initialized first and \(H\) matrices.
Its coordinates are independent standard Gaussians. Let \(P\) inject
these coordinates into the full parameter space, with zero readout block,
so \(\Theta(0)=PG\). For a passive query \(x\), set
\[
 \mathcal F_x=w^\top h^{(L)}(x),\quad f_x=\mathcal F_x/n,\quad
 g_x=\nabla_\Theta\mathcal F_x,\quad H_x=D_\Theta^2\mathcal F_x.
\]
Training weights \(p_a>0\) sum to one. The actual field and its derivative are
\[
 F=-2\sum_a p_ar_ag_a,\quad r_a=\mathcal F_{x_a}/n-y_a,\qquad
 DF=-\frac2n\sum_a p_ag_ag_a^\top-2\sum_a p_ar_aH_a.
 \tag{1}
\]
For its variational propagator \(J(t,s)\), the prediction derivative is
\[
 \nabla_G f_x(t)=n^{-1}P^\top J(t,0)^\top g_x(t).
 \tag{2}
\]
Physical feature and response RMS bounds give
\(\|g_x(t)\|_2\le C(1+\|x\|/\sqrt d)\sqrt n\).
The desired directional estimate is
\[
 \mathbb E\|P^\top J(t,0)^\top g_x(t)\|_2^2\le C_x n,
 \tag{3}
\]
uniformly in time, with global integrability or a valid localization
construction. A normalized response trace is a different quantity.

For a Gaussian-Sobolev function \(u\), Gaussian Poincare says
\(\operatorname{Var}u\le\mathbb E\|\nabla u\|_2^2\).
For completeness the covariance interpolation identity is
\[
 \operatorname{Var}u=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}
 \mathbb E\langle\nabla u(G),
 \nabla u(e^{-s}G+\sqrt{1-e^{-2s}}G')\rangle\,ds.
\]
Gaussian integration by parts differentiates the covariance and proves
this for smooth bounded \(u\); Cauchy--Schwarz gives the inequality,
and Gaussian-Sobolev approximation gives its stated scope. Thus (3)
would imply independent-copy variance at most \(2C_x/n\). Its probability
hypotheses still require proof for the actual neural predictor.

## 2. Averaged response bounds do not imply (3)

Let \(U_0(t,s)\) be the contraction generated by the negative Gram in
(1), and \(R(t,s)=J(t,s)-U_0(t,s)\). On a joint supremum budget \(B\),
the checked Hessian decomposition and residual activity bound imply,
for every fixed \(p\ge2\),
\[
 \sup_{s\le t}\|R(t,s)\|_{p,n}
 \le C_pS+C_pS^2B^{1/p},\qquad
 \|A\|_{p,n}=(n^{-1}\operatorname{tr}|A|^p)^{1/p},
 \tag{4}
\]
when \(C_pS^2<1\). To verify this, normalized Schatten Holder at
exponent \(ph\) bounds the \(h\)-insertion Dyson term by
\[
 \frac1{h!}[CS+CS^2(ph)B^{1/(ph)}]^h.
\]
The bounded part sums to \(C_pS\); the other part sums geometrically
to \(C_pS^2B^{1/p}\). Fixed \(p=2,4,6\) can share one positive
label threshold. The identity remains inside the contraction;
its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm in \(O(n^2)\) dimensions is not bounded.

The unresolved first-order quantity is
\[
 \frac{\|R^\top g_x\|_2^2}{n^2}
 =\frac1{n^2}\operatorname{tr}(RR^\top g_xg_x^\top).
 \tag{5}
\]
The rank-one endpoint satisfies
\(\|g_xg_x^\top\|_{q,n}=n^{-1/q}\|g_x\|_2^2
 =O(C_xn^{1-1/q})\). This width factor prevents the carrier-trace
argument from applying. Hilbert--Schmidt bounds alone give \(O(1)\)
in (5), rather than \(O(1/n)\).
Using the checked coordinate maximum instead gives only
\[
 \|\nabla_Gf_x(t)\|_2
 \le C_xn^{-1/2}\exp\{CS+CS^2\sqrt{\log(e+n)}\}
 \tag{6}
\]
on the good event. The multiplier is unbounded; this is not strict
root-width and, being localized, is not yet an unconditional variance bound.

For a deterministic illustration take a unit vector \(u_n\) and
\[
 J_n=\exp\{\alpha\sqrt{\log(e+n)}\,u_nu_n^\top\},\qquad
 g_n=\sqrt n\,u_n.
\]
For every fixed \(p\), \(\|J_n-I\|_{p,n}\to0\), while
\[
 \|J_n^\top g_n\|_2^2/n^2
 =n^{-1}e^{2\alpha\sqrt{\log(e+n)}}.
\]
Choosing \(u_n=n^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\) even makes \(g_n\)
coordinatewise bounded. A bounded-map conjugation of one diagonal
carrier of size \(S\sqrt{\log n}\) realizes this coefficient
structure, with bounded normalized exponential budget. This example
refutes the deterministic inference from these norms, not the actual
neural theorem. The dependence of the query direction on the network
must enter a positive proof.

## 3. Second-order Poincare gives one positive partial estimate

For two Gaussian-Sobolev derivatives,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}u\le\|\mathbb E\nabla u\|_2^2+
                       \tfrac12\mathbb E\|D^2u\|_{\rm HS}^2.
 \tag{7}
\]
Indeed the degree-one Hermite component contributes exactly the first
term. A degree-\(k\) component contributes \(k(k-1)\) times its
variance to the Hessian squared norm, and \(k(k-1)\ge2\) for \(k\ge2\).
This proves the polynomial case, then Gaussian-Sobolev density gives (7).

The exact prediction Hessian is
\[
 D_G^2 f_x(t)=\frac1nP^\top J(t,0)^\top H_x(t)J(t,0)P
 +\frac1n\int_0^tP^\top J(s,0)^\top A_{v_s}(s)J(s,0)P\,ds,
 \tag{8}
\]
where \(v_s=J(t,s)^\top g_x(t)\) and
\(A_v[U,V]=\langle v,D^2F[U,V]\rangle\).
The second term is the flow second variation and cannot be omitted.

Suppose, for this deterministic estimate, that the joint budget also
controls the one passive query's carriers. Then
\(\|H_x\|_{p,n}\le C_{x,p,B}\). Expanding \(J=U_0+R\), (4) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|U_0^\top H_xU_0\|_{2,n}&\le\|H_x\|_{2,n},\\
 \|R^\top H_xU_0\|_{2,n}&\le\|R\|_{4,n}\|H_x\|_{4,n},\\
 \|R^\top H_xR\|_{2,n}&\le\|R\|_{6,n}^2\|H_x\|_{6,n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Therefore the direct composition term obeys
\[
 \|P^\top J^\top H_xJP/n\|_{\rm HS}\le C_{x,B}/\sqrt n.
 \tag{9}
\]
The added passive-query moment input is not silently inferred from the
training budget. Extending the finite-query cavity construction is a
possible way to supply it. Even granting it does not bound the second
term of (8).

Writing \(T_a=D^3\mathcal F_a\), exact differentiation gives
\[
 A_v=-2\sum_a p_a\left[
 \frac{g_a(H_av)^\top+(H_av)g_a^\top+(g_a^\top v)H_a}{n}
 +r_aT_a[v,\cdot,\cdot]\right].
 \tag{10}
\]
One concrete third-derivative term is
\[
 D_\Theta z_a^{(\ell)\top}
 \operatorname{diag}\!\left[
 k_a^{(\ell)}\phi_\ell'''(z_a^{(\ell)})
       \odot D_\Theta z_a^{(\ell)}v\right]
 D_\Theta z_a^{(\ell)}.
\]
Its normalized squared Hilbert--Schmidt bound requires the mixed moment
\[
 \frac1n\sum_i|k_{a,i}^{(\ell)}|^2
                 |(D_\Theta z_a^{(\ell)}v)_i|^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
The primal budget does not control (11) for \(v=v_s\); using the
carrier maximum recovers the subpower loss. Thus the second-order route
controls the direct Hessian term but leaves this explicit directional
moment, the mean gradient in (7), and probability localization unresolved.

## 4. A concrete adjoint-field continuation target

For fixed terminal \((t,x)\), the exact terminal-value equation is
\[
 -\partial_sv(s)=DF(\Theta(s))^\top v(s),\qquad v(t)=g_x(t).
 \tag{12}
\]
Define \(\alpha_a^{(\ell)}(s)=D_\Theta z_a^{(\ell)}v(s)\).
The checked scalar Hessian formula implies
\[
 |v^\top H_av|\le C\|v\|_2^2+
 C\sum_{\ell,i}|k_{a,i}^{(\ell)}|\,|\alpha_{a,i}^{(\ell)}|^2.
 \tag{13}
\]
In backward integration time the negative Gram dissipates energy, so
only the residual-weighted part of (13) produces growth. A proved bound
\[
 \sup_{0\le s\le t}\frac1n\sum_{\ell,i}
               |\alpha_{a,i}^{(\ell)}(s)|^4\le C_x
 \tag{14}
\]
with the existing fourth carrier moments would give the correct
normalized energy bound after integration over finite residual activity.
It would also control (11). Appropriate expectation, localization and
terminal-time uniformity would still be needed.

Equation (14) is a new response-field assertion. A bidirectional
finite-neuron deletion theorem for the coupled primal and adjoint
terminal-value system is a concrete resolver. The existing primal
insertion theorem gives polynomially small coordinate discrepancies,
so the retained Hessian coefficients are close after their omitted
neuron terms are removed. The omitted Hessian terms have nonvanishing
operator norm and must appear explicitly as adjoint sources.
The existing theorem does not yet estimate those sources or the
adjoint terminal condition. Declaring that the adjoint shares primal
carrier moments would assume the desired new assertion.

## 5. All-time supremum and the Gaussian-domain issue

Pointwise-time variance does not itself control the supremum inside the
query integral. There is a sufficient bridge without a time-net loss.
Since initial predictions vanish, the estimate
\[
 \int_0^\infty
 \left(\mathbb E\|\nabla_G\partial_tf_x(t)\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}dt
 \le C_x/\sqrt n
 \tag{15}
\]
implies, by Poincare, the fundamental theorem of calculus and Minkowski,
\[
 \left(\mathbb E\sup_{t\ge0}|f_x(t;G)-f_x(t;G')|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le\sqrt2C_x/\sqrt n.
 \tag{16}
\]
The query-integrated assertion additionally needs \(\int C_x^2d\mu<\infty\);
bounded-query estimates alone do not establish the finite-second-moment
query scope.

For \(K_{xa}=g_x^\top g_a/n\), the exact velocity and its state gradient are
\[
 \partial_tf_x=-2\sum_a p_ar_aK_{xa},\qquad
 \nabla_\Theta\partial_tf_x=-2\sum_a p_a
 \left[\frac{K_{xa}}n g_a+\frac{r_a}n(H_xg_a+H_ag_x)\right].
 \tag{17}
\]
The first gradient term has no residual factor. To prove (15), one
must use damping of
\(P^\top J(t,0)^\top g_a(t)/n=\nabla_G r_a(t)\).
Decay of the residual value does not imply decay of this derivative.

The completed carrier theorem is a high-probability statement, not a
global expectation bound for sensitivities. Poincare cannot simply be
applied with its good-event indicator inserted. A smooth localization
creates a derivative of the localization; conditioning the Gaussian
measure changes its functional inequality. These terms need proof.

Gaussian rotation \(G_\theta=G\cos\theta+G'\sin\theta\) has an
independent Gaussian tangent at each fixed angle. It still does not
control bad-angle contributions merely from a high-probability
good-gradient bound. A smooth approximation to \(\operatorname{sign}(G)\)
with transition confined to \(|G|\le\varepsilon_n\) has derivative zero
outside an event of probability tending to zero, but variance tending
to one. A Gaussian-Sobolev extension agreeing on good initializations,
or a bound for the bad-angle contribution, is therefore an additional
necessary ingredient in this version of the route.

The exact open bridges are prediction-directed moments (11)/(14),
valid global or localized Gaussian control, and an integrable all-time
estimate such as (15). The checked primal carrier theorem and averaged
response bounds do not alone establish these. This is a limitation of
the present proof route, not a counterexample to dense self-averaging.

## 6. Exact augmented equations and what an insertion theorem must add

The following calculation makes the proposed adjoint continuation
concrete. Fix its terminal time and query, and evaluate every quantity
below at the same backward-running physical time \(s\). Write the
parameter components of \(v\) as \(v_{W^{(1)}},v_{H^{(\ell)}},v_w\).
For each training datum define
\[
 \eta_a^{(\ell)}
   =\phi_\ell'(z_a^{(\ell)})\odot\alpha_a^{(\ell)},\qquad
 \beta_a^{(\ell)}=D_\Theta\delta_a^{(\ell)}v,\qquad
 \chi_a=g_a^\top v/n.
 \tag{18}
\]
These are directional derivatives, not additional trained parameters.
Forward differentiation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \alpha_a^{(1)}&=v_{W^{(1)}}x_a/\sqrt d,\\
 \alpha_a^{(\ell)}
   &=v_{H^{(\ell)}}h_a^{(\ell-1)}/\sqrt n
                         +W^{(\ell)}\eta_a^{(\ell-1)} .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{19}
\]
Backward differentiation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta_a^{(L)}
  &=\phi_L''(z_a^{(L)})\odot w\odot\alpha_a^{(L)}
                      +\phi_L'(z_a^{(L)})\odot v_w,\\
 \beta_a^{(\ell)}
  &=\phi_\ell''(z_a^{(\ell)})\odot k_a^{(\ell)}
                                      \odot\alpha_a^{(\ell)}\\
  &\quad+\phi_\ell'(z_a^{(\ell)})\odot
       \left[(v_{H^{(\ell+1)}})^\top
                             \delta_a^{(\ell+1)}/\sqrt n
                      +(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top\beta_a^{(\ell+1)}\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{20}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (H_av)_w&=\eta_a^{(L)},\\
 (H_av)_{H^{(\ell)}}&=
  [\beta_a^{(\ell)}h_a^{(\ell-1)\top}
                   +\delta_a^{(\ell)}\eta_a^{(\ell-1)\top}]/\sqrt n,\\
 (H_av)_{W^{(1)}}&=\beta_a^{(1)}x_a^\top/\sqrt d .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
Using backward elapsed time \(\sigma=t-s\), equation (12) therefore is
\[
 {dv\over d\sigma}=-2\sum_a p_a[\chi_ag_a+r_aH_av].
 \tag{22}
\]
Equations (19)--(22), together with \(v(t)=g_x(t)\), are an exact finite
network system. No mean-field or Gaussian approximation enters them.

For an internal omitted neuron \(i\) in layer \(j\), let
\(y_i\) denote its current incoming row as a column and \(x_i\) its
current outgoing column. In the retained forward and reverse equations,
their sources are respectively
\[
 e_{i,a}^+=x_i h_{a,i}^{(j)},\qquad
 e_{i,a}^-=y_i\delta_{a,i}^{(j)} .
\]
Their directional variations are exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D e_{i,a}^+[v]
   &=x_i\eta_{a,i}^{(j)}
            +(v_{H^{(j+1)}})_{\cdot i}h_{a,i}^{(j)}/\sqrt n,\\
 D e_{i,a}^-[v]
   &=y_i\beta_{a,i}^{(j)}
            +(v_{H^{(j)}})_{i\cdot}^{\top}
                                    \delta_{a,i}^{(j)}/\sqrt n .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{23}
\]
Thus an augmented insertion problem needs the new scalar controls
\(\eta_{a,i}^{(j)},\beta_{a,i}^{(j)}\) and the new omitted response row
and column. Keeping only the scalar forward control misses the reverse
source, just as it would in the primal proof.

For example, the lower retained gradient contains
\(\delta_{a,i}^{(j)}D_\Theta
[y_i^\top h_a^{(j-1)}]\). Its full directional derivative contains
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\beta_{a,i}^{(j)}D_\Theta
                  [y_i^\top h_a^{(j-1)}]\\
 &\quad+\delta_{a,i}^{(j)}
        D_\Theta^2[y_i^\top h_a^{(j-1)}]v\\
 &\quad+\delta_{a,i}^{(j)}
        D_\Theta h_a^{(j-1)\top}
                         (v_{H^{(j)}})_{i\cdot}^{\top}/\sqrt n .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{24}
\]
Here the first two \(D_\Theta\) derivatives hold \(y_i\) fixed and
differentiate the lower network; the third term accounts for the
directional change of \(y_i\). Formula (24) is a local product-rule
identity. Dependence of the forward controls and the residual must
additionally be differentiated in the full retained vector field.

At the terminal time the omitted response column is small in Euclidean
norm:
\[
 \left\|(v_{H^{(j+1)}})_{\cdot i}/\sqrt n\right\|_2
 =|\!h_{x,i}^{(j)}\!|\,\|\delta_x^{(j+1)}\|_2/n.
 \tag{25}
\]
Its backward evolution follows from (21)--(22); in particular,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 {d\over d\sigma}
       {(v_{H^{(j+1)}})_{\cdot i}\over\sqrt n}
  =-{2\over n}\sum_a p_a\big[
       \chi_a\delta_a^{(j+1)}h_{a,i}^{(j)}
       +r_a\beta_a^{(j+1)}h_{a,i}^{(j)}
       +r_a\delta_a^{(j+1)}\eta_{a,i}^{(j)}\big].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
Writing
\(D_a(u)=\|\delta_a^{(j+1)}(u)\|_2/\sqrt n\) and
\(E_a(u)=\|\beta_a^{(j+1)}(u)\|_2/\sqrt n\), integration from terminal
physical time \(t\) to \(s\le t\) gives the explicit bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left\|{(v_{H^{(j+1)}}(s))_{\cdot i}\over\sqrt n}\right\|_2
 &\le {|h_{x,i}^{(j)}(t)|\,\|\delta_x^{(j+1)}(t)\|_2\over n}\\
 &\quad+{2\over\sqrt n}\sum_a p_a\int_s^t
   \big[|\chi_a(u)|\,|h_{a,i}^{(j)}(u)|D_a(u)\\
 &\hspace{42mm}
   +|r_a(u)|\{E_a(u)|h_{a,i}^{(j)}(u)|
                       +D_a(u)|\eta_{a,i}^{(j)}(u)|\}\big]\,du .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26a}
\]
The first integrand has no residual factor. Uniform bounds for the
new scalar fields and RMS \(E_a\) therefore do not by themselves give
a uniform-in-\(t\) \(O(n^{-1/2})\) column bound: the displayed
\(\chi_a\) contribution additionally requires integrability or damping.
Both the new response bounds and that time-integrability estimate
remain open obligations. The incoming row has the corresponding
transposed formula. Neither is an independent Gaussian row after
training.

The problematic multiplication appears already in (20):
\[
  k_{a,i}^{(\ell)}\alpha_{a,i}^{(\ell)}.
 \tag{27}
\]
If a scalar Volterra comparison with this local coefficient could be
proved, its natural growth factor would be
\(\exp\{CSK_i\}\), where
\(K_i=\max_a\sup_s|k_{a,i}^{(\ell)}(s)|\). The existing empirical
budget does control every fixed moment of that factor when
\[
 CpS^2<\eta,\qquad
 {1\over n}\sum_i e^{CpSK_i}
 \le {1\over n}\sum_i e^{\eta K_i/S}\le B .
 \tag{28}
\]
Thus (27) does not itself disprove moment closure, and small labels
offer a possible positive mechanism. But (19)--(20) also apply the
adapted Gaussian mixers to the response fields. Replacing those
operations by a scalar Volterra bound is the unproved step. Arbitrary
bounded-operator mixers do not preserve coordinate fourth moments or
the entropy needed in the energy estimate.

The terminal-value cavity reference must exclude the Gaussian root
under test from its entire primal path and its entire adjoint path.
The latter has a network-dependent terminal gradient. The primal
finite-deletion theorem does not give this joint independence and
does not bound the new sources in (23)--(26). A new stopped comparison
could attempt to establish it. Mechanically applying a second-order
Taylor reinsertion argument to (20) can request a Lipschitz third
activation derivative, which is absent from the stated \(C^3\) class.
This is a limitation of that proposed method, not a proof that the
theorem needs a fourth derivative: direct first-order comparisons or
averaged remainders could avoid that stronger hypothesis, if proved.

## 7. A block-resampling alternative has the same missing scale

Partition \(G\) into its independent Gaussian rows, across the first
and all hidden initialized matrices. There are \(O(n)\) blocks at
fixed depth. Let \(G^{[b]}\) independently resample one such block.
For every square-integrable scalar prediction, the elementary
conditional-variance decomposition gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}f_x(t;G)
 \le {1\over2}\sum_b
       \mathbb E|f_x(t;G)-f_x(t;G^{[b]})|^2 .
 \tag{29}
\]
Hence \(O(n^{-2})\) expected squared prediction influence per block
would close pointwise strict root width. The deletion/reinsertion
construction is adapted to this independence, but its currently proved
state remainder is not sharp enough as an observable estimate.

In particular, a Euclidean retained-state remainder
\(\|U\|_2\le n^{-c}\), with \(0<c<1/2\), only gives through
\(\|g_x\|_2=O(\sqrt n)\)
\[
 |f_x(\Theta+U)-f_x(\Theta)|
       \le C_x n^{-1/2-c}
 \tag{30}
\]
on the physical tube. Summing the squared generic bound over \(O(n)\)
blocks gives \(O(n^{-2c})\), not \(O(n^{-1})\).
The actual prediction remainder can be smaller, but that needs its own
observable-specific cancellation or concentration argument. The
normalized carrier-response trace is not that argument. This is an
explicit limitation of using the existing state insertion error in
(29), not a lower bound for the true resampling influence.

## 8. Frozen outcome of this bounded attempt

The positive new deterministic result is (4), and its application (9)
to the direct second-derivative composition term. Equations
(18)--(26) identify precisely the additional finite-network system a
strict proof must control. Equations (27)--(28) explain why a small-label
moment proof remains plausible, while identifying the missing adapted
mixer estimate and the required terminal-value deletion construction.
The complete strict self-averaging statement is not proved here.
