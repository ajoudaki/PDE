# Gaussian expectation by streamed polynomial expansion

2026-10-06. Bounded theoretical follow-up. The preceding
`LABEL_SERIES_DECODER.md` remains frozen.

There is a constructive expectation algorithm that never stores or queries
a sampled dense Gaussian initialization. For a polynomial computation, it
enumerates one monomial at a time and integrates that monomial exactly from
its Gaussian root multiplicities. Its workspace is polynomial in the
monomial leaf count and evaluation depth, with a fixed polynomial exponent.
This removes an initialization-oracle gap for a specified polynomial
approximation.

It does not yet give the requested absolute-polylog decoder. Replacing each
activation by a degree-$p$ polynomial produces Gaussian degree at least
$p^L$ already in the first prediction time derivative. A time-jet expansion
does not remove this depth dependence. An exact universal moment-matching
sampler cannot hide it in a seed of logarithmic length: a separate rank
argument below bounds the necessary support size. That rank argument is
about universal cubature, not every possible algorithm for this particular
network expectation.

## 1. Exact expectation without initialized arrays

Let $G_1,\ldots,G_N$ be independent standard real Gaussian variables. For
the actual dense initialization, $N=nd+(L-1)n^2$; the first-layer variables
are these roots directly and a hidden-matrix entry is its root divided by
$\sqrt n$. No value of any $G_i$ will be generated by the algorithm below.

Consider a finite arithmetic computation $P(G)$ using sums and products.
Its leaves are root identifiers $i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ or deterministic
scalar coefficients. The computation may be an implicitly addressed DAG:
the gate type, children and coefficient at a requested address must be
computable from the permitted problem data, the query and bounded work
space. In particular this input description must not contain a table of
the original random initialization.

Let $h$ bound the depth of the expanded scalar computation. Let $A\ge2$
bound its addition fan-in; products can be made binary. Define the
**formal leaf degree** by giving every scalar or Gaussian leaf degree one,
taking the maximum at a sum and the sum at a product. Suppose the output
has formal leaf degree at most $D$. This counts deterministic coefficient
leaves as well as Gaussian leaves; it prevents a constant-only repeated
squaring circuit from hiding exponentially large arithmetic in degree zero.
Let $s_{\rm addr}$ be the space to address and expand one gate.

**Proposition 1.** The exact Gaussian expectation $\mathbb EP(G)$ can be
computed using

\[
 O(Dh)
\]

scalar registers, together with

\[
 O\!\left(Dh(s_{\rm addr}+\log A)+D\log N\right)                 \tag{1}
\]

control bits, in an exact-scalar arithmetic model. The number of arithmetic
operations can be enormous. The algorithm retains no Gaussian sample and
requires no oracle for random-access dense roots.

**Proof.** Distribute products over sums without materializing the expanded
polynomial. A monomial derivation chooses one summand at each visited sum
gate and visits both children of a product gate. Its resulting parse tree
has at most $D$ leaves. Every node lies on the path to some leaf and each
path has at most $h+1$ nodes; hence the parse tree has at most $D(h+1)$
nodes. This also bounds the simultaneous state of a depth-first enumeration
that keeps the left factor's choices while advancing the right factor.
The procedure stores a gate address and a local summand counter per active
node, plus the products of deterministic coefficients. Discarded subtrees
can be reconstructed from these choices. Repeated DAG subexpressions are
recomputed rather than retained.

For a completed monomial, retain its at most $D$ Gaussian root identifiers.
Its coefficient is the product of its deterministic leaves. Scan the list
to count each distinct identifier's multiplicity $r_i$; this needs no
additional length-$N$ array. Independence gives

\[
 \mathbb E\prod_iG_i^{r_i}
 =\begin{cases}
   0,&\text{some }r_i\text{ is odd},\\
   \displaystyle\prod_{i:r_i>0}(r_i-1)!!,&\text{all }r_i\text{ are even}.
  \end{cases}                                                     \tag{2}
\]

To justify the only probabilistic identity used here, integration by parts
against the one-dimensional Gaussian density gives
$\mathbb EG^{2r}=(2r-1)\mathbb EG^{2r-2}$, with initial moment one;
odd moments vanish by symmetry. This proves (2). Multiply (2) by the
monomial coefficient and add it to one accumulator. Finite expansion and
linearity of expectation prove exactness. The parse-tree bound and the
root list give (1). $\square$

This is Wick contraction specialized to the independent scalar roots.
There is no need to store an arbitrary pairing of correlated intermediate
fields: every admissible pairing of equal root identifiers has already
been counted by its double factorial. The root multiplicity list is still
linear in $D$; it has not disappeared.

### Explicit precision qualification

The real-register count is not by itself a bit-space guarantee. A useful
sufficient bound follows from the same parse-tree count. Suppose each
deterministic leaf is a rational specified with at most $B$ bits and
magnitude at most $2^B$. There are at most

\[
 T=A^{C D(h+1)}                                                     \tag{3}
\]

monomial derivations for a universal $C$: encode the visited sum choices
and product-tree structure in $O(D(h+1))$ symbols, padding if necessary.
A monomial has deterministic magnitude at most $2^{BD}$, and its Gaussian
moment is at most $D^{D/2}$. Hence the sum of absolute expected monomial
contributions is at most

\[
 T\,2^{BD}D^{D/2}.                                                 \tag{4}
\]

For each monomial, form the rational coefficient by multiplying its at
most $D$ leaf numerators and denominators. This uses $O(BD)$ bits for each
of the numerator and denominator. Its moment in (2) is an integer with
$O(D\log(D+1))$ bits. Round the resulting rational contribution to a
fixed binary grid with mesh at most $\epsilon/(2T)$, and accumulate on that
same grid. The rounding error sums to at most $\epsilon/2$. Equation (4)
bounds the needed integer part of the accumulator. Thus the numerical
workspace is polynomial, with an absolute exponent, in

\[
 D,\ h,\ B,\ \log A,\ \log N,\ s_{\rm addr},\ \log(1/\epsilon). \tag{5}
\]

This is an explicit finite-precision implementation for rational circuits;
it is not an exact-real encoding of hidden randomness. Irrational inputs,
activation coefficients and times need computable rational approximations
with a propagated error bound. That additional bound is not automatic.
The hidden initialization normalization alone causes no difficulty:
in every surviving monomial each hidden Gaussian root has even multiplicity,
so its associated powers of $1/\sqrt n$ reduce exactly to integer powers
of $1/n$. This observation applies when the computation is expressed in
the original weight coordinates.

In particular, if every quantity in (5) is bounded by a fixed polynomial
in $\log n$, whose exponent is independent of $L,d,m$, this algorithm is
an absolute-polylog-space expectation evaluator. This implication is proved
for the specified circuit; existence of a sufficiently accurate circuit
with those bounds is a separate mathematical requirement.

## 2. Time jets have linear degree growth in order

Here total Gaussian degree counts only powers of the roots. It is distinct
from the formal leaf degree needed for the complete workspace bound.

Let the activation approximation in each layer be a polynomial of degree
at most $p\ge2$. Keep the exact dense architecture, matrix reuse and
gradient-flow mobilities. Regard the resulting finite model as an
approximation candidate, not as the original analytic-activation model.
Define

\[
 s_L=p+p^2+\cdots+p^L,\qquad d_f=1+s_L,\qquad q=2d_f-1=1+2s_L.
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]

The prediction has parameter degree at most $d_f$. Indeed the first
hidden features have degree at most $p$; each new hidden layer multiplies
the previous features by a new weight and then takes a degree-$p$
polynomial, giving $s_\ell=p(1+s_{\ell-1})$. The readout contributes
one additional degree. The squared-loss gradient field has degree at
most $q$: differentiating $(f-y)^2$ costs at most $2d_f-1$ degrees.
Constant mobility factors do not alter that degree.

**Proposition 2.** Write a formal time expansion of the polynomial flow
as $\theta(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}\theta_kt^k$, with initialization linear in
the independent roots and zero initial readout. Then

\[
 \deg_G\theta_k\le1+k(q-1),\qquad
 \deg_G[t^k]f(\theta(t),v)\le d_f+k(q-1).        \tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** In the recurrence
$(k+1)\theta_{k+1}=[t^k]F(\sum_j\theta_jt^j)$, a vector-field
monomial of degree $r\le q$ contributes products with time indices
$j_1+\cdots+j_r=k$. If the bound holds through order $k$, their
Gaussian degree is at most
$r+k(q-1)\le1+(k+1)(q-1)$. This proves the first inequality by
induction from $\deg_G\theta_0\le1$. The identical substitution into
a prediction monomial of degree at most $d_f$ proves the second. $\square$

Thus the degree is linear in the time-jet order, rather than $q^k$.
Equivalently, a rooted ODE expansion tree with $k$ vector-field vertices
adds at most $q-1$ new initial-state leaves at each vertex. Its deterministic
coefficients can also be enumerated along that tree; no stored time history
is needed for a fixed time-jet coefficient.

However, the vector field's degree $q$ itself contains $p^L$. The estimate
does not give a constant depending on depth multiplying a fixed power of
$p$. Nor does it prove convergence of the time expansion on the requested
horizon, or the stability of replacing the original activation by this
polynomial outside its approximation interval.

## 3. The depth dependence is already present at the first derivative

The large degree in (6) is not only a loose gradient-degree estimate.
For clarity take one datum $v$ with at least one nonzero coordinate and a
nonzero label $y$. This is a diagnostic of the polynomial representation,
not a replacement of the general-data target. Suppose every activation
polynomial has degree exactly $p$ and nonzero leading coefficient.

At zero readout, every initial hidden velocity is zero, while
$\dot w(0)=2y h_0^{(L)}(v)$. Consequently the exact first prediction
derivative is

\[
 \dot f_n(0,v)=\frac{2y}{n}\|h_0^{(L)}(v)\|_2^2.                 \tag{8}
\]

As a polynomial in the independent initialization roots, each nonzero top
feature has degree exactly $s_L$ from (6). To verify that its leading part
does not vanish, keep one first-layer input coordinate with nonzero $v_j$
and one chain of neurons through the layers, setting other root variables
to zero. The leading coefficients and the chain weights then give a
nonzero monomial of degree $s_L$.

The sum of squares in (8) has a nonzero highest homogeneous part: the sum
of the squares of the real leading homogeneous feature polynomials cannot
vanish identically unless all those polynomials vanish. Therefore

\[
 \deg_G\dot f_n(0,v)=2s_L\ge2p^L.                                \tag{9}
\]

If $p$ is chosen on a scale $(\log n)^a$, the direct monomial/Wick
construction consequently involves degree $(\log n)^{aL}$. For each fixed
$L$ this is polylogarithmic, but it is not a bound with an absolute exponent
independent of $L$. Increasing the permitted absolute exponent to another
fixed number does not remove this issue as depth varies.

This is not a lower bound on every algorithm for the expectation in (8).
In fact, its population limit is computable by a scalar Gaussian covariance
recursion through the layers. That observation shows exactly why degree
alone cannot prove decoder impossibility. A successful trained analogue
must preserve a comparably compact Gaussian reduction despite forward and
transpose reuse, without merely expanding all Gaussian leaves.

## 4. A universal moment-matching seed cannot avoid degree

One proposed repair is to use a tiny deterministic seed to generate
pseudogaussian entries on demand, average over all seeds, and thereby
integrate every degree-$D$ polynomial. The following elementary rank bound
states what exact universal matching would require.

**Proposition 3.** Let a finite cubature formula on $\mathbb R^N$ have
nodes $z_1,\ldots,z_M$ and arbitrary real weights $w_1,\ldots,w_M$.
If it agrees with standard Gaussian expectation for every polynomial of
total degree at most $2r$, then

\[
 M\ge\binom{N+r}{r}.                                             \tag{10}
\]

In particular, a finite seed selecting at most $2^s$ such nodes must obey
$s\ge\log_2\binom{N+r}{r}$.

**Proof.** The vector space of polynomials of degree at most $r$ has
dimension $H=\binom{N+r}{r}$, by counting nonnegative exponent vectors
whose sum is at most $r$. Choose its monomial basis $p_1,\ldots,p_H$.
The Gaussian Gram matrix
$\mathcal M_{ij}=\mathbb E[p_i(G)p_j(G)]$ is positive definite.
Indeed a nonzero polynomial has a nonzero value somewhere, hence is
bounded away from zero on a small open ball; the Gaussian density is
strictly positive on that ball, so its squared Gaussian integral is
positive. Thus $\operatorname{rank}\mathcal M=H$.

Exactness through degree $2r$ gives

\[
 \mathcal M=\sum_{j=1}^M w_jp(z_j)p(z_j)^T,
 \qquad p(z)=(p_1(z),\ldots,p_H(z))^T.
\]

Each summand has rank at most one, even for negative weights. Therefore
$H\le M$, proving (10). $\square$

For $1\le r\le N$, the product formula for the binomial coefficient gives

\[
 \log_2\binom{N+r}{r}
 \ge r\log_2(N/r).                                                \tag{11}
\]

With $N$ of order $n^2$ and polylogarithmic $r$, this is of order
$r\log n$. Thus a seed that exactly matches *all* the required moments
cannot have a length depending only logarithmically on $r$. This does not
exclude approximate moment matching, integration specialized to one
trajectory polynomial, an implicit deterministic summation method other
than finite-seed cubature, or a network-specific Gaussian reduction.
No stronger lower bound is claimed.

## 6. Frozen conclusion and provenance

The positive result is a concrete Gaussian expectation evaluator for a
specified polynomial computation, with no sampled initialization, no root
oracle and a proved workspace count including a rational finite-precision
version. Time-jet Gaussian degree grows only linearly in jet order. The
specific surviving bottleneck is activation composition: its degree grows
like $p^L$, and the straightforward contraction representation therefore
does not give a polylog exponent independent of depth. A universal exact
cubature seed cannot make its degree dependence logarithmic.

No claim is made that the original all-time analytic trajectory has an
accurate polynomial approximation with the required degree, coefficient
precision or evaluation depth. No claim is made that the displayed degree
is a lower bound on the memory of every specialized expectation algorithm.
The useful next implication would be a trained Gaussian reduction that
integrates nonlinear feature subtrees without expanding their $p^L$ leaves
and retains the reused-matrix response information.
