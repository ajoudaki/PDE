## 2. Physical flow, noisy matrices, and exact row law

The short-program approximation uses the actual nonlinear gradient field.
Projection controls off-trajectory evaluations without changing the supplied
good trajectory. Its Lipschitz constant is of order `sqrt(log(en))`; the
coordinate maximum enters gate subtraction once, while propagation through
the fixed depth adds contributions. This does not replace feature learning
by a kernel or a linearized trajectory.

The collocation proof's positive endpoint quadrature is doing necessary work.
Its endpoint error grows by `1 + O(h sqrt(log(en)))`, rather than by the
interpolation norm at every patch. With `K = J = O(log(en)^2)`, the resulting
error is bounded by a polynomial factor times

\[
 \exp\{C[\log(en)]^{3/2}\}
 \bigl(2^{-K}+8^{-J}\bigr).
\]

This is smaller than every fixed inverse power of `n` at sufficiently large
individual width. The supplied analytic tail covers all later times and
infinity. The finite chronology has at most `C log(en)^8` initialized-matrix
calls. The low-rank displacement representation avoids expanding Picard
iterations into exponentially many named vectors.

Adding matrix-answer noise of scale
`sigma = exp(-log(en)^2)` contributes a per-field defect of at most
`C sigma sqrt(log(en))` on the raw-noise RMS event. The same endpoint stability
argument bounds its accumulated effect by

\[
 CT e^{CT\sqrt{\log(en)}}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,\sigma,
\]

which is again smaller than every fixed inverse power. This choice precedes
the scalar perturbation and is not circular in the later posterior condition
number.

The adaptive Gaussian conditioning calculation is correct under its stated
observable filtration. For a hidden initialized matrix `W`, the likelihood
contains the observed noisy answers, with previous query vectors fixed by the
observed prefix. Consequently the posterior mean solves

\[
 \sigma^2\overline W + (UU^T/n)\overline W

Substitution verifies the proposed three-term low-rank mean and its Sylvester
equation. The answer covariance has a floor `sigma^2 I`, and the rationalized

history-Gram eigenvalue. Its Kronecker resolvent has dimension at most `R^2`.
Thus arbitrary empirical rank deficiency is allowed.

The hidden raw answer noises must remain hidden in this conditioning proof.

## 3. Scalar perturbation and full-tape passive queries

The scalar perturbation is properly distinguished from the exact noisy-matrix
law. Let `C^0` be the exact-moment row program and `C~` its scalar-noisy
perturbation. For fixed iid row packets, bounded Lipschitz row functions give

\[
 \max_{r\le P}|\widetilde C_r-C_r^0|
 \le \eta nP(1+\Lambda)^P
\]

on the scalar-noise event. The same deterministic recurrence covers appended
passive-query instructions. Feeding perturbed Gram summaries into posterior
formulas therefore changes the law, but the packet controls that change
instead of calling it an exact Gaussian posterior.

The stronger conditional query statement can be reconstructed. Fix a full
training tape on the common good event. Generate the original passive query
using fresh raw matrix-answer noises. These noises are still standard Gaussian
after that conditioning, while the whitened innovations need not be. For an
original query answer `y`, its conditional mean `mu`, and covariance
`Gamma >= sigma^2 I`, using the RMS norm
\(\|v\|_{2,n}=\|v\|_2/\sqrt n\),

\[
 g=\Gamma^{-1/2}(y-\mu),\qquad
 \|g\|_{2,n}\le \sigma^{-1}
       (\|y\|_{2,n}+\|\mu\|_{2,n}).
\]

The explicit one-call mean bounds therefore give the declared polynomial
`G_*` in `R` and `sigma^{-1}`, and a coordinate bound `sqrt(n) G_*`.
Using these innovations in the scalar-perturbed query circuit couples its
output to the original query path. The raw-noise RMS failure and scalar-noise
failure bounds are uniform in the sphere input and patch time on the same
training event. This provides a conditional failure below `1/8` and error
below `1/(4n)` at sufficiently large width.

There is no justified replacement of this argument by a typical
`sqrt(log n)` cap on whitened query innovations conditional on the full tape.
The packet explicitly avoids that mistake.

The original bridge's phrase that all genuine moments had fixed entry bounds
was too broad when innovation moments were included. The authorized revision
resolves this: a physical field of RMS at most `B` has innovation cross-moments
bounded by `B G_*`, innovation second moments by `G_*^2`, and the enlarged
Gram table can be capped at `C R(B+G_*)^2`. These caps preserve the required
full-tape query coupling and still have affordable logarithmic descriptions.

## 4. A single posterior kernel for the continuum of queries

The two filtrations must be kept separate. The exact Gaussian formula
conditions on the original observable noisy-matrix history. The decoder
conditions on the scalar-perturbed prefix. It does not follow that the latter
is itself an exact Gaussian matrix history.

A compatible common kernel can nevertheless be constructed from the packet.
The original observed history and its whitened innovation vectors determine
one another chronologically, because every covariance has the positive noise
floor. Start from the iid original row packets and weight their law by the
scalar-prefix likelihood. Given an original row history, use the explicit
Gaussian posterior of the initialized matrices and append fresh raw query
noises. The original query innovations then have the conditional law used by
the exact row construction; applying the perturbed row circuit to those same
innovations gives the perturbed passive-query law.

This construction uses the original uncapped history in the proof of the
matrix posterior, even though the surrogate row functions use prescribed
caps. It defines one posterior of the latent training variables and one
measurable query kernel. Its scalar likelihood is exactly

\[
 L_c(Z)=\prod_{r\le j}\kappa_\eta\!\left(
 c_r-\frac1n\sum_iF_r(Z_i;c_{<r})\right).
\]

Thus the law averaged by the Fourier decoder and the law used in the
full-tape accuracy transfer are the same. The argument works simultaneously
for all fixed query parameters because the Gaussian posterior is defined
before selecting the query. It does not require query-dependent versions of
conditional expectation to share an unexplained null set. Future training
innovations need not be independent of the whitened query innovation under
the original full-tape coupling; those unused future coordinates are absent
from a current-prefix query circuit.

## 5. Current-state semantics and private selected marks

vector. Evaluation at one row argument may recompute past row nodes, but it
does not evaluate a weighted training empirical update, recover a discarded

The clarified bridge also supplies the needed clock convention: complete a

## 6. Fourier evaluation, denominators, and finite precision

independence of rows conditional on realized summaries is assumed.

The numerical error allocations correctly include the large integration
volumes, high frequencies, and number of summands. Projecting the approximate
characteristic function onto the unit disk controls power amplification by

current indices, coordinates, and accumulators are stored. The logarithms of
the grid sizes and the required absolute precision are polynomial in the
specified finite-program parameters.

For each ideal continuous prefix of length `j`, the elementary density

\[
 p_j(C_{\le j})\ge
 \frac{\rho}{P(2B+2)^j}
\]

outside a total probability `rho` over all prefixes, plus their Gaussian
box tails. The likelihood and bounded-test numerator Lipschitz bounds control
their ratios near those ideal prefixes. Hence a sufficiently accurate
rounded prefix evaluates approximately the ideal posterior function. It is
not assigned a nonexistent continuous density, and no claim of exact
conditioning on a quantization bin is needed.

The finite-precision selection supplement is valid. Quantize each realized
moment vector to sup-norm accuracy `epsilon`, then use rational convex
elimination. The selected weighted unquantized moments differ from the source
means by at most `2 epsilon`. This contributes at most

support has at most `P+1` points, and the integer determinant expansion gives
polynomial bit lengths for its rational weights. Exact rank decisions on

also need the ordinary finite-precision input convention: dyadic inputs, or

## 7. Uniform accuracy and absolute exponents

The deterministic proof-only center follows from the dense-pair estimate by
Fubini. It is not stored. Let `A` be the common training event, with
`Pr(A^c) <= delta/16`. The ideal prefixes retain all earlier scalar entries,
so `Pr(A^c | C_{<=j})` is a nonnegative martingale. Appending the proof-only
revelation of `A` gives one event of probability at least `1-delta/4` on which
the actual tape is good and every prefix posterior has bad mass at most
`1/4`.

The full-tape query bound then places at least `5/8` posterior mass within
`b_n(delta/32)+1/(4n)` of the same deterministic center, for every admissible
time and sphere input. Smoothed-CDF tests of error `1/16`, transition width
`1/(4n)`, and final bisection bracket `1/(2n)` give error at most

\[
 2b_n(\delta/32)+\frac{3}{4n}
\]

before any separately reserved final-output rounding. The claimed `1/n`
allowance therefore has slack. Atoms and flat CDF portions do not obstruct
the bisection invariant. Density failures and explicit Gaussian tails fit
within the remaining probability budget. No sphere net or uncountable union
of random failures is needed.

The size counts are compatible with an absolute exponent: `R <= C log(en)^8`,
`P = O(R^2)`, row dimension `d+O(R)`, selected panel size `O(P)`, and raw
panel/history entries `O(P(d+R)+P)`. The largest posterior matrix has dimension

The theorem fixes the admissible problem before taking width large. Its

## 9. The authorized small-matrix supplement closes that obligation

The supplement supplies complete algorithms, so the cited but unavailable
Fejer/Chebyshev note is no longer needed for this assembly. For a symmetric
positive matrix with eigenvalues in `[a,M]`, choose a power of four `m >= M`
and a dyadic lower bound \(0<\underline a\le a\), and set

\[
 X=I-A/m,\qquad 0\preceq X\preceq(1-\underline a/m)I.
\]

Its inverse is the Neumann series in `X`. Its square root is the binomial
series whose positive coefficient magnitudes `b_k` sum to one. Truncation
before degree \(D=(m/\underline a)K\), with `K` logarithmic in the requested precision,
dimension, and bounds, gives Frobenius tail below the allocated tolerance.
Both `log D` and the exact representation length of `X` have the claimed
logarithmic dependence.

The fixed exactly stored contraction is essential. With grid step `u`, a
rounded power update has local Frobenius error at most `C r^2 u`. Right
multiplication by `X` and symmetrization do not enlarge propagated error, so
the `k`th power error is at most `C k r^2 u`. The scalar binomial-coefficient
recurrence has error at most `C k u` while its computed coefficients remain
bounded. Summing the resulting errors gives the supplement's conservative
bound

\[
 C r^3(1+\sqrt m)D^2u.
\]

Thus only `O(log D)` additional fractional bits are needed. A constant
number of matrix arrays, the power counter, and ordinary integer-arithmetic
scratch give the claimed polynomial space. Large degree affects runtime,
not an uncounted matrix or coefficient table.

For an indefinite symmetric `A`, the regularization `A^2+v^2 I` has a known
positive gap. Approximating its square root to Frobenius error `v` and
returning `(A+Y)/2+vI` gives an exactly dyadic PSD matrix: its smallest
eigenvalue is at least `v/2`. The difference from `A_+` is at most `2rv`.
This also checks the negative-eigenvalue and zero-eigenvalue cases. The
conservative radial-cap routine scales by a nonnegative dyadic factor no
larger than the exact permissible factor, and its final multiplication is
exact. It therefore preserves the PSD property and the cap, rather than
merely producing a nearby floating-point array.

The input-perturbation estimates do not assume commutativity. The inverse
uses the resolvent identity; the square-root difference solves the displayed
Sylvester equation, whose Frobenius inner-product bound gives the claimed
half-gap stability. The original Sylvester coefficient system is explicitly
vectorized, including its `R^2` dimension and `R^4` matrix-entry storage.

Finally, the common operand grid and local error budget prevent a hidden
chain of precision doubling through exact dyadic products. The graph has a
polynomial one-step Lipschitz bound, so logarithmic local precision grows
only polynomially in graph size and logarithmic range/gap parameters. Symmetric
rounding and the PSD buffer restore the required domain properties before
later calls; all gapped spectral inputs retain at least half their gap.
These routines can be included in the Fourier row evaluator's counted
subroutine space, including its nested calls.

Accordingly, this supplement closes the original packet's major numerical
proof obligation. Together with the rational selection supplement and the
clarified bridge, the construction establishes the requested retained-storage
and peak-decoding-space conclusion at an unspecified absolute logarithmic
power, subject to the stated inherited hypotheses and input interfaces. The
denominator fallback is a total-function guard off the success event; it does
not require a new statistical argument.
