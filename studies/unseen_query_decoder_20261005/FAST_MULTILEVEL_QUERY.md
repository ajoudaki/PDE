# Multilevel query integration: an exact correlated recurrence and the order barrier

2026-10-06. Bounded author continuation; no experiment, Git operation,
independent review, or promotion.

There is a constructive improvement for the actual leading learned-mean
gate: its Gaussian expectation can be evaluated for general correlated
training inputs by a finite Fourier-coefficient recurrence. Independence
of the training preactivations is unnecessary. The recurrence integrates
all terms through order K, has a factorially small, uniformly bounded row
remainder, and costs O(K (m+1)^3 N_m(2K+1)) arithmetic and index
operations, where

\[
 N_m(p)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,p)}2^j{m\choose j}{p\choose j}.
 \tag{1}
\]

For two training examples this is O(K^3) work and O(K^2) numerical
coordinates. Its error can be certified at root width with
K of order log(n)/log log(n) when the learned-mean strength is fixed.
The same bounded control has a valid expectation error under the
low-entropy posterior law used by the existing decoder.

This does not complete the full query construction. The factorial tail
is a theorem for this displayed learned-mean gate, not for the whole
small-label expansion of the trained network. Every fixed deterministic
truncation of even this gate leaves a positive residual variance. Splitting
that remainder into ordinary independently sampled levels does not remove
the inverse-square accuracy factor. Extending the explicit coefficient
table to the growing collection of history directions also exceeds a
polylogarithmic count. These are failures of specified multilevel/table
arguments, not a lower bound against all neural decoders.

## 1. Original target and exact scope of the positive calculation

Keep the full original target: Gaussian iid initialization, zero readout,
mean squared loss, mobilities (n,1,...,1,n), all hidden layers learning,
arbitrary fixed L>=2, m>=d spanning sphere data, the original complete
label allowance, and a positive training-feature Gram gap. Activation
values may be unbounded. The desired query reads the current retained
state without replay and has one event controlling the whole sphere and
all physical times, including the fitted endpoint.

FAST_EFFICIENCY_RESULT.md supplies a smaller complete response decoder
under its stated inherited inputs, but its query cost still contains n.
The construction below is not substituted into that full cost theorem.
It concerns one exact contribution generated by the actual training
equations, with the omitted contributions identified explicitly.

Use the depth-two notation of FAST_QUERY_STRUCTURE.md, and for the
constructive calculation take

\[
 \phi_1(z)=\sin z,\qquad \phi_2(z)=1+\alpha\sin z,
 \qquad 0<\alpha<1.
 \tag{2}
\]

This is a restricted activation example within the original class,
not an assertion that all allowed activations have a Fourier-polynomial
description. Let v_a=x_a/sqrt(d) and ||v_a||=||v||=1. Define

\[
 Q_{ab}=e^{-1}\sinh(v_a^Tv_b),\qquad
 \kappa_a(v)=e^{-1}\sinh(v_a^Tv),\qquad
 q=\frac{1-e^{-2}}2.
 \tag{3}
\]

These are the initial first-layer feature pairings. In particular
Q_{aa}=q. Let (Z_1,...,Z_m,Z_v) be the centered Gaussian vector with
covariance

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}Q&\kappa(v)\\\kappa(v)^T&q\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{4}
\]

It is positive semidefinite because it is the covariance of the initial
second-layer Gaussian actions on the training and query feature vectors
in their deterministic large-width limit. Alternatively, it is the Gram
of the first-layer sine functions. No inverse or nonsingularity of Q is
needed below. Correlated, nonorthogonal data are allowed.

For a specified real strength eta, put

\[
 S(Z)=\sum_a y_a(1+\alpha\sin Z_a),\qquad
 C_v(Z)=\alpha\sum_a y_a\kappa_a(v)\cos Z_a,
 \tag{5}
\]
\[
 X_{\eta,v}=S(Z)[1+\alpha\sin(Z_v+\eta S(Z)C_v(Z))].
 \tag{6}
\]

The origin of this field is exact. For the actual zero-readout flow,
dot w(0)=2S/m and the direct top-matrix update contributes

\[
 [t^2]z_v^{(2)}(t)\supset\frac2{m^2}S C_v.              \tag{7}
\]

At finite width, replace kappa_a in (7) by its empirical first-layer
pairing; the formula follows by differentiating the actual top-matrix
velocity once. At separately fixed order, the Gaussian moment limit is
(4)--(7), as derived in the allowed structure note. Equation (6) sums
every power of this direct mean inside its gate. The first-matrix
response, later readout changes, and later trained fields remain in the
actual flow and are not represented by setting eta=2t^2/m^2 in (6).
In particular (6) is not a trajectory approximation theorem.

Set

\[
 M_S=(1+\alpha)\sum_a|y_a|,
 \quad M_C(v)=\alpha\sum_a|y_a\kappa_a(v)|,
 \quad A=|\eta|M_SM_C(v).
 \tag{8}
\]

These bound |S|, |C_v|, and |eta S C_v|. For uniform sphere and strength
bounds |eta|<=eta_* one may use
M_C^*=alpha q sum_a|y_a| and A_*=eta_* M_S M_C^*, since
|kappa_a(v)|<=q. A=0 is the exact order-zero case.

## 2. Fourier recurrence that preserves every Gaussian correlation

For u in Z^m write e_u(Z)=exp(i u^TZ). Define the finite coefficient
arrays s_u,c_u by S=sum_u s_u e_u and C_v=sum_u c_u e_u. Their only
possibly nonzero coefficients are

\[
 s_0=\sum_a y_a,\quad
 s_{e_a}=\frac{\alpha y_a}{2i},\quad
 s_{-e_a}=-\frac{\alpha y_a}{2i},\qquad
 c_{e_a}=c_{-e_a}=\frac{\alpha y_a\kappa_a(v)}2.
 \tag{9}
\]

Let d_u=sum_w s_w c_{u-w}, so SC_v=sum_u d_u e_u. The array d has
at most C(m+1)^2 potentially nonzero entries, all with ||u||_1<=2.
Define

\[
 p_{0,u}=s_u,\qquad
 p_{k+1,u}=\frac{i\eta}{k+1}\sum_w d_w p_{k,u-w}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Induction using multiplication of Fourier polynomials proves exactly

\[
 \sum_u p_{k,u}e_u(Z)=S(Z)\frac{(i\eta S(Z)C_v(Z))^k}{k!},
 \qquad p_{k,u}=0\quad\text{if }\|u\|_1>2k+1.          \tag{11}
\]

The Gaussian characteristic function gives, including singular (4),

\[
 w_v(u):=\mathbb E e^{i(Z_v+u^TZ)}
 =\exp[-\tfrac12(u^TQu+2\kappa(v)^Tu+q)].                \tag{12}
\]

The quadratic expression in (12) is nonnegative by (4), so 0<w_v(u)<=1.
This identity follows by writing the Gaussian vector as a fixed linear
map of independent standard Gaussians and multiplying their elementary
characteristic functions. It never makes the coordinates Z_a independent.

The expectation of the order-K row control is therefore

\[
 T_K(\eta,v)=\sum_a y_a+
       \alpha\operatorname{Im}\sum_{k=0}^K\sum_u p_{k,u}w_v(u).
 \tag{13}
\]

Its actual row control is

\[
 X_K=S+\alpha\operatorname{Im}\left[
 e^{iZ_v}\sum_{k=0}^K S\frac{(i\eta S C_v)^k}{k!}\right].
 \tag{14}
\]

Thus T_K=E_mu X_K for the Gaussian law mu in (4). Repeated sample
indices and repeated Fourier frequencies have already been combined
in (10). Enumerating every term in the expansion of S^(k+1) C_v^k,
or every binary sign configuration of each term, is unnecessary.

To verify (1), choose j nonzero coordinates, choose their 2^j signs,
and choose positive integer magnitudes whose sum is at most p. The
latter count is binom(p,j), by subtracting one from each magnitude
and summing the resulting weak compositions. These choices partition
the integer vectors with ||u||_1<=p. In particular

\[
 {m+p\choose p}\le N_m(p)\le(2p+1)^m.                  \tag{15}
\]

The lower bound counts just the nonnegative vectors.

## 3. Exact tail, posterior control mean, and sampling order

Taylor's integral remainder for a real number x is

\[
 e^{ix}-\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{(ix)^k}{k!}
 =\frac{(ix)^{K+1}}{K!}\int_0^1(1-u)^K e^{iux}\,du.
\]

Since |e^{iux}|=1, (8), (14) give the uniform row bound

\[
 |X_{\eta,v}-X_K|\le B_K,
 \qquad B_K:=\alpha M_S\frac{A^{K+1}}{(K+1)!}.           \tag{16}
\]

This is stronger than a geometric majorant with an extra exp(A) factor.
It is valid under every row law; no posterior tail transfer is required.
In particular its residual second moment is at most B_K^2 under nu.
Also |X_K|<=M_S(1+alpha)+B_K. If D(nu||mu)<=h, bounded-function
total variation consequently proves

\[
 |\mathbb E_\nu X_K-T_K|
 \le[M_S(1+\alpha)+B_K]\sqrt{2h}.                       \tag{17}
\]

For vector controls the same statement holds using a pointwise
Euclidean bound. Equation (17) is the bounded-control interface from
FAST_QUERY_CONTROL_VARIATE.md. It does not pretend that the prior
and posterior means agree exactly.

Suppose T_K is computed to deterministic error e_quad and the remainder
is estimated by the existing prior-block median rule with s rows per
block, where s h<=1/128. Its scalar error is bounded by

\[
 e_{\rm quad}+[M_S(1+\alpha)+B_K]\sqrt{2h}
                                      +4B_K/\sqrt s,   \tag{18}
\]

at the block-amplified probability stated in the supplied rule. The
remainder can be evaluated at a packet from (14), with O(K+m) ordinary
operations once that packet and its query context are supplied. A
finite seed and a whole-domain probability event remain governed by
the existing sampler interface; (18) by itself supplies neither for
a new full network construction.

For h=polylog(n)/n, fixed bounded strength A>0, and
s=polylog(n), the sufficient certificate 4B_K/sqrt(s)<=n^(-1/2)
times a prescribed logarithmic factor requires a growing K. The least
order meeting this factorial certificate satisfies

\[
 K+1=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)
                         \frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.     \tag{19}
\]

Indeed integral comparison for sum_{j=1}^k log j gives
log(k!)=k log k-k+O(log(k+1)). Taking logarithms in (16) reduces the
condition to k log k-k log(eA)>=log(n)/2+O(log log(n)); substitution
of k=c log(n)/log log(n) proves (19), with the usual slightly larger
c as a sufficient choice. This is the order of this error certificate,
not a lower bound for all approximations to the gate. For varying A,
K=O(A+log(n)) is a simpler sufficient bound from k!>=(k/e)^k.
Uniformity over bounded |eta| and sphere v follows by using A_*.

The full original labels are fixed before n tends to infinity. Small
fixed labels can reduce A and the onset width. They do not make a
fixed nonzero factorial remainder decrease with n. Conversely, setting
s to a logarithmic power changes (19) only in its lower-order terms.
Sampling the remainder therefore does not keep the number of exactly
integrated leading orders fixed at root-width accuracy.

One can also dispense with remainder sampling for this restricted gate:
take B_K below the desired local tolerance and use T_K alone. Its
posterior error is bounded by (17)+B_K+e_quad. Thus the constructive
content is the counted integral (13); multilevel sampling is optional,
not the source of its efficient evaluation.

## 4. Complete cost for the correlated leading gate

At every order store the current and next p arrays on
||u||_1<=2K+1, the sparse d array, and an accumulated scalar expectation.
Each entry update in (10) uses at most C(m+1)^2 coefficient terms.
For a fully specified index implementation, list the frequency vectors
lexicographically. Translation by a fixed sparse frequency preserves
that order, so merge its translated old list with the new list and add
coefficients at matching vectors. Each comparison uses at most m
integer-coordinate comparisons. Repeat for the C(m+1)^2 entries of d.
This adds one factor m to the coefficient-operation count. Computing
(12) directly costs O(m^2) arithmetic per frequency; one may cache
those weights in another array of the same size. Thus

\[
 W_{\rm arith+index}\le C(K+1)(m+1)^3N_m(2K+1),\qquad
 S_{\rm words}\le C[N_m(2K+1)+m^2+d].                   \tag{20}
\]

Frequency vectors and loop indices cost an additional
O(m log(K+2)) bits per stored vector in this explicit implementation.
The numerical-word row of (20) counts the coefficient arrays; add these
index bits, or O(m N_m(2K+1)) ordinary small integer registers if
registers are the accounting unit. Each comparison uses O(log(K+2))
bit work. No unbounded integer packs an array into one coordinate.

The training input Gram and (3) cost O(m^2 d) input arithmetic and
O(m^2) elementary kernel calls; a new query costs O(md) input arithmetic
and O(m) kernel calls. Directly computing all weights (12) adds
N_m(2K+1) real exponential calls. These are at counted precision, not
free Gaussian-integral primitives. There are no dense rows, discarded
weight accesses, or historical training updates in (9)--(13).

For a numerical error e_quad, coefficient amplification is controlled by

\[
 \sum_u|s_u|\le M_S,\quad \sum_u|c_u|\le M_C,
 \quad \sum_u|p_{k,u}|\le M_S A^k/k!,\quad
 \sum_{k\ge0}\sum_u|p_{k,u}|\le M_S e^A.                \tag{21}
\]

An error inserted at order j is amplified into later orders by products
A^(k-j)j!/k!, whose sum is at most e^A because j!/(j+l)!<=1/l!.
The final weights have magnitude at most one. Allowing an extra
polynomial factor for all arithmetic slots, sufficient working guard
precision is therefore

\[
 O\left(A+\log\frac{(K+2)(m+1)^3N_m(2K+1)(1+M_S)}
                         {e_{\rm quad}}\right)          \tag{22}
\]

bits, beyond the lengths of the supplied numbers. Kernel/label/query
input errors must fit the same allowance. Perturbing the quadratic
exponent in (12) costs at most a factor C(K+1)^2 times the kernel
entry error on a sufficiently small input-error neighborhood; this
adds only logarithmic guard positions to (22). Actual elementary and
activation/data evaluator work and workspace are separate additions.

For fixed m, (15), (19), (20) give O(K^(m+1)) arithmetic and O(K^m)
coefficient storage, with the displayed input factors. At m=2 this
gives O(K^3) work and O(K^2) coefficients at general correlated data.
The exponent depends on m; it is not an absolute fourth/fifth-power
bound over the original general-data class. In particular the exact
correlated evaluation repairs the independence limitation of the
earlier bivariate generating-product formula, but does not provide
its dimension-independent count.

## 5. Fixed-order multilevel sampling has a genuine variance remainder

The preceding factorial estimate might still overstate the necessary
order if every fixed remainder had zero variance. It does not. The
following argument concerns this actual leading gate, even after
integrating the last fresh query Gaussian exactly.

Use the admissible orthogonal data v_1=e_1,v_2=e_2 and
v=(e_1+e_2)/sqrt(2). Then Q=qI_2 and kappa_1,kappa_2>0.
Choose unequal positive labels y_1,y_2 inside the full original
allowance. Set b_a=kappa_a/q and
sigma_v^2=q-sum_a kappa_a^2/q>=0. Conditional Gaussian integration
replaces e^{iZ_v} by exp(-sigma_v^2/2) exp(i b^TZ).
For eta!=0 define the remainder without its nonzero constant
alpha exp(-sigma_v^2/2) by

\[
 R_K(Z)=S(Z)\operatorname{Im}\left[e^{ib^TZ}
 \left(e^{i\eta S C_v}-\sum_{k=0}^K\frac{(i\eta S C_v)^k}{k!}
 \right)\right].                                      \tag{23}
\]

For every fixed K, Var_mu(R_K)>0. To prove it, put
z_*=(pi/2,pi/2), so C_v(z_*)=0 and
S_*=S(z_*)=(1+alpha)(y_1+y_2)>0. The Jacobian determinant of
the map z -> (b^Tz,S(z)C_v(z)) at z_* is

\[
 \frac{S_*\alpha\kappa_1\kappa_2}{q}(y_1-y_2)\ne0.      \tag{24}
\]

Indeed grad S(z_*)=0 and partial_a C_v(z_*)=-alpha y_a kappa_a.
The inverse function theorem applies to this analytic map with
invertible derivative: some neighborhood of z_* is mapped
diffeomorphically onto an open neighborhood of (b^Tz_*,0).
For every sufficiently small nonzero second coordinate D, the complex
Taylor remainder e^(i eta D)-sum_{k<=K}(i eta D)^k/k! is nonzero,
because its first nonzero coefficient is (i eta D)^(K+1)/(K+1)!.
As the first coordinate varies through an interval, its product with
e^(i phase) has nonzero imaginary part somewhere. Since S stays
positive near z_*, (23) is nonzero at some point of that neighborhood,
but is zero at z_*. A continuous function with positive Gaussian
density cannot be almost surely constant while taking these different
values. This proves the variance assertion.

For fixed K the remainder is bounded by some M_K<infinity. If
D(nu||mu)<=h, the bounded-function total-variation inequality gives

\[
 |\operatorname{Var}_\nu R_K-\operatorname{Var}_\mu R_K|
                         \le5M_K^2\sqrt{h/2}.           \tag{25}
\]

The second moments differ by at most M_K^2 TV, since R_K^2 lies
in [0,M_K^2]. The means differ by at most 2M_K TV, so their squares
differ by at most 4M_K^2 TV. Add these inequalities and use
TV<=sqrt(h/2). Thus the fixed-order variance remains bounded below
by a positive constant for all sufficiently small h. This establishes
the relevant posterior statement as well as the prior statement.

For clarity, ordinary independent multilevel sample means obey an
exact allocation constraint. If level j has variance v_j, one-sample
cost c_j>0, and N_j independent samples, its total estimator variance
is sum_j v_j/N_j and its work is sum_j c_jN_j. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \left(\sum_j\sqrt{v_jc_j}\right)^2
 \le\left(\sum_jv_j/N_j\right)\left(\sum_jc_jN_j\right).
 \tag{26}
\]

Requiring variance at most epsilon^2 therefore forces work at least
epsilon^(-2)(sum_j sqrt(v_j c_j))^2. For a fixed unintegrated
nonzero-variance level, that is an inverse-square accuracy cost even
if all later variances decay factorially. One may instead sample the
entire coupled remainder (23), but its fixed-K variance is also positive.
This is a limitation of these unbiased independent-level/sample-mean
designs. It is not a sample lower bound for symbolic integration,
arbitrary correlated estimators, or general neural decoders.

## 6. Where extending this recurrence to the full source fails

The exact recurrence (10) has only m basic frequencies. The actual
current source has a growing number R(n) of Gaussian packet directions
and nonlinear fields; SANE_DECODER_CORE.md correctly charges their
row evaluation but does not integrate their joint nonlinear law.
Even granting an additional finite-frequency normal form with R
generators, the same dense coefficient-table plan would store

\[
 N_R(2K+1)\ge{R+2K+1\choose2K+1}                       \tag{27}
\]

entries. For example, with R comparable to log(n)^(5/2) and the
fixed-strength certificate (19), logarithms of the right side have
order log(n). Indeed p=2K+1 is comparable to log(n)/log log(n),
R/p is comparable to log(n)^(3/2) log log(n), and
log binom(R+p,p) is of order p log(R/p). Thus this particular table
has n to a positive constant power entries, rather than a
polylogarithmic count. Streaming the entries saves their simultaneous
storage but not the enumeration work. No claim that all these
coefficients are independent or nonzero in the actual neural source
is needed to reject that allocated-table algorithm.

There are two logically earlier obstacles as well. First, the allowed
full activation class is strip analytic, not necessarily a finite
trigonometric polynomial. A Fourier dictionary and a truncation theorem
for its trained compositions still have to be supplied. Second, the
factorial tail in (16) only expands the scalar exponential of the
specified direct learned mean. It does not include the other response
paths of the actual nonlinear flow. The existing residual-curvature
Dyson expansion has a factorial operator tail, but its contractions
contain the full trajectory and noncommuting curvature; it is not
identified with (10). The allowed label-series source likewise does
not give a width-uniform all-time analytic disk containing the full
original labels. Those missing implications cannot be inserted merely
because the first few response coefficients can be integrated exactly.

The periodic conditional averaging in FAST_QUERY_CONTROL_VARIATE.md
can be performed before (23) when its symmetry applies. It sums the
integer representatives exactly, but leaves the nonlinear bounded
torus variables and remaining Gaussian packet directions. It changes
neither the need to control the full omitted response nor the number
of directions in a direct extension of (27). Arbitrary spanning data
also lack the product periodic representation used there; (12) is
the specific new correlated-data repair for the present finite gate.

## 7. Frozen conclusion and provenance

The constructive result is the correlated Gaussian recurrence
(9)--(14), with the posterior-valid factorial control (16)--(18) and
the complete count (20)--(22). It evaluates an actual leading
feature-learning gate, including all powers of its learned mean,
without a fresh query table, dense-row access, or training replay.
It does not freeze those branches in a claimed approximation to the
full network.

The negative result is equally specific: fixed deterministic order
leaves positive posterior variance for this gate, and ordinary
multilevel allocation does not remove the inverse-square cost of a
remaining fixed-variance level. The growing Fourier-table extension
also fails the requested count. An alternative representation that
sums the full growing response without that table remains open.
No full all-sphere/all-time/endpoint decoder or universal impossibility
theorem is asserted, and the checked complete decoder's cost table
is unchanged.
