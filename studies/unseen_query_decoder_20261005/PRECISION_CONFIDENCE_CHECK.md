## 2. Finite tests, blocks, seeds, and live arrays

For one fixed context a coordinate bad-block probability is at most
\(1/8\), by posterior Chebyshev plus product-law total variation. With
\(O(R)\) coordinates and odd \(J\), its full bad-median probability is
at most \(CR2^{-J/2}\). Taking
\[
 F\ge C+\log(Q|\mathcal A|R/\delta),\qquad J\ge CF
\]
gives constructive choices \(F,J\le C(d+1)p\).
Independent successive stage seeds justify conditioning on previous seeds:
each code then supplies one fixed incoming context. Reuse of a seed across
codes is allowed by the union bound; reuse across dependent stages would
not justify this proof.

The complete-pair schedule gives \(P\asymp R^2\). Together with
\(B_F\ge1\) and \(\log(1/\eta)\asymp p\), it implies
\[
 K_+=\max\{1,P\log(1+B_F^2/\eta^2)/(2\alpha)\}
       \asymp R^2p/\alpha.
\]
Here \(\alpha\) is still a fixed scientific confidence share. For
\(K_+\le\widehat K\le2K_+\), \(n\ge512\widehat K\), and
\(s=\lfloor n/(128\widehat K)\rfloor\), the floor argument gives both
\[
 \frac{n}{256\widehat K}\le s\le
 \frac{n}{128\widehat K}\le\frac{Cn}{R^2p}.
\]
Also \(\operatorname{TV}(\nu^{\otimes s},\mu^{\otimes s})
\le\sqrt{sK_+/(2n)}\le1/16\).
These are two-sided estimates for a sufficient information certificate;
neither estimates the true information from below.

A one-pass test of the entire coordinate-median failure uses one vector
of block sums and two vectors of above/below threshold counters. At most
\(O(R)\) finite words are live between packets. Thresholds can be
hardwired as finite cut positions among possible dyadic block sums; the
test does not need access to a population oracle. With \(D\le CR\) and
\[
 E=1+\lceil\log_2(Js+2)\rceil\le Cp,
\]
the sums, row/block counters, and thresholds need \(S_{\rm test}\le CRp\)
bits. Within-packet finite row evaluation is charged separately and does
not enlarge the state across packets of this block transition test.

The supplied generator theorem therefore permits
\[
 A=C(Dp+S_{\rm test}+F+E)\le CRp,
\]
because \(d+1\le CR\). It uses \(C(L+1)Rp^2\) retained seed bits,
\(CR^2p^3\) work per packet, and \(CRp\) generator scratch. The larger
failure exponent is explicitly in \(A\); it is not inserted into a word
or dropped. Seed generation and writes fit the initialization envelope.

The actual decoder, unlike the proof test, keeps all \(J\) block-mean
vectors until the stage medians are taken. This costs
\(CRJp\le C(d+1)Rp^2\) bits. Incoming contexts and coefficients remain
fixed while collecting all coordinates; the arrays are discarded before
the next stage. Together with selected packets, metric, marks, history
and coefficient arrays of \(CR^2p\) bits, the peak after initialization is
\[
 C\{R^2p+(L+d+2)Rp^2\}.
\]
Raw retained memory is no larger. No inequality \(p\le R\) was used.

The implemented vector error remains
\[
 CB_{\rm pass}\sqrt{(R+1)(\widehat K+1)/n},
\]
with scalar second-moment error
\(CB_{\rm pass}^2\sqrt{(\widehat K+1)/n}\). This follows by combining
the \(O(R)\) coordinate tolerances and the lower bound on \(s\).
The smaller population-comparison radius cannot replace this error.
Its propagated remainder and the additional sampling width gate are
unchanged conditions; dropping \(d+1\) from \(p\) does not remove them.
