## 2. A valid complete pair schedule

At a completed availability boundary for a new named field, acquire its
pair with every previously available named field and with itself, except
for pairs already acquired and retained. Include the constant field.
Both operands use immutable creation-time scalar arguments, exact dyadic
products, exact accumulation, and the prescribed noisy scalar rounding.
The newly acquired pairs form a batch: their operands are fixed before
the batch, and one pair acquisition does not alter another operand.

The matrix-call chronology has an essential exception to the word
"boundary." In FAST_FINITE_SOURCE_BRIDGE.md (9), innovation contractions
\(\widehat t\) are needed before the answer \(\widehat y\). They
remain inside that call, with their original grid h_t and with the
coefficient arrays frozen. The complete-table extension adds only the
remaining pairs after the call completes. No matrix call or new
coefficient solve is inserted between \(\widehat t\) and
\(\widehat y\). Already consumed innovation coordinates can be paired;
future packet coordinates cannot be revealed early.

If the actual named-field count, including one, is N, the complete
unordered table has N(N+1)/2 pairs. Mandatory intrinsic acquisitions
may add a constant-factor number of repeated measurements; retain their
original creation arguments and noise marks. Therefore, for R comparable
to N,

\[
                       cR^2\le P\le CR^2.               \tag{2}
\]

These measurements are of actual finite source fields. They are not
channels carrying a fabricated constant answer to increase an information
bound. The source's common row packet can support pairs of any of its
already-created coordinate functions. Such auxiliary packet-table
statistics are not identified with a neural population inner product
between different layers; the physical flow uses only its specified
pair entries. Extra pairs add observations, noise marks, storage, and
statistical error-budget terms, all counted here.

The extension does not change the physical velocities. Conditional on a
completed full proof history, each extra pair is a function of the fields
already in that history plus fresh independent scalar noise. It introduces
no new initialized-matrix information beyond that full history. For the
coarser retained scalar posterior it does change the law and its information
budget. FAST_FINITE_POSTERIOR.md applies to the enlarged finite program
with its actual P; the pending passive theorem must use that same program.

The metric construction remains valid because all tests are pairs of

Use min(h,h_t) in the common metric tolerance. Private metric/packet

\[

 \eta=2^{-\lceil c_\eta p_0\rceil}.

## 4. Information, block length, error, and width

Let alpha be the explicit confidence share in the all-prefix posterior
information estimate. For the enlarged finite schedule define

\[
 K_+=\max\left\{1,\frac{P}{2\alpha}
                       \log(1+B_F^2/\eta^2)\right\}.
\]

Use a certified dyadic upper approximation satisfying both inequalities

\[
 K_+\le\widehat K\le2K_+,
 \quad s=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{128\widehat K}\right\rfloor,
 \quad n\ge512\widehat K.                              \tag{5}
\]

The upper inequality for K_hat is present in FAST_COMPOSITION.md but
omitted from the local candidate's displayed prescription. It is needed
for the statistical and width accounting, even though only the lower
inequality is used for a query-work upper bound.

Equations (2)--(4), B_F>=1, and 0<alpha<1 imply

\[
 cR^2p/\alpha\le K_+\le CR^2p/\alpha,
 \qquad
 \frac{n}{256\widehat K}\le s\le\frac{n}{128\widehat K}
                          \le\frac{Cn}{R^2p}.           \tag{6}
\]

For the lower block bound, n/(128 K_hat)>=4, and floor u>=u/2 for u>=2.
The lower bound on K_+ is a lower bound on a chosen *upper certificate*;
it is not a lower bound on the true mutual information.

Here is the statistical cost of this block choice. At a good prefix the
finite-posterior lemma supplies D(nu||mu)<=K_+/n. Its product-law bound
gives

\[
 \|\nu^{\otimes s}-\mu^{\otimes s}\|_{\rm TV}
 \le\sqrt{sK_+/(2n)}\le1/16.
 \tag{7}
\]

Suppose the passive interface supplies whitened marks U in R^r with
nu(UU^T)<=I, r<=CR, and a bounded passive test |G|<=B_stat. Under nu^s,
the variance of one coordinate's block mean of UG is at most B_stat^2/s.
Chebyshev at 4B_stat/sqrt(s) costs 1/16; (7) adds at most 1/16 under
the implemented prior product law. Independent odd-J block medians
therefore fail at probability at most 2r 2^(-J/2), and on success

\[
 \|\widehat b-\nu(UG)\|_2
 \le4B_{\rm stat}\sqrt{r/s}
 \le C B_{\rm stat}\sqrt{R\widehat K/n}
 \le C B_{\rm stat}R^{3/2}\sqrt{p/(\alpha n)}.            \tag{8}
\]

For the scalar second moment,

\[
 |\widehat c-\nu(G^2)|
 \le C B_{\rm stat}^2\sqrt{\widehat K/n}
 \le C B_{\rm stat}^2R\sqrt{p/(\alpha n)}.                \tag{9}
\]

The factor sqrt(R) in (8) must be retained. A separate population-center
estimate involving sqrt((K_++R+1)/n) does not replace this coordinate
median bound. Also B_stat is the small physical passive-test cap, not the
large numerical pair cap B_F. FAST_COMPOSITION.md gives a possible
physical cap C beta^(110L) sqrt(d+1) Z^(3/2); its applicability to the
new finite passive law remains part of the stated premise.

Thus smaller blocks preserve the proof, at the larger explicit errors
(8)--(9). The final fixed-depth propagation must use these errors, all
finite/rounding terms, and the actual confidence allocation alpha.
The comparison of that resulting remainder with the original dense-pair
certificate is still an explicit eventual-width obligation. It cannot
be inferred merely by calling both rates n^(-1/2+o(1)). No claim here
quantifies the inherited scientific width threshold.

In particular, decreasing alpha shortens the chosen block and is favorable
to the resource upper bound, but increases the statistical radius and
the width requirement. The sampling gate has scale R^2p/alpha, and the
source, horizon, nY>=1, and dense-certificate comparison gates are all
retained. Their confidence dependence is not erased from the theorem.

## 5. Prepare once, and keep all block vectors

At each passive stage, hold the input context and all prepared coefficients
fixed. A packet produces the entire vector of cross-moment integrands
and its scalar second-moment integrand. Accumulate that vector for each
of the s rows in a block, store the resulting p-bit mean vector, and
repeat for J blocks. After all J vectors have been stored, take the
median of each coordinate and discard the block vectors before the next
stage. The next stage uses an independent retained seed.

The physical external-grid entropy and confidence/count logarithms give
J<=Cp. The J by r mean table occupies at most CRp^2 bits. Its vector
accumulator occupies CRp bits. Both fit

\[
            C\{R^2p+Q Rp^2\}                            \tag{10}
\]

alongside the model, coefficient arrays, and retained independent seeds.
No coordinate restart is needed. Holding the context fixed until every
median is completed is necessary: changing a later coordinate's context
would define a different estimator.

The full-vector sampling event also fits the seed allowance without a
scalar-state assumption. For a fixed context and fixed rational proof
thresholds, a one-pass bad-median tester keeps r running block sums and
two r-vectors of counts for block means above/below their thresholds.
A coordinate median crosses a threshold exactly when a majority of its
block means crosses it. Its between-packet state is therefore

\[
 S_{\rm test}\le CRp,
\]

including row/block counters. The test need not store J vectors. Its
thresholds are hardwired proof advice, never decoder inputs. Packet
scratch is cleared between packets. With D<=CR, external failure-union
logarithm F<=Cp, and E=1+ceil(log_2(Js+2))<=Cp, the inherited generator
interface uses the conservative block parameter

\[
 A=C(Dp+S_{\rm test}+F+E)\le CRp.
\]

It therefore retains at most CAE<=CRp^2 seed bits per stage and uses
CA^2E<=CR^2p^3 hash work per packet. This explicitly includes the larger
vector test state. One can also test coordinates separately and union
bound; that alternative is unnecessary for the bound.

The same finite-state event transfers the iid failure estimate in (8)
and (9). No assertion of independence of pseudorandom rows, or theorem
for repeated reading of a random stream, is used. Independent stage seeds
and the physical external-code union keep the reachable-context argument
unchanged.

## 6. Full modular resource ledger

The finite table contains O(R) fields and packet coordinates per row.
At p-bit field precision the exact metric algorithm uses

\[
 W_{\rm metric}\le CnR^5p^3,\qquad
 S_{\rm metric}\le C(nRp+R^3p).                         \tag{11}
\]

This includes the determinant-improvement scan, rank selection, expanded
O(Rp)-bit integer intermediates, the final metric, and its rounding.
Only its O(p)-bit rounded entries remain after setup. The metric is never
inverted during acquisition. The full source table is discarded after
selection; private selected packets remain inaccessible to the query.

Source generation adds the following distinct work terms:

\[
 C(nR+R^2)p^4
 +C(R^5p^2+R^4p^3)
 +CnR^2p^2.                                             \tag{12}
\]

These are, respectively, finite Gaussian row/scalar marks; shared
coefficient preparation; and row arithmetic plus complete empirical
pairs. During source generation the field table is present, so old row
fields are cached and each new field is evaluated incrementally. One
must not multiply the whole R^2 row interpreter by every one of the
P acquisitions when deriving the last source term. Source storage is
CnRp bits. Later seed generation and retained seeds add CQRp^2 work/bits;
their work is below the displayed preparation allowance.

All compact updates, with conditioning arrays shared by causal phase,
cost

\[
                     C(R^5p^2+R^4p^3).                 \tag{13}
\]

For a direct loose verification, P=O(R^2) updates can each evaluate at
most CR selected packets using CR^2 scalar row operations: CR^5p^2
work. Exact metric bilinear reductions contribute only CR^4p^2.
There are CR new coefficient phases, not P independent spectral solves;
the core's preparation count supplies the two terms of (13).

A query packet uses

\[
                 C(Rp^4+R^2p^3)                         \tag{14}
\]

internal work. The first term is finite Gaussian generation. Ordinary
row arithmetic, including the full whitening matrix-vector product,
is CR^2p^2 and is covered by the second term; generator work is
CR^2p^3. Preparing coefficients once per stage costs (13).

With simultaneous vectors there are QJs<=CQsp packet visits, not
CQspR visits. Equations (6) and (14) yield

\[
 W_{\rm query}\le CQ\{s(Rp^5+R^2p^4)+R^5p^2+R^4p^3\}
 \le CQ\{n(p^4/R+p^3)+R^5p^2+R^4p^3\}.                 \tag{15}
\]

Computing J vector means costs at most CRJp^2. Merging sorted p-bit
means costs CRJ log(J+1)p<=CRp^3; this is below the preparation term
CR^4p^3. No sort factor is omitted. Taking R>=1 also gives the simpler
stream allowance CQn(p^4+p^3), without assuming p<=R.

The complete internal memory statements are

\[
\begin{split}
 S_{\rm retained},S_{\rm train/query}
     &\le C(R^2p+QRp^2),\\
 S_{\rm init}
     &\le C(nRp+R^3p+QRp^2+R^2p).
\end{split}                                              \tag{16}
\]

The second line keeps later model/seed storage visible before taking a
major-parameter envelope. It can also be reduced using the width gate,
but that reduction is not necessary below.
