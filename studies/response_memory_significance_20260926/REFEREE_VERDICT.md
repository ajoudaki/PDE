# Final independent referee verdict

## Decision

**Accept as a strong specialized theory contribution, subject only to the two minor wording and archival conditions stated below.** I find both compact-horizon convergence theorems mathematically correct and self-contained in `CANDIDATE_V2`, with the response-clock theorem understood exactly as stated for positive initial residual. All five substantive repairs requested in Round 1 are present in V2. No new theorem, experiment, or complexity result is needed for acceptance.

The central result is an autonomous finite-scalar representation of the learned internal-matrix increments of one fixed finite deep nonlinear gradient-flow network, together with uniform approximation of its complete physical parameter trajectory on every prescribed finite horizon. The ordinary activity clock gives `O_T(P^-1)`; the joint response-speed clock gives `O_T(P^-2)` under the stronger activation regularity and positive-initial-residual hypotheses.

This result may truthfully be called a **breakthrough in the narrow mathematical sense of resolving the self-consistent finite-width response-history trajectory-closure obstruction**: the projected histories are generated by the approximate network itself, yet an exact bilinear cancellation reduces the physical update defect to a product of projection errors, and a noncircular continuation argument controls the resulting nonlinear feedback. It is not a breakthrough in total model memory, runtime, empirical rate validation, width-uniform population closure, all-time tracking, or unrestricted historical priority. In formal title and abstract language, “strong specialized theory result” or “new within the reviewed nonexhaustive comparison set” is more precise than the unqualified word “breakthrough.”

## Mathematical adjudication

### Ordinary residual-activity clock

The ordinary-clock theorem is sound and complete under its stated `C^{1,1}_loc` assumptions.

1. The moment ODE is the differentiated shifted-Legendre projection under the dilating activity coordinate. Its initial conditions and zero backward prefix reconstruct every initialized internal matrix exactly (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 330–356).
2. The Legendre tail normalization is correct: the weighted Sturm–Liouville energy gives `1/[P(P+1)]` squared projection error (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 394–420).
3. Differentiating the projected bilinear reconstruction cancels both mixed terms. The physical velocity defect is exactly a product of the forward and backward endpoint projection errors, and its time integral is controlled by the two projection energies (lines 464–502).
4. Only the forward histories need an `H^1` tail estimate. Endpoint evaluation costs `O(P)`, but the Legendre tail supplies the compensating `O(P^-1)` norm factor, so the depth induction does not lose an order (lines 504–555).
5. Dense loss dissipation supplies global finite-time existence and an explicit compact comparison ball. The integrated defect, Gronwall comparison, first-exit exclusion, and compact continuation prove
   
   \[
   \sup_{t\le T}\|\widehat\theta_P(t)-\theta(t)\|
   \le C_T/\sqrt{P(P+1)}=O_T(P^{-1})
   \]
   
   for sufficiently large `P` (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 363–387 and 423–588). The globally bounded activation-and-slope corollary correctly extends existence through every finite time for every fixed `P >= 1` (lines 590–622).

The independent local derivation reaches the same result in `DEEP_ACTIVATION_ERROR_THEOREM.md`, Sections 1–5 (lines 13–297), and `ORACLE_FINITE_HORIZON_BOUND.md`, Sections 3–7 (lines 84–339). I find no circular accuracy assumption, missing depth factor, or signed-error substitution in V2.

### Joint response-speed clock

The response-clock theorem is also sound and self-contained for positive initial residual.

1. The clock rate `dL/dt = rho + ||dPsi/dt||` makes the concatenated response path one-Lipschitz in `L`. The physical-history measure has density at most one, so weighted best approximation is bounded by ordinary Legendre approximation (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 624–647 and 787–805).
2. The stored Gram and moments give endpoint fits `HG^-1 e` and `UG^-1 e`. Product differentiation cancels all dilation terms and yields the exact product-error physical defect (lines 649–681).
3. The proof obtains a raw-energy, order-independent coarse integrated-defect and total-variation bound before using a sharp tail or assuming a clock cap (lines 742–759). This resolves the main potential circularity.
4. Positive dense residual has a finite-horizon lower bound. On the stopped residual tube, the normalized-residual derivative is an orthogonal projection divided by a residual bounded away from zero; compact smoothness therefore bounds `D Psi`, and the coarse path variation then bounds `L_P` independently of `P` (lines 702–785).
5. The unit-speed joint tail gives `1/[P(P+1)]` total projection energy. The exact product defect, Gronwall estimate, explicit order conditions, residual/physical stop exclusion, fixed-`P` prefix-Gram positivity, and compact continuation then give
   
   \[
   \sup_{t\le T}\|\widehat\theta_P(t)-\theta(t)\|
   \le C_T e^{K_TT}/[P(P+1)]=O_T(P^{-2})
   \]
   
   for `P >= P_0(T)` (lines 683–700 and 787–845).
6. The clock is executable without a nonlinear implicit solve: the physical velocities are formed first, directional forward and reverse passes produce `dPsi/dt`, and only then are the clock rate, moments, and Gram advanced (lines 847–874).

The detailed independent derivations in `RESPONSE_CLOCK_UNCONDITIONAL_BOUND.md`, Sections 2–8 (lines 51–351), and `DEEP_ACTIVATION_ERROR_THEOREM.md`, Sections 6–7 (lines 340–461), agree with V2. A `P`-uniform Gram condition number is neither proved nor required for exact fixed-order ODE continuation.

V2 also fixes the former zero-residual definition defect. The normalized response `r/rho` and response-clock ODE are defined only for positive initial residual; at zero initial residual, dense gradient flow is the separate constant physical branch and needs no response-clock state (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 627–632 and 683–700). V2 does not claim uniqueness of an undefined `0/0` extension.

This contract is stated consistently in V2's definitions and source-bounded comparison (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 85–121 and 224–252). The repaired definition of “finite-scalar” correctly permits dependence on fixed `n,m,H,P` (lines 108–119). It does not mean width-independent dimension.

## Novelty relative to the supplied precedents

The correct novelty distinction is between **ingredient precedent** and **a prior theorem satisfying the complete neural closure contract**.

- **HiPPO.** Definition 1, Theorem 2, Propositions 3–6, and Appendix D.3 equation (29) of `hippo_2008.07669.pdf` already provide online projection of a cumulative supplied signal, scaled-Legendre memory, and the triangular dilation dynamics. Those are direct ingredients and are not novel here. HiPPO does not couple endogenous neural forward and backward histories, reconstruct learned matrices, or prove nonlinear complete-parameter trajectory control.
- **Neural Tangent Hierarchy.** Equations (2.1)–(2.2) and (2.7) of `neural_tangent_hierarchy_1909.08156.pdf` provide an exact output/kernel hierarchy and an autonomous top-kernel truncation. Theorem 2.6 gives a width-dependent regime and output/kernel error; equation (2.11) treats test-point tensors. A direct order-`p` training hierarchy has up to `O(m^p)` sample-indexed values before symmetry reductions. It controls wide/NTK-scaled outputs and kernels, not the complete physical parameter path of one fixed finite network.
- **Tensor Programs IV.** The Dynamical Dichotomy, Theorems 3.6/3.8, Corollary 3.9, Definition 5.1, Theorems 5.6/7.4, and the training-time discussion in `tensor_programs_iv_2011.14522.pdf` establish leading-order infinite-width feature-learning laws in coordinate random variables, including past-step-indexed variables in the explicit recursion. This is an infinite-width, fixed-discrete-step law rather than a fixed-finite-network trajectory closure.
- **Feature-learning DMFT and finite-width DMFT.** Sections 3–4, Section 7, and Table 1 of `feature_learning_dmft_2205.09653.pdf` give self-consistent two-time kernels/single-site processes and the reported (O(M^2N_t^2)) memory and (O(M^3N_t^3)) time. `finite_width_dmft_2304.03408.pdf` gives perturbative (N^{-1/2}) fluctuations and (N^{-1}) mean corrections around the width limit. Neither is an exact autonomous closure of one finite realization's complete parameter path.
- **Multilayer mean field and Neural Feature Flow.** The mean-field ODEs and Theorems 7/15 of `multilayer_mean_field_2001.11443.pdf`, and Definition 1 plus Theorems 1–4 of `neural_feature_flow_2007.01452.pdf`, supply autonomous width-limit field or measure dynamics and quantitative finite-network coupling under their own regimes. Their states have finitely many field types but infinitely many scalar degrees of freedom, and their controlled objects differ from fixed-network matrix-increment reconstruction.
- **Supplied local population theorem.** `BOOK_OBSERVABLE_CLOSURE.qmd`, Section C.4.7.9, especially theorem `thm-docs-global-nonlinear-l12101` at lines 4721–4731 and the saved-state construction at lines 4888–4964, is the closest positive local precedent. It proves increasing finite-type autonomous systems, restartable from saved current population state, with compact-time whole-circle prediction and declared-observation convergence for a specific two-hidden-layer nonlinear population flow. Its state contains two probability populations plus a finite action-contraction matrix and therefore infinitely many scalar degrees of freedom; it gives no order-to-error resource rate and does not approximate the complete parameter path of one fixed finite realization.
- **Lazy, initialization, shallow mean-field, and Mori–Zwanzig precedents** establish neighboring regimes or the general resolved/memory/unresolved decomposition. They do not meet the complete contract above.

I therefore find, **within the supplied nonexhaustive source set**, no prior theorem that simultaneously satisfies every contract item. The novel mathematical content is not online Legendre memory, autonomy by itself, hierarchy truncation by itself, or population feature dynamics. It is the paired endogenous neural response reconstruction, exact product-of-projection-errors cancellation, and feedback/continuation theorem for the complete path of one fixed finite network. V2 states this bounded conclusion correctly (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 247–252 and 1201–1223).

## Learned-increment compression versus total state and runtime

The learned-increment compression claim is real but conditional. Variant A stores `2(H-1)mnP` history factors and has learned rank at most `mP` per internal link. Variant B stores the same history factors, a shared symmetric `P x P` Gram and a clock, and has learned rank at most `m(P+1)` because of the prefix correction (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 882–914). The history factors alone beat dense moving internal matrices exactly when `2mP < n`. The complete Variant-B moving-state crossover is

\[
2(H-1)mnP+\frac{P(P+1)}2+1<(H-1)n^2.
\]

Neither theorem proves that an accuracy-sufficient `P >= P_0(T)` lies below either crossover. At fixed dimensions, increasing `P` eventually loses the moving-state count advantage (lines 931–948).

There is no total-memory or runtime compression theorem. Restartable storage retains every dense `W_0`; Variant B also retains prefix factors and Gram state. Hence total minimal closure storage is strictly larger than a minimal dense current-weight implementation (lines 950–957). A learned-correction action costs `O(nmP)`, but the complete action still includes `W_0` and costs `O(n^2+nmP)` per vector; Variant B adds `O(P^3)` factorization, `O((H-1)nmP^2)` solves, Gram dilation, and directional passes (lines 916–929). The sufficient response-clock moving-state scaling is correctly

\[
O\!\left(Hmn\varepsilon^{-1/2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\right),
\]

not merely `O(Hmn epsilon^-1/2)` (lines 959–975).

These facts **do not diminish the central representation-and-trajectory theorem**, whose object is the learned increment and whose challenge is self-consistent nonlinear feedback. They **do materially diminish any broad compression, efficient-training, or systems-breakthrough claim**. Dense gradient flow already has an elapsed-time-independent current-weight state; the closure's elapsed-time independence is relative to the literal response-history representation, not a removal of a growing-memory requirement from the standard dense solver.

## Empirical adjudication

The experiments support implementation and selected low-order endpoint-predictor fidelity. They do not validate either same-time parameter-trajectory rate.

- The shallow circle panel uses five selected stress tasks and one shared initialization; its small reported `P=7` discrepancies have the stated numerical-resolution limits (`MOMENT_RESULTS.md`, Sections “Experimental design,” “Comparison,” and “Fresh dense reference validation,” lines 47–141; `CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 1072–1105).
- The three-hidden-layer width-4096 circle panel reports `P=3` whole-circle discrepancies from about `2.82e-4` to `5.85e-2`, nonmonotone order behavior on two tasks, and substantial hidden-feature motion (`DEEP_CIRCLE_RESULTS.md`, lines 88–220; candidate lines 1107–1128).
- The MNIST panel reports held-out discrepancies `0.00324756, 0.00108443, 0.00119922` for `P=1,2,3`, so `P=3` is slightly worse than `P=2`. Its total storage and runtime are worse than dense: 158.78127/165.03127/171.28128 MiB versus 152.53125 MiB and 139.89330/149.92219/152.26930 seconds versus 102.97555 seconds (`MNIST100_RESULTS.md`, Sections “Prediction fidelity” and “Size, memory and runtime,” lines 35–90; candidate lines 1130–1160).
- The response-clock panels include a step-sensitive GELU depth-three case, Gram condition numbers up to about 330.9, response-clock runtime 1.79–3.99 times ordinary-clock runtime in the matched rows, and failures/nonuniform behavior in the 82-case sweep (`CANONICAL_FLOW.md`, Sections “Completed response-clock comparison,” “P4/P5 extension,” and “Broad new-clock sweep,” lines 233–428; candidate lines 1049–1070).
- The rank-matched factor control supports the stated 29 valid comparisons, but `A(0)=0` and `B_ij(0) ~ N(0,1/r)` match the dense initial middle-matrix velocity only in expectation, not for either realized seed. V2 now discloses this and does not generalize beyond the chosen Euclidean factor flow, seeds, metric, or compute budget (`FACTOR_CONTROL_RESULTS.md`, lines 15–97; candidate lines 1018–1033).

The panels mostly compare separately stopped matched-loss predictors, generally with one initialization. They do not measure same-time complete-parameter errors, fit a `P^-1` or `P^-2` slope, establish robustness across initializations, certify continuous-flow error in the sensitive case, or establish practical superiority. V2 says all of this plainly (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 1035–1070). The empirical limitations block an empirical-breakthrough claim but do not weaken a theorem proved analytically.

## Horizon and width limitations

“Global on compact horizons” is used correctly: for each fixed finite `T`, the complete path is controlled on all of `[0,T]`, while the admissible order and constants may depend on `T` (`CANDIDATE_V2.tex`, lines 96–106). Neither theorem supplies one order valid for all `t >= 0`, a `T`-uniform constant, or an asymptotic stability theorem.

The results also keep `n,m,H` fixed as `P -> infinity`. Constants and `P_0` may depend on width, depth, sample count, data, initialization, and horizon. The diagonal construction in lines 986–1003 is valid only after importing an independent dense width-limit theorem and choosing `P` after `n`; it proves neither a fixed-`P` width-uniform closure nor commuting limits. Retention of `W_0` leaves width-uniform operator compression open.

These are genuine limitations of generality. They do not undermine correctness inside the stated contract. They do prevent the result from being presented as an all-time theory, a population-limit closure, or a width-scalable training algorithm.
