## The present breakthrough case and the remaining disagreement

The central construction gives rich Gaussian neural training an initialized state that can advance and restart without retaining its entire training history. It preserves the correlations produced by reuse of the initialized middle operator in both directions. Increasing the retained initialized dictionary converges to the specified nonlinear flow, and further numerical limits give finite algorithms. This is a real approximation capability, not just an exact but unclosed hierarchy.

There are two distinctions essential to understanding that statement. At fixed dictionary order, the population state still contains two probability laws; quadrature is needed to obtain finitely many scalars. And the proved complete sequence includes an exhaustive initialized-word tail, whereas the small polynomial core used in experiments has no separate general density theorem. The present result gives qualitative convergence in its stated domains, not an effective accuracy-to-resource selector.

Those domains matter: actual finite-network identification for bounded-label circle laws is proved on the short horizon **T=1/200**, while the executable numerical theorem separately specifies its represented correlated-arc family. The **T40** extension uses a fixed tower-small neighborhood of the two orthogonal axes. The arbitrary-dimensional first-order experiments do not themselves establish a trained-network convergence theorem. None of these scopes is substituted for another in the present B argument.

The advocate regards **constructive current-state realizability itself** as an obstacle-removing contribution for feature-learning theory/computation. H01 shows why a qualitative approximation result can be a breakthrough without rates; H07 shows the value of realizing an existing limit; H19 supports reusable Gaussian observation structure. The critic accepts the force of this interpretation and explicitly widens the old H judgment to H–B. Their H center reflects the remaining consequence question: does the established representation theorem change access to rich training as substantially as those anchors, or is it nearer the substantial controlled realizations in H10/H18/H21/H23?

That is a genuine significance crux. It is not a disagreement about whether the approximation theorem exists, and the critic does not impose a universal rate, benchmark or long-time requirement. Both recognize that H20 already supplies the target's rich parameterization family, and that related rich dynamics/computation already exist. Neither credits the work with the first rich regime, the first computable rich limit, or the first low-rank training method. Familiar algebraic ingredients also do not establish redundancy; H06/H07 caution against that opposite shortcut.

## The independent routes that qualify

All routes preserve the two-hidden-tanh model, Gaussian initialization, unhalved square loss and physical mobilities of the assessed work, except that R4c explicitly permits every fixed input dimension. The common **C4** class consists of equally weighted laws on two, three or four distinct circle directions, labels ±1 with both signs, pairwise geodesic separations in $[\pi/12,11\pi/12]$, and every common rotation. The bounds exclude duplicate and antipodal label conflicts while retaining a continuous family of correlated interactions.

Here **B** means the frozen breakthrough/groundbreaking category, **H** highly significant, and **S** significant. These are sufficient packages, not compulsory steps or an additive checklist. Every theorem must identify the actual target flow; a different easier model or an unknown-trajectory oracle does not qualify.

R1a, the corresponding singular clock for an isolated opposite-label pair, remains **B/H for the advocate and H–B/H for the critic**. R1b adds the interaction mechanism that resolves that crux. An exact leading constant is welcome but unnecessary: the frozen package permits explicit uniform two-sided constant factors with the correct powers and logarithms. The own initial-NTK baseline is already explicit; replaying the rich flow until the event occurs is not a forecast. H18/H16 are the decisive historical comparisons.

R2 earns credit through its omitted-state consequence, not the name “energy.” It does not require finite-particle all-time numerics or endpoint selection. Fixed order still contains laws. R3 requires actual loss convergence, not merely globality of a stationary or convergent point. It does not require a rate, solver certificate or unique off-training function. These distinctions follow H14/H15/H21 and avoid bundling unrelated accomplishments.

R4 asks what training selects among possible functions. Oddness, arbitrarily high representable odd harmonics and a fitted tanh-shaped curve do not answer it. The static selection principle cannot contain the unknown trained path or define the endpoint by integrating that path. L2 convergence plus training values is enough; uniform convergence to a possibly discontinuous endpoint would be an unnecessary extra gate. H16/H17 support the significance of a restricted algorithmic selection law. The broad R4b disagreement concerns the principle's transfer beyond circle geometry; R4c resolves it through a reusable function-bias mechanism, not dimension counting.

R5 is stronger than a bound involving the unknown exact-trajectory defect or a comparison between successive approximations. It certifies the actual rich limit and its specified paired hidden laws, not only the reduced solver's own ODE. Polynomial accuracy cost is required because it is this route's promised effective-access capability, not because all breakthroughs require rates. H03/H06/H07/H21 are the relevant comparisons.

R6 is not a relabeled constant-factor speedup. Its exact family has angles $(0,\pi/6,\pi/3,\pi/2)$, equal masses, and online label choices $(+,-,+,-)$, $(+,+,-,-)$ or $(+,-,-,+)$ for each episode. It requires uniform prediction and current/initial-current Gram accuracy on a fixed 256-direction panel for every finite label sequence. Storage counts bits; total scientific bit work includes every integration step and numerical precision. The explicit $O(\log K)$ episode counter and $O(K\log K)$ bookkeeping are separately allowed. A complete proved algorithm suffices; empirical comparisons validate an implementation rather than impose another grade gate. H06/H07/H23/H24 calibrate the new computational capability. The old three-MNIST-pair matched-fidelity speed/memory package remains H/H.

R7 deliberately carries no advance B promise. A general information classification might merit more, but it needs a precise regular action-query model, a dynamic lower bound and a matching useful repair. “Use a complete reducing observable space” restates existing structure, and “append the full-word tail” repeats existing density. A single useful correction can be H without becoming a framework, as H25 illustrates.

## What is already known and therefore excluded from these upgrades

The frozen canonical sources contain more than the particular closure contribution being graded. We checked them to avoid proposing an existing result as a future breakthrough, not to expand the present grade.

- The orthogonal opposite-label tanh pair already has a feature clock, global fitting, exponential loss decay and a whole-circle endpoint defined by stopping its feature ODE. R1/R3/R4 must supply their new correlated-family consequences.
- Fixed-order closure already has exact dissipation, finite-time global existence and restart. R2 requires all-time **target approximation**, and R6 requires uniform finite-information fidelity under repeated forcing.
- C.4.9 already derives a nonlinear constrained current-state selection flow for a finite added-atom episode, with a justified inverse-contamination slow clock. R4 requires a path-independent endpoint principle; R6 requires arbitrary repeated episodes.
- C.4.10 already learns an independently specified Fourier target family through finite-mode contraction toward a stated floor, with a class-derived stop and sampling/noise control. R3's arbitrary-accuracy fitting and R4's selected endpoint are additional consequences.
- C.5 already proves a strict local test-risk advantage at matched training loss for its fixed three-point cosine teacher. A new result merely saying that features can improve this comparison would not settle any larger package.

The nearby public literature was also matched at statement level. Chizat–Bach's homogeneous mean-field max-margin identification assumes the relevant limiting objects converge; Lyu–Li's homogeneous exponential-loss results identify limiting-direction stationarity. Neither is the asserted unique tanh/squared-loss endpoint. Chen–Yang–Zhao–Gu's inspected feature-independence theorem and finite-stop corollary do not prove actual asymptotic fitting for the present initialization. These are scope distinctions, not penalties for limitations. See [Chizat–Bach](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/chizat20a/chizat20a.pdf), [Lyu–Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05890), and [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09565).

Both assessors endorse R3 as the tentative closest consensus specialist-B route, with R1b a plausible competing front-runner. They do **not** claim confidence in the relative order. R3 avoids singular-geometry asymptotics, endpoint selection and an algorithmic complexity polynomial; that reduces its number of additional obligations but does not make global rich-flow control a small lemma. R1b is more sharply diagnostic, but its required acceleration or obstruction relative to the kernel is itself an unestablished scientific hypothesis. If that separation fails, a correct clock theorem could remain valuable while failing this particular frozen B package.

R5 should not be ranked easiest simply because code exists. The present error quantity is defined along compact sets of the unknown actual trajectory. Strong convergence and enumeration of a dense dictionary give no rate, no computable stopping rule and no polynomial cost. Accounting for the full initialized-word tail, quadrature, conditioning and precision is a substantive theoretical gap. The package is finite-horizon, but finite horizon alone does not make certification inexpensive or automatic.

R2 requires replacing compact finite-time control by something uniform in time. The reference's bounded homogeneous propagator and horizon-independent source control under small integrated perturbation are relevant precedents, but neither proves the uniform nonlinear C4 truncation bound. R4a requires an actual selected-function mechanism rather than a more accurate fit to snapshots. ## Cheaper useful work and logical dependencies

The nearest work to existing implementation is controlled finite-horizon fidelity: independently vary network width, population quadrature, initializer quadrature, closure order, time step and precision; distinguish output accuracy from hidden-observable accuracy. The old M1 already pins a finite-law comparison and discrimination margins. Its implementation infrastructure exists, but success is not assumed and no new runs were authorized here. Completing it is valuable H-level evidence, not an automatic B claim.

A second useful preliminary is the **obstruction branch of R7**: determine whether an omitted initialized component leads to a reachable positive-time error for the polynomial core. Merely stating non-density of an unrelated static space does not suffice. This is a bounded diagnostic question; neither assessor asserts that the theorem is cheap, and core-only convergence through all of T40 is a larger obligation. A rigorous finite-time defect estimate or a smaller clock law can likewise be worthwhile without reaching the consensus B package.

The routes relate logically without becoming a compulsory research sequence:

| Relationship | What does and does not follow |
|---|---|
| R4c → R4b → R4a | The broader selected-function classes include the smaller ones; they need not be proved in that order. |
| R4a → fitting on C4 | Its selected zero-loss limit includes R3's fitting consequence, but R3 alone does not select the off-training function. |
| R2 → finite-horizon population approximation | It does not imply R5's finite algorithm, paired-law certificate or polynomial cost. |
| R5 and R6 | R5 covers whole-circle paired laws at T40; R6 covers a fixed panel's predictions/Grams for arbitrary repeated forcing. Neither qualification silently contains the other. |
| R1b | A clock law neither determines the endpoint function nor requires a certified closure solver. |
| R7 | A core-only failure could motivate repair but would not refute the full compatible hierarchy or decide other routes. |

The limits must remain separate:

```text
finite Gaussian network at fixed T  -- width n → ∞ --> actual rich flow at T
finite initialized computation      -- justified numerical refinements,
                                      then order N → ∞ --> same flow at T

R1: define width-first clock, then take the specified δ → 0 limit.
R3/R4: width first at each finite time, then t → ∞.
R2: prove the approximation error bound uniformly over all t before N → ∞.
R5: replace qualitative nested limits only with a proved accuracy schedule.
R6: identify every fixed finite episode sequence, then prove uniform finite-bit
    accuracy/storage/work over all episode counts and allowed sequences.
```

No equality such as P=n identifies quadrature error with network-width error. No saved time grid certifies a continuous-time supremum. A counterexample to a required property fails the package; an unclosed proof or unresolved numerical control is inconclusive. These distinctions apply equally to historical anchors and the target work.
