## The strongest case I can defend

The central achievement is a constructive bridge between a rich Gaussian network-training limit and a hierarchy of autonomous, initialized approximations. It retains the transpose-induced response correlations, lets both hidden representations change, and provides actual-network identification and convergence of specified joint observations. A finite computation evolves from its own state and can restart. That is stronger than an exact but unclosed hierarchy, a formal description of the limit, or an unexplained low-rank architectural guess.

The bridge is not vacuous because the initialized action is generally not Hilbert–Schmidt: truncating a matrix in a strong weight norm is not the claim being made. The approximation uses strong action and adjoint convergence on compact trajectory families, while the learned increment is Hilbert–Schmidt. The initialized observable spaces are reducing, contain the trajectory, and admit dense bounded-word features. The exact trajectory enters the proof's error quantities, not the algorithm's inputs. This distinction answers the apparent oracle objection without converting qualitative convergence into a computable certificate.

The most favorable historical comparisons are H07, H10, H18, H19, H21 and H23. H07 demonstrates that a computational realization of an existing limit can be important. H10 demonstrates that strengthening a known training description is substantial without being credited as the discovery of the original object. H18 permits a special-family dynamics result to be significant. H19 supports a reusable Gaussian-observation calculus without a universal finite-size error rate. H21 separates a hierarchy's existence from approximation control. H23 shows that nonlinear limiting computation is already valuable even when the state or derivation is formal/history-based. The target brings a different combination of autonomy, initialization and convergence to that neighborhood of contributions.

H01 matters as a check against an unfair criterion: the absence of an order-versus-error rate is not enough to reject major qualitative value. H04/H05 also show that a theorem need not solve training or ordinary tasks to remove a real obstruction. Conversely, H06 warns against granting broad computational impact merely from an attractive formula: its practical access was reusable. Here one image task and unresolved higher-order numerical controls support feasibility, not the same broad access claim.

The best argument for present B is that compressing rich training into a fixed-state initialized system changes what can be evolved without accumulating history, while preserving more than predictions. I considered that argument seriously.
