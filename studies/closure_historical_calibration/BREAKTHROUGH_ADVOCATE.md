A clock law can explain when training happens; an energy can control what truncation discards; geometry can force fitting; a selection principle can predict the function; a certificate can make the rich flow effectively accessible; and a resource theorem can allow continued computation without accumulating history. None requires all the others.

## Strong cases and concessions made in direct debate

For **R1**, I initially favored an exact leading constant. The critic correctly objected that this was stronger than needed to establish the significance of a learning-time law. The final qualification permits correct power/logarithmic scaling with explicit uniform two-sided constants, while retaining a parametric departure from the own initial-NTK clock and a genuine interacting mechanism. A fitted event time is not prediction. My isolated-pair B judgment remains stronger than the critic's H–B; the four-point extension resolves that particular crux without asking for all data or dimensions.

For **R2**, the useful content of an energy is what it controls. The original loss already decreases, and fixed-order closure already exists through every finite time. We agreed on a genuinely different consequence: a computable order-error bound uniform over all times on C4, for predictions and both layers' current/current and initial/current Grams. I initially bundled finite-particle/precision resources independent of horizon. The critic correctly removed that extra numerical demand. The mathematical uniform-in-time population approximation is already a specialist breakthrough if proved by a new tail-feedback mechanism. A finite list of scalars is not falsely attributed to fixed population order.

For **R3**, the critic initially suggested accompanying global fitting with closure approximation through every finite horizon. I challenged that addition: a rich-flow mechanism forcing loss to zero can be a major advance in its own right. The final package does not require a solver certificate, rate, selected endpoint or uniform-in-time finite-width limit. It does require actual fitting from the specified initialization, rather than assuming convergence or a positive Gram gap. H14/H15 support this distinction. The newly inspected Chen et al. feature-independence theorem is a substantive predecessor, but its finite-stop corollary is not actual asymptotic convergence, and its readout initialization differs.

For **R4**, representability must not be confused with what training selects. Oddness, unbounded possible odd harmonics and fitted tanh-shaped curves do not identify a data-only function. We require a unique selected prediction and actual convergence to it. The critic's proposal to use L2 plus training-point convergence rather than uniform endpoint convergence was a valuable minimality correction: a discontinuous selected limit can still be a major discovery. I assign broad B to a principle for all finite circle data; the critic retains H–B. We agree on broad B for the same mechanism on all finite non-antipodal sphere datasets at each fixed dimension, without adding rates, depth generality or a benchmark. The broader consequence is transferable implicit bias, not dimension counting.

For **R5**, a posteriori language is insufficient if the certificate depends on the unknown exact flow or only compares two approximate runs. The explicit polynomial accuracy-resource guarantee and termination make the capability objectively settleable. Both paired hidden laws and predictions are included because the target is a rich observable flow, not merely a scalar fit. There is no requirement for a particular practical hardware budget or ε=.01 demonstration. This route makes rates relevant to its own claimed computational consequence; it does not retroactively require rates from present T2+T3 or from every historical breakthrough.

For **R6**, I accept the critic's concrete switching-task family as a separate computation route. It requires scientific working storage counted in bits independent of the number of episodes, and total scientific bit operations linear in episodes at fixed accuracy, including all integration steps and precision. A logarithmic episode counter is accounted separately. It is not enough to use fixed-dimensional exact-real state, hide precision growth or replay the input history. A complete algorithmic proof suffices; benchmarks would validate an implementation rather than set an arbitrary significance quota. The old three-pair MNIST constant-factor savings remain H/H.

For **R7**, I do not force a B label. The distinction between the complete initialized-word dictionary and the experimental core deserves a theorem, including the possibility that a positive-time dynamical error remains even as polynomial degree increases. A static non-density observation without dynamical consequence is weaker. A general sharp information classification could matter much more, but we have not specified a non-tautological bounded B package; it remains uncontracted. No negative result has been proved in this assessment, and no counterexample is assumed.

I retain the exact historical-limit scrutiny in these comparisons. I provisionally rank **R3 as the closest consensus specialist-B package**, with **R1b a competing front-runner**. It does not need singular asymptotics, an endpoint principle or a resource polynomial. The existing orthogonal global construction, local correlated theory and data-response analysis provide concrete structure. But global source/reused-adjoint control and noncollapse that actually forces residual decay remain a missing mechanism. Calling it “one bridge” must not suggest that it is a routine continuation argument.

R1b has an especially clear scientific discriminator: the own full initial-NTK clock is explicit, and the output is a geometry-derived power/log law. Its family is bounded and the desired nonlinear interaction is specific. Against that favorable case, the existing orthogonal feature clock does not solve the near-collision singular limit, the κ-uniform interacting problem, or the required parametric departure. The departure might not hold. No existing sparse-angle plot establishes the asymptotic claim. This makes R1b an attractive competing route without a basis for confidently calling it easier than R3.

I do **not** reliably order R2, R4a and R5. R2 needs a replacement for compact-finite-trajectory approximation that stays effective for all times. The orthogonal reference's uniform homogeneous propagator and the small-control-mass source theorem are useful bounded precedents, not the uniform nonlinear C4 error. R4a needs a genuinely new static selection mechanism and actual endpoint convergence; a fitted shape or the reference's stopped feature ODE cannot supply it. R5 has an existing solver and a convergence architecture, but no quantitative full-word approximation complexity, solver-accessible defect or uniform polynomial numerical resource bound. Existing code makes a claim testable; it does not discharge the theorem's missing information.

R6 is furthest from current evidence. Its arbitrary repeated forcing demands control of accumulated error and retained information indefinitely, counted in bits. Exact current-state restart with two laws does not imply stable finite-information continuation. No permitted source campaign tests the proposed switching family. This does not prove impossibility, nor lower its conditional B value; it identifies an unestablished mechanism of compression/stability or forgetting.

I separately regard R4b as the least expanded broad-B selection route in my own judgment, while the critic retains broad H–B there. Extending a selected-function principle across dimensions is not the first missing step: such a principle and actual endpoint convergence are missing already for the smaller class.
