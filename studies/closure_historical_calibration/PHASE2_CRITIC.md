## Construction, without hiding the remaining state

The dense model is a bias-free network with two tanh hidden layers:

\[
u=x/\sqrt d,\qquad h_1=\tanh(W_1u),\qquad h_2=\tanh(Ah_1),\qquad f_n=c^Th_2/n.
\]

Independent stored Gaussian entry variances are \((1,1/n,1/n^2)\). The loss is the probability-weighted, unhalved squared residual. Gradient-flow mobilities for \((W_1,A,c)\) are \((n,1,n)\); numerical Heun steps approximate this flow. The finite initial readout is random, although the limiting population readout is zero.

The closure constructs fixed initialized random features \(b_1,b_2\), using bounded words involving both the initial Gaussian action and its actual adjoint. It retains two joint populations, containing the fixed marks and evolving first-layer weights/readout, plus an evolving coefficient matrix \(M\). For a unit input \(u\), write

\[
 a(u)=\mathbb E_1[b_1\tanh(w\cdot u)],\quad
 h_2(u)=\tanh(b_2^TMa(u)),\quad
 d(u)=\mathbb E_2[b_2c(1-h_2(u)^2)],\quad
 q(u)=b_1^TM^Td(u),\quad f(u)=\mathbb E_2[ch_2(u)].
\]

With \(r=f-y\), the evolution is

\[
\dot w=-2\int r(1-h_1^2)q\,u\,d\mu,\qquad
\dot c=-2\int rh_2\,d\mu,\qquad
\dot M=-2\int rd\,a^T\,d\mu.
\]

Initialization is \(w=g,c=0,M=D\), where \(D=\mathbb E_2[b_2(A_0b_1)^T]\). The fixed marks and their correlations matter. Replacing them by independent coordinates or dropping the reverse response changes the construction.

Thus the solver summarizes the initial random network once, then evolves current variables. It need not query the limiting Gaussian operator or reconstruct earlier training times during evolution. But “no weights” and “a finite-dimensional population state” would both misdescribe it. At finite quadrature, with feature matrices \(B_1,B_2\), its effective middle map is exactly

\[
A_{\rm eff}=B_2MB_1^T\operatorname{diag}(p_1).
\]

It is a factorized network with nonlinear evolving \(w,c\) and a learned core. Before population quadrature, the two retained laws remain infinite-dimensional objects. Ridge normalization produces contractions rather than exact orthogonal projections; at fixed nodes the induced middle-map flow is generally a two-sided filtered update, not identical to Euclidean projected dense-network gradient flow.

These equations are in the frozen `docs/global_nonlinear.md`, H3.N1–H3.N6, and were compared directly with `code/pde/observable_solver.py:105–255`, `P1_ENGINE.py:1–235` and `NETWORK_ENGINE.py:1–90` in the authorized frozen packet. Inspection establishes a formula match, not a fresh execution check.

The T2 proof mechanism deserves precise credit. The finite-rank contractions \(Q_{\ell,N}\) converge strongly to the identity; \(Q_{2,N}A_0Q_{1,N}\) approximates the initialized action and adjoint strongly on the relevant compact families. It need not approximate the generally non-Hilbert–Schmidt \(A_0\) in operator norm. The learned perturbation is Hilbert–Schmidt. Target-dependent projection errors in H2.10 are proof quantities, not hidden runtime oracle inputs. The H2.15–H2.16 stability estimate converts those errors to trajectory control via a tail/Osgood argument. The lack of a computable order-versus-error bound limits certification, not the existence of the approximation capability.

The exact limits must remain separate:

1. Dense-network width \(n\to\infty\) identifies the actual population training law under the stated model/law/horizon assumptions.
2. Closure order \(N\to\infty\) approximates that population target. Fixed \(N\) still retains full mark populations, and finite order is generally approximate.
3. The numerical theorem removes arithmetic, time integration, input quadrature, population quadrature \(P\), initializer quadrature \(Q\), and source-regularization errors **in the stated order**, before the outer order limit. In execution order of iterated limits: precision, time steps, input quadrature, P, Q, source regularization, then N.

There is no theorem here that setting \(P=n\), increasing all parameters together, or increasing N alone follows the same limit. In the MNIST implementation even nominal P contains only P/2 independent base draws per population because of antithetic pairs. Finite data/time panels do not certify continuous suprema. These distinctions are analogous to keeping H08's separate width limits and H21's order/time/width conditions distinct.

## Empirical evidence: surviving capability after controls

The following is evidence from reports, not rerun data. Greater reporting detail is not counted against the target.

The strongest circle pattern is that N1→N3 improves actual-network function agreement in all nine sparse geometries across the paired and multi-point campaigns. In the four-point 30° case, all available order-improvement checks pass and relative errors improve approximately 7.46%→3.89%→2.08%. This is meaningful evidence for a useful approximation hierarchy. N3→N5 improves only three of the six multi-point cases, however; other geometries have missing/failed quadrature controls. No theorem or experiment establishes monotone finite-order accuracy. N denotes initialized mark order, not an angular Fourier cutoff.

The T40 axes/small-arc campaign reports N5 output RMS around .007 and favorable Gram-order trends, but its complete agreement criterion is inconclusive: N3 quadrature changes of roughly .00545 in prediction and .00712 in loss are not a bound on N5. The ±30° arc campaign improves one hidden layer while the first-layer trend can worsen. Long continuation to T640 fails 314/416 of the original strict settling conditions; N5 layer-two Gram discrepancy can exceed N1. These observations block an empirical convergence/endpoint claim, not the short-time theorem.

Shifted XOR gives an actual feature-learning contrast: the dense network's final loss is about .00273 versus .38998 for the initial readout-only control at equal time. Yet N5 whole-trajectory loss discrepancy reaches .4287 and the quadrature controls remain unresolved. The readout-only baseline is not the full NTK. Later full-NTK results must be kept separate. In the quadrant example an N5 output RMSE around .0457 coexists with layer-two relative Gram error around .649, close to the frozen-feature comparator's .679; output agreement alone does not establish hidden-law fidelity. The matched-loss initial-NTK computation uses vastly different physical times and gives extrapolation/function differences, not a held-out teacher-risk theorem.

The endpoint campaign contains a stable finite-time separation, but none of eighteen trajectories meets the frozen plateau criterion. Last complete common time is 1000; N5 quadrature and network half-step checks still miss their thresholds. The incomplete time-1100 run is not evidence. No endpoint selection, asymptotic hierarchy failure, or asymptotic success can be inferred.

For original-input MNIST 3 versus 5, width/nominal P4096, p=1, and three seeds, relative RMS versus the dense-network mean is 5.32–5.48% at common T600 and 4.45–4.61% at approximately matched own training loss. These are independently trained low-state predictions, not a fit to dense outputs. Worst individual errors and sign disagreements do not uniformly improve under matching. Doubling width and P together reduces measured discrepancies but confounds finite-width and quadrature effects.

Train-only centered PCA retains 240 coordinates and 98.00167% of centered variance, but only 53.05% of original uncentered energy; there is no renormalization. At a common attainable loss the PCA closure differs from its own PCA dense reference by 4.09–4.62%, while comparison to the original-input reference is 10.41–11.23%. Thus PCA's change of representation must not be attributed to closure accuracy. Crossed same-GPU timing at fixed T100 reports 3.75–3.96-fold integration speed and 80.20% less peak allocated training memory against the PCA dense network. This is a useful measured capability. It is not time-to-matched-loss, a certified asymptotic complexity improvement, or superiority to other reduced methods. Half-step changes are smaller than primary gaps; full-horizon float64 controls, higher-order MNIST, and other digit pairs/tasks are absent.

H22 shows why an output-matching or architecture result cannot inherit a task-general learnability claim. H13/H21 show why finite-order errors and unresolved controls must be attached to their own claims rather than used to reject a different limiting theorem.
