## DMFT: a direct, symmetric comparison

H23 is **Bordelon–Pehlevan (2022), Self-Consistent Dynamical Field Theory of Kernel Evolution in Wide Neural Networks**. It is not the whole DMFT tradition, all subsequent neural DMFT, or every use of dynamical mean-field methods.

The frozen table says **H / S–H**: the slash separates *specialist* from *broader deep-learning-theory* significance; the dash is a range within the broader audience. It does not mean one combined H-to-S score. The frozen residual discussion says both assessors center H23 at H specialist, while the advocate already allowed a speculative B upper interpretation for computational unification. The comparison must not pit that historical center against only the target's B endpoint.

| Question | H23's surviving contribution | T2+T3's surviving contribution |
|---|---|---|
| What access is added? | Self-consistent two-time kernels and response quantities, a representation-level stochastic process and alternating nonlinear sampling computation, with tractable linear-network reductions. | An initialized autonomous approximation sequence preserving both Gaussian action orientations and designated observations, with convergence and an executable ordered numerical realization. |
| What was already known? | The construction recovers an earlier Yang–Hu feature-learning process; not all underlying rich dynamics are new. | Rich limiting dynamics and projection/low-rank motifs already existed; the target does not discover the rich regime or first computable feature-learning limit. |
| What is not supplied? | A general certified numerical error law or finite autonomous state independent of accumulated history. | A proved general accuracy-to-resource selector, matched computational dominance, or a new predictive law for what rich training learns. Fixed population order is not finite scalar storage. |
| What does this imply for significance? | New self-consistent and computational access remains substantial even when the underlying process existed. | New autonomous and convergent access remains substantial even when the underlying process existed. Distinctness alone does not establish a higher significance category. |

My decisive challenge was that “the dynamics already existed” cannot discount H23 while the target gets a preferred B merely for changing the representation of a known flow. Both must be assessed by the additional consequence. Conversely, familiar projection algebra does not eliminate the target's initialized convergence bridge. Both positive and critical tests are symmetric.

The target has a rigorous approximation capability that the inspected H23 paper does not supply. That is a precise theorem-level distinction, not a proof that the target is more significant. H23's representation and computational tools have scientific content apart from formal-versus-proved status. Evidence confidence and significance must remain separate. The different initialization/scaling and retained information also prevent treating these as two interchangeable solvers on which a dominance test has already been performed.

Autonomy at a chosen order and numerical resolution is also different from horizon-independent resources at fixed target accuracy: the order and quadrature needed to maintain accuracy may grow with the horizon. This is a specific boundary on the claimed computational consequence, not a universal demand for an approximation rate. A qualitative autonomy theorem can still support a B interpretation when its surviving consequence is judged sufficiently important.

**My direct conclusion is comparable/overlapping, not strictly higher or lower.** Both specialist centers are H; the broader target H overlaps H23's S–H at H. The advocate now agrees with that preferred comparison. An upper-B interpretation remains possible for the target, and the historical H23 record already exposes the advocate's analogous boundary. No historical case was rerated to produce this agreement. No claim about superiority to DMFT as an entire tradition follows.
