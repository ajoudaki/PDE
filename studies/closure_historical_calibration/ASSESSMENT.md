## What the construction achieves

A wide two-hidden-layer network contains a large matrix connecting its hidden layers. During training, the effect of that matrix and its transpose repeatedly feeds back into the changing neurons. Keeping only a few averages can lose the correlations responsible for that feedback; keeping every earlier training step can make a limiting description expensive.

This program constructs a fixed dictionary of correlated random features from the network's initialization. It then evolves the first-layer neurons, the output coefficients, and a smaller matrix describing the middle connection in that dictionary. Every update is computed from the current state. The finite computation can be saved and restarted without the training history. Enlarging the dictionary recovers the actual wide-network gradient-flow limit on the theorem's specified domains, while finite quadrature turns the population equations into executable computations.

The principal advance is this **particular initialization-aware autonomous approximation with a convergence guarantee**. It combines a sufficiently informative observable description, the correlations produced by reusing a Gaussian matrix and its transpose, and a stable approximation of the changing nonlinear flow. Low-rank matrices, projected coefficient training, feature-learning limits and autonomous population descriptions all have predecessors. Their existence does not supply this combined guarantee, but rules out claiming those ingredients themselves as new.

“Finite” needs care. At fixed dictionary order the mathematical state still contains two probability distributions. A finite scalar implementation also chooses finitely many population nodes. Its memory is independent of elapsed step count at fixed resolution, not independent of dictionary size, input dimension, particles, arithmetic precision or requested accuracy.

## Model and equation crosswalk

Write $u=x/\sqrt d$. The assessed network is bias-free, with two tanh hidden layers and scalar output:

\[
h^1=\tanh(W_1u),\qquad h^2=\tanh(W_2h^1),\qquad
f_n=c^Th^2/n.
\]

Stored Gaussian entry variances are $1,1/n,1/n^2$. The loss is the probability-weighted **unhalved** squared residual; physical gradient-flow mobilities of $(W_1,W_2,c)$ are $(n,1,n)$. The finite network retains its random initial readout. Its limiting readout is zero. Changing the readout variance or using ordinary unscaled gradient descent changes the comparison.

On the canonical population carrier, the middle action is $A=A_0+K$, with $A_0$ a bounded initialized Gaussian action and $K$ a Hilbert–Schmidt learned increment. The reverse action is the actual adjoint. If $r=f-y$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_1&=\tanh(w\cdot u),&h_2&=\tanh(Ah_1),\\
\delta_2&=c(1-h_2^2),&q&=A^*\delta_2,\\
\dot w&=-2\int r(1-h_1^2)q\,u\,d\mu,&
\dot K&=-2\int r\,\delta_2\otimes h_1\,d\mu,\\
\dot c&=-2\int r h_2\,d\mu.&
\end{aligned}
\]

For initialized raw features $\psi_{\ell,N}$, use $G_\ell=E\psi_\ell\psi_\ell^T$, $L_\ell L_\ell^T=G_\ell+\eta_N I$, and $b_\ell=L_\ell^{-1}\psi_\ell$. Preserve the full joint lower marks $(b_1,g)$, upper marks $b_2$, and their coupling to the moving variables. Set

\[
C=E_2[\psi_2(A_0\psi_1)^T],\qquad D=L_2^{-1}CL_1^{-T},
\qquad (w_0,c_0,M_0)=(g,0,D).
\]

The finite-order equations use

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(u)&=E_1[b_1\tanh(w\cdot u)],&h_2&=\tanh(b_2^TMa),\\
d(u)&=E_2[b_2c(1-h_2^2)],&q&=b_1^TM^Td,\\
f&=E_2[ch_2],&\dot M&=-2\int r\,d a^T\,d\mu,
\end{aligned}
\]

with the corresponding row/readout equations above. Initialization fixes the dictionary, not the learned hidden activations. $M$ evolves as a full matrix. At population quadrature, with rows of $B_\ell$ equal to $b_\ell^T$, the effective middle matrix is exactly

\[
W_{\mathrm{eff}}=B_2 M B_1^T\operatorname{diag}(p_1).
\]

Thus there is a familiar fixed-factor matrix parameterization inside the solver. Its induced metric, correlated dictionary and target limit matter: it is not ordinary gradient flow of an arbitrary independently initialized low-rank network. Ridge normalization yields positive filters $Q_{\ell,N}=U_{\ell,N}U_{\ell,N}^*$, generally not orthogonal projections. The convergence argument uses $Q_{\ell,N}\to I$ strongly, both directions of $Q_2A_0Q_1$, and convergence of the filtered learned rank sources on compact sets visited by the exact trajectory. It does not assert operator-norm approximation of $A_0$.

### The limits are different claims

Here $n$ is network width; $N$ is closure order (called $p$ in the first-order experiments); $P$ is population quadrature; $Q$ is initializer quadrature; $m$ is input quadrature; $J$ is time-step count; $p_{\rm arith}$ is arithmetic precision; and $\varepsilon$ regularizes source covariances. These cannot be identified with each other.

```text
actual finite network, width n
             │ n → ∞, fixed law/model/horizon and fixed observations
             ▼
canonical nonlinear population gradient flow  ◄── N → ∞ ── exact order-N population closure
                                                                  ▲
                                                                  │ inner numerical limits
                                                        finite numerical closure
```

The numerical arrow means, in the stated successive order,

\[
p_{\rm arith}\to\infty,\ J\to\infty,\ m\to\infty,\ P\to\infty,
\ Q\to\infty,\ \varepsilon\downarrow0,\ \text{then }N\to\infty.
\]

The outer parameters are held fixed while each inner limit is taken. The canonical model and applicable law/horizon stay fixed throughout. The width limit is a separate identification of the same target, not a required dense-network run in the numerical initializer. Fixed-$p=1$ MNIST with $n$ and $P$ both doubled does not execute the order limit or isolate either of the first two approximations. T3's uniform-circle theorem is also stronger than the saved finite panels used in experiments; a panel does not certify that supremum.

## Why this grade follows from the frozen cases

The contribution exceeds a bounded correction such as H25: it enables autonomous approximation of an evolving operator/population system, with actual-network identification and executable consistency. Its closest present neighbors are H10, H18, H21 and H23: substantial advances in the description or computation of training dynamics with differing restrictions. It adds a convergence-controlled autonomous construction where a formal/history-based description or an exact hierarchy alone does not suffice. Those comparisons support Highly significant specialist credit even before a usable rate or broad benchmark exists.

H01 and H07 prohibit a blanket quantitative-rate gate. H04/H05 show that a scoped constructive theorem can be important without generic optimization success. H18 shows that special-family dynamics need not be dismissed as trivial. Conversely, H09/H20 receive breakthrough credit for introducing a governing object/regime/classification with broader reusable consequences. Here the rich regime, Gaussian reuse calculus and low-rank training motif preexist; the demonstrated consequence of their new assembly is narrower. That is the present argument against Breakthrough, not an accounting of how many limitations were disclosed.

H06 and H07 make the computational route to higher impact credible. The MNIST/PCA result strengthens that route, but present savings are measured at a fixed implementation/horizon and one task. The construction could still become a broad computational advance without a new architecture or an all-time theorem. It would have to establish the relevant reusable access or cost-versus-fidelity capability, rather than receive that credit from the phrase “no history” alone. Landmark status remains retrospective; its absence is no publication-time penalty.

**Evidence confidence is separate.** The inspected mathematical statements, decisive construction/proof architecture and numerical reporting are coherent enough to support a report-and-theorem assessment. This was not a complete independent proof audit or reproduction. Confidence is stronger for exactly stated construction identities and reported finite-run comparisons than for exhaustive priority, broad extrapolation, or practical high-order convergence. A newly found proof defect would change the established-claim ledger; the current grade is not a certification that every dependency has been independently reproved.

## Established, supported and prospective

Established in the supplied theory: T1–T4 in their stated domains, the distinct limit structure, and T5's initialized identities/storage. Established as reported finite computations: the specified T6/T7 measurements and their controls. Supported implications: useful low-order autonomous approximations can capture substantial nonlinear feature dynamics, and at least this image task admits meaningful memory/time savings. Speculation: broad general-dimensional convergence at training-relevant horizons, affordable accuracy selection, competitive advantage over reduced alternatives, and a law selecting rich-training endpoints.

Already enabled are reproducible finite-state restarts, independent resolution studies, higher-order circle comparisons, and first-order arbitrary-dimensional computation. A general-dimensional convergence theorem needs more than substituting $d$ into the block initializer: it needs an adequate full dictionary, source/tail control, actual-network identification and stability in that scope. A useful finite-order certificate must replace the inaccessible exact-trajectory defect in the proof with computable information or an explicit a priori bound. Endpoint selection needs a mechanism and control of the endpoint limit, not another fitted shape. These are new mathematical or computational obligations; none is inferred automatically from implementation availability.
