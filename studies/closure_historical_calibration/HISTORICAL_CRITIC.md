## Selection and labels

The advocate and critic jointly selected H01–H24, then added H25 because a corrective estimator paper supplied a useful neighboring comparison to the original random-features method. The set was selected before target access, without a target grade distribution. It emphasizes approximation, wide-network limits and dynamics. Statistical generalization, PAC-Bayes, information theory, non-neural optimization, and modern architecture theory are incompletely covered. H24 is deliberately an adjacent model-reduction comparison, not a deep-learning-theory contribution by retroactive association.

H01 and H02 are distinct: single-hidden-layer density versus broader activation/measure/derivative approximation, with derivative predecessors explicitly acknowledged. H12 and H13 share a contemporary mean-field discovery; H13's separate comparison concerns fluctuations and long-time error, not another credit for the same law of large numbers. H08 and H15 group closely related contemporaneous contributions for calibration; their hypotheses must never be blended into one fictional theorem.

Abbreviations: **S** significant; **H** highly significant; **B** breakthrough, also covering the otherwise insufficiently distinguishable word *groundbreaking*. **Below-S** means below the relevant field-level significance threshold, not worthless or incorrect. **Landmark** is a separately evidenced historical-impact qualification. Ratings below concern publication time and explicitly name the reference field. Ranges represent uncertainty and judgment differences, not numerical confidence intervals.

## Individual challenges, responses, and dispositions

### H01 — Cybenko (1989)

Primary: [Approximation by superpositions of a sigmoidal function](https://papers.baulab.info/papers/Cybenko-1989.pdf). Read introduction/predecessors, Theorems 1–3 and concluding discussion, especially pp. 305–309 and 312.

**Challenge.** Uniform density on a compact cube permits unrestricted neuron count and weights. It gives neither an approximation rate nor an algorithm or generalization bound. Contemporary Hornik–Stinchcombe–White/Funahashi work and earlier ridge-function constructions prevent a sole-inventor history. These restrictions block an efficient-learning claim, but do not weaken the actual density theorem.

**Positive case and response.** The advocate accepted the restrictions and argued that settling a genuine one-hidden-layer representational obstacle was itself a major capability. The critic agrees: a qualitative theorem can be B without rates. Publication: B in approximation and broad neural-network theory. Historical landmark is defensible as part of the foundational universal-approximation contribution, rather than a claim of unique priority. H02/H03 and the later [Leshno et al. characterization](https://pinkus.net.technion.ac.il/files/2021/02/neural.pdf) provide concrete continuing use.

### H02 — Hornik (1991)

Primary: [Approximation capabilities](https://web.njit.edu/~usman/courses/cs675_summer19/hornik-nn-1991.pdf). Web extraction failed; direct public PDF download succeeded. Read pp. 251–254, Theorems 1–4 and discussion; local extracted text has some OCR defects checked against repeated statement/prose.

**Challenge.** Removing sigmoidal restrictions, allowing finite input measures and strengthening smooth approximation does not introduce universal approximation afresh. Derivative approximation already appeared in Hornik et al. (1990). Bounded/nonconstant and smoothness conditions matter; polynomial activations show that unrestricted activation generality would be false. Missing rates have the same consequence as H01, not a larger penalty.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepted significant broad/high specialist rather than a second foundational breakthrough. The remaining capability is a substantially enlarged and unified approximation class. S broad, H specialist, with boundary uncertainty. [Leshno et al. (1993), pp. 861–862](https://pinkus.net.technion.ac.il/files/2021/02/neural.pdf) explicitly restates the 1991 theorems and generalizes one to a necessary-and-sufficient activation characterization: a concrete independent extension, not citation-count evidence.

### H03 — Barron (1993)

Primary: [Universal approximation bounds](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~brecht/cs838docs/93.Barron.Universal.pdf). Scanned PDF; OCR and reading of pp. 930–933, including Proposition 1, Theorem 1, predecessor discussion and computational caveats. No lower-bound proof audit.

**Challenge.** The dimension-independent exponent in squared error applies to a Fourier-moment class; its norm can grow exponentially with dimension. The paper explicitly says computational search is not proved polynomial. Adjustable ridge functions are compared with fixed linear spaces; it is not a separation from every other adaptive approximation scheme. Jones's earlier approximation machinery is relevant.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepted these exact restrictions. The quantitative rate and matched fixed-basis obstruction still go substantially beyond H01/H02. B specialist/H broad is defensible; missing efficient optimization limits practical training claims rather than erasing approximation significance. [Bach (2017), §§2, 2.5, 4.1](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume18/14-546/14-546.pdf) explicitly develops the resulting functional framework and approximation rates. Broad historical landmark remains less certain than specialist foundational influence.

### H04 — Telgarsky (2016)

Primary: [Benefits of depth](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/telgarsky16.pdf). Read §1, Theorems 1.1/1.2, semi-algebraic setup and proof-architecture discussion.

**Challenge.** The witness is specially constructed and highly oscillatory. The main comparison uses depth of order k cubed against depth k, not every adjacent-depth pair. It establishes representation separation, not efficient gradient learning or typical-data advantage. Boolean circuit depth hierarchies are predecessors, though their gates do not directly establish this theorem.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepted all qualifications: composition versus oscillation yields a reusable lower-bound mechanism with broad comparator classes. H broad/B specialist remains reasonable. No benchmark is required to establish the representation claim. Subsequent [Safran–Lee (2022), introduction and related work](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/safran22a/safran22a.pdf) treats this result as a motivation and a warning that expressive witnesses can be hard to optimize. That is substantive influence and a limit on explanatory reach, not refutation.

### H05 — Eldan–Shamir (2016)

Primary: [The power of depth](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v49/eldan16.pdf). Read §1, Assumptions 1/2, Theorem 1, remarks and related work.

**Challenge.** The theorem chooses a radial-like target and input measure, and compares depth two with three. It is not a generic natural-task or learnability claim. Prior Boolean/threshold separations and contemporaneous work need acknowledgement.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate emphasizes, and the critic accepts, that the shallow competitor has unrestricted real weights: the exponential obstruction is not manufactured by a small-weight restriction. A single added layer yields a genuine new representation capability for common activations. H broad/B specialist; no forced finer ranking against H04. [Safran–Lee (2022)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/safran22a/safran22a.pdf) explicitly develops optimization-based separations in response to this literature, documenting continuing use while exposing what expressivity alone leaves open.

### H06 — Rahimi–Recht (2007)

Primary: [Random features for large-scale kernel machines](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~brecht/papers/07.rah.rec.nips.pdf). Read §§1–5, Bochner construction, Claims 1/2, feature algorithms and experimental setup.

**Challenge.** Bochner's theorem and Monte Carlo approximation predate the paper. Features remain tied to specified kernels; kernel error is not automatically prediction or statistical error. Finite-dimensional storage/computation still grows with accuracy and input dimension. H25 later distinguishes the two feature estimators and corrects applicability/constants of the original uniform bound.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate argues for a computational breakthrough: explicit random features make established linear solvers available to kernel learning, with demonstrated scale benefits. The critic accepts B in computational kernel learning and favors H broad deep-learning theory; the advocate allows H–B. This difference is retained. H25 and [Sriperumbudur–Szabó (2015), §§1–3](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~szabo/publications/sriperumbudurszabo15optimal.pdf) document independent, technically substantive development. Correction of one bound does not invalidate the method.

### H07 — Williams (1997; conference 1996)

Primary: [Computing with infinite networks](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/1996/file/ae5e3ce40e0404a45ecacaaf05e5f735-Paper.pdf). Read §§1–4, GP regression equations, covariance construction and discussion.

**Challenge.** Neal's GP limit is an explicit predecessor. Analytic covariances concern selected activations/weight priors; direct Gaussian regression conditions on hyperparameters, and arbitrary hyperprior integration is not solved. Cubic sample-size linear algebra and lack of feature learning constrain scale/scope.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepted these restrictions; the new capability is usable analytic inference with infinite networks. A finite-width rate is not necessary for that exact limiting-model computation. H specialist/S–H broad. H08 explicitly builds on Williams's shallow-network GP computations, establishing concrete continuation. This source set does not independently establish universal practical dominance or a broad landmark label.

### H08 — Lee et al. / Matthews et al. (2018)

Primaries: [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.00165), [Matthews et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.11271). Read Lee §2 setup/recursion; Matthews introduction, Theorem 4/Corollary 5 and width/topology discussion.

**Challenge.** Independent contemporaneous formulations differ: Lee's §2.3 sequential induction must not substitute for Matthews's simultaneous-width theorem. Lee also advertises an alternative marginalization argument in Appendix C; that appendix was not re-audited here. Matthews assumes Gaussian independent weights, continuous linear-envelope activation, fixed depth and countable input sets with the specified weak topology. Neither is a generic trained-feature limit. Earlier Neal/Williams and compositional kernels constrain novelty claims.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts the distinction. A deep-network prior with computable recursive covariance plus a rigorous simultaneous-width justification is a substantial extension: H specialist and broad. H19 explicitly resolves architecture/dependency restrictions left by the fully connected results. No original finite-width training or general Bayesian deep-learning equivalence is inferred.

### H09 — Jacot–Gabriel–Hongler (2018)

Primary: [Neural tangent kernel](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.07572). Read §§3–5, Theorems 1/2, positive-definiteness proposition and appendix statement clarifying sequential limits.

**Challenge.** Initialization requires Lipschitz activation; training theorem imposes twice differentiability with bounded second derivative. The width limit is sequential, depth fixed, and finite horizons require bounded integrated training direction. Kernel freezing excludes rich feature evolution; a generic finite-width explanation would be unsupported.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate and critic agree that these are scope boundaries around a new function-space training object and limiting equivalence. B broad and specialist is defensible without an all-time rate. H10/H11/H15/H19/H21 supply multiple substantive refinements and alternatives rather than mere citations; this makes a historical landmark qualification defensible for the NTK framework. It remains false that the framework explains every successful network.

### H10 — Lee et al. (2019)

Primary: [Wide neural networks evolve as linear models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.06720). Read linearized model setup, Theorems 2.1/2.2 and accompanying global-convergence discussion.

**Challenge.** The all-time approximation uses the stated smoothness/conditioning setup, a positive limiting Gram gap, squared loss, and small learning rate. Increasing width with fixed data/depth is not a uniform theorem for arbitrary datasets, depths or feature-learning scales.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts H rather than another B: simultaneous widths, quantitative linearization and test-prediction distribution strengthen a prior conceptual framework. The critic agrees. This is an example where all-time control improves a particular claim but is not a universal significance prerequisite. H21 explicitly compares its sharper kernel-change estimates with H10. Historical influence beyond the wider NTK program is not isolated quantitatively here.

### H11 — Chizat–Oyallon–Bach (2019)

Primary: [On lazy training](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/8559-on-lazy-training-in-differentiable-programming.pdf). Read scaling setup and Theorems 2.2–2.5.

**Challenge.** General finite-time laziness requires smooth-model assumptions and centered output; stronger uniform-time results impose strong convexity and differential nondegeneracy. Selected experiments that rich training does better do not prove universal inferiority of laziness. The theory does not supersede NTK identification within its scope.

**Response/disposition.** Both agree the scaling diagnosis works beyond a width-specific story and explains a mechanism, so H broad; H–B specialist remains a reasonable range. H20 explicitly organizes lazy versus feature-learning parameterizations, and H23 adopts the lazy/rich distinction in its model. Formal influence is evidenced; no generic practical prediction is inferred from the terminology alone.

### H12 — Mei–Montanari–Nguyen (2018)

Primary: [A mean field view](https://web.stanford.edu/~montanar/RESEARCH/FILEPAP/mean_field.pdf). Read setup, assumptions A1–A3, Theorem 3 and risk/long-time discussion; not the 103-page proof supplement in full.

**Challenge.** The general quantitative approximation is on finite horizons with exponentially growing time dependence, bounded activation/labels and regular interaction derivatives. The shallow probability-measure dynamics is not automatically globally convergent, finite-dimensional, or computationally easy. Propagation of chaos is older machinery; concurrent neural mean-field results must be credited.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate and critic accept that a feature-moving probability-law description is a major new neural-learning capability. H–B broad/B specialist is defensible under the explicit scope. Lack of uniform-time identification prevents an unconditional optimization claim but does not invalidate compact-time dynamics. H20/H22 provide concrete use of the mean-field viewpoint beyond a static kernel. Credit for the common 2018 development is not assigned exclusively to this paper.

### H13 — Rotskoff–Vanden-Eijnden (2018)

Primary: [Parameters as interacting particles](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/196f5641aa9dc87067da4ff90fd81e7b-Paper.pdf). Read §1 key assumptions and §§2–3, Propositions 3.1–3.3; visually inspected printed p. 5 to distinguish source typography from text-extraction error. No supplementary proof audit.

**Challenge.** Smooth/full-support initialization, discriminatory representation and asymptotic assumptions restrict optimization/fluctuation statements. The population/large-data loss and online access to new target evaluations prevent a generic finite-dataset generalization interpretation. Distinct value beyond H12 lies in fluctuations and self-healing, not counting the shared LLN twice. Printed fluctuation normalizations in (19)/(20) have signs inconsistent with surrounding CLT/rate prose; the critic will not certify an exact finite-width error rate from those displays.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate correctly objects that an apparent display error is not an established theorem failure. Accepted: retain particle/fluctuation-program value and H specialist/S–H broad; lower confidence in the precise advertised near-inverse-width rate, not the significance score merely because the critique is longer. Precise rate validation and case-specific independent long-run influence remain uncertain. A full theorem repair was outside assignment.

### H14 — Chizat–Bach (2018)

Primary: [Global convergence using optimal transport](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/file/a1afc58c6ca9540d057299ec3016d726-Paper.pdf). Read lifted optimization setup, Assumptions 3.2/3.4, Theorems 3.3/3.5 and following remarks.

**Challenge.** The principal statement assumes convergence of the Wasserstein flow before concluding global optimality. Positive homogeneity, support separation and regularity conditions are substantive; the separation initialization is inherently a continuum condition. The title cannot be read as unconditional finite-network GD convergence.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate emphasizes the escape/topological mechanism and conditional interchange of width/time limits, beyond mere convexity of risk over measures. The critic accepts H broad/H–B specialist. The convergence assumption limits the achieved optimization claim more directly than missing rates limit H01's density claim. Subsequent influence may be drawn from inspected advocate evidence; the critic does not infer it merely from an optimal-transport formulation.

### H15 — Du et al. / Allen-Zhu–Li–Song (2019)

Primaries: [Du et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/du19c/du19c.pdf), [Allen-Zhu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/allen-zhu19a/allen-zhu19a.pdf). Read architecture/data setup, Du Theorems 5.1 and residual-network comparison, Allen-Zhu Theorems 1/2 and local-neighborhood discussion.

**Challenge.** Du's smooth-activation fully connected width bound has exponential depth dependence; the residual case differs. Allen-Zhu's ReLU/polynomial-depth theorem has different width/separation requirements. Large width, conditioning and small steps limit relevance to ordinary finite networks; interpolation is not generalization. Grouping the wave must not hide these differences.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate agrees: training deep nonlinear parameters by first-order methods with a stated finite algorithmic guarantee overcomes a real proof obstacle even if its successful mechanism stays near initialization. B specialist/H–B broad. H10 and H21 explicitly build/refine this style of control. The result does not claim or need to prove all nonlinear feature-learning advantages to have this significance.

### H16 — Soudry et al. (2018)

Primary: [Implicit bias on separable data](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume19/18-188/18-188.pdf). Read assumptions, Theorems 3/5, conference-versus-journal correction and boosting/regularization comparisons, pp. 1–7.

**Challenge.** Homogeneous linear predictors, separability, suitable loss tails and small fixed GD steps are central. Convergence is in direction while parameter norms diverge; it is logarithmically slow. The result does not imply every dataset generalizes better or all optimizers select the same separator. The original conference generic-data theorem should not inherit the later all-data claim without version identification.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate and critic agree that identifying algorithm-induced max margin without an explicit norm penalty is a B mechanism in implicit-bias theory, H–B broad. Earlier boosting and vanishing-regularization paths do not already prove the GD statement. [Lyu–Li (2020), §2 and main results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05890) explicitly extends the program to homogeneous networks with a KKT qualification: concrete independent influence, not unlimited validation.

### H17 — Gunasekar et al. (2017)

Primary: [Implicit regularization in matrix factorization](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/58191d2a914c6dae66371c9dcdc91b41-Paper.pdf). Read §§1–4, Conjecture and Theorem 1. Also read [Li et al. (2021), introduction and Example 5.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.09839).

**Challenge.** The proved nuclear-norm claim concerns commuting PSD measurements, vanishing isotropic initialization, gradient flow and an existing interpolating limit. The broad noncommuting claim was a conjecture supported by experiments. Li et al.'s concrete completion counterexample defeats that broad conjecture, not the conditional commuting theorem.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts the distinction. Formulating and partially resolving reparameterization-dependent implicit bias helped produce a more accurate low-rank dynamical account. H specialist/S–H broad at publication; later influence is real even though one proposed universal explanation failed. This is not permission to rate a false claim as proved. Contemporary reviewer criticism was used only as reception context, never theorem evidence.

### H18 — Saxe–McClelland–Ganguli (2014)

Primary: [Exact deep-linear learning dynamics](https://www.saxelab.org/assets/papers/Saxe2014.pdf). Read §§1–2, whitening reduction, §1.3 aligned initial conditions, exact trajectory and depth/initialization discussion.

**Challenge.** The exact solutions are structured trajectories, not arbitrary initial conditions of all deep linear networks. Whitening/alignment and continuous-time training are essential. Linear input-output maps retain nonlinear parameter dynamics but cannot represent generic nonlinear feature learning.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts the scope and argues that sequential mode learning and initialization mechanisms remain a substantial solvable model. H broad; critic central H specialist, advocate permits H–B. The structured reduction is not penalized simply for lacking universal nonlinear coverage. [Braun et al. (2022)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/2b3bb2c95195130977a51b3bb251c40a-Paper-Conference.pdf) extends the exact-dynamics program to prior knowledge; this is substantive but shares an author, so it is weaker independent-reception evidence than an external survey.

### H19 — Yang (2019), Tensor Programs I

Primary: [Tensor Programs I](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/5e69fda38cda2060819766569fd93aa5-Paper.pdf). Read program language/setup, controlled-function definition, Theorem 5.4 and Corollary 5.5.

**Challenge.** The theorem handles finite programs with specified Gaussian initialization and controlled nonlinearities, and the GP readout requires unused independent Gaussian weights. The title does not cover arbitrary attention scaling, arbitrary dependency structures or all training times. Earlier GP limits and the author's earlier 2019 tensor-program paper constrain novelty attribution.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts these boundaries: a common rigorous language resolving weight sharing and dependency obstacles is a major reusable capability. H broad/H–B specialist. H20 and H23 explicitly use or recover the tensor-program framework, establishing concrete methodological continuation; neither retroactively turns the initialization theorem into a trained-network theorem.

### H20 — Yang–Hu (2021)

Primary: [Feature learning in infinite-width networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yang21c/yang21c.pdf). Read §§2–4, Theorems 3.2/3.5/3.7, definitions and limiting-time caveats. Full appendix proof/hypothesis audit not performed.

**Challenge.** The dichotomy concerns stable, nontrivial abc parameterizations under the paper's activation conditions; linear activation is an explicitly stated exception. Width tends to infinity at fixed training times/depth. Feature movement alone does not guarantee useful representations, generalization or optimizer convergence. Earlier shallow mean-field feature learning prevents a first-feature-learning-limit claim.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts this and emphasizes deep constructive limits plus parameterization classification and maximal-update prescription. B specialist/H–B broad remains defensible. H23 explicitly recovers this stochastic process by another route. Later broad engineering influence needs separately inspected evidence; it cannot be inferred just from the notation µP.

### H21 — Huang–Yau (2020)

Primary: [Neural tangent hierarchy](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/huang20l/huang20l.pdf). Read Assumptions 2.1/2.2, Theorems 2.3/2.6, truncation system and time/width discussion.

**Challenge.** Increasing order requires higher bounded activation derivatives and quantitative independence of larger input subsets; input dimension constrains the latter. The truncation theorem assumes a Gram gap, a width-dependent horizon and constants not controlled uniformly in order. It does not establish convergence as order tends to infinity for a fixed arbitrary network, or an efficient solver in a rich regime.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate agrees. The quantitative finite-order error control, beyond a merely exact infinite hierarchy, is the real advance. H specialist/S–H broad. H20 distinguishes the finite-width hierarchy from a feature-learning infinite-width limit. I also inspected [Bartlett–Montanari–Rakhlin §5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09177): it explicitly attributes the higher-order kernel equation to Huang–Yau and explains iterated approximations. This establishes independent substantive uptake, not landmark status or general solver effectiveness.

### H22 — Abbe–Boix-Adserà–Misiakiewicz (2022)

Primary: [Merged-staircase property](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/abbe22a/abbe22a.pdf). Read model/strong-learning definition, Theorems 7/9, sufficiency caveats, activation footnote and conclusion; no appendix proof audit.

**Challenge.** Necessity is for the specified initialization and mean-field linear-in-ambient-dimension scaling, not all SGD. Sufficiency is generic in coefficients with tailored activation hypotheses; the displayed polynomial is bounded outside the relevant interval. Latent sparsity is fixed and its cost can be very large, so O(d) is not a uniformly efficient statement in sparsity.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts each restriction. A learnability characterization and actual separation from linear feature methods go beyond expressivity alone, retaining H broad/H–B specialist. Subsequent impact is comparatively immature and remains uncertain unless the advocate's inspected follow-on evidence substantiates it. The absence of generic data/architecture coverage limits transfer, not the established sparse-function mechanism.

### H23 — Bordelon–Pehlevan (2022)

Primary: [2022 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09653), v3 dated 4 October 2022; [conference record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/d027a5c93d484a4312cc486d399c62c1-Abstract-Conference.html). Read §§3–4/7 and algorithm description; formal path-integral setup and linear special-case algebra.

**Challenge.** Width-independent order parameters remain functions of two times and samples. Fixed-time/fixed-sample width asymptotics do not give a finite autonomous state or sample/time-independent cost. Nonlinear self-consistency uses numerical sampling; the paper does not give general solver convergence or error certificates. The field derivation recovers an earlier Yang–Hu process, so not every underlying dynamical fact is new.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts these distinctions. The remaining positive capability is a self-consistent representation-level description and practical solver beyond static/low-order approximations. Critic central H specialist, S–H broad; advocate leaves specialist B arguable. Rigor confidence and scientific value are recorded separately: formal derivation plus demonstrations are not proof, and lack of proof alone does not make the new descriptive capability insignificant. Broad historical-impact qualification remains provisional.

### H24 — Schmid (2010)

Primary: [Dynamic mode decomposition](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Peter-Schmid-5/publication/278621822_Dynamic_mode_decomposition_of_experimental_data/links/5602b96708ae849b3c0e4bef/Dynamic-mode-decomposition-of-experimental-data.pdf). Read §§1–2, intersnapshot map, companion/SVD implementations and source assumptions. [Tu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.0041), §§1–2 inspected for reception and clarification.

**Challenge.** POD, Arnoldi/Krylov and Koopman analysis are predecessors. A fixed linear map over a sampling window is exact for linear dynamics but only an approximation for general nonlinear data. Snapshot rank, noise and sampling matter; no general nonlinear forecasting/closure guarantee is established.

**Response/disposition.** Both accept B in experimental/data-driven fluid dynamics: a usable matrix-free temporal decomposition is a real new capability despite absent universal error certificates. Broad deep-learning-theory relevance is S/adjacent, not automatically B. Tu et al. develops exact versus projected DMD, linear consistency and connections to system identification, documenting independent uptake and failure modes. This case guards against both requiring a universal theorem for every breakthrough and transferring specialist status across fields without justification.

### H25 — Sutherland–Schneider (2015)

Primary: [On the error of random Fourier features](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02785). Read estimator definitions, variance comparison, Propositions 1/2/9 and approximation-to-prediction discussion.

**Challenge.** The result corrects/refines an existing approximation method; it does not introduce a new representation or learning regime. Uniform variance superiority is established for the Gaussian case, not all kernels. Prediction perturbation depends on the relevant learning setup/conditioning.

**Response/disposition.** The advocate accepts S specialist/Below-S–S broad. The estimator distinction and valid error-to-prediction bridge are useful, not merely cosmetic constants. [Sriperumbudur–Szabó (2015), §1 footnote and §§2–3](https://www.gatsby.ucl.ac.uk/~szabo/publications/sriperumbudurszabo15optimal.pdf) explicitly compares the corrected constants and variants before obtaining stronger rates. This substantiates narrow technical influence, not a broad field transformation.

## Debate completeness and residual uncertainty

Every H01–H25 received an ID-specific substantive critic challenge and advocate response in direct messages. The advocate's positive cases were checked against primary setup/statement material by the critic; read coverage is above. The exact rate issue in H13, incomplete proof-level checking of technical activation conditions for H20, immature impact of H22/H23, and limited independent impact separation for several extensions are explicit uncertainties, not hidden defects. Shared ranges do not imply agreement on every central label: the critic favors lower endpoints for H06 broad, H18 specialist and H23 specialist, for the reasons given.

This record establishes neither target relevance nor a target verdict. The calibration must be audited against the advocate's final synthesis, and exact file hashes jointly approved, before any Phase 2 access.
