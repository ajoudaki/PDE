The strongest conclusion is the following. If a finite control realization
approximates the **directional output field** with error eta on a certified
reachable set, and the exact frozen-state residual map is strongly contracting
there, then its loss and its own activity clock approximate the exact ones at
the same physical time, uniformly for all time, with error O(eta). A control
signature truncation gives an explicit autonomous scalar realization. With
D=m+1 control letters and geometric source error exp(-beta p), its state count
is O(epsilon^(-log(D)/beta)); it is strictly subquadratic only when
beta > log(D)/2. Finite total activity supplies no such source rate.

## 1. Exact control form and initial learning

Work first at a fixed finite width n with the exact Gaussian mixer and its
transpose. The input/label normalization and all network definitions are those
in the README. Write z=(w,A,v_1,...,v_m,k_1,...,k_m,tau), and let
F(z)=(f_1(z),...,f_m(z)) in R^m. Put r=F(z)-y, rho=||r||_2/sqrt(m),
L=||r||_2^2/m. At initialization F(z_0)=0 and ||r_0||_2=sqrt(m).

There are D=m+1 smooth vector fields X_0,...,X_m on tau>0 such that

    z_dot = sum_{i=0}^m X_i(z) c_i(r),
    c_0(r)=||r||_2/sqrt(m),  c_a(r)=r_a  (1<=a<=m).

Throughout this note, an **admissible prescribed control** is generated by
an arbitrary locally integrable residual input u(t) in R^m:

    c(t)=c(u(t))=(||u(t)||_2/sqrt(m),u_1(t),...,u_m(t)).

The input u need not equal the controlled plant's own residual. All
prescribed-control plant, reachable-set and source claims below refer only
to this family, not to arbitrary signed (m+1)-component controls. In
particular c_0>=0 and, from the prescribed initial tau_0=1,

    tau(t)=1+integral_0^t ||u(s)||_2/sqrt(m) ds >=1.

The only nonzero components of X_0 are

    (X_0 k_b)=(h_b-k_b)/tau,     X_0 tau=1.

For 1<=a<=m, the nonzero components of X_a are

    X_a w=-(2/m)g_a,
    X_a A=-(2/m)ell_a x_a^T/sqrt(d),
    X_a v_b=-2 delta_ab d_a.

In particular, all state motion vanishes at r=0. The nonsmooth norm at r=0
is in the finite-dimensional feedback c, not in these vector fields.

Define H_i(z)=X_i F(z) in R^m and

    G_z(u)=sum_i H_i(z)c_i(u).

Then r_dot=G_z(r) is exact. The control map obeys

    ||c(u)-c(v)||_1 <= alpha ||u-v||_2,
    ||c(u)||_1 <= alpha ||u||_2,
    alpha=(m+1)/sqrt(m).

At initialization the only nonzero component of X_a is its w component.
Consequently, with Q_ab=n^(-1)g_a(z_0)^Tg_b(z_0),

    H_0(z_0)=0,   [H_b(z_0)]_a=-(2/m)Q_ab,
    r_dot(0)=(2/m)Qy,
    L_dot(0)=-(4/m^2)y^TQy.

Every truncation below of order p>=1 reproduces these identities exactly.
No learned feature is frozen in the defining controlled plant or coefficients.

### The activity clock is not physical time

Let s(t)=integral_0^t rho(u)du. Before r vanishes,

    dz/ds = X_0(z)+sum_a X_a(z)u_a,
    u_a=r_a/rho,  ||u||_2=sqrt(m),
    dt/ds=1/rho,  tau=1+s.

If rho decays exponentially in physical time, s has a finite endpoint while
t diverges. Thus a uniform error at equal s is not automatically a uniform
error at equal t. The theorem below compares the two systems directly in
physical time, including their separately generated clocks.

### Cumulative controls alone do not remove order information

Take a!=b. With the convention [X_a,X_b]=X_a X_b-X_b X_a, at z_0,

    [X_a,X_b]v_b = (4/m)g_a odot (1-g_b^2).

Indeed X_b v_b=-2w odot(1-g_b^2), X_a w=-(2/m)g_a,
and X_a v_b is identically zero. This bracket is nonzero for generic
nondegenerate data/initialization. The two orders of a small a-control and
b-control therefore give different hidden states even when their cumulative
controls agree. At z_0 the instantaneous output is blind to some such hidden
directions because w=0; that does not justify discarding them in future output
evolution. A closure based only on the m accumulated residuals and s needs a
separate theorem controlling this order information.

The order-dependence statement is valid within the admissible control family.
Positive residual-input pulses u=e_a and u=e_b generate the fields
Y_a=X_a+X_0/sqrt(m) and Y_b=X_b+X_0/sqrt(m). At z_0, the cross-bracket
terms involving X_0 vanish in the v_b component because X_0w=X_0v_b=0 and
w=0. Hence [Y_a,Y_b]v_b(z_0) equals the displayed nonzero bracket. The two
pulse orders have identical integrated clock and residual controls.

## 2. An explicit finite autonomous scalar system

A word w=(i_1,...,i_k) has letters in {0,...,m}; the empty word is denoted by
empty. Write X_w=X_{i_1}...X_{i_k} as an ordered composition of directional
derivatives, and define the initialized scalar coefficient vector

    C_w=(X_w F)(z_0) in R^m,     C_empty=F(z_0)=0.

These coefficients are differentiated from the initialized exact vector
fields. They do not use a future training trace or an independently resampled
Gaussian mixer. At finite n they have a finite recursive differentiation
algorithm using the supplied A_0,W_0 and W_0^T.

For an order p>=1 retain one scalar S_w for each word with 1<=|w|<=p.
Set S_empty=1 and initialize all other S_w to zero. Define

    r_tilde = sum_{|w|<=p} C_w S_w-y,
    rho_tilde=||r_tilde||_2/sqrt(m),
    S_dot_{wi}=S_w c_i(r_tilde),    |w|<=p-1.

This is autonomous and restartable from the retained scalars. Its output is
L_tilde=||r_tilde||_2^2/m. At r_tilde=0 every scalar stops moving. Its own
activity clock is exactly s_tilde=S_(0), and tau_tilde=1+S_(0).
For finite p the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz, including at r_tilde=0.

For an admissible prescribed control c(t)=c(u(t)), the same triangular
equations generate

    S_(i_1...i_k)(t)
      = integral_{0<t_1<...<t_k<t} c_{i_1}(t_1)...c_{i_k}(t_k)dt_1...dt_k.

If a(t)=integral_0^t ||c(u)||_1 du, then

    sum_{|w|=k}|S_w(t)| <= a(t)^k/k!.

To see the last inequality, sum the absolute integrands over all words and
integrate the symmetric product of ||c(t_j)||_1 over one ordered simplex;
the full cube consists of k! such simplexes up to null boundaries.

Differentiating the finite output gives

    r_tilde_dot=sum_i H_{p,i}(S)c_i(r_tilde),
    H_{p,i}(S)=sum_{|w|<=p-1} C_{wi} S_w.

Thus the relevant approximation source is the error in these directional
output fields, not merely the error in an initialized Taylor coefficient or
the value of F at some control endpoint.

This is a control-series construction, related to response hierarchies. It
does not resolve their source-growth problem by changing their name. Its
additional content here is the physical-time stability transfer below and
the resulting explicit complexity threshold.

## 3. Conditional physical-time theorem

All constants and hypotheses below are deterministic. To obtain a width
independent or population conclusion, the same constants, source bounds and
coefficient-computation guarantees must hold uniformly in width, and the
population target itself must first be supplied with a valid existence/limit
theorem. Neither assertion is implicit here.

Fix a norm on the finite-width state space and a controlled reachable set
Omega containing z_0. Use exactly the admissible residual-input family
defined in Section 1. Assume that for every finite T and every
u in L^1([0,T];R^m) with integral_0^T ||c(u(t))||_1 dt<=A, the controlled
path starting at z_0 exists within Omega throughout [0,T], where

    R_0=sqrt(m),  alpha=(m+1)/sqrt(m),  A>alpha R_0/lambda.

For example, Omega can be a closed subset of an open smooth domain together
with a separately proved continuation bound. Its definition cannot use the
unknown future training trajectory. Assume on Omega:

1. For all i, ||X_i(z)||<=B and
   ||X_i(z)-X_i(z')||<=L_X||z-z'||.
2. For all i, ||H_i(z)-H_i(z')||_2<=L_H||z-z'||.
3. For every z in Omega and every u,v in R^m,

       <u-v,G_z(u)-G_z(v)> <= -lambda ||u-v||_2^2,

   with lambda>0. This is contraction of the residual map when z is held
   fixed; it is stronger than decreasing loss along one trajectory.
4. For every admissibly controlled path z^c starting at z_0 with variation <=A,
   and the signature S^c driven by that same control, the source bound is

       max_i ||H_i(z^c(t))-H_{p,i}(S^c(t))||_2 <= eta_p.

   This hypothesis concerns an entire allowed family of forced paths. It is
   not a fitted error bound on the unknown closed-loop trajectory.

Choose p so that

    alpha eta_p <= lambda-alpha R_0/A.

The right side is strictly positive. Define

    D_*=(B alpha A/lambda)
          exp[(L_X+B alpha L_H/lambda)A],
    E_*=(alpha R_0/lambda)(1+L_H D_*),
    C_loss=(2R_0/m)E_*.

Then the exact and scalar systems are globally defined and satisfy, for all
t>=0,

    ||r(t)||_2 <= R_0 exp(-lambda t),
    ||r_tilde(t)||_2 <= R_0 exp[-(lambda-alpha eta_p)t],
    ||r(t)-r_tilde(t)||_2 <= E_* eta_p,
    |L(t)-L_tilde(t)| <= C_loss eta_p,
    |s(t)-s_tilde(t)| <=
       [A/(lambda sqrt(m))](1+L_H D_*) eta_p.

All retained scalar coordinates have finite limits as t tends to infinity,
and their limiting residual is zero. This is terminal stability along the
certified reachable family; it does not assert attraction to one isolated
point of the full signature state space. The equilibrium set includes neutral
directions that leave the residual zero.

### Proof

Drive a conceptual copy z_bar of the exact controlled plant by the scalar
system's own control c(r_tilde). This is a proof device, not moving state kept
by the approximation. The source estimate compares H_p(S) with H(z_bar).
Both c(r) and c(r_tilde) are admissible: before any putative finite-time exit,
their residual inputs are continuous and therefore locally integrable.
Because G_z(0)=0, contraction with v=0 gives

    d^+ ||r||_2/dt <= -lambda||r||_2,

and, as long as the scalar system's control variation stays below A,

    d^+ ||r_tilde||_2/dt
      <=-(lambda-alpha eta_p)||r_tilde||_2.

Here the source contribution has norm at most
eta_p||c(r_tilde)||_1 <=alpha eta_p||r_tilde||_2.
Thus the scalar total control variation is at most

    alpha R_0/(lambda-alpha eta_p) <= A.

For every finite time this variation is strictly less than its infinite-time
upper bound. The prescribed-control continuation assumption therefore keeps
z_bar in Omega. The exact path has variation at most alpha R_0/lambda<A.
The signature bound keeps every retained S_w bounded by A^|w|/|w|!, so its
locally Lipschitz ODE cannot have a finite-time blowup. This closes the
continuation argument for both systems.

Put e(t)=||r(t)-r_tilde(t)||_2, d(t)=||z(t)-z_bar(t)||, and
R_tilde(t)=||r_tilde(t)||_2. Subtract the two controlled plant equations,
using the same control c(r_tilde) for the Lipschitz state difference, to obtain

    d(t) <= B alpha integral_0^t e(u)du
             +L_X alpha integral_0^t R_tilde(u)d(u)du.             (1)

Subtract the two residual equations. At the exact state z, compare the two
residual arguments by the contraction hypothesis; compare H(z) and H(z_bar)
by L_H and then use the source bound. The upper Dini derivative satisfies

    d^+e/dt <= -lambda e+alpha R_tilde(L_H d+eta_p),  e(0)=0.      (2)

The use of a Dini derivative covers zero values of e without division by e.
Integrating (2) and discarding the nonnegative terminal e(t) yields

    integral_0^t e <= (alpha/lambda)
                         integral_0^t R_tilde(L_H d+eta_p).       (3)

Let V(t)=integral_0^t R_tilde <=A/alpha. Combining (1) and (3),

    d(t) <= integral_0^t R_tilde(u)[K d(u)+b eta_p]du,
    K=L_X alpha+B alpha^2 L_H/lambda,
    b=B alpha^2/lambda.

Iterating this scalar integral inequality, or differentiating its right-hand
side and multiplying by exp(-K V(t)), gives

    d(t) <= b eta_p V(t)exp(K V(t)) <= D_* eta_p.

This formula is also valid when K=0. Inserting it in the integrated-factor
form of (2), and using R_tilde<=R_0, gives

    e(t) <= alpha(1+L_H D_*)eta_p
               integral_0^t exp[-lambda(t-u)]R_tilde(u)du
           <=E_* eta_p.

The loss estimate follows from

    |L-L_tilde| <= (||r||_2+||r_tilde||_2)e/m <=(2R_0/m)e.

The clocks obey

    |s(t)-s_tilde(t)| <= (1/sqrt(m))integral_0^t e(u)du.

Use (3), d<=D_*eta_p and V<=A/alpha to get the stated clock bound. No inversion
of s(t), comparison at unequal physical times, or training-time cutoff enters
this proof. Finally, each |S_dot_w| has a finite integral because its parent
coordinate is bounded and the driving controls are integrable. Thus every
S_w converges, while the exponential residual bound gives residual zero at
the limit. This proves the theorem.

### What contraction would require in this model

Let M(z) have columns H_a(z), 1<=a<=m, and let b(z)=H_0(z). Then

    G_z(u)=M(z)u+b(z)||u||_2/sqrt(m).

A sufficient, directly checkable condition for hypothesis 3 is

    sym M(z) <=-[lambda+||b(z)||_2/sqrt(m)] I_m.

Indeed the extra norm term contributes at most
||b||_2||u-v||_2^2/sqrt(m), by the reverse triangle inequality.
At initialization b=0 and M=-(2/m)Q, so a positive minimum eigenvalue of Q
gives an initial contraction margin. It does not give a margin throughout
Omega: the response-memory terms can be nonsymmetric and the k-clock motion
contributes b. This route contains no proof that the displayed condition
persists for generic q=1 training, or even that actual training always fits.

## 4. A source lemma with all factorials exposed

Fix a finite T and u in L^1([0,T];R^m), put c=c(u), and assume
integral_0^T ||c(t)||_1dt<=A. Let z^c be its admissibly controlled path in
Omega, supplied by Section 3. No negative clock input is allowed in this
source lemma.

Assume for a specified p that every word w of length p and each i satisfies

    sup_{z in Omega} ||X_w H_i(z)||_2 <= M_p.

Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to H_i(z^c(t)), then to the
directional derivative appearing in its integral, repeating p times. The
terms through length p-1 are precisely H_{p,i}(S^c). The remainder contains
X_w H_i evaluated at the earliest time of each ordered p-simplex. The sum of
absolute control products over all such words is bounded by A^p/p!, hence

    eta_p <= M_p A^p/p!.                                       (4)

The derivative bound is needed on the controlled reachable set, not merely
at z_0. Initialized coefficients alone provide no Taylor remainder estimate.
The smoothness used in this finite-order identity is valid for the finite
width vector fields on tau>0; the size and width-uniformity of M_p are separate
questions.

For example, if one actually proves

    M_p <= M p! R^(-p)  for every p>=1,

then (4) gives eta_p<=M q^p, q=A/R. This is useful only when q<1.
More generally, M_p<=M C^p(p!)^sigma gives

    eta_p <= M(CA)^p(p!)^(sigma-1).

For sigma>1 this upper bound diverges as p tends to infinity at any fixed
A>0. It can justify a best finite truncation, not arbitrary-accuracy
convergence. For sigma=1 a positive radius and a small enough A are needed.
For sigma<1 it gives a stronger tail. No value of sigma is asserted for the
q=1 initialized Gaussian population.

## 5. Scalar count, storage, work, and the exact threshold

The moving scalar count is

    S_p=sum_{k=1}^p D^k=(D^(p+1)-D)/(D-1),   D=m+1>=2.

Residuals and loss are algebraic outputs; retaining them redundantly adds
only O(m) scalars. Coefficient storage is m(1+S_p) real scalars. A direct RHS
evaluation takes O(m S_p) arithmetic operations: form the m residual outputs
and update each signature coordinate once. These counts do not include
precision or one-time coefficient generation.

At finite n, coefficient generation consists of m S_p ordered directional
derivative coefficients of orders at most p, evaluated at the known initial
network. This is a finite recursive automatic/symbolic differentiation task
on the original network circuit, with repeated use of the true W_0 and W_0^T.
It requires reading at least the O(n^2+nd) supplied initialization and may
be very expensive in p. This route supplies neither a width independent
initialization-work bound nor a fast population coefficient algorithm.
It does not hide that work in an oracle. After coefficients are generated,
the original neuron state/mixer need not move or be accessed online.

Coefficient precision is also a source term. If every coefficient C_(wi)
with |w|=k has Euclidean error at most delta_(k+1), its contribution to the
directional-output source is at most

    sum_{k=0}^{p-1} delta_(k+1) A^k/k!.

This follows from the signature absolute-sum bound, and can be added to
eta_p in the theorem. Treating arbitrarily precise coefficients as free is
not required for the statement.

Suppose eta_p<=M exp(-beta p), beta>0, with every theorem constant independent
of p and epsilon. Taking

    p >= beta^(-1) log(M C_loss/epsilon)

and increasing p by a fixed amount to meet the stability threshold gives

    S(epsilon)=O(epsilon^(-gamma)),   gamma=log(D)/beta.

Therefore this source rate certifies S(epsilon)=o(epsilon^(-2)) when
beta>log(D)/2. In the analytic derivative example beta=log(R/A), so the
condition is R/A>sqrt(D). A radius barely larger than A proves convergence
but may give a state exponent larger than two. A finite activity bound by
itself implies none of these radius or rate inequalities.

At the threshold beta=log(D)/2, a stronger source estimate
eta_p<=M p^(-b)D^(-p/2), b>0, also suffices: solving the bound gives
S(epsilon)=O(epsilon^(-2)[log(1/epsilon)]^(-2b)). Without that extra gain,
the geometric estimate only gives O(epsilon^(-2)), not little-o.
Any conversion of these scalar counts to o(n) at epsilon proportional to
n^(-1/2) remains conditional on the separate matched dense/root-width error
estimate stipulated in the study contract.

## 6. Why Gaussian tanh initialization does not supply the missing rate

The implication “tanh is analytic and initialization is Gaussian, therefore
all initialized aggregate control series have a uniform positive radius” is
invalid. A short counterexample to that implication is

    h(s)=E[tanh^2(sZ)],   Z standard Gaussian.

For each fixed derivative order, real derivatives of tanh^2 are bounded,
and E|Z|^k is finite; differentiation under the expectation is therefore
justified by domination. Thus h is C-infinity on the real line. If

    tanh^2(z)=sum_{k>=1} a_k z^(2k),

then its nearest complex poles are at z=+/-i*pi/2, so
limsup |a_k|^(1/(2k))=2/pi. The Taylor coefficient of h at order 2k is

    a_k E[Z^(2k)]=a_k(2k-1)!!.

Since ((2k-1)!!)^(1/(2k)) tends to infinity, these coefficients have unbounded
root limsup and the Taylor series of h has radius zero. For the last growth
fact, (2k-1)!!>=k! and k!>=(k/2)^(floor(k/2)) already suffice.

This is not a counterexample to the q=1 approximation conjecture or a proof
that its particular Lie series diverges. It demonstrates why an asserted
Gaussian coefficient-rate lemma needs an actual proof. Real smoothness,
bounded activation, finite moments of every fixed order, and correct jets
do not supply the missing convergence rate.

Truncating Gaussian tails to get bounded directions introduces an additional
tail error and an epsilon-dependent analytic radius. Re-expanding on many
small control intervals then requires coefficients at their new full states.
Without a separately proved finite atlas or coefficient-update closure,
those re-expansions either retain the full neuron state or require an oracle.
This route does not claim that local analytic charts solve the global
compression problem. Likewise, noncommutativity prevents replacing every
chart by just an endpoint in cumulative-control space.

## 7. The remaining lemma and its precise logical force

For the actual q=1 Gaussian model on a stated generic correlated-data class,
one would need an **initialization-computable reachable certificate** that
supplies Omega, A, B, L_X, L_H and lambda as above, together with coefficients
whose directional-output source, uniformly over the admissible residual-input
paths c(u) with total control variation at most A, satisfies

    eta_p <= M exp(-beta p),   beta>log(m+1)/2,

or another rate/cost pair giving the same little-o state count. All constants
must be uniform in the width limit under consideration. Coefficients must
come from the supplied initialization and exact reused mixer, with controlled
finite precision, rather than population response queries or a training
trace. Existence and identification of the population target remain separate
obligations. This is the missing lemma, not an established property.

Its two substantive parts are independent:

* The contraction/reachability certificate gives terminal stability and
  finite activity, but produces no small approximation source.
* The source-rate/coefficient-computation certificate gives compression of
  the directional fields, but does not establish that the actual q=1 flow
  is contracting or fitting.

A proof of either part without the other would remain useful and would not
prove the complete target. A source estimate only on an unknown exact
trajectory is insufficient for the feedback theorem; one needs the allowed
forced-path family, or a different explicitly proved robustness argument.

The theorem's assumption of frozen-state contraction is deliberately strong.
It may fail even if the actual flow learns, in which case this witness and
stability proof need replacement. The analytic radius condition may fail
even if another resummation works. Neither failure is an impossibility
theorem for finite scalar approximation.
