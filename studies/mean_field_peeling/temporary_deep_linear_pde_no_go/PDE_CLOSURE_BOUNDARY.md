# Three hidden linear layers: the exact PDE closure boundary

## 1. Verdict

There are two distinct statements that must not be conflated.

1. The finite-contraction ODE no-go **does extend** to every fixed local PDE
   of finite differential order whose fields, uniformly over their spatial
   variables, are bounded-degree complete contractions of the current network
   tensors. Mixed spatial partial derivatives do not evade the obstruction.

2. The unrestricted assertion that no fixed finite number of scalar PDE
   fields can represent the dynamics is **false**. One scalar field governed
   by the linear transport equation stores the unbounded output
   Lie-derivative hierarchy in its spatial profile. Field count is not
   numerical state dimension.

The theorem below proves both claims and identifies the exact missing
hypothesis: finite contraction provenance of the whole spatial jet.

No external research theorem is used.

## 2. Exact model and feature derivation

At width \(n\), let

\[
a,x\in\mathbb R^n,\qquad B,R\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},\qquad
f_n=a^{\mathsf T}RBx.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The feature-ascent vector field is

\[
\begin{aligned}
a'&=RBx,&R'&=a(Bx)^{\mathsf T},\\
B'&=(R^{\mathsf T}a)x^{\mathsf T},&
x'&=B^{\mathsf T}R^{\mathsf T}a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Write \(\mathscr D_n\) for the derivation on polynomials in the entries of
\((a,R,B,x)\) induced by (2.2). For squared loss \((y-f_n)^2\), fix
\(\eta>0\). Physical gradient flow is generated by

\[
\mathscr X_n=2\eta(y-f_n)\mathscr D_n.
\tag{2.3}
\]

All no-go statements below are state-universal: their identities must hold
for every state in a nonempty open set and every sufficiently large width,
not merely along the Gaussian initialization orbit.

## 3. The PDE class

Fix independently of \(n\):

- a spatial domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d\), possibly with a smooth
  boundary, with coefficient functions smooth up to that boundary;
- a number \(q\) of scalar fields;
- finite differential orders \(r,\rho\);
- a tensor-degree bound \(D\);
- finitely many readout points \(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_s\in\Omega\).

A typed complete-contraction graph is a scalar tensor network made from
copies of \(a,x,B,R\), with every layer index paired and summed. Its tensor
degree is the number of tensor copies. Let \(P_G^{(n)}\) denote the resulting
polynomial.

A **uniform bounded-contraction encoder** is a family

\[
U_n=(U_{1,n},\ldots,U_{q,n}),\qquad
U_{\alpha,n}:\Omega\times\Theta_n\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
\tag{3.1}
\]

of the form

\[
U_{\alpha,n}(\zeta;\theta)
=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G_D}c_{\alpha,G,n}(\zeta)P_G^{(n)}(\theta).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Here \(\mathcal G_D\) is the finite set of typed graphs of degree at most
\(D\), and the coefficient functions are smooth. They may depend on \(n\);
this only strengthens the no-go theorem. Finiteness of \(\mathcal G_D\)
follows because there are finitely many tensor occurrences, typed index
vertices, and pairings at bounded degree.

A fixed local differential-polynomial PDE closure consists of polynomials
\(F_\alpha\), independent of \(n\) and the state, such that

\[
\mathscr D_nU_{\alpha,n}(\zeta;\theta)
=F_\alpha\!\left(
\zeta,
\bigl(\partial_\zeta^\beta U_{\gamma,n}(\zeta;\theta)\bigr)_
{1\le\gamma\le q,\ |\beta|\le r}
\right).
\tag{3.3}
\]

The coefficients of \(F_\alpha\) may be arbitrary fixed smooth functions of
\(\zeta\). All mixed partial derivatives in the \(d\) spatial variables are
allowed. The output is required to be a fixed polynomial readout of finitely
many finite jets,

\[
f_n=G\!\left(
\bigl(\partial_\zeta^\beta U_{\alpha,n}(\zeta_j;\theta)\bigr)_
{\alpha,j,\ |\beta|\le\rho}
\right).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Taking \(U_{1,n}(\zeta_1)=f_n\) is the special case corresponding exactly
to the earlier ODE theorem.

In the analytic variant, \(F_\alpha\) and \(G\) are fixed functions analytic
in their finite jet arguments on open sets containing every realized
zero-network jet tuple to which they are applied. The closure identities are
required on a network-state neighborhood on which those compositions remain
inside the analytic domains. This specifies the jet-space basepoints; they
need not be the origin because (3.2) may contain an empty-graph term.

The phrase “bounded-degree PDE” is otherwise ambiguous. In (3.2) the bound
concerns dependence of the encoded fields on the network tensors; in (3.3)
it concerns the local differential expression. Both are needed for a
finite-information theorem.

## 4. Main theorem

### Theorem 1

For the network (2.1)--(2.2):

1. No encoder and local PDE satisfying (3.1)--(3.4) exists for all
   sufficiently large \(n\).

2. The same is true if the differential polynomials and readout are replaced
   by real-analytic germs and the identities hold on a full neighborhood of
   the zero network state.

3. The same two conclusions hold for the physical derivation (2.3), for
   every label \(y\in\mathbb R\) and every fixed \(\eta>0\).

4. If condition (3.2) is deleted, the corresponding universal no-PDE claim
   is false in the same output-semiconjugacy sense as (3.4). Feature time has
   a state-universal local-germ output realization, and physical time has a
   global forward, autonomous, restartable output realization, using one
   scalar field, one spatial variable, and the fixed degree-one, first-order
   PDE

   \[
   \partial_tU=\partial_sU.
   \tag{4.1}
   \]

Thus the theorem rules out finite bounded-contraction PDE closures, not
PDEs whose spatial profiles store infinitely many moments.

## 5. Stable independence of contraction graphs

We first prove the coefficient-comparison fact used below.

### Lemma 2

Every fixed finite family of pairwise nonisomorphic typed complete-
contraction graphs has linearly independent contraction polynomials in all
sufficiently large widths.

### Proof

Treat the tensor entries as independent commuting indeterminates. Enlarge
the graph class temporarily to typed incidence networks in which an index
vertex may have arbitrary valence. For the finite family under
consideration, take the finite closure of its graphs under all
type-compatible quotients of index vertices. For any network \(H\) in this
auxiliary class, let \(I_H^{(n)}\) be its injective contraction: distinct
index vertices of the same type must receive distinct indices.

Splitting unrestricted index maps according to their equality partition gives

\[
P_G^{(n)}=\sum_\pi I_{G/\pi}^{(n)},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \(\pi\) ranges over type-compatible partitions. Quotients need not be
pairwise complete contractions, which is why the auxiliary incidence class
was introduced.

Injective contractions of pairwise nonisomorphic auxiliary networks are
independent in sufficiently large width. Indeed, label every index vertex of
one network injectively. The resulting tensor-entry monomial records each
tensor type and all incident typed indices, so the same monomial can occur
for another injective network only if the two incidence networks are
type-preservingly isomorphic.

Now suppose \(\sum_G c_GP_G^{(n)}=0\) for the original complete-contraction
graphs. Choose among the graphs with \(c_G\ne0\) one having a maximal number
of index vertices. In the expansion (5.1), the discrete partition contributes
\(I_G\). A nontrivial quotient has fewer index vertices, and no graph with
nonzero coefficient has more vertices than \(G\). Hence no other term can
cancel the coefficient \(c_G\) of \(I_G\). Therefore \(c_G=0\), a
contradiction. Descending in the number of vertices proves that every
coefficient vanishes. \(\square\)

The stable-width qualification is necessary: fixed-dimensional
Cayley--Hamilton identities relate sufficiently long words.

## 6. The connected-path ladder

Put

\[
A=RB,\qquad C=A^{\mathsf T}A=B^{\mathsf T}R^{\mathsf T}RB.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let \(\mathscr D_{0,n}\) retain only endpoint differentiation:

\[
\mathscr D_{0,n}a=Ax,\qquad
\mathscr D_{0,n}x=A^{\mathsf T}a,\qquad
\mathscr D_{0,n}A=0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For \(j\ge0\), define

\[
U_j=a^{\mathsf T}AC^jx,\quad
X_j=x^{\mathsf T}C^{j+1}x,\quad
Y_j=a^{\mathsf T}(AA^{\mathsf T})^{j+1}a.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Using
\(AC^jA^{\mathsf T}=(AA^{\mathsf T})^{j+1}\) and
\((AA^{\mathsf T})^{j+1}A=AC^{j+1}\), the product rule gives

\[
\mathscr D_{0,n}U_j=X_j+Y_j,\qquad
\mathscr D_{0,n}X_j=\mathscr D_{0,n}Y_j=2U_{j+1}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since \(U_0=f_n\), induction yields

\[
\mathscr D_{0,n}^{\,2m-1}f_n
=4^{m-1}\left[
x^{\mathsf T}C^mx+a^{\mathsf T}(AA^{\mathsf T})^ma
\right].
\tag{6.5}
\]

Every coordinate of (2.2) is a monomial with coefficient \(+1\). Therefore
every product-rule history in the full derivation has a nonnegative integer
coefficient. The endpoint-only histories form a subset of those histories.
Consequently

\[
\mathscr D_n^{\,2m-1}f_n
\quad\text{contains with positive coefficient}\quad
4^{m-1}\Gamma_m,
\qquad
\Gamma_m=x^{\mathsf T}C^mx.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The graph \(\Gamma_m\) is connected, has tensor degree \(4m+2\), and the
\(\Gamma_m\) are pairwise nonisomorphic.

## 7. Why finite spatial derivatives do not help

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the finite set of connected graph types that occur in
the fields (3.2). Define

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathcal S}^{(n)}
=\operatorname{span}\{P_H^{(n)}:
\text{every connected component of }H\text{ belongs to }\mathcal S\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is a unital algebra because multiplication of contraction polynomials is
disjoint union of their graphs.

Spatial differentiation in (3.2) differentiates only the coefficient
functions. Hence, for every multi-index \(\beta\), every field jet
\(\partial_\zeta^\beta U_{\alpha,n}\) belongs to
\(\mathcal A_{\mathcal S}^{(n)}\). This remains true at arbitrarily high
spatial order; the key point is the common finite graph alphabet, not a
finite truncation of the jet.

For completeness, introduce formal jet variables \(u_{\alpha,\beta}\). The
total spatial derivatives on the jet algebra are

\[
D_i^{\mathrm{tot}}
=\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_i}
+\sum_{\gamma,\delta}
u_{\gamma,\delta+e_i}
\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\gamma,\delta}},
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{tot}}^\beta
=\prod_{i=1}^d(D_i^{\mathrm{tot}})^{\beta_i}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For the finitely many readout points, use independent jet copies
\(u_{\alpha,\beta}^{(j)}\). The evolutionary prolongation of (3.3) is

\[
\mathbf T_FH
=\sum_{j,\alpha,\beta}
\frac{\partial H}{\partial u_{\alpha,\beta}^{(j)}}
\left(D_{\mathrm{tot}}^\beta F_\alpha\right)
(\zeta_j,u^{(j)}).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Only finitely many terms occur for any fixed differential polynomial \(H\).
The state derivation and spatial differentiation act on different variables
and therefore commute. The chain and product rules give

\[
\mathscr D_n H(j^\infty U_n)
=(\mathbf T_FH)(j^\infty U_n).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Applying (7.4) repeatedly to the readout polynomial gives

\[
\mathscr D_n^k f_n
=(\mathbf T_F^kG)(j^\infty U_n).
\tag{7.5}
\]

For each fixed \(k\), the right-hand side uses only finitely many jets and is
a finite polynomial in them. It consequently belongs to
\(\mathcal A_{\mathcal S}^{(n)}\).

Choose \(m\) with \(\Gamma_m\notin\mathcal S\). Equation (7.5) says that
\(\mathscr D_n^{2m-1}f_n\) is a linear combination of graphs whose connected
components lie in \(\mathcal S\), while (6.6) says its coefficient of the new
connected graph \(\Gamma_m\) is positive. Lemma 2 makes these graph
coefficients independent in sufficiently large width. This contradiction
proves Theorem 1(1).

If (3.3)--(3.4) were initially assumed only on a nonempty open set of network
states, both sides after substitution would be polynomials in the tensor
entries. A real polynomial that vanishes on an open set has every coefficient
zero (restrict successively to one-dimensional coordinate lines). Hence the
identities are polynomial identities, and the same coefficient comparison is
valid.

Notice that iteration may increase the spatial derivative order without
bound. It still cannot help: every such derivative remains in the same
finite graph alphabet.

## 8. Analytic differential expressions

Suppose instead that the PDE and readout satisfy the analytic-domain
condition stated after (3.4). Scale all four tensors by \(\lambda\). Every
field jet is then a polynomial in \(\lambda\) of degree at most \(D\).
For every jet variable, recenter at its complete zero-state value,
\[
v_{\alpha,\beta,n}(\zeta;\lambda)
=\partial^\beta U_{\alpha,n}(\zeta;\lambda\theta)
-\partial^\beta U_{\alpha,n}(\zeta;0).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Every nonzero \(v_{\alpha,\beta,n}\) has strictly positive
\(\lambda\)-order.

Taylor-expand each analytic function at the corresponding zero-state jet
tuple. At any prescribed power \(\lambda^N\), only finitely many Taylor
monomials can contribute because each nonconstant centered factor has order
at least one. Each contribution is a product of field-jet coefficients and
hence has connected components only from \(\mathcal S\).

The derivation \(\mathscr D_n\) maps a homogeneous tensor polynomial of
degree \(d\) to one of degree \(d+2\). Thus
\(\mathscr D_n^{2m-1}f_n\) is homogeneous of degree \(4m+2\). Comparing the
coefficient of \(\lambda^{4m+2}\) in the analytic version of (7.5) again
places it in \(\mathcal A_{\mathcal S}^{(n)}\), contradicting (6.6). This
proves Theorem 1(2).

## 9. Physical gradient flow

For \(y\ne0\), split

\[
\mathscr X_n=2\eta y\mathscr D_n-2\eta f_n\mathscr D_n.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The first summand raises tensor degree by two; the second raises it by six.
Hence the lowest-degree homogeneous component of
\(\mathscr X_n^k f_n\) is uniquely

\[
(2\eta y)^k\mathscr D_n^kf_n.
\tag{9.2}
\]

At \(k=2m-1\), its \(\Gamma_m\) coefficient is nonzero, so the preceding
argument applies degree by degree.

For \(y=0\), write

\[
\mathscr X_n^kf_n=(-2\eta)^kQ_k,\qquad
Q_0=f_n,\qquad Q_{k+1}=f_n\mathscr D_nQ_k.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Induction shows that \(Q_k\) contains
\(f_n^k\mathscr D_n^kf_n\) with positive coefficient: applying
\(f_n\mathscr D_n\) to that term produces
\(f_n^{k+1}\mathscr D_n^{k+1}f_n\), and all other graph coefficients remain
nonnegative. For \(k=2m-1\), one connected component of this term is
\(\Gamma_m\). Choosing \(m\) beyond \(\mathcal S\) gives the same stable
graph contradiction. This proves Theorem 1(3).

## 10. Counterexamples to a broad PDE no-go

### 10.1 Feature time: an exact local-germ PDE

Let \(\Phi_n^s\) be the local feature flow generated by (2.2). For a current
state \(\theta\), define the germ at \(s=0\) of one scalar profile

\[
E_n(\theta)(s)=f_n(\Phi_n^s\theta).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Starting from \(\theta_0\), set

\[
U_n(t,s)=E_n(\Phi_n^t\theta_0)(s)
=f_n(\Phi_n^{t+s}\theta_0).
\tag{10.2}
\]

Wherever \(t,s,t+s\) lie in the local existence interval, the flow property
gives

\[
\partial_tU_n=\partial_sU_n,\qquad
U_n(t,0)=f_n(\Phi_n^t\theta_0),
\tag{10.3}
\]

and

\[
E_n(\Phi_n^t\theta)(s)=E_n(\theta)(s+t).
\tag{10.4}
\]

Thus translation of germs is autonomous and restartable for every
sufficiently small time for which the translated germ is defined.

The one-field count, differential order, and degree are independent of
\(n\). A common germ radius exists on a common operator-norm neighborhood.
On \(\mathbb R\oplus(\mathbb R^n)^{\oplus3}\), define

\[
\mathcal C=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&a^{\mathsf T}\\
x&0&0&0\\
0&B&0&0\\
0&0&R&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10.5a}
\]

Then (2.2) is

\[
\mathcal C'=(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3.
\tag{10.5}
\]

On \(\|\mathcal C\|,\|\widetilde{\mathcal C}\|\le2\varepsilon\),

\[
\|(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3\|\le8\varepsilon^3,\qquad
\|(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3-(\widetilde{\mathcal C}^{\mathsf T})^3\|
\le12\varepsilon^2\|\mathcal C-\widetilde{\mathcal C}\|.
\tag{10.6}
\]

For initial norm at most \(\varepsilon\) and
\(|t|\le(24\varepsilon^2)^{-1}\), the Picard integral map stays inside the
\(2\varepsilon\)-ball because
\(\varepsilon+8\varepsilon^3|t|\le4\varepsilon/3\), and its contraction
constant is at most \(12\varepsilon^2|t|\le1/2\). This proves the asserted
uniform local flow directly.

Moreover,

\[
\partial_s^kE_n(\theta)(0)=\mathscr D_n^kf_n(\theta).
\tag{10.7}
\]

By (6.6), the \((2m-1)\)-st spatial jet contains \(\Gamma_m\). The one field
has hidden an unbounded graph alphabet in its infinite Taylor jet and
therefore violates exactly (3.2). This is a local-germ representation, not a
claim that feature flow is complete.

### 10.2 Physical time: a global fixed-domain PDE

For physical time the counterexample is a genuine forward semigroup on a
fixed field space, because the finite-width physical flow is global. With
\(\mathcal C\) as above, the four diagonal blocks of \(\mathcal C^4\) all
have trace \(f_n\), and (2.3) gives
\[
f_n=\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal C^4,\qquad
\dot{\mathcal C}=2\eta e(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3.
\tag{10.7a}
\]
Put

\[
K=\|(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,\qquad
e=y-\tfrac14\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal C^4.
\tag{10.8}
\]

Direct differentiation of the four parameter blocks gives

\[
\dot e=-2\eta eK,\qquad
\frac d{dt}e^2=-4\eta e^2K.
\tag{10.9}
\]

Every length-three path around the four-cycle contains an endpoint rank-one
block, so \(\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C^3\le4\). Consequently
\(\|(\mathcal C^{\mathsf T})^3\|_1\le2\sqrt K\). For every finite \(T\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal C(T)-\mathcal C(0)\|_1
&\le4\eta\int_0^T|e|\sqrt K\,dt\\
&\le4\eta\sqrt T
\left(\int_0^Te^2K\,dt\right)^{1/2}\\
&\le2|e(0)|\sqrt{\eta T}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.10}
\]

The last inequality uses (10.9). Thus the finite-dimensional state remains
bounded on every finite interval. The locally Lipschitz polynomial ODE
therefore extends for all \(t\ge0\): if a maximal finite endpoint existed,
the state would remain in a compact ball on which the vector field is
bounded and Lipschitz, so one more local Picard step would extend it.

Let \(\Psi_n^t\) denote this global physical flow and define, on the fixed
half-line,

\[
\widehat E_n(\theta)(s)=f_n(\Psi_n^s\theta),
\qquad s\in[0,\infty).
\tag{10.11}
\]

On \(C([0,\infty))\) with the compact-open topology, right translation
\(S_tu(s)=u(s+t)\) is a continuous semigroup: on every compact \(s\)-interval,
uniform continuity on the slightly larger compact interval gives continuity
in \(t\). Along the physical flow,

\[
\widehat E_n(\Psi_n^t\theta)=S_t\widehat E_n(\theta),\qquad
\partial_tU=\partial_sU,\qquad
U(t,0)=f_n(\Psi_n^t\theta).
\tag{10.12}
\]

For \(C^1\) profiles this PDE is uniquely solved by
\(U(t,s)=U(0,t+s)\). Indeed, along
\(\tau\mapsto(t-\tau,s+\tau)\), the derivative is
\(-U_t+U_s=0\). Hence (10.12) is an autonomous, restartable, globally
well-posed forward PDE on a fixed domain.

Together, the local feature-germ construction and the global physical
construction show that locality, finite differential order, and fixed field
count do not by themselves imply finite information. Neither construction
uses the width-limit IDE or an external theorem.

## 11. A quantitative necessity theorem

The proof gives more than a dichotomy. Suppose an order-\(r\) PDE with a
readout of order \(\rho\) is exact and assume this width-uniform local
finiteness condition: for every fixed jet cutoff \(J\), the union over all
widths, fields, and \(|\beta|\le J\) of the graph supports of
\(\partial^\beta U_{\alpha,n}\) is finite. This permits the support and its
maximum degree to grow with \(J\), but prevents a fixed low-order jet from
hiding width-dependent arbitrarily long words. Formula (7.5) shows that
\(\mathscr D_n^k f_n\) uses jets only through order at most \(\rho+kr\).

At \(k=2m-1\), the relevant finite jet cutoff has one graph alphabet
independent of width, so Lemma 2 applies in its stable range. The connected
graph \(\Gamma_m\) cannot be created as a product of smaller connected
components. Stable independence therefore forces at least one participating
spatial jet of order at most

\[
\rho+(2m-1)r
\tag{11.1}
\]

to contain \(\Gamma_m\) already. Its contraction degree is at least
\(4m+2\). Thus every exact PDE must pay for the ladder through unbounded
contraction complexity in its spatial jet.

For \(r=0\), (11.1) says no new spatial jet is ever accessible and recovers
the finite-scalar ODE no-go.

## 12. Final logical boundary

The rigorous resolution is

\[
\boxed{\text{no fixed finite-jet local PDE over a finite bounded-degree
contraction alphabet}}
\tag{12.1}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{\text{one fixed scalar transport PDE suffices if arbitrary spatial
profiles are allowed}.}
\tag{12.2}
\]

Accordingly, the sentence “the three-hidden-layer linear model has no PDE
with \(O(1)\) scalar fields” is false. The correct negative theorem is
(12.1). It is sharp: the spatial jet, not the number of displayed fields, is
where the missing infinitely many ordered-word coordinates can reside.
