# A single-source autonomous MSE closure for the depth-two linear network

Status: theorem and proof, 20 August 2026.

## 1. Model and answer

Take one unit-normalized sample and a bias-free, equal-width network with two
trainable identity hidden layers.  After projecting the first-layer weights
onto that one sample, its output is exactly

\[
f_n(A,W,u)=n^{-3/2}A^T W u,
\qquad A,u\in\mathbb R^n,\quad W\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

All entries of `A(0)`, `u(0)`, and `W(0)` are independent `N(0,1)`.  Every
layer is trained by the full-MSE muP gradient flow

\[
\dot\theta=-\eta n\nabla_\theta (y_\star-f_n)^2,
\qquad \eta>0.
\tag{1.2}
\]

There is a deterministic, autonomous, single-source integro-differential
description of the width-first loss curve.  It has one complex spectral field
and its velocity (equivalently four real scalar fields), one scalar residual,
and one fixed scalar source measure.  None of these objects grows with `n` or
with a requested Taylor order.

Let

\[
\Lambda=\{-\tfrac12\}\cup(0,4)
\]

and define the single source

\[
\boxed{
d\nu(\lambda)=\frac34\,\delta_{-1/2}(d\lambda)
+\frac{(1+\lambda)\sqrt{\lambda(4-\lambda)}}
       {\pi(1+2\lambda)}\mathbf 1_{(0,4)}(\lambda)\,d\lambda .}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Define real initialization profiles `alpha` and `beta` by

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\alpha&\beta\\ \hline
\lambda=-\tfrac12&1&0,\\[2mm]
0<\lambda<4&[2(1+\lambda)]^{-1/2}&
\left[\dfrac{1+2\lambda}{2(1+\lambda)}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{array}
\tag{1.4}
\]

For a complex field `psi(lambda,t)`, its velocity `pi(lambda,t)`, and a real
residual `e(t)`, set

\[
r(t)=\int_\Lambda |\psi(\lambda,t)|^2\,d\nu(\lambda),
\tag{1.5}
\]

\[
K(t)=\int_\Lambda
\left\{|\pi(\lambda,t)|^2+
[\lambda+2r(t)]|\psi(\lambda,t)|^2\right\}
\,d\nu(\lambda).
\tag{1.6}
\]

The closed physical-time system is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\psi(\lambda,t)&=2\eta e(t)\pi(\lambda,t),\\
\partial_t\pi(\lambda,t)&=2\eta e(t)
[\lambda+2r(t)]\psi(\lambda,t),\\
\dot e(t)&=-2\eta e(t)K(t),
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1.7}
\]

with

\[
\psi(\lambda,0)=\alpha(\lambda),\qquad
\pi(\lambda,0)=i\beta(\lambda),\qquad e(0)=y_\star.
\tag{1.8}
\]

The desired observables are immediate:

\[
\boxed{
f(t)=y_\star-e(t)
=\operatorname{Re}\int_\Lambda\overline{\psi}\,\pi\,d\nu,
\qquad L(t)=e(t)^2.}
\tag{1.9}
\]

In particular,

\[
\dot L(t)=-4\eta K(t)L(t).
\tag{1.10}
\]

If the loss is instead normalized as \(\tfrac12(y_\star-f)^2\), replace
every factor \(2\eta\) in (1.7) by \(\eta\).  Nothing else changes; this is
only a physical-time rescaling.

Complex notation only combines two real initialization channels.  Thus
(1.7) is a real four-field IDE plus one scalar ODE if complex fields are
undesired.

## 2. Precise mean-field theorem

**Theorem.**  Under (1.1)--(1.2), system (1.7)--(1.8) has a unique global
solution for every fixed `y_star` in `R`.  For every finite `T`,

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left(|f_n(t)-f(t)|+|(y_\star-f_n(t))^2-L(t)|\right)
\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathbb P}0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Moreover \(K(t)>0\), the sign of \(e(t)\) is preserved, and the training
vector field vanishes exactly when \(e(t)=0\).  Also,

\[
e(t)\longrightarrow0,\qquad L(t)\longrightarrow0
\quad(t\to\infty).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The order in (2.1) is width first on compact physical-time intervals.  The
long-time assertion (2.2) is then an assertion about the limiting system; it
does not assert a uniform interchange of `n -> infinity` and `t -> infinity`.

## 3. Exact finite-width spectral reduction

Introduce normalized variables

\[
x=A/\sqrt n,\qquad y=u/\sqrt n,\qquad B=W/\sqrt n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The output is `f_n=x^TBy`.  Let `s` denote feature-ascent time, namely the
flow generated by `D_n=n grad(f_n) dot grad`.  Direct differentiation gives

\[
x_s=By,\qquad y_s=B^Tx,\qquad B_s=xy^T.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Two exact invariants are

\[
C_n=BB^T-xx^T,
\qquad \delta_n=\|x\|^2-\|y\|^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed, both derivatives vanish after substituting (3.2).  Consequently

\[
x_{ss}=(C_n+\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2)x
=(C_n+2r_n-\delta_n)x,
\qquad r_n=\|x\|^2,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and

\[
f_n=x^Tx_s=\tfrac12(r_n)_s.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The corresponding scalar feature kernel is exactly

\[
K_n=(f_n)_s=n\|\nabla f_n\|^2
=\|By\|^2+\|B^Tx\|^2+\|x\|^2\|y\|^2\ge0.
\tag{3.5a}
\]

Let `E_n` be the spectral resolution of the fixed matrix `C_n`, put
`x_0=x(0)` and `v_0=B(0)y(0)`, and define the positive `2 by 2`
matrix-valued spectral measure

\[
\Sigma_n(S)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\langle x_0,E_n(S)x_0\rangle&
\langle x_0,E_n(S)v_0\rangle\\
\langle v_0,E_n(S)x_0\rangle&
\langle v_0,E_n(S)v_0\rangle
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For the vector mode `U_n=(a_n,b_n)^T`, solve

\[
(U_n)_{ss}=(\lambda+2r_n-\delta_n)U_n,
\qquad U_n(\lambda,0)=\binom{1}{0},
\quad (U_n)_s(\lambda,0)=\binom{0}{1}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Functional calculus in (3.4) gives the exact identities

\[
x(s)=a_n(C_n,s)x_0+b_n(C_n,s)v_0,
\tag{3.8}
\]

\[
r_n(s)=\int U_n(\lambda,s)^T\,d\Sigma_n(\lambda)\,
U_n(\lambda,s).
\tag{3.9}
\]

The output and feature kernel are read from the same state:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_n(s)&=\int U_n^T\,d\Sigma_n\,(U_n)_s,\\
K_n(s)&=\int (U_n)_s^T\,d\Sigma_n\,(U_n)_s
+\int(\lambda+2r_n-\delta_n)U_n^T\,d\Sigma_n\,U_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus finite width already has an exact spectral oscillator.  Its source has
`n` atoms, however, so (3.6) by itself would not meet the frozen O(1) target.

## 4. Deterministic limit of the source

Let `M_n=B(0)B(0)^T`.  The Marchenko--Pastur theorem and the extreme-eigenvalue
theorem apply because `B(0)` is square with iid centered entries of variance
`1/n` and finite fourth moment.  Hence its empirical spectral measure tends
to

\[
d\rho_{MP}(\lambda)=\frac1{2\pi}
\sqrt{\frac{4-\lambda}{\lambda}}
\mathbf1_{(0,4)}(\lambda)d\lambda,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and `||M_n|| -> 4` in probability (in fact almost surely).  The rank-one
perturbation in `C_n=M_n-x_0x_0^T` does not change its empirical limit.

For `z` off the real spectrum, put

\[
a_n(z)=x_0^T(z-M_n)^{-1}x_0.
\]

Conditional Gaussian concentration and (4.1) give
`a_n(z) -> m_MP(z)`, where the resolvent convention is

\[
m_{MP}(z)=\int\frac{d\rho_{MP}(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}.
\]

Sherman--Morrison now yields

\[
x_0^T(z-C_n)^{-1}x_0
=\frac{a_n(z)}{1+a_n(z)}
\longrightarrow \frac{m_{MP}(z)}{1+m_{MP}(z)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Stieltjes inversion of (4.2), including the pole where `m_MP=-1`, gives

\[
d\rho_x(\lambda)=\frac34\delta_{-1/2}(d\lambda)
+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda(4-\lambda)}}
{2\pi(1+2\lambda)}\mathbf1_{(0,4)}(\lambda)d\lambda.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For completeness, the pole is at `lambda=-1/2`; writing
`z=1/[m(1-m)]` shows that the residue of `m/(1+m)` there is `3/4`.

For every polynomial `p`, Gaussian concentration in the independent vector
`y_0` gives

\[
v_0^Tp(C_n)v_0-\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}[M_np(C_n)]
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

Since `M_n=C_n+x_0x_0^T`, the rank-one term disappears after normalized
trace.  Therefore

\[
d\rho_v(\lambda)=\lambda\,d\rho_{MP}(\lambda)
=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda(4-\lambda)}}{2\pi}
\mathbf1_{(0,4)}(\lambda)d\lambda.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Finally, conditional centering in `y_0` and

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{y_0}
[x_0^Tp(C_n)B(0)y_0]
\le \frac1n\|B(0)\|^2\|p(C_n)\|^2\|x_0\|^2
\]

make the cross spectral measure vanish.  Polynomial approximation on a
common high-probability compact spectral interval then proves entrywise weak
convergence

\[
d\Sigma_n\Longrightarrow
d\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}d\rho_x&0\\0&d\rho_v\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \delta_n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equations (1.3)--(1.4) are exactly the scalar-source encoding of (4.5):

\[
\nu=\rho_x+\rho_v,\qquad
\alpha^2\nu=\rho_x,\qquad \beta^2\nu=\rho_v.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The real and imaginary parts of `psi` therefore reproduce the two diagonal
channels without a cross term.

The remaining initialization quantities also converge with the required
normalization:

\[
\delta_n\to0,\qquad f_n(0)=x_0^TB(0)y_0\to0,
\qquad r(0)=1,\quad F(0)=0,\quad K(0)=3.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The first two limits are laws of large numbers (the output has variance
\(O(n^{-1})\)); the last three follow from (4.3)--(4.6), including
\(\int\lambda\,d\rho_x=0\).  Thus the negative atom is part of the exact
source but does not spoil the initial kernel normalization.

## 5. Positive-time existence and identification

First ignore physical MSE time and solve the limiting feature oscillator

\[
\psi_s=\pi,\qquad
\pi_s=(\lambda+2r)\psi,
\qquad r=\int|\psi|^2d\nu,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with (1.8)'s field data.  On

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(\nu;\mathbb C)\times L^2(\nu;\mathbb C),
\]

multiplication by \(\lambda\) is bounded because
\(\operatorname{supp}\nu\subset[-1/2,4]\).  The right-hand side of (5.1) is
locally Lipschitz: \(r\) is a continuous quadratic form and the remaining operations
are bounded multilinear maps.  Picard--Lindelof therefore supplies a unique
maximal solution.

The same observation applies directly to (1.7) on
\(\mathcal H\times\mathbb R\); its vector field is autonomous and locally
Lipschitz.  In particular, the current triple \((\psi,\pi,e)\) is a restart
state; no past trajectory or externally supplied time change is present.

It remains to justify that this positive-time solution, rather than only its
formal jet, is the mean-field limit.  For this step use the equivalent
two-real-channel form of (5.1), with source \(d\Sigma\) and initial data
\(U=(1,0)^T,\ U_s=(0,1)^T\).  Work on a high-probability event on which
all finite-width spectra lie in one fixed compact interval and the total
variations of the four entries of \(\Sigma_n\) are uniformly bounded.
Such events have probability tending to one by the operator-norm limit and
the laws of large numbers for \(x_0,v_0\).

Fix a compact feature interval \([0,S]\) inside the maximal interval of
the limit and stop the finite-width solution if its field norm reaches
twice a bound for the limiting field.  For the stopped solution,

\[
\begin{aligned}
|r_n(q)-r(q)|
&\le C_S\|U_n(\cdot,q)-U(\cdot,q)\|_\infty+\varepsilon_n(S),\\
\varepsilon_n(S)
&=\sup_{0\le q\le S}\left|
\int U(\lambda,q)^T[d\Sigma_n-d\Sigma]U(\lambda,q)
\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The second limit follows from weak convergence because
\(\{U(\cdot,q)U(\cdot,q)^T:0\le q\le S\}\) is compact in the uniform
topology; a finite uniform net reduces the supremum to finitely many weak
convergence tests.  Subtracting the integral forms of the two mode systems
therefore gives

\[
E_n(S)\le c_S
\left(\varepsilon_n(S)+|\delta_n|
+\int_0^S E_n(q)dq\right),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where \(E_n\) is the supremum error of the fields and scalar readouts.
Gronwall's inequality makes \(E_n(S)\to0\) in probability.  The resulting error is smaller than
the stopping margin, so the stopping time exceeds \(S\); finitely many
overlapping intervals give every compact subinterval of the maximal
feature-time interval.  This closes the positive-time step without an
analyticity or Taylor-series assumption.

Physical gradient flow is not an independent assumption.  From (1.2),

\[
\frac{ds}{dt}=2\eta(y_\star-F(s)),
\qquad F(s)=\frac12r_s(s)
=\operatorname{Re}\int\overline\psi\,\pi\,d\nu.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Applying the chain rule to (5.1) and differentiating
`e=y_star-F` gives exactly (1.7).  Conversely, local uniqueness shows that
every solution of (1.7) is this reparameterized feature solution.  For any
finite physical horizon, the limiting clock has compact image strictly
inside the maximal feature interval.  Uniform convergence of \(F_n\) there
and Gronwall stability of the scalar clock ODE keep the finite clocks in the
same compact interval and prove (2.1).

One can also verify the loss constraint internally, without invoking (5.4):

\[
\frac d{dt}\operatorname{Re}\int\overline\psi\pi d\nu
=2\eta eK=-\dot e.
\]

Its initial value is zero, so (1.9) is preserved by the autonomous IDE.

## 6. Why physical time is global

Let `F` and `K=F_s` refer to feature time.  From (5.1),

\[
K=\int\{|\pi|^2+(\lambda+2r)|\psi|^2\}\,d\nu
\ge 2r^2-\tfrac12r.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Initially `r=1` and `F=0`.  Since `r_s=2F`, (6.1) implies, by a first-exit
argument, that for positive feature time `r>=1`, `F>0`, and `K>0`.  Moreover

\[
r_{ss}=2K\ge 4r^2-r\ge3r^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Multiplying by `r_s>=0` gives

\[
(r_s)^2\ge2(r^3-1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed, the time needed to move from \(r=1\) to arbitrarily large \(r\) is
bounded by

\[
\int_1^\infty\frac{dq}{\sqrt{2(q^3-1)}}<\infty.
\tag{6.3a}
\]

Hence the feature solution blows up at a finite `S_star`, and
`F(s) -> +infinity` as `s` increases to `S_star`.  The diagonal two-channel
initialization and uniqueness give
\(\psi(-s)=\overline{\psi(s)}\) and
\(\pi(-s)=-\overline{\pi(s)}\).  Hence \(r\) is even and \(F\) is odd, so

\[
F:(-S_\star,S_\star)\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]

is a strictly increasing bijection.

For any finite label, let `s_star=F^{-1}(y_star)`.  The physical clock solves

\[
\dot s=2\eta[y_\star-F(s)].
\tag{6.4}
\]

It approaches `s_star` monotonically but cannot reach it in finite time:
because `K(s_star)>0`, the integral of
`1/[y_star-F(s)]` diverges logarithmically at `s_star`.  Thus physical time is
global even though raw feature-ascent time has a finite blow-up.  Equations
(2.2) follow, and more precisely

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log|e(t)|=-2\eta K(s_\star),
\qquad
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log L(t)=-4\eta K(s_\star).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Formula (6.5) is for \(y_\star\ne0\).  For `y_star=0`, the limiting
network is already at zero loss and (1.7) is stationary.

## 7. Relation to the Stieltjes specialization

Because `F` is strictly increasing, one may eliminate feature time and write

\[
\mathcal K(z)=K(F^{-1}(z)),\qquad
\dot f=2\eta(y_\star-f)\mathcal K(f).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The Stieltjes question concerns a particular transformed Taylor-moment
representation of `mathcal K`.  It is therefore a specialization of the
broader closure question, not a prerequisite for (1.7).  The integral in
(1.5)--(1.6) retains the entire continuous spectral source and hence all of
its moments at once.  No Hankel positivity claim is used anywhere in the
existence, identification, or loss theorem.

The dynamical source \(\nu\) is not a proposed Stieltjes representing
measure for the output-kernel moments \(\mu_j\).  In particular, its atom at
\(-1/2\) creates no conflict with the Stieltjes conjecture.

The earlier exact Taylor computation through `F^(81)(0)` is an independent
cross-check of this closure, but the positive-time result rests on the
finite-width invariant, source convergence, and ODE stability above rather
than on formal-series matching.

## 8. Claim boundary

This proves an O(1) **field** closure: one fixed one-dimensional spectral
domain, one complex field/velocity pair, one scalar residual, and one
explicit source measure.  It does not claim a finite-dimensional scalar ODE
with no continuum coordinate, an elementary closed form for `F` or `K`, or
the all-order Stieltjes property.  None of those stronger statements is
needed for the requested autonomous mean-field MSE description.
