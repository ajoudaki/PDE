# Independent derivation: exact depth-three skeleton

Status: derived without inspecting any depth-three project study or accepted
derivative value, 20 August 2026.

## 1. Normalized feature flow

Put

\[
x=A/\sqrt n,\qquad D=V/\sqrt n,\qquad
B=W/\sqrt n,\qquad y=u/\sqrt n.
\]

Then

\[
f=x^TDB y.
\]

For feature time \(s\), generated by \(n\nabla f\cdot\nabla\), direct
differentiation of the four parameter blocks gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
x'&=DB y,\\
D'&=x(By)^T,\\
B'&=(D^Tx)y^T,\\
y'&=B^TD^Tx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
\]

With

\[
z=By,\qquad p=D^Tx,\qquad P=DB,\qquad
a=\|x\|^2,\quad b=\|y\|^2,
\]

the prediction is \(f=p^Tz=x^TPy\), and

\[
x'=Py,\qquad y'=P^Tx,\qquad D'=xz^T,\qquad B'=py^T.
\tag{1.2}
\]

All identities in this note are exact at finite width.

## 2. Conserved adjacent Gram differences

The three balanced-Gram differences

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_L&=DD^T-xx^T,\\
C_M&=D^TD-BB^T,\\
C_R&=B^TB-yy^T
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

are constant.  For example,

\[
(DD^T)'=xz^TD^T+Dz x^T=x(x')^T+x'x^T=(xx^T)'.
\]

The other two cancellations are

\[
(D^TD)'=zp^T+pz^T=(BB^T)',
\]

\[
(B^TB)'=y(B^Tp)^T+(B^Tp)y^T=(yy^T)'.
\]

Also

\[
a'=b'=2f,\qquad \delta=a-b
\tag{2.2}
\]

is conserved.  These are the exact analogues of the depth-two balanced
invariant, but they live on three different layer spaces and do not commute
through the intervening maps.

## 3. Exact product-map reduction

The effective middle product \(P=DB\) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
P'
&=D'B+DB'\\
&=xy^TB^TB+DD^Txy^T\\
&=C_Lxy^T+xy^TC_R+(a+b)xy^T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the complete four-map flow projects exactly to

\[
\begin{aligned}
x'&=Py,\\
y'&=P^Tx,\\
P'&=C_Lxy^T+xy^TC_R+qxy^T,\\
q&=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2,\qquad q'=4x^TPy.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The middle invariant \(C_M\) is not lost: together with the original maps it
determines the relative position of \(P(0)\) with respect to \(C_L,C_R\).
After \(P(0)\) is fixed, (3.2) needs only \(C_L,C_R\).

The exact feature-kernel readout is

\[
\begin{aligned}
K=f'
&=\|DB y\|^2+\|B^TD^Tx\|^2
  +\|x\|^2\|By\|^2+\|D^Tx\|^2\|y\|^2\\
&=\|Py\|^2+\|P^Tx\|^2+a\|z\|^2+b\|p\|^2
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

## 4. What diagonalizing the constant outer invariants gives

Choose orthogonal eigenbases

\[
C_L=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^T,\qquad
C_R=V\operatorname{diag}(\mu_j)V^T
\]

and set

\[
X=U^Tx,\qquad Y=V^Ty,\qquad Q=U^TPV.
\]

Equation (3.2) becomes

\[
\begin{aligned}
X_i'&=\sum_jQ_{ij}Y_j,\\
Y_j'&=\sum_iQ_{ij}X_i,\\
Q_{ij}'&=(\lambda_i+\mu_j+q)X_iY_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is an exact current-time description.  It is not an admissible
compression: \(Q\) has \(n^2\) entries, and its initial entries retain the
relative eigenvector information between the two invariant layer spaces.

The natural rescaling makes the obstruction visible.  Typically

\[
X_i,Y_j,Q_{ij}=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Writing \(\xi_i=\sqrt nX_i\), \(\upsilon_j=\sqrt nY_j\), and
\(\Gamma_{ij}=\sqrt nQ_{ij}\) gives

\[
\xi_i'=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_j\Gamma_{ij}\upsilon_j,\qquad
\upsilon_j'=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_i\Gamma_{ij}\xi_i,\qquad
\Gamma_{ij}'=\frac{\lambda_i+\mu_j+q}{\sqrt n}\xi_i\upsilon_j.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The initial kernel is therefore in random-operator scaling, not graphon
scaling: its entries vanish pointwise while its collective action remains
order one.  Replacing it by its pointwise or classical averaged limit gives
zero and destroys even the leading dynamics.

### 4.1 Exact initialization decomposition and the Haar bridge

The obstruction can be isolated exactly.  At \(s=0\), write

\[
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i),\qquad
M=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_j),\qquad
X=U^Tx,\qquad Y=V^Ty.
\]

Because

\[
U^TDD^TU=\Lambda+XX^T,\qquad
V^TB^TBV=M+YY^T,
\]

the left and right polar decompositions of the independent Gaussian
matrices give, in distribution,

\[
U^TD=(\Lambda+XX^T)^{1/2}O_L,\qquad
BV=O_R(M+YY^T)^{1/2},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where \(O_L,O_R\) are independent Haar orthogonal matrices, independent of
the displayed outer spectral data.  This independence follows directly
from the left/right orthogonal invariance of a square iid Gaussian matrix:
conditional on its Gram matrix, its unused polar factor is Haar.

Consequently

\[
Q(0)=(\Lambda+XX^T)^{1/2}
H_n
(M+YY^T)^{1/2},
\qquad H_n=O_LO_R,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where \(H_n\) is an independent Haar orthogonal bridge.  The two outer
endpoint spectral measures separately have the explicit depth-two limit

\[
d\rho_x(\lambda)=\frac34\delta_{-1/2}(d\lambda)
+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda(4-\lambda)}}{2\pi(1+2\lambda)}
\mathbf1_{(0,4)}(\lambda)d\lambda,
\tag{4.5}
\]

but these two marginal measures do not determine the bridge \(H_n\).

The bridge has no nonzero pointwise deterministic kernel limit.  Its
individual entries tend to zero, whereas for every vector \(v\),
\(\|H_nv\|=\|v\|\).  Thus pointwise averaging loses an order-one action.
Keeping \(H_n\) itself is a width-dependent matrix and violates the
contract.

### 4.2 Why a finite marginal spectral list does not repair this

A first cross-spectral object can be written schematically as

\[
d\chi_1(\lambda,\mu)
=x^TE_L(d\lambda)P(0)E_R(d\mu)y.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Repeated differentiation of (4.1) composes the initial bridge in alternating
directions.  It therefore produces objects of the form

\[
x^TE_L(d\lambda_0)P(0)E_R(d\mu_1)P(0)^T
E_L(d\lambda_2)\cdots P(0)E_R(d\mu_k)y
\tag{4.7}
\]

with unbounded alternating length.  A measure such as (4.6) records one
crossing but not (4.7) at arbitrary length.  Recording all lengths is the
forbidden noncommutative word hierarchy.

This does not prove that no admissible resummation exists.  It identifies
the exact requirement on one: an explicit finite collection of classical
source fields and current equations must resum the Haar-bridge compositions
without retaining \(H_n\), a two-time response kernel, or an unbounded word
index.

## 5. Cyclic polynomial identity

Let the directed block-cycle matrix on
\(\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\oplus\mathbb R^n\) be

\[
\mathcal Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&x^T\\
y&0&0&0\\
0&B&0&0\\
0&0&D&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Each block of \((\mathcal Q^3)^T\) is the gradient of the four-cycle product
with respect to the corresponding block.  Hence (1.1) is exactly

\[
\mathcal Q'=(\mathcal Q^3)^T.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\Delta=\mathcal Q^T\mathcal Q-\mathcal Q\mathcal Q^T
\tag{5.3}
\]

is constant, because both Gram derivatives equal
\(\mathcal Q^4+(\mathcal Q^T)^4\).  Its diagonal blocks are precisely the
adjacent balanced-Gram differences (and the endpoint norm difference).

Equations (5.2)--(5.3) are a compact finite-width algebraic identity, but
\(\mathcal Q\) is a width-dependent nonnormal operator.  Treating its
limiting noncommutative law or an abstract operator realization as one state
variable is explicitly forbidden by the contract.  Route R3 is admissible
only if (5.2) can be reduced further to finitely many scalar fields on an
explicit classical source.

The conserved self-commutator does not by itself provide that reduction.
The reordering identity

\[
\mathcal Q^T\mathcal Q=\mathcal Q\mathcal Q^T+\Delta
\tag{5.4}
\]

introduces \(\Delta\), but \(\Delta\) is not central:
\([\Delta,\mathcal Q(0)]\ne0\) with probability one.  Differentiation then
generates

\[
\frac d{ds}[\Delta,\mathcal Q]
=[\Delta,(\mathcal Q^T)^3],
\tag{5.5}
\]

and subsequent derivatives generate iterated noncommutative words.  Keeping
their abstract operator closure would violate the contract.  This blocks
the present cyclic-spectral witness; it is not a theorem excluding a
different admissible resummation.

## 6. Endpoint second derivative and the middle-invariant obstruction

The direct analogue of the depth-two second-order reduction also stops at a
specific moving conjugate.  From

\[
z'=(BB^T+bI)p,\qquad p=D^Tx,
\]

and \(BB^T=D^TD-C_M\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
x''
&=x\|z\|^2+D(BB^T+bI)D^Tx\\
&=C_L^2x+(a+b)C_Lx\\
&\quad+
\bigl(\|z\|^2+x^TC_Lx+a^2+ab\bigr)x
-D C_M D^Tx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At depth two the corresponding equation contains only one fixed conserved
operator plus scalar integrals.  Here

\[
G(s)=D(s)C_M D(s)^T
\tag{6.2}
\]

is not constant.  Its derivative is

\[
G'=xw^T+wx^T,\qquad w=D C_M z,
\tag{6.3}
\]

and differentiating \(w\) introduces further actions of \(C_M\), \(B\), and
\(D\).  Diagonalizing \(C_M\) merely transfers the unresolved relative
basis information to the outer invariants.  Thus route R1 does not yield a
finite fixed-source oscillator by the depth-two elimination.

## 7. The natural scalar hierarchy does not close

The smallest scalar enlargement already produces new ordered contractions.
Define

\[
\alpha=x^TPP^Tx,\quad \beta=y^TP^TPy,\quad
\ell=x^TC_Lx,\quad r=y^TC_Ry.
\]

Then

\[
f'=\alpha+\beta+b\ell+ar+qab.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The invariant quadratic forms satisfy

\[
\ell'=2h_L,\qquad r'=2h_R,\qquad
h_L=x^TC_LPy,\quad h_R=x^TPC_Ry.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Differentiating the first two norm terms gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha'
&=2\,y^TP^TPP^Tx
+2\ell f+2a h_R+2qaf,\\
\beta'
&=2\,x^TPP^TPy
+2b h_L+2rf+2qbf.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus even this scalar route creates the ordered cubic contractions
\(y^TP^TPP^Tx\) and \(x^TPP^TPy\).  Their derivatives create longer words
and insert \(C_L,C_R\) in different positions.  No algebraic identity found
in Sections 2--6 reduces these words to a fixed finite scalar list.

This defeats the natural finite-Gram ODE witness, not the existence of every
possible finite-dimensional ODE.

## 8. Physical clock

For a single sample, the full-MSE parameter vector field is a scalar
multiple of the feature vector field.  Therefore every finite-width MSE
trajectory is an exact reparameterization:

\[
\frac{ds}{dt}=2\eta[y_\star-F_n(s)],\qquad
f_n(t)=F_n(s(t)),\qquad
L_n(t)=[y_\star-F_n(s(t))]^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus any admissible feature-time closure immediately gives an autonomous
physical-time closure by adjoining the scalar residual or clock.  The
unresolved problem is the admissible compression and width-limit
identification of (3.2), not the one-sample MSE time conversion.
