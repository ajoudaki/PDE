# Hostile audit of `FIXED_H_QUADRATIC_BRIDGE.md`

## Verdict

**The stopping-removal calculation is valid conditional on the asserted
arbitrary-moment stopped coupling, but the document does not prove that
coupling.**  In particular, the claimed \(64^\Lambda\) tower is not derived
from the actual chronological response grammar, and the claimed uniform
\(O(n^{-1/2})\) estimates for adaptive reused-matrix actions do not follow
from the four elementary scalar inequalities alone.

There is a second gap in the spectral argument: strict positivity of every
population Gram is asserted through an unproved “fresh Gaussian with
\(\tau>0\)” induction.  The claim is false at \(h=0\), which is included in
the proposition as written, and for \(h\ne0\) the proof that every required
conditional residual variance is positive is circular unless the exact
row/column chronology is exposed.

Thus the bridge does not yet close the unconditional quadratic width limit.
What it does prove is the following useful conditional lemma:

> If the stopped adaptive-conditioning construction has arbitrary fixed
> \(L^r\) error \(O(n^{-1/2})\), and if the relevant population spectral gap
> is positive, then the crude raw-network polynomial bound removes the
> stopping event and gives uniform integrability.

## 1. Population polynomial integrability

This part passes.  At fixed finite horizon, every OMFP value and source
derivative generated by a quadratic activation is a finite polynomial of a
finite Gaussian vector.  It therefore has every finite moment.  Gaussian
integration by parts remains valid at singular covariance by writing
\(X=BZ\) and integrating in the standard source \(Z\); no inverse covariance
is required.

This establishes integrability of the formal response identities.  It does
not establish invertibility of any empirical or population Gram.

## 2. The spectral-gap claim is not proved

The argument says that every new preactivation is \(x+\tau G\) with
\(\tau>0\), and then invokes polynomial dependence on the fresh \(G\).  But
\(\tau>0\) is exactly the positivity of the conditional residual variance
which the Gram-rank induction must prove.  For a reused \(W/W^T\) chronology,
one must show, at every row and column action, that the proposed fresh
innovation has not already fallen in the span of previous actions.  The
document does not list those actions or prove the assertion for them.

There is an immediate boundary counterexample to the proposition as stated:
at \(h=0\), all time copies of a feature coincide, so any Gram containing
two time copies is singular.  Therefore no
\(\gamma_{N,0,p,q}>0\) exists.  The no-go application only uses \(h>0\), so
this can be repaired by restricting the proposition, but the nonzero-step
rank induction is still missing.

The earlier singular-Gaussian identity (1.1) cannot repair this by itself.
One must either:

1. formulate the adaptive bridge with pseudoinverses/rank-restricted
   conditioning and prove constant rank, or
2. give the full chronological fresh-innovation proof for every Gram used.

## 3. Why the factor \(64\) is not established

The inequalities

\[
 \|\psi(X)\|_r\lesssim1+\|X\|_{2r}^2,
 \qquad
 \|XY\|_r\le\|X\|_{2r}\|Y\|_{2r}
\]

show only that any *specified finite polynomial expression* can be
controlled by some higher finite moment.  They do not prove that one
chronological OMFP entry costs at most a factor \(64\) in moment order.

Three missing checks matter:

- A response row contains all historical source derivatives, products with
  moving fields, and reused-adjoint correction terms.  The document does
  not expand this row and count the maximal product arity and number of
  nested quadratic compositions.
- A reused Gaussian matrix action is not an independent Gaussian action.
  Its conditional decomposition includes empirical Gram inverses,
  projections, response numerators, and a residual action.  An
  \(L^r\), \(O(n^{-1/2})\) perturbation estimate for that entire expression
  is asserted but not stated or proved.
- The claimed count
  \(\Lambda=3(2N+1)+1\) is unexplained.  At depth two there are
  \(O(N^2)\) historical Gram and response entries.  They may be bundled, but
  then one must give a vector/matrix norm estimate for the whole bundle;
  calling the count “micro-entries” is not consistent with the literal
  number of scalar entries.

Because of these omissions, the implication

\[
 \text{initial }L^{64^\Lambda r}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{all stopped errors are }O(n^{-1/2})\text{ in }L^r
\]

has not been proved.  Consequently the arbitrary-polynomial bad-event bound

\[
 \mathbb P(\mathcal B_n)\le C_m n^{-m}
\]

is also unproved.

This gap is repairable in principle without an all-order norm: for each
fixed \(N\), write a finite ledger of all chronological operations, assign
to each operation its exact polynomial/product moment cost, and recurse
backward from the requested terminal order.  A much larger finite tower
would be harmless.  But the finite ledger and its local estimates must
actually be supplied.

## 4. Audit of the crude raw-network bounds

Apart from typographical omissions in the displayed definition and (3.1),
the width powers in (3.2)--(3.5) are valid.  If

\[
 X_s=1+\max_i|a_i^s|+\max_j|u_j^s|+max_{i,j}|W_{ij}^s|,
\]

then

\[
\begin{aligned}
|H|&\lesssim X_s^2,\\
|z|&\lesssim \sqrt n X_s^3,\\
|\psi(z)|&\lesssim nX_s^6,\\
|C|&\lesssim \sqrt n X_s^4,\\
|b|&\lesssim nX_s^5.
\end{aligned}
\]

The three updates imply

\[
 X_{s+1}\le KnX_s^6,
\]

and hence, with \(c_s=(6^s-1)/5\),

\[
 X_s\le K^{c_s}n^{c_s}X_0^{6^s}.
\]

The terminal average obeys

\[
 |f_{N,n}|\lesssim nX_N^7
 \lesssim n^{1+7c_N}X_0^{7\cdot6^N}
\]

up to a fixed \((N,h,p,q)\)-constant.  Since at most \(n^2+2n\) independent
Gaussians enter \(X_0\),

\[
 \mathbb EX_0^r\le C_rn^2
\]

for each fixed \(r\).  There is no hidden superpolynomial factor in \(n\)
here.

Assume now the unproved estimate
\(\mathbb P(\mathcal B_n)\le C_mn^{-m}\).  Let

\[
 A_N=1+7c_N,qquad B_N=7\cdot6^N.
\]

For the document's choice \(1+\delta=3/2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[|f_{N,n}|^{3/2}\mathbf1_{\mathcal B_n}]
&\le(\mathbb E|f_{N,n}|^3)^{1/2}
       \mathbb P(\mathcal B_n)^{1/2}\\
&\le C n^{(3A_N+2-m)/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus choosing \(m>3A_N+2\) makes the bad-event term vanish.  The same
argument works for any finite collection of raw polynomial fields after
increasing \(m\).  Therefore the stopping-removal step genuinely closes
and leaves no hidden \(n\)-factor **if** the arbitrary-\(m\) probability
bound has first been proved.

## 5. Uniform integrability

On the good event, an actual stopped coupling bounded in some
\(L^{1+\epsilon}\), together with convergence in probability, would give
uniform integrability.  Section 4 makes the complementary bad-event
\(L^{1+\delta}\) mass vanish.  This final implication is correct.

But “the identical argument applies to every response numerator” needs a
finite polynomial degree bound for those numerators.  Such a bound exists at
fixed horizon once the exact response recursion is written, but it is not
written here.  This is another manifestation of the missing finite ledger,
not a flaw in the max-bound idea.

## Required repairs

Before this bridge can be used as an unconditional input to the no-go
theorem, it needs:

1. restriction to \(h\ne0\), or a singular-Gram formulation at \(h=0\);
2. an explicit noncircular rank/innovation induction for the exact reused
   row/column chronology;
3. a complete finite operation ledger at depth two;
4. a proved local \(L^r\) stability estimate for each listed adaptive
   conditioning operation, including its inverse/pseudoinverse terms;
5. a backward moment-order recursion derived from that ledger (the number
   \(64^\Lambda\) may be replaced by any explicit finite tower); and
6. the resulting proof of arbitrary-order
   \(O(n^{-1/2})\) Gram/response errors and the bad-event estimate.

Until then, `FIXED_H_QUADRATIC_BRIDGE.md` is a plausible proof program plus
a correct stopping-removal lemma, not a complete bridge.
