# Hostile audit: normalized quadratic and ReLU loss-gradient mesh limits

## 1. Contract and limit order

Use the `q=1,L=2` network

\[
 H_j=\phi(u_j),\qquad z_i=n^{-1/2}\sum_jW_{ij}H_j,
 \qquad f_n=n^{-1}\sum_i a_i\phi(z_i),
\]

with iid standard-Gaussian initialization and the usual constant muP
metric.  For the half-square one-sample loss

\[
 \ell_n=\frac12(1-f_n)^2,
\]

one Euler loss-descent step is

\[
 \theta^{s+1}=\theta^s+h(1-f_n^s)\nabla_{\mu P}f_n^s. \tag{1.1}
\]

The width-first mesh question means:

1. for every fixed finite schedule, take `n -> infinity`;
2. only then let the number of steps tend to infinity with mesh
   `h -> 0` at fixed physical horizon;
3. require at least uniform compact-time convergence to a continuous
   output/loss readout with the initialization trace.

Pointwise convergence at each positive time to a function discontinuous at
zero is a different, post-initial-layer object.  A terminal coarse/fine loss
difference tending to zero does not by itself establish the contract above.

## 2. What is established at fixed schedule

### Normalized quadratic

Let

\[
 \phi(x)=c x^2,\qquad c=3^{-1/2}.
\]

For any fixed number of steps and fixed step sizes, (1.1) is a finite
polynomial `NETSOR^T+Moment` program.  The residual `1-f_n^s` is itself one
of the empirical-moment scalars allowed by the program language.  The
polynomially-smooth finite-program theorem used and audited in
`temporary_quadratic_l2_order5/FIXED_H_QUADRATIC_WIDTH_LEMMA.md` therefore
gives almost-sure and every-finite-`L^r` convergence of the output and loss.
Thus the fixed-schedule loss program has a deterministic width limit.

This statement is pointwise in schedule length.  Its constants are not
uniform in the number of steps.

### Exact ReLU

For

\[
 \phi(x)=\sqrt2\,x_+,
\]

the corresponding fixed-step actual-network theorem has not been proved in
the project.  The program contains the discontinuous query
`1_{z>0}` at adaptive preactivations generated by repeated use of the same
`W` and `W^T`.  The smooth polynomial theorem does not apply.  The missing
result must prove non-atomicity of every queried limiting gate, singular-Gram
safe adaptive conditioning, convergence of all response coefficients, and
terminal uniform integrability.  These are the explicit open bridges in
`temporary_linear_growth_uniform_counterexample/RELU_LEAKY_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md`.

Thus an unconditional ReLU continuous-time theorem currently fails already
at the fixed-step-identification rung.

## 3. A width-first initial-layer theorem for normalized `x^2`

The quadratic case can be decided negatively for a classical continuous
initial trace.  The argument keeps the required order of limits.

For a deterministic nonnegative feature-step schedule
`epsilon=(epsilon_0,...,epsilon_(N-1))`, let

\[
 \Phi_N(\epsilon)
\]

be the width-first expected output after those feature-ascent steps.  At
every finite width, the feature Euler map and terminal output are polynomials
with nonnegative coefficients in the raw variables, the step variables, and
the positive constant `c`.  Gaussian expectation kills odd monomials and is
positive on every even monomial.  Therefore

\[
 0\le\epsilon_s\le\widetilde\epsilon_s\ \text{for all }s
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Phi_N(\epsilon)\le\Phi_N(\widetilde\epsilon).       \tag{3.1}
\]

The inequality passes to the fixed-schedule width limit.

Let `p_s^h` be the deterministic width-first output of (1.1).  The Gaussian
program semantics of the empirical residual gives the exact adaptive
feature-step representation

\[
 p_N^h=\Phi_N(\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{N-1}),
 \qquad \epsilon_s=h(1-p_s^h).                         \tag{3.2}
\]

Fix `delta in (0,1)` and define the first grid hitting time

\[
 \tau_h(\delta)=h\inf\{s\ge0:p_s^h\ge\delta\}.         \tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

Along any vanishing uniform mesh for which the required fixed programs are
taken width first,

\[
 \boxed{\tau_h(\delta)\longrightarrow0.}                \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose that no hit occurs during the first `N` steps.  Then
`p_s^h<delta` for `s<N`, so (3.2) gives

\[
 \epsilon_s\ge(1-\delta)h.
\]

By (3.1),

\[
 p_N^h\ge F_N((1-\delta)h),                            \tag{3.5}
\]

where `F_N` is the constant-step feature-ascent output.

The proved all-order quadratic theorem gives, for every fixed `rho>0`,

\[
 F_{2t}(\rho/t)\longrightarrow+\infty.                 \tag{3.6}
\]

Indeed, its positive-polynomial comparison proves
`F_(2t)(rho/t)-F_t(2rho/t) -> +infinity`, and the coarse expectation is
nonnegative.  This theorem includes the normalized pure quadratic
coefficient `c=1/sqrt(3)`.

Now take any `tau>0` and, on a mesh `h`, choose an even
`N_h=2t_h<=tau/h` with `t_h h -> tau/2`.  If no hit occurred before
`N_h`, then for all sufficiently small `h`,

\[
 (1-\delta)h\ge\frac{(1-\delta)\tau/4}{t_h}.
\]

Using (3.1), (3.5), and (3.6),

\[
 p_{N_h}^h
 \ge F_{2t_h}\left(\frac{(1-\delta)\tau/4}{t_h}\right)
 \longrightarrow+\infty,
\]

contradicting `p_(N_h)^h<delta`.  Hence a hit occurs before every fixed
positive `tau` for all sufficiently fine meshes.  This is (3.4).  Every
width limit used above is taken at its fixed finite `N_h` before `h` is sent
to zero.  QED.

### Consequence

Since `p_0^h=0` and there are grid times tending to zero at which
`p_s^h>=delta`, the width-first Euler outputs cannot converge uniformly on a
compact interval to a continuous output initialized at zero.

For the loss itself, use the natural continuous Euler interpolation during
the first crossing step:

\[
 \theta(\lambda)=\theta_s+
 \lambda h(1-p_s^h)\nabla f(\theta_s),\qquad0\le\lambda\le1.
\]

The width-first output along this one fractional step is a continuous
polynomial of `lambda`.  It starts below `delta` and ends at least `delta`,
so it equals `delta` at some interpolated time tending to zero.  At that
time

\[
 \ell=\frac12(1-\delta)^2\ne\frac12=\ell(0).           \tag{3.7}
\]

Therefore the naturally interpolated losses are not equicontinuous at zero
and cannot converge uniformly to a continuous loss with the correct initial
condition.

This does not exclude a generalized post-layer limit, possibly with an
instantaneous jump at zero.  It also does not imply that the terminal scalar
pair

\[
 L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)
\]

stays nonzero: both discretizations could traverse the same initial layer
and have close terminal losses.  Hence terminal time-doubling is not a
complete convergence criterion.

## 4. Independent finite-width continuous-flow check for `x^2`

The covariant-Schur theorem in
`quadratic_compiler/operator_ide_resolution/CANONICAL_CONCENTRATION_NO_GO_COVARIANT_SCHUR.md`
proves a fixed output change by physical time
`O(1/sqrt(log n))` for the raw quadratic network.  Multiplying both
activations by `c` multiplies the network output by `c^3` and only rescales
feature and physical time by fixed positive constants; the same
concentration layer therefore holds for the RMS-normalized quadratic.

That theorem alone has the opposite limit order (continuous finite-width
flow before width) and would not decide the width-first mesh question.  It
is independent corroboration of the initial-layer mechanism.  Theorem 3.1
is the argument that decides the width-first order.

## 5. ReLU well-posedness obstruction and present verdict

Even apart from the missing fixed-step width theorem, a classical ReLU
gradient field is discontinuous at gates.  In a normal gate coordinate its
two one-sided velocities can have the form

\[
 \dot r=p\quad(r<0),\qquad \dot r=q\quad(r>0).
\]

If `p>0>q`, both sides point into the gate and no fixed value assigned at
the kink supplies the ordinary crossing solution; a Filippov sticking rule
changes the model.  If `p<0<q`, continuations from the gate are nonunique.
The finite-network switching audit recorded in
`nonlinear_activation_operator_ide/EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md` constructs regular
two-neuron states of both types.  The top-gate Gaussian calculation for the
present network has

\[
 p=Y,\qquad q=Y+2\sqrt2 A
\]

at normalized ReLU initialization, so both sign geometries have positive
Gaussian measure at the local level.  Turning that local calculation into a
canonical-path hitting theorem is not presently proved.

Thus the exact ReLU verdict is **open/undefined under the current
contract**, not positive:

1. a derivative convention, Filippov/subgradient selection, or smoothing
   prescription must first be fixed;
2. the actual fixed-step indicator DAG must be identified width first;
3. a common-source reachable-state sewing estimate and uniqueness theorem
   must then be proved;
4. output and squared-loss uniform integrability and continuity must be
   established.

The conditional local paired expansion

\[
 L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)=t(9-D_{\rm ReLU})h^2+o_t(h^2)
\]

does not close any of these gates.  At fixed total time `T=2th`, its leading
term is `O(1/t)`, so it is compatible with a flow; a fixed-`t` expansion is
not a horizon-uniform sewing theorem.

## 6. Minimum theorem that would settle a positive case

For a chosen activation and solution convention, construct a common
width-first population state space `X`, a restartable one-step map `S_h`, and
continuous output/loss readouts.  For every compact horizon, prove from
activation/source data that all variable-step reachable paths stay in one
set on which

\[
 \|S_hU-S_hV\|\le(1+L_Th)\|U-V\|,
\]

\[
 \|S_h^2U-S_{2h}U\|\le h\rho_T(h),
 \qquad \sum_{j\ge0}\rho_T(h_02^{-j})<\infty.          \tag{6.1}
\]

An audited Osgood analogue is also sufficient.  One must additionally prove
fixed-step finite-network identification on that same source, state/readout
uniform integrability, uniqueness, and the correct initialization trace.
For `x^2`, Theorem 3.1 rules out such a theorem with a continuous initial
output/loss trace.  For exact ReLU, every item in this paragraph remains
open.
