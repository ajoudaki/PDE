# Audit: paired loss discrepancy for one-sample square-loss training

## 1. Convention and the object that actually has a width limit

Use the half-square loss

\[
 \ell(f)=\frac12(1-f)^2.
\]

At finite width the exact update is

\[
 \theta_{r+1}=\theta_r+\eta(1-f_n(\theta_r))\nabla f_n(\theta_r).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus loss descent is feature ascent with a recomputed, state-dependent
step.  If instead the loss is defined without the factor `1/2`, all formulas
below use the replacement `eta -> 2 eta` and the observable is multiplied by
two.

There are two possible annealed quantities:

\[
 L_{r,n}=\mathbb E\,\frac12(1-f_{r,n})^2,
 \qquad
 \frac12(1-F_r)^2,
 \quad F_r=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E f_{r,n}.
\]

They agree in the width limit only after proving `L^2` self-averaging of the
trained output.  Mean convergence alone is insufficient.

For two trajectories trained by (1.1), their endpoint losses satisfy the
exact identity

\[
 L^{\rm fine}-L^{\rm coarse}
 =(f^{\rm fine}-f^{\rm coarse})
 \left(\frac{f^{\rm fine}+f^{\rm coarse}}2-1\right).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

This identity does not allow one to substitute the feature-ascent outputs:
the two trajectories in (1.2) are generated by the adaptive multiplier
`1-f`, not by constant-step feature ascent.

## 2. Exact smooth paired expansion

Let `L` be a `C^4` scalar function on a Hilbert space, put

\[
 g=\nabla L,\qquad H=\nabla^2L,\qquad T=\nabla^3L,
 \qquad V=-g,
\]

and let `L_N(h)` denote the loss after `N` explicit-Euler steps
`x^+=x+hV(x)`, starting at `x`.  Define

\[
 a=g^THg,\qquad b=g^TH^2g,\qquad c=T[g,g,g].
\]

Then, for every fixed integer `t`,

\[
 L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)
 =tah^2+t\left\{(2-4t)b+(\tfrac12-t)c\right\}h^3+O_t(h^4).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The sign in front of `c` in (2.1) follows from the convention
`c=T[g,g,g]`.  To derive it, define the one-step pullback operators

\[
 Aq=Dq[V],\qquad Bq=\tfrac12D^2q[V,V],\qquad
 Cq=\tfrac16D^3q[V,V,V].
\]

Then

\[
 P_h=I+hA+h^2B+h^3C+O(h^4)
\]

and direct noncommutative multiplication gives

\[
 P_h^{2t}-P_{2h}^{t}
 =th^2(A^2-2B)
 +th^3\{ -6C+(3-2t)(AB+BA)+2(t-1)A^3\}
 +O_t(h^4).                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

Acting on `L`,

\[
 (A^2-2B)L=a,
\]

\[
 CL=-\frac c6,\qquad
 (AB+BA)L=-2b-\frac32c,\qquad
 A^3L=-4b-2c.
\]

Substitution in (2.2) yields (2.1).  For the control loss
`L(x)=x^2/2`, (2.1) gives the exact first terms of
`(1-h)^{4t}/2-(1-2h)^{2t}/2`, including coefficient `t(2-4t)` at
order three.

## 3. Specialization to `L=(1-f)^2/2`

At an initialization point with `f=0`, put

\[
 u=\nabla f,\quad S=\|u\|^2,\quad
 R=u^T(\nabla^2f)u,\quad
 Q=u^T(\nabla^2f)^2u,\quad
 U=\nabla^3f[u,u,u].
\]

The contractions in (2.1) are

\[
 a=S^2-R,
 \qquad
 b=S^3-2SR+Q,
 \qquad
 c=U-3SR.                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)
={}&t(S^2-R)h^2\\
 &+t\left[(2-4t)(S^3-2SR+Q)
 +(\tfrac12-t)(U-3SR)\right]h^3+O_t(h^4).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{3.2}
\]

For the normalized `q=1,L=2` network at Gaussian initialization, let

\[
 d=\mathbb E\phi'(G)^2,qquad \mathbb E\phi(G)^2=1.
\]

The three parameter blocks give the initialization tangent-kernel value

\[
 S=1+d+d^2.                                           \tag{3.3}
\]

Readout reflection makes the width-limit cellwise contraction `R` zero.
For normalized ReLU and normalized leaky ReLU, `d=1`, hence `S=3` and the
smooth-cell part of the leading coefficient is

\[
 9t h^2.                                               \tag{3.4}
\]

Equations (2.1)--(3.4) may be applied to the width-first limit only after
the corresponding `C^3` operator-DAG regularity and uniform integrability
have been proved.  They must not be obtained by a finite-width Taylor
expansion followed by an unproved exchange of width and step limits.

## 4. Hard ReLU/leaky-ReLU candidate and its status

The existing marked-gate calculation for feature ascent gives the
conditional right-sided top-gate contribution

\[
 tD_{a,b}h^2,
\]

where

\[
 D_{a,b}=
 \frac{3(a-b)}{4\pi\sqrt e}
 \int_{2b}^{2a}
 \frac{(y-2b)(2a-y)}{(1+y^2/e)^{5/2}}\,dy,
 \qquad e=\frac{a^4+b^4}{2}.
\]

At initialization, loss descent has the same normal gate velocities as
feature ascent, while `ell'(0)=-1`.  Therefore the corresponding candidate
loss expansion is

\[
 L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)
 =t(9-D_{a,b})h^2+o_t(h^2),\qquad h\downarrow0.       \tag{4.1}
\]

For ReLU,

\[
 D_{\rm ReLU}=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt5}\right).
\]

Formula (4.1) is conditional, for exactly the same reasons as the hard-kink
feature-ascent formula: the indicator-valued fixed-step reused-matrix DAG
and its complete marked-source response intertwining have not been proved.
Changing the scalar multiplier from `1` to `1-f` does not close either
bridge.

## 5. Why the expansion does not decide the continuous-time limit

At fixed total time `T=2th`, the leading term in (2.1) or (4.1) is

\[
 t h^2=\frac{T^2}{4t}.
\]

It therefore tends to zero.  A nonzero `h^2` paired term is the ordinary
first-order Euler discretization error, not an obstruction to gradient
flow.  In fact, for any finite Taylor order `m`, autonomous Euler chronology
makes the order-`h^m` paired coefficient a polynomial of degree at most
`m-1` in `t`; at `h=T/(2t)` every such fixed-order contribution is
`O(1/t)`.  No finite jet can prove or disprove convergence.  All-source-order
control is necessary.

A sufficient noncircular estimate is easy to state.  Suppose, on a set
containing both Euler trajectories up to time `T`, that

\[
 \|V\|\le M,\qquad \operatorname{Lip}(V)\le\Lambda,qquad
 \operatorname{Lip}(L)\le G.
\]

Writing `T_h(x)=x+hV(x)`, one pair has exact state defect

\[
 T_h^2x-T_{2h}x=h\{V(x+hV(x))-V(x)\},
\]

so its norm is at most `Lambda M h^2`.  Iterating and using
`Lip(T_h^2)<=(1+Lambda h)^2` yields

\[
 |L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)|
 \le G\Lambda M\,t h^2 e^{2\Lambda th}.          \tag{5.1}
\]

For `h=T/(2t)`, the right side is

\[
 \frac{G\Lambda M e^{\Lambda T}T^2}{4t}.
\]

Along dyadic refinements this is summable and proves convergence.  For the
actual width-first OMFP dynamics, however, one must derive `M,Lambda,G`
from activation envelopes and population moments uniformly on the time
interval.  Defining them from the unknown trained trajectory would be
circular.  Exact ReLU/leaky-ReLU additionally lack a Lipschitz classical
gradient field at gate surfaces, so (5.1) does not apply without a proved
nonsmooth replacement and uniqueness theorem.

Hence neither the local expansion nor the previously constructed
super-fifth normalized remainder settles existence of the continuous-time
loss-gradient limit.  The correct verdict for the present exact `L=2`
width-first ReLU/leaky-ReLU problem is open, with (4.1) only a conditional
initial boundary coefficient.

## 6. Exact order-five pullback ledger

For completeness, keep the notation of Section 2 and define, for every
`k>=1`,

\[
 T_kq=\frac1{k!}D^kq[V,\ldots,V].
\]

Here `V` is frozen inside the displayed derivative of `q`, although
`T_k` itself is an operator with state-dependent `V`.  For `1<=r<=m`, set

\[
 W_{m,r}=
 \sum_{\substack{k_1+\cdots+k_r=m\\k_i\ge1}}
 T_{k_1}\cdots T_{k_r},                         \tag{6.1}
\]

with right-to-left operator action, and

\[
 \Lambda_{m,r}=(W_{m,r}L)(x_0),\qquad
 q_{m,r}(t)={2t\choose r}-2^m{t\choose r}.       \tag{6.2}
\]

Since the one-step pullback is `P_h=I+sum_(k>=1)h^kT_k`, the binomial
identity for the single operator `P_h-I` gives exactly

\[
 [h^m]\{L_{2t}(h)-L_t(2h)\}
 =\sum_{r=1}^m q_{m,r}(t)\Lambda_{m,r}.          \tag{6.3}
\]

The rows through order five are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
m&(q_{m,1},\ldots,q_{m,m})\\ \hline
2&(-2t,\ t)\\
3&(-6t,\ -2t^2+3t,\ 2t(t-1))\\
4&(-14t,\ -6t^2+7t,\
-\frac43t^3+6t^2-\frac{14}3t,\
2t^3-\frac{11}2t^2+\frac72t)\\
5&(-30t,\ -14t^2+15t,\
-4t^3+14t^2-10t,\\
&\qquad -\frac23t^4+6t^3-\frac{77}6t^2+\frac{15}2t,\
\frac43t^4-7t^3+\frac{35}3t^2-6t).
\end{array}                                                    \tag{6.4}
\]

Thus every literal coefficient at order `m` has degree at most `m-1` in
`t`.  Put `D_t(r)=L_(2t)(r)-L_t(2r)`.  If this scalar paired map is `C^6`
on the full segment between zero and `h`, its exact Taylor remainder is

\[
 R_6(h)=\frac{h^6}{5!}\int_0^1(1-s)^5
 D_t^{(6)}(s h)\,ds.                               \tag{6.5}
\]

Formula (6.5) is not a uniform estimate: bounding its integrand by an
output-derived supremum would be circular, and fixed-`t` regularity gives
no control as `t` grows.  For the hard activations the required classical
`C^6` regularity is itself false at switching surfaces.
