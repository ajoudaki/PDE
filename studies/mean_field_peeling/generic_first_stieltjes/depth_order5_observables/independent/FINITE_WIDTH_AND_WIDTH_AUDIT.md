# Exact finite-width, equality-partition, and transpose-response audit

## 1. Exact finite-width skeleton

Fix one sample.  Write ordinary feature and backpropagation derivatives as

\[
 X_{\ell,r}=\left.\partial_s^r X_\ell(s)\right|_0,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\ell,r}=\left.\partial_s^r\Delta_\ell(s)\right|_0,
\]

and, for a hidden matrix layer,

\[
 Z_{\ell,r}=\left.\partial_s^r
 \frac{W^\ell(s)X_{\ell-1}(s)}{\sqrt n}\right|_0.
\]

The feature-ascent ODE gives the exact finite-width identities

\[
 W^{\ell,(r+1)}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{a=0}^{r}{r\choose a}
 \Delta_{\ell,a}X_{\ell-1,r-a}^{\mathsf T},
\tag{1.1}
\]

\[
 Z_{\ell,r}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{a=0}^{r}{r\choose a}
 W^{\ell,(a)}X_{\ell-1,r-a}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

These are identities of the finite network, before expectation or a width
limit.  For

\[
 \widehat\Gamma^{\ell}_{rs}
 =n^{-1}\langle X_{\ell,r},X_{\ell,s}\rangle,
\]

substituting (1.1) into (1.2) at order four enumerates every weight-flow
branch:

| matrix jet | exact contribution to `Z_(ell,4)` |
|---|---|
| `W_0 X_4` | `F_4 := n^(-1/2) W_0 X_4` |
| `4 W_1 X_3` | `4 Delta_0 Gammahat_03` |
| `6 W_2 X_2` | `6 Delta_1 Gammahat_02 + 6 Delta_0 Gammahat_12` |
| `4 W_3 X_1` | `4 Delta_2 Gammahat_01 + 8 Delta_1 Gammahat_11 + 4 Delta_0 Gammahat_12` |
| `W_4 X_0` | `Delta_3 Gammahat_00 + 3 Delta_2 Gammahat_01 + 3 Delta_1 Gammahat_02 + Delta_0 Gammahat_03` |

Consequently the exact regrouping is

\[
\boxed{
 Z_{\ell,4}=F_{\ell,4}
 +(5\widehat\Gamma_{03}+10\widehat\Gamma_{12})\Delta_0
 +(9\widehat\Gamma_{02}+8\widehat\Gamma_{11})\Delta_1
 +7\widehat\Gamma_{01}\Delta_2
 +\widehat\Gamma_{00}\Delta_3.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

No branch of the finite-width product rule is omitted in (1.3).

The five equality partitions of the fourth activation derivative are

| partition of 4 | term in `X_(ell,4)` | multiplicity |
|---|---|---:|
| `1+1+1+1` | `phi^(4) Z_1^4` | 1 |
| `2+1+1` | `phi^(3) Z_1^2 Z_2` | 6 |
| `2+2` | `phi'' Z_2^2` | 3 |
| `3+1` | `phi'' Z_1 Z_3` | 4 |
| `4` | `phi' Z_4` | 1 |

Thus, exactly at finite width,

\[
\boxed{
 X_{\ell,4}=\phi^{(4)}Z_1^4+6\phi^{(3)}Z_1^2Z_2
 +3\phi''Z_2^2+4\phi''Z_1Z_3+\phi'Z_4.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The two finite-width implementations in `finite_width_hidden_jet.py` encode
(1.1)--(1.4) independently: one uses ordinary series coefficients, while the
other uses ordinary derivatives and the displayed binomial/Bell rules.

## 2. Complete leading-width and transpose-response split

The full response-aware population peel writes the order-four preactivation
in ordinary-derivative normalization as

\[
 Z_4=F_4+L_{40}d_0+L_{41}d_1+L_{42}d_2+L_{43}d_3.
\tag{2.1}
\]

There are exactly four transpose-response branches, one for each reverse jet
`d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3`.  Before parity reduction their coefficients are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
s&\text{ordinary Gram part}&\text{transpose response}\cr\hline
0&5\Gamma_{03}+10\Gamma_{12}&a_{40}\cr
1&9\Gamma_{02}+8\Gamma_{11}&a_{41}\cr
2&7\Gamma_{01}&a_{42}\cr
3&\Gamma_{00}&a_{43}.
\end{array}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Here `a_(4s)` is the correctly factorial-rescaled derivative of the lower
feature-four jet with respect to the order-`s` transpose innovation.  Readout
reflection sends `X_r` to `(-1)^r X_r` and the order-`s` reverse innovation
to `(-1)^(s+1)` times itself.  Hence

\[
 \Gamma_{03}=\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{01}=a_{40}=a_{42}=0
\tag{2.3}
\]

in the annealed deterministic population state.  Under unit Gram,
`Gamma_00=1`, and therefore

\[
\boxed{
 L_{41}=9\Gamma_{02}+8\Gamma_{11}+a_{41},
 \qquad L_{43}=1+a_{43}.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Equation (2.3) is a population parity reduction, not a false claim that the
odd empirical Grams in (1.3) vanish seedwise at finite width.

The frozen local polynomial explicitly retains all five reverse Gaussian
coordinates `(E0,E1,E2,J2,J3)` and the carrier responses
`(c10,c21,d21,d30,d32)`.  Its reverse Wick covariance table is

\[
 B_{00}=b,\ B_{02}=e02,\ B_{03}=r02,\ B_{11}=e11,\
 B_{14}=r13,\ B_{22}=e22,\ B_{23}=rfm,\ B_{33}=r22,
\tag{2.5}
\]

with every other entry zero by grade/parity.  Thus the `s=1` and `s=3`
transpose branches are contracted rather than silently treated as
independent.  The vanished `s=0,2` branches are present before the exact
reflection projection (2.3).

## 3. Equality partitions and width exponents

After each normalized Gaussian sum has been reduced to its conditional Gram,
expand every remaining product of empirical averages into neuron labels.  If
there are `m` labels and their equality pattern is the set partition `pi`,
then, relative to the all-distinct sector, its exact free-index exponent is

\[
 n^{|\pi|-m}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The local order-four head contains at most five neuron labels: the observed
row plus the four labels generated by `Z_1^4` (the reverse terms have the same
or smaller maximum).  The complete partition census needed locally is

| labels `m` | blocks `|pi|` from leading to diagonal | partition counts | relative powers |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | `n^0` |
| 2 | 2,1 | 1,1 | `n^0,n^-1` |
| 3 | 3,2,1 | 1,3,1 | `n^0,n^-1,n^-2` |
| 4 | 4,3,2,1 | 1,6,7,1 | `n^0,n^-1,n^-2,n^-3` |
| 5 | 5,4,3,2,1 | 1,10,25,15,1 | `n^0,n^-1,n^-2,n^-3,n^-4` |

The counts are the Stirling numbers of the second kind and sum to the Bell
numbers `1,2,5,15,52`.  No equality class is discarded before its exponent
is assigned.  All accidental collisions have at least one negative power and
belong to finite-width corrections.  The same-row/same-column contractions
that compensate a Gaussian normalization are not classified as accidental:
they are peeled first and appear either in the covariance tables or in the
four explicit transpose responses (2.2).  This ordering prevents the common
error of losing a leading transpose branch by calling it a diagonal sector.

## 4. Independent Wick--Stein contraction

The independent local producer represents the five forward innovations as

\[
 (F_1,F_2^{\rm frozen},F_2^{\rm moving},F_3^{\rm moving},F_4^{\rm moving})
\]

and uses the complete nonzero forward covariance table

\[
\begin{gathered}
 H_{00}=1,\quad H_{02}=u,\quad H_{03}=q02,\quad H_{05}=\gamma04,\\
 H_{11}=w,\quad H_{14}=q13,\quad H_{22}=y,\quad
 H_{23}=qfm,\quad H_{33}=q22.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4.1}
\]

It first Wick-pairs every reverse innovation using (2.5), then Wick-pairs or
Stein-contracts every forward innovation using (4.1).  The recursion aborts
if it would generate `phi^(6)`.  Before contraction `X_4` has 51 distinct
local coordinate monomials.  After contraction the three transitions have
respectively 83, 20, and 1 scalar monomials, zero residual Gaussian symbols,
and maximum activation derivative four.

This is a formal Gaussian normal form until compared to a separate reference.
The atomwise comparisons and finite-width gates are recorded in the audit
artifact, not assumed here.
