# Two-input raw-cubic MSE channel: exact local kernel result

## Bottom line

For two unit-RMS inputs with correlation \(\rho\), two hidden layers with raw
cubic activation, equal labels \((1,1)\), and the same all-block metric as the
one-input calculation, the genuine two-example average-MSE flow reduces in
the deterministic width-first exchange-symmetric sector to

\[
\dot g=2\eta(1-g)K_+(g;\rho),
\qquad
\dot{\mathcal L}=-4\eta K_+(g;\rho)\mathcal L,
\]

where

\[
g=\frac{f_1+f_2}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal L=\frac12\{(f_1-1)^2+(f_2-1)^2\}=(1-g)^2.
\]

The exact Gaussian-program calculation determines the local kernel through
quadratic order in the output:

\[
K_+(y;\rho)
=a(\rho)+\frac{b(\rho)}{2a(\rho)^2}y^2+O(y^4),
\]

with \(a=F_+'(0;\rho)\) and \(b=F_+^{(3)}(0;\rho)\) given below.

## Why this is the actual MSE flow

For a general two-output state the correct object is the tangent-kernel
matrix

\[
\Theta_{rs}=n\nabla f_r\mathbin\cdot\nabla f_s,
\qquad
\dot f=-\eta\Theta(f-\mathbf1).
\]

In the deterministic width-first/formal limit, equal input norms, iid
initialization, and equal labels make the system exchange invariant.  It
starts at \(f_1=f_2=0\) and remains on \(f_1=f_2=g\).  There

\[
\nabla\mathcal L=(g-1)(\nabla f_1+\nabla f_2)
=2(g-1)\nabla g.
\]

Consequently the physical MSE parameter velocity is exactly

\[
\dot\theta=-\eta n\nabla\mathcal L
=2\eta(1-g)n\nabla g,
\]

which is a scalar reparameterization of the feature-ascent curve generated by
\(D_+=n\nabla g\cdot\nabla\).  Thus

\[
K_+(g;\rho)=n\|\nabla g\|^2
=\frac{\Theta_{11}+\Theta_{12}}2
\]

is precisely the plus-channel eigenvalue divided by two.

This scalar reduction is not exact for an individual finite random network:
finite-width fluctuations generally give \(f_1\ne f_2\) and
\(\Theta_{11}\ne\Theta_{22}\).  Such a realization requires the full vector
flow and full \(2\times2\) matrix.  The scalar statement is exact at the same
width-first formal/deterministic level as the earlier one-input derivative
jets.

## Exact feature derivatives

Let

\[
F_+(s;\rho)
=\lim_{n\to\infty}E[g(\theta(s))],
\qquad
\dot\theta=n\nabla g.
\]

Readout reflection gives

\[
F_+(0;\rho)=F_+^{(2)}(0;\rho)=0.
\]

The initial speed is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
a(\rho)=F_+'(0;\rho)

+45684\rho^3+39366\rho^5\\
&+32076\rho^7+8424\rho^9.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
a(\rho)=\frac{81}{2}(1+\rho)A(\rho),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}

+1972\rho^4\\
&-1000\rho^5+1000\rho^6-208\rho^7+208\rho^8.
\end{aligned}
\]

The exact third derivative is

\boxed{b(\rho)=F_+^{(3)}(0;\rho)

## Output-coordinate kernel derivatives

Define

\[
K_+(y;\rho)
=F_+'\!\left(F_+^{-1}(y;\rho);\rho\right).
\]

Since \(F_+\) is odd, \(K_+\) is even.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{K_+(0;\rho)=\frac{81}{2}(1+\rho)A(\rho),}
\]

\[
\boxed{K_+'(0;\rho)=0,}
\]

and the first nontrivial derivative is

\[
\boxed{
K_+''(0;\rho)
=\frac{F_+^{(3)}(0;\rho)}{F_+'(0;\rho)^2}
=\frac{24P(\rho)}{A(\rho)^2}.}
\]

Thus

\[
K_+(y;\rho)
=\frac{81}{2}(1+\rho)A(\rho)
+\frac{12P(\rho)}{A(\rho)^2}y^2+O(y^4).
\]

Exact Sturm counts show that neither \(A\) nor \(P\) has a real zero on
\([-1,1]\), and their endpoint values are positive.  Hence
\(K_+(0;\rho)>0\) for \(-1<\rho\le1\), and
\(K_+''(0;\rho)>0\) throughout the nondegenerate interval.

At \(\rho=-1\), the odd network maps antipodal inputs to opposite outputs,
so the equal-label plus direction vanishes identically.  Both \(F_+'(0)\)
and \(F_+^{(3)}(0)\) vanish, and \(F_+^{-1}\) is not defined.  The displayed
rational curvature has a removable limit there, but it is not an
output-coordinate kernel for the degenerate channel.

## Initial full tangent-kernel matrix

At initialization,

\[
\Theta(0;\rho)=
\begin{pmatrix}305775&C(\rho)\\C(\rho)&305775\end{pmatrix},
\]

where

\[
C(\rho)=54675\rho+91368\rho^3+78732\rho^5
+64152\rho^7+16848\rho^9.
\]

Indeed \((305775+C)/2=K_+(0;\rho)\).  This makes the relation to the full
two-output MSE dynamics explicit rather than hiding the matrix parent object.

## Initial loss derivatives

Writing \(a(\rho)=K_+(0;\rho)\) and
\(b(\rho)=F_+^{(3)}(0;\rho)\), the exact local loss identities are

\[
\mathcal L(0)=1,
\qquad
\dot{\mathcal L}(0)=-4\eta a,
\]

\[
\ddot{\mathcal L}(0)=16\eta^2a^2,
\]

\[
\mathcal L^{(3)}(0)
=-16\eta^3\left(4a^3+b\right).
\]

Thus the newly computed \(F_+^{(3)}\), equivalently \(K_+''(0)\), first
enters the third time derivative of the physical MSE loss.

## Validation

Two exact Gaussian-program assemblers were run over \(\mathbb Q[\rho]\): an
ordinary-Taylor recurrence and an independently weighted
derivative-normalized recurrence.  They agree coefficient-for-coefficient.
The complete paired run took 9.47 seconds and 29,748 KiB peak RSS.

An older, independently derived fully contracted fixed-batch Gaussian normal
form was then evaluated for the cubic activation at

\[
\rho\in\left\{-\frac34,-\frac13,0,\frac25,\frac34\right\}.
\]

It matched both \(F_+'(0)\) and \(F_+^{(3)}(0)\) exactly at all five unused
holdouts.  The calculation also passes exact readout parity, sample-exchange,
direct gradient-block, one-input endpoint, antipodal degeneracy, source-hash,
and Sturm interval gates.

## Claim boundary

The established result is a fixed-order width-first formal/deterministic
calculation and an exact local reduction of the two-example MSE on its
exchange-symmetric orbit.  It does not establish positive-time existence or
self-averaging, global invertibility of \(F_+\), or scalar closure for a
generic finite-width/asymmetric trajectory.  Those questions require the
full matrix-valued dynamics and separate limit arguments.
