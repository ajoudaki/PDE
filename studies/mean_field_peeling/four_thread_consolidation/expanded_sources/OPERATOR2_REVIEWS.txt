### 3. What finite-width energy bounds do establish

In the usual μP coordinates \(a=\alpha/n\), write
\[
f=\frac1n X_3^\top\alpha,\qquad
H_1=W_1X,\quad H_2=W_2X_1,\quad H_3=W_3X_2.
\]
With
\[
Q_3=(\alpha r^\top)\odot\phi'(H_3),\quad
Q_2=(W_3^\top Q_3)\odot\phi'(H_2),\quad
Q_1=(W_2^\top Q_2)\odot\phi'(H_1),
\]
the μP gradient flow has
\[
\dot W_1=-Q_1X^\top,\quad
\dot W_2=-\frac1nQ_2X_1^\top,\quad
\dot W_3=-\frac1nQ_3X_2^\top,\quad
\dot\alpha=-X_3r.
\]

For a lower-bounded empirical loss,
\[
\int_0^T\!\left[
\frac1n\|\dot W_1\|_F^2+
\|\dot W_2\|_F^2+
\|\dot W_3\|_F^2+
\frac1n\|\dot\alpha\|_2^2
\right]dt
\le \mathcal L(0)-\inf\mathcal L .
\]
Together with Gaussian operator-norm bounds for \(W_2(0),W_3(0)\), this yields width-uniform reachable-ball and \(1/2\)-Hölder estimates in
\[
d_n(\theta,\tilde\theta)=
\frac{\|W_1-\tilde W_1\|_F}{\sqrt n}
+\|W_2-\tilde W_2\|_{\rm op}
+\|W_3-\tilde W_3\|_{\rm op}
+\frac{\|\alpha-\tilde\alpha\|_2}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Hence output tightness is plausible.

### 4. Why compactness does not complete the proof

The tempting claim that the vector fields are uniformly Lipschitz in \(d_n\) is false from these bounds alone. For example, subtracting two \(Q_2\) equations produces
\[
(\widetilde W_3^\top\widetilde Q_3)\odot
\bigl[\phi'(H_2)-\phi'(\widetilde H_2)\bigr].
\]
Its normalized \(L^2\) norm satisfies only
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\|\cdot\|_F
\le
\|\phi''\|_\infty
\|\widetilde W_3^\top\widetilde Q_3\|_\infty
\frac{\|H_2-\widetilde H_2\|_F}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Energy controls the first factor only in normalized \(L^2\), not \(L^\infty\). For nondegenerate Gaussian disorder its maximum typically grows at least like \(\sqrt{\log n}\). Hölder estimates replace this by an \(L^4\) requirement; propagating that estimate requires \(L^8\), then \(L^{16}\), recreating the growing response/program hierarchy.

Thus bare Arzelà–Ascoli compactness yields subsequences but cannot pass through the nonlinear forward/backward products or identify the Gaussian response terms. The missing result is precisely a finite-width response/moment-tail stability estimate establishing (A)–(B). Limiting-DAG row-sum or boundedness estimates control propagation after taking \(n=\infty\); they do not bound finite-width error production.

### 5. Literature audit

- Yang–Hu explicitly restrict their TP results to training time independent of width; their Master Theorem is for a fixed finite program. The paper remarks that a quantitative theorem might be possible but does not provide one. [Tensor Programs IV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.14522)
- Nguyen–Pham’s Theorem 15 does give uniform compact-time width/step bounds of order \(n^{-c}+\epsilon^c\), but for classical mean-field forward sums \(n^{-1}\sum_j w_{ij}x_j\) with width-uniform \(O(1)\) sub-Gaussian weights. Matching \(n^{-1/2}\sum_j Z_{ij}x_j\) would require \(w_{ij}=\sqrt n\,Z_{ij}\), violating their initialization hypothesis. [Nguyen–Pham](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.11443)
- Sirignano–Spiliopoulos jointly send iterations and widths to infinity, but use \(1/N\) mean-field normalization, compactly supported initialization, and sequential layer-width limits—not the two-Gaussian-matrix μP limit. [Sirignano–Spiliopoulos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04440)
- The 2026 GFOM/DMFT theorem fixes a finite iteration horizon \(T\) and treats a single iid Gaussian design matrix; it supplies another fixed-program theorem, not growing-step control for two trained middle matrices. [Dandi et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.14573)

So the defensible conclusion is: no diagonal or inspected off-the-shelf theorem closes the limit. A direct random-ODE proof remains viable, but only after proving a genuinely new uniform response/tail stability or fine-to-coarse shadowing estimate; compactness and limiting small-time bounds alone are insufficient.
