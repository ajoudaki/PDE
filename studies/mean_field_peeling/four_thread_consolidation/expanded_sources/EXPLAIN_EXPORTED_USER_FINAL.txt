\[

\[

A&=J(X), & H&=\arctan(A), & S&=BH,\\
G&=\arctan(S), &D&=Cq(S), &e&=\langle C,G\rangle-1,
\end{aligned}

\dot B=-2e\,D\otimes H,\qquad
\dot C=-2eG.

- \(C\) learns from the final features \(G\).
- \(D=Cq(S)\) is the error signal gated through the second activation.
- \(B\) receives a rank-one update \(D\otimes H\).
- \(B^*D\) propagates the signal back to the first layer.

The unusual phrase “action-law flow” is important. The marginal distributions of \(A\), \(S\), and \(C\) do not determine the future: knowing \(S=BH\) does not determine \(B^*D\). That richer state is autonomous and restartable: its current value, without its history or initialization array, determines its future.

The convergence claim is correspondingly strong. Along the full width sequence:

- exact discrete GD converges in probability, uniformly on every fixed time interval;
- the empirical distributions of the complete paths \(A_i(\cdot)\) and \(S_j(\cdot)\) converge in \(W_2(C([0,T]))\);
- the output, loss, three scaled kernel blocks, and hidden-layer velocity energies also converge.

\[

\[
A(t)-A_0=O(t^2),\qquad
B(t)-B_0=O(t^2),\qquad
S(t)-S_0=O(t^2),

\[
K^A(t)=\alpha t^2+o(t^2),\qquad
K^B(t)=\beta t^2+o(t^2),\qquad \alpha,\beta>0,

ASSISTANT The theorem’s main goal is not merely to compute the limiting output \(f(t)\). It is to prove that the entire training process of a wide, two-hidden-layer network approaches a well-defined infinite-width dynamical system in which both hidden layers genuinely learn.

## Conceptual goal

For every width \(n\), training is:

- random, because the parameters are randomly initialized;
- discrete, because it uses gradient-descent steps;
- very high-dimensional, with \(n^2\) middle-layer weights;
- width-dependent, because initialization and learning rates change with \(n\).

We ask whether, as \(n\to\infty\), this complicated random system settles into a predictable macroscopic evolution.

There are several ways this could fail:

1. The dynamics might blow up or have no width limit.
2. Only the output weights might learn while the hidden features freeze.
3. The activation might effectively become affine, producing a disguised linear model.
4. A limiting formula might exist but fail to be autonomous: predicting its future might require remembering the entire initialization or training history.
5. Different subsequences of widths might produce different limits.

The theorem rules out all five failures for this particular network and scaling. Its conceptual conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Wide-network training self-averages into one closed, deterministic,
nonlinear feature-learning flow.}
}
\]

“Deterministic” here means deterministic at the level of laws. The limiting variables can be represented as random variables, but their joint law and its evolution are fixed rather than depending on the particular finite-width initialization.

## Exact mathematical goal

Let \(k\) be the GD iteration and set

\[
t=k\eta_n,\qquad \eta_n=n^{-2}.
\]

Thus observing a fixed macroscopic time \(t\) means running approximately \(tn^2\) GD steps.

The proof uses the exact transformed state

\[
\Theta_n^{\mathrm{GD}}(t)
=
\bigl(X_n^{\mathrm{GD}}(t),B_n^{\mathrm{GD}}(t),C_n^{\mathrm{GD}}(t)\bigr),
\]

where

\[
C_n=nc_n,\qquad
X_n=F(A_n),\qquad
F(a)=a+\frac{a^3}{3}.
\]

Here:

- \(X_n(t)\in\mathbb R^n\) represents the first-layer preactivations through an invertible change of coordinates;
- \(B_n(t)\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) is the middle weight matrix;
- \(C_n(t)\in\mathbb R^n\) is the naturally rescaled readout vector.

The primary theorem is the existence of a deterministic continuum state \(\Theta(t)\) such that, for every finite \(T\),

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
d_{\mathrm{act}}\!\left(
\Theta_n^{\mathrm{GD}}(t),\Theta(t)
\right)
\longrightarrow 0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]

Explicitly, this means that for every \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
\mathbb P\!\left(
\sup_{0\le t\le T}

\right)>\varepsilon
\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The distance \(d_{\mathrm{act}}\) is richer than a distance between ordinary neuron distributions. It tests the joint laws and inner products of every finite collection of quantities generated from the current state using:

- the middle operator \(B\);
- its adjoint \(B^*\);
- the activation and derivative gates;
- finite compositions of those operations.

This richer state is necessary because knowing the law of

\[
S=BH
\]

does not determine the law of

\[
B^*D.
\]

But \(B^*D\) determines the next first-layer velocity. Therefore the marginal laws of the neurons alone do not contain enough information to predict training.

The continuum state evolves autonomously:

\[
\dot X=-2eB^*D,\qquad
\dot B=-2e\,D\otimes H,\qquad
\dot C=-2eG,
\]

with all the derived quantities \(A,H,S,G,D,e\) determined by the current \((X,B,C)\). Autonomy is expressed by the semigroup identity

\[
\Phi_t\bigl(\Phi_s(\Theta_0)\bigr)
=
\Phi_{t+s}(\Theta_0).
\]

So if training is stopped at time \(s\), the action law at that moment contains everything needed to restart it. Neither the original matrix nor the past trajectory must be attached as hidden memory.

The theorem then makes the convergence tangible through the neuron paths. Define the empirical law of the entire first-layer trajectories by

\[
\Lambda_n^A
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\delta_{A_{n,i}(\cdot)}.
\]

This is a probability measure on \(C([0,T])\), the space of continuous real-valued paths. The theorem proves

\[
\Lambda_n^A\longrightarrow \mathcal L(A(\cdot))
\quad\text{in }W_2(C([0,T])),
\]

and similarly

\[
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{S_{n,j}(\cdot)}
\longrightarrow \mathcal L(S(\cdot)).
\]

Thus it is not merely saying that the neuron distributions converge separately at each time. It says that the distributions of their complete time trajectories converge, including temporal correlations and second moments.

Finally, the theorem must show that the resulting limit is nontrivial. For sufficiently small \(T>0\), it proves

\[
\int_0^T\mathbb E|\dot A(t)|^2\,dt>0,
\qquad
\int_0^T\mathbb E|\dot S(t)|^2\,dt>0.
\]

Therefore both hidden layers really move. It also proves positive displacement:

\[
\mathbb E|A(T)-A(0)|^2>0,\qquad
\mathbb E|S(T)-S(0)|^2>0.
\]

All three parameter blocks contribute positively to training, and the total tangent kernel satisfies

\[
K(t)\neq K(0)
\]

for small nonzero \(t\). This rules out a frozen-kernel limit.

Nonlinearity is expressed by

\[
\inf_{a,b\in\mathbb R}
\mathbb E\left[
\bigl(\arctan Z(t)-a-bZ(t)\bigr)^2
\right]>0
\]

for each hidden preactivation \(Z=A,S\). Hence the activation cannot be replaced, even in mean square under the evolving hidden law, by an affine function.

So the substantive target has three parts:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Fidelity: exact finite GD converges to the proposed limit;}\\[2mm]
\text{Closure: the current limiting state determines its own future;}\\[2mm]
\text{Nondegeneracy: both hidden layers move and remain genuinely nonlinear.}
\end{array}}
\]

This is a finite-time scaling-limit theorem. It does not claim that the loss eventually reaches zero, that training finds a global optimizer, or that the result already extends to arbitrary datasets and architectures.

\[

>0,
\]

\[

\frac{d}{dt}z^{(2)}(t)
\right|^2dt
>0.
\]

\left|

\right|^2>0,
\]

\left|
z^{(2)}(T)-z^{(2)}(0)
\right|^2>0.
\]

Velocity energy rules out “it moves only at an isolated instant.” Displacement rules out “it moves but returns exactly to where it started.”

## 7. The kernel criterion for all three layers

Define the scaled contribution of layer \(\ell\) to the tangent kernel by

\[
K_n^{(\ell)}
=
\lambda_{\ell,n}
\sum_{\alpha\in W_n^{(\ell)}}
\left(
\frac{\partial f_n}
{\partial W_{n,\alpha}^{(\ell)}}
\right)^2.
\]

The total scaled kernel is

\[
K_n
=
K_n^{(1)}+K_n^{(2)}+K_n^{(3)}.
\]

For the continuous gradient flow,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}f_n
=
-2(f_n-1)K_n
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{d}{dt}L_n
=
-4(f_n-1)^2K_n.
\]

Thus \(K_n^{(\ell)}\) measures how strongly layer \(\ell\) contributes to the instantaneous decrease of the loss.

The theorem proves convergence of all three blocks and, for sufficiently small \(T>0\),

\[
\int_0^T K^{(\ell)}(t)\,dt>0,
\qquad \ell=1,2,3.
\]

It also proves that

\[
K(t)
\]

is not constant. This distinguishes the limit from a frozen tangent-kernel regime.

## 8. The genuine-nonlinearity criterion

Movement alone does not guarantee nonlinear feature learning. The hidden variables could move only inside a regime where \(\phi\) behaves effectively like an affine function.

For each hidden layer \(\ell=1,2\), define

\[
\mathcal N^{(\ell)}(t)
=
\inf_{a,b\in\mathbb R}
\mathbb E
\left[
\left(
\phi(z^{(\ell)}(t))
-a-bz^{(\ell)}(t)
\right)^2
\right].
\]

This is the smallest mean-square error obtained by approximating the activation with an affine function of the preactivation.

The theorem proves, for sufficiently small time,

\[
\mathcal N^{(1)}(t)>0,
\qquad
\mathcal N^{(2)}(t)>0.
\]

Therefore neither hidden activation has collapsed to an affine transformation under its evolving population distribution.

The main objective is consequently the conjunction of three claims:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\textbf{Fidelity:}&
\text{exact finite-width GD converges to the proposed law-level dynamics};\\[1mm]
\textbf{Closure:}&
\text{the current limiting action law uniquely determines its future};\\[1mm]
\textbf{Nondegeneracy:}&
\text{both hidden layers move, all layers affect learning, and }\phi
\text{ remains nonlinear}.
\end{array}
}
\]

This is not a theorem about eventual global optimization. It does not prove \(L(t)\to0\) as \(t\to\infty\). It is a rigorous finite-time theorem identifying the true infinite-width training dynamics and proving that those dynamics are genuinely nonlinear and feature-learning.

r(t)=\mathbb E[P\phi(F^{-1}(X(t)))]-y.
\]

Write

\[
X(t)=X_0-Ps(t).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{ds(t)}{dt}
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
P\phi(F^{-1}(X_0-Ps(t)))
\right]-y.
\]

The right-hand side is bounded and 1-Lipschitz in \(s\), so this scalar ODE has a unique global solution. Therefore the pop flow is well-defined and autonomous.

Pop Euler is ordinary Euler for this same equation. Its error satisfies

\[
\max_{m\delta\le T}|s_m-s(m\delta)|
\le
\frac R2(e^T-1)\delta,
\qquad
R=\frac\pi2+|y|.
\]

Because

\[
X_m=X_0-Ps_m,
\qquad
X(t)=X_0-Ps(t),
\]

the same estimate controls \(X_m-X(m\delta)\). Including the movement between grid points gives

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
|X^{\mathrm{pop\ Euler}}(t)-X^{\mathrm{pop\ flow}}(t)|
\le
C_T\delta.
\]

---

## 6. Add the five errors

For any time \(t\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
\\
&\le
|\text{finite GD avg}-\text{finite flow avg}|
&&\text{(actual step)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{finite flow avg}-\text{finite Euler avg}|
&&\text{(finite mesh)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{finite Euler avg}-\text{oracle avg}|
&&\text{(feedback)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{oracle avg}-\text{pop Euler avg}|
&&\text{(sampling of }\psi\text{)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{pop Euler avg}-\text{pop flow avg}|
&&\text{(pop mesh)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(\psi\) is \(L_\psi\)-Lipschitz in \(z\), the five terms are bounded by

\[
L_\psi C_T
\left(
\eta_n+\eta_n\sqrt n+\eta_n^2n
\right),
\]

\[
L_\psi C_T\delta,
\]

\[
L_\psi(e^T-1)\pi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
},
\]

\[
B_\psi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
},
\]

and

\[
L_\psi C_T\delta.
\]

Therefore, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\sup_{t\le T}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
\\
&\le
L_\psi C_T
\left(
\eta_n+\eta_n\sqrt n+\eta_n^2n
\right)
\\
&\quad+
2L_\psi C_T\delta
\\
&\quad+
\left[
L_\psi(e^T-1)\pi+B_\psi
\right]
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now:

1. Choose \(\delta\) small enough to control both mesh errors.
2. Keep \(\delta\) fixed and take \(n\to\infty\).
3. The learning-rate term vanishes because

   \[
   \eta_n\sqrt n\to0.
   \]

4. The two sampling terms vanish because \(M\) is fixed.
5. Finally let \(\delta\to0\).

This proves uniform compact-time convergence in probability.

The theorem in the audited document fixes

\[
\eta_n=n^{-2},
\]

which satisfies

\[
\eta_n\sqrt n=n^{-3/2}\to0.
\]

More generally, the displayed proof estimate closes for every learning-rate sequence satisfying

\[
\eta_n=o(n^{-1/2}).
\]
