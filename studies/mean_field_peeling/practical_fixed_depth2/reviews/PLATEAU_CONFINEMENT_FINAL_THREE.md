Reviewed the entire supplied `PLATEAU_CONFINEMENT.md` independently. No linked files, other reviews, or experiments were used. I found no counterexample or gap in the stated confinement theorem, its constants, or its conditional pathwise population consequence.

1. **Freezing with realized controls.** Fixing the realized measurable coefficients gives a spatial Lipschitz bound `Lip(g) sum_i |a_i(t)|` in `L^1`. Comparison with the constant solution at a point where every selected gate vanishes proves uniqueness both forward and backward from any reached time. This remains valid for coefficients originally generated by feedback: the proof only compares solutions for their fixed realized values. It does not assume uniqueness of the original feedback system.

2. **Planar geometry and arbitrary switching.** For a nonconstant trajectory, freezing excludes a time at which all open strips are absent. Outside the union of closed double-strip intersections there is therefore exactly one open strip, and all other closed strips are absent. Its index is locally constant, hence constant on each connected component of the time complement. Integrating the equation gives displacement along its unit normal, with scalar displacement equal to the change in its strip coordinate and bounded by two. Continuity extends this bound to component endpoints. Since the visited-intersection time set is closed, every complementary component has an endpoint in that set whenever the set is nonempty. This also covers components adjoining the initial or final time and infinitely many components. Constant paths satisfy all three assertions separately. For two gates, visiting both closed strips without visiting their intersection is impossible by the same fixed-index argument.

3. **Double-strip constant and rank two.** The pair Gram eigenvalues are `1 ± rho`, so the inverse quadratic form on strip coordinates is bounded by `2/(1-|rho|) ≤ 2/delta`. Thus `B_delta = sqrt(2/delta)` is valid. Pairwise separation excludes rank one and ensures that every pair spans the common plane in rank two. The planar lemma gives the stated projected bound with `D_delta = B_delta + 2`; restoring the constant orthogonal component gives exactly the claimed additive bound on the full norm.

4. **Last visits in rank three.** Empty open-gate time sets remove the corresponding gates everywhere, reducing to the two-gate estimate. Otherwise continuity puts each last-activation supremum in its closed strip, whether or not that supremum is an open-gate time. After the earliest such supremum, its gate vanishes, and the remaining pair has fixed transverse coordinate. Both remaining closed strips are visited on that suffix, including when last-activation times tie. The two-gate lemma consequently bounds the entire projected suffix, including its start, by `D_delta`. A suffix consisting of the final point satisfies both strip constraints directly. Thus neither endpoint activation nor an unattained supremum leaves a gap.

5. **Remaining coordinate and final constant.** At the suffix start, the last closed-strip inequality for the removed gate yields `|r| eta_k ≤ 1 + D_delta`. Minimizing the Gram quadratic form over coefficient vectors with `c_k = 1` gives `eta_k^2 ≥ kappa`. Hence the projected endpoint norm is at most `D_delta + (1 + D_delta)/sqrt(kappa)`. Adding the fixed component orthogonal to the full input span yields the stated full-space bound. The separate rank-two argument handles exact degeneracy; the report correctly makes no uniform claim for this rank-three constant as the smallest eigenvalue tends to zero. Time restriction and reversal preserve the equation with integrable controls, validating the subinterval assertion.

6. **Population and discrete-time limits.** The stated single probability-one event supplies locally absolutely continuous paths, locally integrable realized coefficients, and the integral equation on every compact interval. These are sufficient to apply the deterministic result simultaneously at all times of each such path, without any uniform bound on coefficient integrals. The displayed inequality transfers finite `L^p` initial norm moments for `p ≥ 1`. An almost-everywhere differential identity without absolute continuity would not suffice, and the report explicitly excludes that weaker hypothesis. Its application to a raw or capped flow is conditional on the stated row equation and coefficient regularity. The report does not infer population existence, bounds on the other variables, or a discrete-time confinement theorem; an arbitrarily large Euler control at a nonzero gate indeed defeats such a discrete-time conclusion.
