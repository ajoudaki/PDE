# Bounded follow-up: why fourth-order positivity has not yielded a high-order lower bound

This scoped theoretical follow-up uses the same-study lower and upper routes,
the supervisor's assignment and cross-check, and the maintained notation.
No experiment, external source, or other study was used. It is an author
derivation, not an independent review. The source identity below was also
reconstructed independently by the supervisor. The diagnostic here is outside
the earlier source hashes recorded in TANH_CHECK.md.

**Outcome:** no growing-even-order prediction-error lower bound was obtained.
The next source derivative already contains an indefinite curvature term,
so the fourth-order sum of squares does not iterate. Conditional Gaussian
chaos remains a possible mechanism, but neither the complete coefficient nor
its propagation to prediction error is controlled by the isolated moments.

## Exact sixth-tensor contraction

Use the two-hidden-layer tanh network, initialization, half-MSE convention,
and mobilities \((n,1,n)\) of TANH_LOWER_ROUTE.md. For the fixed label vector
\(y\), define

\[
H(w)=\frac1m\sum_{a=1}^m y_a h_a(w),\qquad
F(u,w)=\frac{u^\top H(w)}n,
\]

where \(w=(W^{(1)},W^{(2)})\) comprises the hidden weights. The hidden
metric is

\[
\|V\|_{\rm hid}^2=\|V^{(1)}\|_F^2/n+\|V^{(2)}\|_F^2.
\]

For \(J(w)=D_wH(w)\), the adjoint convention is
\(\langle J(w)^*b,V\rangle_{\rm hid}=b^\top J(w)[V]/n\).
The constant-source flow generated by \(F\), with source time \(s\), is

\[
\frac{du}{ds}=H(w),\qquad
\frac{dw}{ds}=J(w)^*u,\qquad u(0)=0,\quad w(0)=w_0.
\]

This auxiliary source is the exact metric-gradient direction
\(M\nabla F\). It is not assumed to be the physical training flow.
All following derivatives are evaluated at \(s=0\). Put

\[
h=H(w_0),\quad J=D_wH(w_0),\quad
A=J^*h,\quad B=JA.
\]

Thus \(A\) is a hidden parameter increment and \(B\in\mathbb R^n\) is
a feature increment. Parity of the source equations gives odd \(u\) and
even \(w-w_0\). Direct differentiation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
u'&=h,&w''&=A,&u'''&=B,\\
w^{(4)}
 &=J^*B+3\big(D_w^2H(w_0)[A,\cdot]\big)^*h,
&u^{(5)}&=Jw^{(4)}+3D_w^2H(w_0)[A,A].
\end{aligned}
\]

For example, these identities result by substituting
\(u=sh+s^3B/6+O(s^5)\) and
\(w=w_0+s^2A/2+O(s^4)\) into the two source equations. Pairing the
last identity with \(h\), and using the defining adjoints, yields

\[
\frac{h^\top u^{(5)}}n
=\frac{\|B\|_2^2}n
 +6\,\frac{h^\top D_w^2H(w_0)[A,A]}n.
\]

Since \(F=u^\top u'/n\), its fifth derivative is
\(6h^\top u^{(5)}/n+10\|u'''\|_2^2/n\). Consequently

\[
F^{(5)}(0)
=16\frac{\|JA\|_2^2}n
 +36\frac{h^\top D_w^2H(w_0)[A,A]}n. \tag{1}
\]

In the standard tensor convention this is exactly

\[
F^{(5)}(0)=\frac1{m^6}
\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_6}
y_{a_1}\cdots y_{a_6}K^{(6)}_{a_1\cdots a_6}(0).
\]

For comparison, the preceding derivative is
\(F'''(0)=4\|A\|_{\rm hid}^2\). The feature-energy function
\(\mathcal E(w)=\|H(w)\|_2^2/(2n)\) has gradient \(A\) at \(w_0\), so
another exact form of (1) is

\[
F^{(5)}(0)
=36D_w^2\mathcal E(w_0)[A,A]
 -20\frac{\|JA\|_2^2}n. \tag{2}
\]

There is no convexity premise on this feature energy. Gaussian Poincare
controlled \(\|A\|_{\rm hid}^2\) in the fourth-order argument; it supplies
no sign for the Hessian in (2).

An elementary diagnostic makes the cancellation explicit. For the scalar
feature map \(H(w)=\tanh w\), put \(h=\tanh w_0\) and \(p=1-h^2\).
Then \(J=p\), \(A=ph\), and \(H''=-2hp\), so

\[
F'''(0)=4h^2p^2,\qquad
F^{(5)}(0)=8h^2p^3(2-11h^2).
\]

The fifth derivative changes sign, and it vanishes when \(h^2=2/11\)
although the third derivative is strictly positive. This scalar calculation
is only a diagnostic for the proposed energy induction. It is not a
counterexample for the specified Gaussian two-hidden-layer network, and it
does not determine the sign of that network's averaged sixth contraction.

## What the conditional Gaussian route supplies

Condition on the initialized first-layer feature matrix
\(X=[h_a^{(1)}]_{a=1}^m\) and second-layer preactivations
\(Z=W^{(2)}X\). Let \(P_X\) be the orthogonal projection onto the column
space of \(X\). Gaussian conditioning gives the exact representation

\[
W^{(2)}=ZX^++R(I-P_X),
\]

where \(X^+\) is the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse and \(R\) has independent
\(N(0,1/n)\) entries, independent of the conditioned quantities.
For

\[
B_a=\frac{y_a}{m}
       \big(H(w_0)\odot\operatorname{sech}^2 Z_a\big),\qquad
g_a=(I-P_X)R^\top B_a,
\]

the initial first-layer source acceleration contains these exact Gaussian
terms, with covariance

\[
\mathbb E[g_ag_b^\top\mid X,Z]
=\frac{B_a^\top B_b}{n}(I-P_X).
\]

In particular each fixed coordinate has

\[
\mathbb E[(g_a)_j^{2k}\mid X,Z]
 =(2k-1)!!
 \left[\frac{\|B_a\|_2^2}{n}(I-P_X)_{jj}\right]^k. \tag{3}
\]

These factorial moments survive boundedness of tanh. They do not establish
a high-order tensor lower bound. Each initialized response tensor contains
many conditional polynomials in \(R\). Orthogonality of Gaussian Hermite
degrees would protect a **specified nonzero component of the complete
coefficient** against terms of other degrees. Formula (3) neither identifies
that component nor excludes cancellation among terms of the same degree.
Operator contractions and repeated matrix actions cannot be replaced by one
selected diagonal moment.

Even a completed coefficient estimate would leave two prediction-level
obligations. First, the exact dense observable also has Gaussian chaos
components: a large component of the truncated observable must be compared
with its counterpart in the dense prediction. A coefficient moment by itself
does not bound the difference. Second, a moment lower bound needs a probability
bound at the required \(n^{-1/2}\) scale; it can otherwise be supported on
increasingly rare Gaussian realizations.

The clock issue is separate. Under a prescribed constant source, successive
tensors contract into an ordinary source Taylor polynomial. Under general
correlated-data training, the driving coefficients are
\((y_a-f_a(t))/m\), and the frozen hierarchy uses its own predictions in
those coefficients. The residual vector need not remain proportional to
\(y\): already \(\dot f(0)=K(0)y/m\), whose direction need not be \(y\).
Therefore (1)--(3) provide initialization identities, not the error of a
single-variable Taylor polynomial evaluated along the physical training
trajectory.

## Exact unresolved bridge

A growing-order lower bound still needs a signed or otherwise
cancellation-resistant component of the **omitted observable response**,
uniformly over the relevant finite orders, together with a lower propagation
estimate under the two systems' own residuals. Neither the source energy
identity nor the conditional Gaussian moments above supplies it.

The bounded follow-up stops at this obstruction. It does not establish
convergence, divergence, \(q(n)\to\infty\), a logarithmic necessary order, or
any width-dependent storage lower bound.
