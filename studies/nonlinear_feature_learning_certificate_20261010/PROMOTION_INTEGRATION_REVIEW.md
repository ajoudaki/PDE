## Scientific interfaces, notation and preservation

The translation preserves the compact paper's scientific content. Its readout $w$ becomes the book's stored $W^{(L+1)}$; $\phi_\ell$ becomes $\phi^{(\ell)}$; finite normalized norms and pairings are expanded as ordinary Euclidean/Frobenius expressions with explicit factors of $n$. The hidden inverse-mobility norm is exactly the established parameter norm with its readout component set to zero. The added frozen-flow equation makes the comparator explicit without changing it.

I checked the residual convention $r=f-y$, loss $m^{-1}\sum_a r_a^2$, readout normalization $n^{-1}$, and block mobilities $(n,1,\ldots,1,n)$. With $k=2/m$, the zero-readout initialization gives $\dot W^{(L+1)}(0)=k\sum_a y_a h_a^{(L)}(0)$, hence the displayed frozen-flow coefficient $2/(mn)$. The initial hidden accelerations have coefficient $k^2$ in the first layer and $k^2/n$ in later layers. Their squared inverse-mobility energies use $1/n$ only for the first layer. The kernel contraction and time integration consequently retain the paper's cubic coefficient $2/(3k)=m/3$.

The inherited setup supplies fixed $m\ge2$, $L\ge2$, positive label scale, analytic activations with bounded derivatives, the feature Gram gap and the small-label condition. The new nonaffinity and strict nonparallel-input conditions are explicitly confined to the activity theorem and its supporting proof; they do not narrow the older compression result. These conditions imply $d\ge2$ and a training span of dimension at least two. They allow singular input Grams: the proof never inverts $Q^{(0)}$, and the Schur-product lower bound uses positive diagonals rather than input full rank. The proof also retains nonzero activation means, arbitrary nonzero label directions and initialization randomness.

The initial-geometry proof supplies deterministic witnesses before initialization. The backward-field argument retains matrix reuse through its conditional Gaussian projection, and specifies empirical quadratic-Wasserstein convergence and second moments. The real-time argument uses the existing fitting event, initial-field tail truncation and actual feature increments, rather than treating a derivative at zero as a trajectory estimate. The nonlinear first-layer increment is explicitly projected off affine functions on the sphere of the training span.

The theorem distinguishes four conclusions correctly: compressed-predictor nonaffinity of order $t$, compressed-predictor departure from the coupled dense frozen kernel of order $t^3$, dense hidden-feature motion of order $t^2$, and the dense first-layer projected mean-square increment of order $t^4$. It does not assign dense-neuron identities to compressed coordinates, compare against every kernel method, assert a trained population limit, extend GF to GD, or infer endpoint or test-risk gaps.

The time and probability quantifiers are compatible with the compression interfaces. For each fixed positive time, Legendre's absolute error $CY/(\sqrt n[\log(en)]^3)$ and the two selected models' $Y/n$ error vanish. Applying the triangle inequality transfers the positive dense gaps. The proof correctly uses those absolute errors rather than relying only on a relative-variability ratio. The remainder convention permits one deterministic sufficiently small interval for the dense estimates, while the compressed conclusions explicitly avoid arbitrary sequences of times tending to zero with width. Constants may depend on the complete fixed problem; they are not silently identified with the older activation-and-depth-only constant $C$.

Taylor's witnesses are passive inputs with no supplied labels or added training terms. They are appended before initialization, so the existing panel interface applies. Since $m+p\ge2$, increasing the panel count by at most four multiplies its linear factor by at most three and its quadratic factor by at most nine. This preserves the storage orders with the stated augmented count.

The original chapter is preserved byte for byte outside the single insertion immediately before its existing assembly heading. The insertion has 26 new stable identifiers with no collision, and all 20 distinct new reference targets are unique in the assembled chapter. There is no dependence on study files or paper-local labels in the promoted prose. The fitting proof and three approximation constructions are referenced, not duplicated. The new early-time certificates add a mechanism-preservation conclusion to the compression chapter. The index and configuration retain chapter 9 in Part II, immediately before Part III's learning results; this is a suitable local placement and introduces no new chapter or part.
