# Fixed-horizon width bridge for the quadratic training program

## Lemma

Fix (N<\infty), (h\in\mathbb R), and

\[
 \psi(x)=px+qx^2.
\]

For the (q=1,L=2) width-(n) simultaneous-ascent network, every terminal
empirical scalar in the (N)-step program, in particular (f_n^N),
converges almost surely and in every finite (L^r) to the corresponding
width-first Gaussian-program scalar.  Hence

\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E f_n^N=F_N(h).           \tag{1}
\]

The statement is pointwise in the fixed pair ((N,h)); its constants are
not uniform in (N).

## Proof

The polynomially-smooth fixed-program theorem used here has the following
specialized form.  A finite NETSOR\({}^\top\)+Moment program generated by
finitely many iid Gaussian vector sources, finitely many iid Gaussian
matrices used any fixed number of times in both orientations, coordinatewise
(C^\infty) functions whose derivatives of every order have polynomial
growth, and finitely many empirical-moment scalars has a deterministic
Gaussian-program limit.  Each terminal empirical scalar converges almost
surely and in (L^r) for every finite (r).  This is the all-orders
polynomially-smooth conclusion recorded and hypothesis-checked in
`generic_first_stieltjes/PROBABILISTIC_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md`, Section 1.

It remains to verify that trained-matrix evolution is literally one such
fixed program.  Write

\[
H^s=\psi(u^s),\qquad C^s=a^s\psi'(z^s),
\]

and let (W^0) denote the initialized connector.  Iterating the matrix
update gives the exact identity

\[
W^s=W^0+\frac h{\sqrt n}\sum_{r<s}C^r(H^r)^T.       \tag{2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
z^s&=\frac{W^0H^s}{\sqrt n}
 +h\sum_{r<s}Q_{rs}^{(n)}C^r,
&Q_{rs}^{(n)}&=\frac1n(H^r)^TH^s,\\
b^s&=\frac{(W^0)^TC^s}{\sqrt n}
 +h\sum_{r<s}K_{rs}^{(n)}H^r,
&K_{rs}^{(n)}&=\frac1n(C^r)^TC^s.                 \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Starting with the independent Gaussian vectors (a^0,u^0), equations
(3) and

\[
a^{s+1}=a^s+h\psi(z^s),\qquad
u^{s+1}=u^s+h b^s\psi'(u^s)                       \tag{4}
\]

give a finite chronological program with the action list

\[
W^0H^0,(W^0)^TC^0,\ldots,
W^0H^{N-1},(W^0)^TC^{N-1},W^0H^N.                 \tag{5}
\]

All remaining lines are coordinatewise sums or products and the moment
scalars (Q_{rs}^{(n)},K_{rs}^{(n)}).  Thus (2)--(5) encode the actual
trained network without replacing the reused matrix by independent copies.

The functions (psi) and (psi') are polynomials.  They are
(C^\infty), every derivative has polynomial growth, and derivatives above
orders two and one respectively vanish.  Gaussian initialization supplies
all finite source moments.  The program has (2N+1<\infty) matrix actions
and finitely many scalar lines.  Every hypothesis of the specialized theorem
is therefore satisfied.

It follows that the terminal moment scalar

\[
f_n^N=\frac1n\sum_i a_i^N\psi(z_i^N)
\]

converges in (L^r), (r<\infty), to its Gaussian-program value.  Taking
(r=1) proves (1).  Matrix reuse, transpose responses, unbounded quadratic
growth, and uniform integrability are all covered by the invoked theorem;
no Gram inverse or full-rank assumption is used.  \(\square\)

## Relation to a direct adaptive-conditioning proof

At a fixed horizon one can also run the stopped adaptive-conditioning
induction with a backward exponent tower: every quadratic composition or
product merely multiplies the required moment exponent by a finite factor,
so the tower terminates after (2N+1) actions.  That proves the stopped
ledger.  Removing stopping directly still requires a uniform high-moment
bound for the raw, unstopped polynomial program; a naive Hilbert--Schmidt
argument can lose a power of (n).  The polynomially-smooth fixed-program
theorem supplies precisely this missing (L^r) control.  Therefore there is
no width-bridge gap in the lemma, but claiming that the older
linear-growth adaptive-conditioning proof applies verbatim would be
incorrect.
