# Primary-literature boundary

Sources inspected on2026-10-01:

- Wang, Zhu, Torralba and Efros, *Dataset Distillation*, arXiv1811.10959v3 (2020 revision of2018 paper): https://arxiv.org/html/1811.10959v3 . The work optimizes small synthetic datasets so a learner trained on them performs well on the original task, including fixed-initialization settings. Thus differentiating through learning to design data is established; this study's eight-label calibration is a narrow proof of use, not a new distillation formulation.
- Cui, Wang, Si and Hsieh, *Scaling Up Dataset Distillation to ImageNet-1K with Constant Memory*, ICML2023, pp6565–6590: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/cui23e.html ; technical paper https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/cui23e/cui23e.pdf . Sections3–4 give TESLA's trajectory-matching objective, rearranged gradient computation and soft-label assignment. It claims the same trajectory-matching updates with memory independent of the number of matching steps. Consequently reduced evolving state here cannot support a novelty claim of first memory-efficient differentiation through learning. Our comparison axis is a smaller learned-state representation of an approximate inner learner, with a held-out prediction objective, and it has not been benchmarked against TESLA.
- Cazenavette et al., *Dataset Distillation by Matching Training Trajectories*, CVPR2022: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11932 and https://georgecazenavette.github.io/mtt-distillation/ . It optimizes synthetic data using endpoints of multi-step student training against stored expert trajectories. Our experiment requires neither an expert-trajectory store nor a parameter-endpoint matching objective; that difference describes scope, not superiority.

The current response-memory paper proves prediction approximation in a specified small-label setting. It does not prove derivative approximation with respect to synthetic labels, interchange of a closure limit and differentiation, or preservation of the outer optimum. All such utility claims here must come from explicit derivative checks and dense retraining. A lower moving-coordinate count does not imply lower total memory: initialized matrices, activations and reverse-mode graphs remain.
