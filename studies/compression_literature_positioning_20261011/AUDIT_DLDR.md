## What is actually reduced

The method records a substantial ordinary training prefix, computes leading principal directions of the parameter snapshots, and restarts training in their span. If the dense parameter vector has \(D\) entries and the chosen span has dimension \(r\), training evolves \(r\) coordinates but retains a \(D\times r\) basis and a full network evaluation. Algorithm 2 explicitly computes the full gradient before projecting it. Consequently a tiny number of trainable coordinates does not establish tiny **total retained storage**, cheap evaluation, or a width-polylogarithmic emulator. Storing snapshots during setup also costs \(D\) times their number.

Examples include 30-epoch setup for ResNet8, 50/100-epoch prefixes in CIFAR comparisons, and 60 of a 90-epoch ImageNet baseline. Reported spaces have roughly 40 coordinates for CIFAR and 120 for ImageNet. The setup is not initialization-only information extraction in the no-rollout sense. This is disclosed by the authors. Our own rollout-based initializer must be compared honestly on costs and guarantees, not portrayed as using no computed dense evolution.

## Claim-level assessment

The low-dimensional hypothesis is supported empirically, not established by a theorem for generic deep nonlinear networks. Matching task accuracy after a restarted projected training run does not prove that the original dense **prediction path** has been reproduced. The samples used for PCA already come from a trained path; explaining their variance does not certify future closed-loop approximation. No rate relating \(r\) to width, a prescribed prediction error, or dense-seed variability appears in the fully read versions.

There is a specific logical issue with the claimed verification criterion on p. 3414: successful optimization inside a small subspace does not imply that the original unrestricted optimization trajectory lies in that subspace. For an elementary exact test, take the strictly convex loss \(\mathcal L(u,v)=\frac12(u-1)^2+(v-1)^2\), initialized at zero. Its full gradient flow is \((u(t),v(t))=(1-e^{-t},1-e^{-2t})\), a curved path not contained in any line. Training restricted to the line \(u=v=s\) nevertheless reaches the same unique minimizer and zero loss. This refutes the general implication used to motivate the criterion, not the numerical observation that particular dense trajectories have rapidly decaying snapshot singular values. Equal endpoint performance is evidence of low-dimensional **trainability**, not by itself evidence of trajectory fidelity. The two can coexist but require separate measurements.

The proposed P-BFGS uses the rank-reduced Hessian as a heuristic. Approximating \(H\) by \(PH_0P^\top\) does not alone bound the error of replacing \(H^{-1}\) by the pseudoinverse of that approximation. Small discarded eigenvalues are exactly where inversion is most sensitive. This is not treated as a proved theorem in this audit.

A concrete optimizer qualification: §4.3 invokes positive BFGS curvature \(y^\top s>0\), then specifies only Armijo backtracking. Armijo alone does not ensure that condition on a nonconvex loss. For the one-dimensional local model \(f(x)=-x^2/2\), starting at \(x=1\) with inverse-Hessian estimate one, every positive step \(s=\alpha\) satisfies Armijo for \(0<c<1\), while \(y^\top s=-\alpha^2<0\). The same example can be embedded locally in a smooth bounded-below loss; global unboundedness is not the cause. A curvature test, damped update or stronger line-search condition is needed for a positive-definiteness guarantee. This concerns the printed algorithmic justification, not proof that the reported runs failed. Code was not executed.

The experiments are valuable evidence that a training-derived small search space can preserve or improve test accuracy, across several architectures and label-noise settings. The source reports a roughly 30% runtime improvement including setup, with setup exceeding 80% of the new procedure's runtime. The title's broad “true” wording should therefore be read as empirical support in those settings, not an unconditional width-uniform compression theorem.

The newly supplied appendix does not add a compression theorem. Appendix A varies setup from 10 to 100 epochs with 40 directions, sampling four times per epoch when the epoch count is below 40. Appendix B compares restarting at initialization to warm-starting after the recorded prefix; its explanation of the generalization difference is expressly not rigorous. Appendix C uses pretrained ImageNet models, 5/10/15 fine-tuning epochs, 30 directions and one projected BFGS epoch, with SWA as a comparison. It records the additional SWA pass to recalibrate batch normalization. These details close the protocol gap but do not remove the trained-prefix, dense-basis or trajectory-guarantee distinctions. The published optimizer still specifies Armijo rather than a curvature-enforcing condition, so the local counterexample above is not superseded by this appendix.

## Positioning

This is a close **empirical dimensionality-reduction** precedent. Credit it explicitly for learning a tiny optimization subspace and exploiting it algorithmically. The unresolved question relative to our manuscript is whether a compact **stored representation**, rather than a small coordinate vector attached to a dense basis, autonomously shadows a specified dense learner through convergence with a certified prediction norm. The complete published paper and appendix do not establish that contract. Confidence is high in the displayed state/cost accounting and the two elementary logical checks; experimental outcomes are author-reported, not independently reproduced.
