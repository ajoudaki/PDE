## Introduction

Understanding a learning system begins with identifying what it must remember. Compressing a trained predictor is not enough: two models with the same current predictions can respond differently to the next training update. A more demanding form of compression retains a small state that continues learning on its own. Such a state does not yet explain every learned representation, but it identifies information sufficient to reproduce the learning process. We ask whether increasing the width of a deep neural network makes this dynamical description small.

The reference is the finite network itself, not an assumed ideal population predictor. Independent initializations of the same architecture generate different prediction trajectories. This variability provides a natural accuracy benchmark: can a much smaller autonomous system track one dense realization more closely than another independently initialized dense realization does? The comparison must use the same inputs and the same training horizon. We require control throughout training, including the fitted limit, rather than at a selected checkpoint.

We answer this question for fully connected networks of arbitrary fixed depth, with Gaussian hidden-weight initialization, zero initial readout, and the specified layerwise learning-rate scaling. Under strip-analytic activation regularity, a positive initial population feature-Gram gap, and a sufficiently small but fixed nonzero label scale, the dense trajectory admits autonomous compressed representations whose error is negligible relative to actual dense-run variability. For each fixed admissible problem, the guarantee holds at every sufficiently large individual width with any prescribed fixed confidence. The allowed label scale does not decrease with width. The comparison uses a proved lower bound on that variability, not merely an upper envelope that could overestimate it. Precisely, the ratio of two complete-trajectory supremum errors tends to zero in probability; this is not a ratio bound separately at every time or a lower bound on endpoint variability.

Two constructions compress the total retained state to a polylogarithm of width. Harmonic compression controls every input on the sphere and retains \(O((\log n)^{3d+2})\) real coordinates at fixed problem parameters. Taylor compression retains \(O((\log n)^3)\) coordinates for a finite panel whose inputs are supplied at initialization; their test labels are not used. Both achieve prediction error of order \(n^{-1}\) over the complete trajectory. Legendre compression provides an intermediate reduction: it replaces moving response histories by polynomial moments, reducing the evolving state to \(n^{5/4+o(1)}\), while retaining the fixed dense mixers. The theorem keeps the sample, geometry and dimension dependence explicit and counts the selected models' fixed coefficients as well as their moving state.

Depth makes this a different problem from compressing a population of shallow neurons. With one hidden layer and fixed input dimension, each neuron is a particle with a fixed-dimensional parameter vector. Between two wide hidden layers, interactions pass through a matrix with quadratically many entries. Merely replacing that matrix by an integral operator does not supply a small finite representation. Nor does observing a low-dimensional trajectory show that a separately evolved reduced model will remain on it. The total-storage Harmonic and Taylor constructions remove the retained dense environment without losing the feedback that determines future learning; Legendre instead reduces the moving response memory while retaining the fixed mixers.

The common idea is to compress the responses through which training acts, rather than approximate every weight independently. Temporal regularity makes response histories compressible. Legendre retains their orthogonal moments; Harmonic additionally uses regularity across the input sphere; Taylor represents only the response curves required by a declared finite panel. The decisive second step is dynamical stability. Approximation errors alter subsequent features and residuals, so a compact record of the dense path is not automatically a compact learner. The proofs preserve the source geometry responsible for descent and exploit cancellations between readout error and residual feedback. This controls accumulated error through the finite total residual activity, avoiding a generic amplification factor that grows exponentially with the training horizon.

The result does not obtain compression by replacing the reference with a lazy or linear model. Under local additional nondegeneracy assumptions, a complementary theorem gives width-independent early-time separation from affine input predictors and from the dense network's own frozen initial-kernel predictor, and shows that every dense hidden layer moves. Accurate compressed predictions inherit the predictor separations. This is an early-time certificate of nonlinear feature learning, not a claim that compressed hidden coordinates coincide with dense neurons or that every possible adaptive-kernel theory is excluded.

The retained-state theorem and the cost of constructing that state are separate. An initialization-derivative construction establishes information provenance; efficient alternatives compute and discard numerical continuations from the initial data. For fixed problem parameters, the latter have quadratic-in-width times polylogarithmic cost for a supplied dense reference, or linear-in-width times polylogarithmic cost when jointly generating a fresh implicit Gaussian reference. Neither route gives a free conversion of arbitrary supplied dense matrices into linear setup work. State counts are real-coordinate bounds, not bit-complexity or universal practical-speedup claims.

## Related work and positioning

### Compression assumptions versus compression derived from dense training

[Universal compression theory](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/bc71544dfef150412977e59d221b6597-Paper-Conference.pdf) connects weighted support reduction to permutation symmetry and regularity. Its dynamic statement requires the trained observable to retain the stipulated fixed-dimensional analytic structure; its discussion leaves the compression rate for networks with all hidden layers wide unresolved. This is precisely where a deep-network reduction needs additional work: the effective object dimension and the analytic constants of the training map cannot simply be assumed uniform as width and training time grow. Our response estimates and stability analysis discharge model-specific obligations of this kind for the stated dense dynamics.

[Dynamical low-rank training](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/7e98b00eeafcdaeb0c5661fb9355be3a-Paper-Conference.pdf) evolves nonlinear weight factors and gives a robust approximation analysis. Its error premise bounds the component of the dense gradient normal to a low-rank manifold near the trajectory. It does not establish that premise for the ordinary all-wide Gaussian network considered here. A conditional low-rank approximation theorem is different from deriving compressibility and its error scale from that network's initialization, data and training equations. Our selected constructions compress response geometry; they need not posit that the full evolving dense weight matrix has small rank.

### Computable limits and finite-width closed expansions

The [projected infinite-width model of Yang–Santacroce–Hu](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tUMr0Iox8XW) is an especially relevant computational advance. It deliberately uses structured low-rank initialization and projected updates, yielding an explicit feature-learning limit whose representation appends gradient directions during training. The resulting storage grows with the number of updates. The source proves convergence to this projected limit, not fidelity of that limit to an unrestricted finite Gaussian dense trajectory. In contrast, our reference remains the specified dense learner, and the compressed state's fidelity to that reference is part of the theorem.

The [neural tangent hierarchy](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/huang20l.html), [diagrammatic finite-width expansions](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=S1gFvANKDS), and [Roberts–Yaida–Hanin's effective theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10165) provide initialized finite-order closed descriptions, including corrections to frozen-kernel training and fitted predictions. Their existence must not be obscured. The distinction is the training regime and the remainder guarantee: at fixed depth their expansions describe corrections around kernel scaling, whereas our nonlinear feature-learning certificate is width-independent. The diagrammatic general deep nonlinear power counting is partly conjectural, and the explicit perturbative endpoint formulas do not alone furnish a pathwise remainder uniform through fitting. Our separate hierarchy-storage benchmark concerns its stated hard family and literal tensor representation; it is not a complexity lower bound against all implementations of these earlier theories.

### From limiting descriptions to finite sufficient state

Mean-field limits, tensor programs and dynamical mean-field theory already describe nonlinear feature learning in deep networks. They are genuine dynamical theories; the distinction is not whether they possess dynamics, but whether they provide a small **finite retained representation** of the particular finite network being approximated. [Nguyen–Pham](https://doi.org/10.4171/MSL/42) and [Neural Hilbert Ladders](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v25/23-1225.html) use evolving functions or measures. [Tensor Programs IV](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yang21c.html) establishes fixed-step limits in feature-learning parameterizations. The explicit general deep evaluation in [DMFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09653v3) retains sample- and time-indexed kernels and causal response histories. These descriptions reveal substantial structure without, by themselves, supplying the bounded retained state and complete-trajectory approximation proved here.

Finite-width fluctuations are also established objects of study, not an unaddressed subject. [Pham–Nguyen](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/2639ba2137371773aa1e64e7735cdb30-Abstract.html) constructs an initialization-coupled sampled-neuron correction and states quantitative second-order finite-horizon estimates; [Bordelon–Pehlevan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03408v3) develops a formal dynamical fluctuation theory and studies prediction variance. Their relevant representations remain function-valued or history-dependent. Even granting the strongest advertised finite-horizon statements, an additional construction and stability theorem are needed to turn them into a small autonomous emulator through the fitted endpoint. Our claim is that full conjunction, not priority for analyzing seed fluctuations or constructing a coupled correction.

### Empirical compression of training and predictors

[Neural Tangent Transfer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/liu20o.html) directly anticipates the objective of finding a sparse initialization whose subsequent output trajectory follows a dense teacher. Its exact theorem concerns linear models under perfect initial output and kernel matching, while its deep nonlinear demonstrations are empirical. [Intrinsic dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08838), [tiny gradient subspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04754), and [dynamic linear dimensionality reduction](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3178101) provide further evidence that training can concentrate in small search spaces. A small trainable coordinate vector can nevertheless retain a dense basis and require a full network evaluation. [Koopman forecasting](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/169806bb68ccbf5e6f96ddc60c40a044-Paper.pdf) and [DMD acceleration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14371) autonomously extrapolate from recorded training segments, without proving the present retained-state and all-time seed-relative contract.

These methods, along with pruning, distillation and lottery-ticket approaches, are important algorithmic precedents. Task accuracy, a matched endpoint, a few fitted coordinates and a certified trajectory emulator are nevertheless different achievements. Our experiments address some of these distinctions empirically; the theorem's contribution is to control a specified finite dense trajectory in a single norm with an explicit stored-state bound.

### Complementary feature-learning and reduction theories

Deep nonlinear feature learning itself is not new. The structured training analyses of [Nichani–Damian–Lee](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/236b6a814a1d2c0ff504ca7bf380f7ff-Paper-Conference.pdf) and [Allen-Zhu–Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04413) address representation and sample efficiency rather than small-state emulation of a given dense trajectory. [Adaptive-kernel endpoint theories](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/lauditi25a.html) give additional finite task-adapted descriptions under their equilibrium, scaling and regularization qualifications. Our local feature-learning theorem serves a different purpose: it certifies that the reference being compressed has not collapsed to an affine predictor or its own frozen initial kernel.

The physical analogy is memory reduction. Eliminating microscopic coordinates generally produces memory and unresolved forcing, as in [Mori–Zwanzig/optimal prediction](https://math.huji.ac.il/~razk/Publications/PDF/CHK00.pdf). A finite collection of auxiliary moments can provide an approximate Markovian representation of that memory. Here regular response histories supply the approximation space, and closed-loop stability makes that representation useful for learning. This analogy explains the organizing principle; it is not an assertion that an existing projection theorem already proves our compression result.

## Editorial choices behind this draft

The introduction leads with the positive theorem, not a list of defects in other proofs. The related-work section grants each source its strongest relevant contribution before specifying the missing implication. Detailed counterexamples belong in the internal audit or a carefully checked technical comparison, not in a rhetorical attack on the field.

Do not replace these qualifications with “first theory of deep feature learning,” “first finite closed training model,” “all prior compression is endpoint-only,” “mean field is not a dynamical system,” or “all competing limits assume no feature learning.” Those statements are contradicted by the sources. Conversely, do not reduce the positive result to “another low-dimensional description”: it is a **quantitatively sufficient autonomous state for a finite nonlinear learner, through convergence and below its realized seed-variability scale**.
