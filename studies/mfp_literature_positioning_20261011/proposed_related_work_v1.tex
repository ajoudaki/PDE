\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Gaussian program limits and feature learning.}
Tensor Programs III provides the closest probabilistic framework
\cite{yang2021tp3}. Its Box~1 defines the Gaussian source-response
recursion for reused matrices and transposes. The scalar-feedback extension
in Theorem~E.15 permits pseudo-Lipschitz maps without a rank-stability
assumption; the smooth polynomial-growth maps used here satisfy that
condition. Its handling of singular Gaussian laws also precedes MFP's
formal-source bookkeeping. Non-Gaussian Tensor Programs states almost-sure
and finite-$L^p$ convergence and develops the raw-entry moment bounds used
here \cite{golikov2022nongaus}. For our fixed deterministic initial scalars,
the Gaussian limit theorem and those moment bounds give the required
expectation convergence. Our proof makes this specialization and its
derivative compilation explicit. The later Tensor Programs IVb extends
the language to nonlinear outer operations, constructs backpropagation
programs, and gives a master theorem including finite-$L^p$ convergence
under polynomial smoothness and uniform input moments
\cite{yang2023tp4b}. Its proved Gaussian construction applies to our fixed
deterministic initial scalars. Its language and adaptive-update scope are
broader than the MFP frontend; our contribution is not an extension beyond
that probabilistic scope.

Tensor Programs IV derives recursive Gaussian limits for fixed finite
training histories in feature-learning parameterizations
\cite{yang2021tp4}. Its maximal-update model agrees with the normalization
of our showcased MLP after rescaling the vector parameter blocks. In
particular, our feature-kernel jets do not introduce a different
feature-learning regime. That paper also calculates selected derivatives
of post-update features and outputs. MFP supplies a uniform query language
and compiler for admitted derivatives rather than a new principle that
such finite calculations have Gaussian-program limits.

\paragraph{Higher derivatives, tangent hierarchies and diagrams.}
The neural tangent hierarchy organizes repeated gradient contractions of
network outputs and studies quantitative truncation of training dynamics
in the tangent-kernel scaling \cite{huang2020nth}. Chen, He and
Su give a recursive symbolic program for this hierarchy and present
selected Gaussian initialization calculations in a shallow network,
using them to motivate label-dependent kernels \cite{chen2020lantk}.
These are direct predecessors for derivative recurrences and
label-dependent kernel expressions. Their particular hierarchy and
normalization differ from an arbitrary admitted MFP query and from our
feature-kernel example. Their stated positive-time approximation theorems
address a temporal question beyond the finite initial jets studied here.

Feynman-diagram methods organize Gaussian contractions and finite-width
asymptotics of products of neural derivative tensors
\cite{dyer2019feynman}. Aitken and Gur-Ari establish the corresponding
correlation-function scaling theorem for fixed polynomial activations
\cite{aitken2020nonlinear}. Wick expansion in that setting yields explicit
coefficients, not merely an existence result. These methods can retain
finite-width information that MFP's leading-limit graph discards. MFP
instead allows its stated smooth polynomial-growth activation class and
compiles finite derivative and update programs into recursively specified
Gaussian integrals. Its restricted activation-moment reduction uses
classical Wick and Stein identities rather than introducing a new
Gaussian integration principle.

\paragraph{Executable infinite networks and symbolic tensor calculus.}
Neural Tangents translates supported architectures into numerical infinite
NNGP and NTK kernels, and also provides empirical kernels, prediction
solvers and arbitrary-order finite-network Taylor expansions
\cite{novak2020neuraltangents}. Its current activation interface includes
both user-supplied Gaussian moment functions and numerical quadrature;
its supported layer families include particular weight-sharing cases.
It therefore provides a substantially stronger baseline than a library
restricted to first derivatives or closed-form activations. MFP's returned
object is different: a symbolic Gaussian-expectation graph for an admitted
derivative/update program, with the general named-matrix response rule
applied automatically.

Tensorgrad supplies symbolic tensor differentiation, symbolic Gaussian
means and covariances, tensor Gaussian integration by parts, and shared
matrix algebra \cite{ahle2026tensorgrad}. These capabilities already
compose into some of our elementary moving-derivative examples. The
additional MFP transformation eliminates the growing-width matrix
calculation and supplies a fixed-dimensional limiting graph for its
declared neural-program class. Conversely, Tensorgrad admits tensor
algebra beyond our restricted frontend, and Neural Tangents offers
numerical integration and architectural functionality that MFP does not
replace. The comparison concerns the scope and semantics of the complete
transformation, not priority for symbolic differentiation or Gaussian
expectation nodes separately.
