# MFP master theorem and compiler: literature assessment and positioning

Review date: 11 October 2026. Target: the committed `paper_MFP/main.tex` and its bundled compiler. The user's clarification makes the master theorem's usable calculus and the compiler the central objects of comparison; an alternative proof technique is not treated as a contribution by itself. This study does not edit the manuscript.

## Main judgment

**The strongest defensible contribution is an explicit, executable derivative calculus for a precisely declared class of Gaussian neural programs, with a constructive correctness theorem and worked symbolic calculations.** It brings finite physical differentiation, represented updates, matrix-reuse responses, singular source bookkeeping and Gaussian expectation output under one documented interface.

The evidence does **not** support positioning MFP as the first general Gaussian program limit theorem, the first recursive Gaussian description of feature learning, the first high-order neural derivative hierarchy, or the first symbolic Gaussian-expectation engine. These components have substantial and sometimes almost literal predecessors. This is consistent with the manuscript's existing statement that it does not claim a new general probabilistic program theorem.

This is a meaningful contribution if presented at the right level: a user supplies an admitted observable and derivative request; the construction supplies the finite-width derivative program and its width-independent expectation graph. Reconstructing that complete transformation by hand from an abstract program theorem is precisely the work the calculus and implementation organize. However, “a useful implementation with a rigorous contract” is better supported here than an unqualified claim of a fundamentally stronger limit theory or an efficient general solver.

The review found specific errors and proof gaps in several predecessors. They are documented with locations and examples below and in the individual reports. **None of the verified issues restores a broad first-master-theorem novelty claim.** Some concern inputs outside MFP's assumptions; others have elementary repairs or concern positive-time/classification claims that MFP does not prove.

The diagram and NTH results are not uniformly weaker MFP versions: their parameterization can retain order-one random initial outputs, and they study finite-width corrections or positive-time trajectories. A deterministic mean-field scalar limit answers a different question. Conversely, treating their initialization bounds as a general smooth program compiler would overstate what they provide.

## The overlaps that most constrain positioning

**1. The primitive Gaussian recursion is prior art.** TP III Box 1, pp8–10, contains the same source groups, uncentered covariance entries and expected formal source derivatives. Theorem E.15, p33, gives rank-free scalar feedback for pseudo-Lipschitz maps; MFP's smooth polynomial-growth class satisfies that condition. Singular duplicate sources alter individual formal derivative coefficients but not the contracted response. This is a semantic clarification to implement carefully, not a new limiting law.

**2. All-Lp convergence is not a new probabilistic extension in the actual MFP class.** TP III alone leaves its general polynomial expectation extension open. NGTP's Appendix J supplies the relevant raw-entry bounds, including adaptive reuse and all fixed derivative orders. Combining those bounds with Gaussian convergence yields the MFP scalar expectation and all-Lp result for fixed deterministic initial scalars. One must not infer novel all-Lp theory by citing only TP III's narrower expectation appendix while omitting NGTP.

The later **TP IVb Theorem 2.6.10, p23**, states all finite Lp convergence in a larger smooth program language; Setup 2.6.4 explicitly imposes the uniform initial-scalar moments missing from NGTP's literal wording. Its Gaussian proof in §§4.7–4.8 also supplies an integrated-program/equivalence route for the initial-scalar rate class containing MFP's fixed constants, so the unresolved TP III Appendix N check need not be the sole foundation of this overlap. Its notation O-tilde(n^-1/2) means n^(1/2-epsilon) times the error tends to zero almost surely for every epsilon>0; it is not an ordinary numerical error bound C/sqrt(n). The extension to arbitrarily slowly convergent initial scalars is expressly deferred in §4.9.1; MFP does not need that extension. The non-Gaussian broader-language proof is compressed and delegates estimates to earlier work; the Gaussian MFP subclass can instead use its Gaussian proof with the verified NGTP moment bounds.

**3. The featured training regime is already maximal update.** Write MFP's first-layer stored weights B and readout a, with output a^T h/n and vector mobilities n. Setting u=B/sqrt(n), v=a/sqrt(n) gives TP IV's maximal-update variables with ordinary Euclidean updates. This is an equality of finite-width models after parameter change, not a loose analogy. TP IV Algorithm 1 also expressly prescribes compilation to recursive Gaussian calculations.

**4. Higher derivatives and symbolic labels have real precedents.** NTH and Dyer define exact successive gradient contractions. LANTK Appendix B.1 gives a symbolic recursion for arbitrary finite hierarchy order, and its label-quadratic construction is directly relevant to MFP's example. TP IV differentiates feature moments and outputs with respect to a one-step learning rate. A one-step second derivative differs from a moving-flow second derivative, but the latter is still ordinary finite AD followed by the existing program semantics when closure holds.

**5. Symbolic Gaussian integration and AD already coexist in software.** An executed Tensorgrad program reproduced E[D_V^2(x^2)]=120 for V(x)=-x^3, as well as symbolic Gaussian mean/covariance moments. Neural Tangents already supports arbitrary finite Taylor order and current generic activation moment/quadrature interfaces. MFP's advantage to demonstrate is the complete width-eliminating transformation for admitted reused-matrix neural programs, not the presence of differentiation and expectation symbols.

## What MFP adds, and how strongly to describe it

The [manuscript assessment](manuscript_assessment.md) records the exact theory/API boundary. Within the reviewed corpus, the supported contribution has four connected parts:

1. **A checkable neural derivative interface.** Accepted types, normalization factors, matrix identities, parameter directions, contractions and updates are explicit. Exact finite AD includes scalar adjoints, moving residuals, represented matrix states, and frozen-seed conventions. This reduces the user's burden of proving that each new calculation fits an abstract limit theorem.
2. **One constructive correctness statement for the interface.** The theorem ties the admitted syntax to exact finite differentiation and then to deterministic Gaussian expectation limits, with moment control. Its self-contained proof and hypothesis accounting are useful even where the probabilistic conclusion is inherited or recoverable from earlier machinery.
3. **An executable symbolic result.** The returned object records named sources, covariances, integrands, shared subexpressions and applied rules. It eliminates width from the probabilistic calculation. Generic integrals stay explicit; special polynomial cases reduce exactly. No inspected alternative exposed this same complete frontend-to-limit graph contract.
4. **A delimited stronger reduction and demanding examples.** The initialization normal form has an actual syntactic restriction and terminating Wick/Stein reduction. The examples exercise reuse, singularity, updates, physical moving derivatives, and symbolic geometry/labels in one system. Their value is that a new admitted query is handled by the existing rules rather than requiring a query-specific probability derivation.

The difference is primarily constructive, organizational and executable. It should not be inflated into a new universality class. It is also more substantial than printing an already known scalar recurrence: the finite derivative language, limiting semantics and implementation must agree across the declared class. A claim of firstness for that exact combination remains a bounded literature inference, not a theorem; use a concrete description instead.

## What should and should not be claimed

| Candidate sentence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| “MFP gives a constructive calculus and symbolic compiler for the admitted finite neural derivative/update language.” | Supported, with the stated theorem and implementation qualifications. |
| “The compiler returns an explicit finite Gaussian-expectation graph for the infinite-width observable.” | Supported; evaluation of a generic integral may remain symbolic. |
| “MFP preserves actual matrix reuse and treats singular formal sources consistently.” | Supported capability; credit TP source-response semantics. |
| “MFP extends Gaussian program theory to every finite derivative.” | Too broad. Say it proves compilation for its declared derivative operations, which then fall within smooth program theory. |
| “Arbitrary fixed depth, sample count and derivative order are allowed.” | Supported as fixed finite quantifiers in the admitted language; not a scalability guarantee or uniform limit in these parameters. |
| “The program accepts arbitrary symbolic covariance matrices.” | Misleading frontend claim. It accepts symbolic geometry factors on shared Gaussian roots; lower-level expression covariance is a separate interface. |
| “Earlier methods assume away reuse, scalar feedback, singularity, labels or higher derivatives.” | False as a general comparison. Some narrower applications do, the closest program theories do not. |
| “MFP is the first symbolic Gaussian neural calculus / first feature-learning compiler.” | Unsupported; strong component and recipe precedents exist. |
| “The master theorem computes exact finite-width expectations.” | False in general. Finite derivative compilation is exact; Gaussian evaluation gives the width limit. |
| “The kernel-jet example computes the entire NTK.” | False: it computes the specified final hidden feature kernel/readout block. |
| “Finite compilation proves efficient computation or positive-time reconstruction.” | False; neither follows. |

## Proof scrutiny and its consequences

The individual reports distinguish a stated theorem, reconstructed valid argument, missing hypothesis, false printed formula, and unresolved proof. Significant findings include:

- **NGTP, Setup 3.6 and Appendix K:** finite moments at each width do not imply uniform integrability of initial scalar sequences; a rare-spike example violates the literal all-Lp statement. The printed reverse recurrence misses chains of scalar averages. Fixed deterministic MFP scalars avoid the first issue, and the Gaussian MFP specialization can use Appendix J without the defective non-Gaussian interpolation branch. Ordinary scalar adjoints repair the latter algebra.
- **TP III, Appendix N:** the rank-removal proof contains a malformed normalization and a compressed small-direction coefficient assertion that this review did not independently complete. This does not refute its theorem or remove its prior statement. The full base transpose theorem was reconstructed. MFP supplies its own smooth perturbation/clipping route.
- **NTH, Appendix B and Corollary A.5:** the printed argument differentiates norm inequalities and drops residual derivatives; the stated stretched-exponential probability is incompatible with a polylogarithmic bound on maximum Gaussian coordinates. Exact hierarchy identities survive; the review does not certify the quantitative time theorem as printed.
- **TP IV, latest Appendix H.7:** direct one-layer calculations disagree with printed second-derivative factors, and the small-smoothing claim E[phi_sigma''(Z)^2]->0 is false (it diverges as 1/sigma). This concerns the classification proof branch, not the exact finite-update lowering or existence of the cited Gaussian-program evaluator.
- **LANTK, Appendix B.3:** a squared Gaussian readout factor is assigned its fourth moment, and ReLU distributional derivatives are not the same as classical finite-network derivatives before expectation. Singular/repeated data require further treatment. The smooth finite symbolic recursion remains valid.
- **Dyer/Aitken:** missing smooth-activation integrability, a first-layer normalization typo, and incorrect local coefficients/recurrence factors have explicit checks. The corrected polynomial contraction theorem and exact finite hierarchy remain substantial prior results. The inaccessible ICLR version is not accused of retaining preprint errors.

The later TP IVb explicitly requires uniform initial-scalar moments and its general backpropagation definition includes scalar adjoints and average edges. Thus NGTP's earlier printed defects should not be portrayed as unresolved limitations of every subsequent Tensor Programs formulation. The TP IVb report's description of the non-Gaussian interpolation architecture is conditional on correct finite backpropagation; the separately verified NGTP recurrence error limits that printed dependency chain. The Gaussian-plus-moments route used for the MFP comparison bypasses it.

Fresh independent checks of the consequential NTH/NGTP/LANTK findings are recorded in [independent_proof_checks.md](independent_proof_checks.md). The [fresh MFP audit](independent_mfp_audit.md) read the complete manuscript and guide, reconstructed its central proof bridges, and found no substantive gap or example outside the stated scope; it also records the remaining caller obligations and checks it did not perform. Additional independent TP IV/diagram formula checks are recorded in [independent_formula_checks.md](independent_formula_checks.md). Remaining single-reviewer or unresolved observations are not silently promoted to certified results. No proof defect is used as a shortcut to a priority claim. Full formal verification of every predecessor or every MFP execution was not performed.

The final independent formula check also supplies a complete one-hidden-layer repair of TP IV's intended nonzero cubic update effect, while leaving the full classification proof uncertified. Its exact Aitken example gives a mean coefficient of 192 rather than the printed 132 and variance of order n^-2 rather than the case study's n^-3; the correctly applied general corollary already predicts n^-2. Dyer's discrete correction is repaired by summing update indices from zero. These checks sharpen the distinction between faulty displayed calculations and the valid prior mechanisms they illustrate.

## Evidence, cost, and what would strengthen a submission

All **72 bundled MFP tests passed**. Its operational examples, symbolic/cubic MLP derivative examples and all three order-two kernel-jet variants regenerated successfully into this study's namespace. Logs and exact source/output hashes are preserved. Tensorgrad's small symbolic examples were executed; its more elaborate attempted simplifications hit bounded timeouts, which are not evidence of impossibility or a controlled speed comparison. Neural Tangents, GP4A and the papers' training experiments were not reproduced.

There is no measured MFP efficiency advantage. The graph can grow quickly, Gaussian integrals may be high-dimensional, and exact polynomial arithmetic has its own size cost. Prior methods can be stronger on numerical acceleration, supported layer families, arbitrary tensor contractions or finite-width corrections. MFP should be evaluated on completeness and inspectability of the supported transformation before making a speed claim.

For a research submission, the most persuasive additional evidence would be a fixed set of nontrivial derivative/update queries, showing the same unchanged frontend/compiler processing all of them, the resulting graph sizes and costs, and independent finite-width or exact polynomial checks. A matched baseline must use the same parameterization and observable. This is a recommendation, not a claim that such a comparative benchmark has already been run or a change authorized by this review.
