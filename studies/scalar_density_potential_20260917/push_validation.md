   sets. Continuity makes them closed; loss monotonicity makes them nested.

   closes the otherwise invalid jump from a moving witness to a fixed one.
4. **No initial stalls:** Gaussian integration by parts gives strict
   monotonicity of the initialized feature in the first input coordinate.
   Analytic continuation and ordered exponential tails prove independence
   of distinct positive tanh scales. Exhaustive signed-group cancellation
   excludes a zero initial gradient when q differs from 1/2. Compactness
   and short-time continuity strengthen this to uniform initial progress.
5. **Genuine compatible triples:** equilateral inputs have no parallel pair
   and are linearly separable with fixed margin. The weighted class centers
   are not collinear. The lower differential Gram is positive definite by
   isolating each direction along perpendicular rays. A local contraction

   the upper Gram then gives an exact finite fitting readout. Both the
   marks and M_0 remain fixed in this representability construction.
6. **Potential consequence:** exponential decay from a finite initial value
   contradicts the loss floor whenever the comparison tends to zero and

   omega(0)=0 was added: a mere right-hand limit does not rule out a jump
   at zero and Phi identically zero. This correction is in the final hash.
   The reviewer also checked the subsequently added class-center determinant.
7. **Nearby delays:** for each fixed finite T and target ell<1/2, continuity
   supplies nearby losses above ell at time T. Monotonicity supplies the
   hitting-time lower bound. This is an extended-limit statement, without
   asserting that nearby trajectories fit, and without a quantitative rate

## Claim update and remaining gap

**Proved and internally checked:** every rotation orbit of a nonparallel
triple with the specified balanced unequal positive weights contains an
initially moving canonical trajectory with loss at least 1/2 forever.
Even an equilateral, linearly separable family has this property, and it
has uniform initial progress and finite representability throughout.

**Refuted:** deleting only initial equilibria suffices for a finite
exponential-to-zero loss-controlling potential on every genuine triple.
The rate may be data-dependent; the refutation still holds.

**Open:** a positive theorem outside a further data-defined singular set;
an almost-everywhere theorem; location or explicit formula for a bad angle;
whether a bad angle has an invertible initial readout Gram; whether any
particular successful asymmetric family admits the desired potential.
The new theorem is not a proof against the full p=1 dictionary or a
data-adaptive scalar probe. It does not identify an optimal potential.
