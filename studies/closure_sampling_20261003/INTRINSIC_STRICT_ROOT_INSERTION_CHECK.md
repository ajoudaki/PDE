## 2. The training event can be independent of the passive matrix

Let $V$ be the deterministic span of the unit training inputs, and let
$U,E$ have orthonormal columns spanning $V,V^\perp$, respectively.
The exact first-weight equation gives

\[
 A(t)E=A(0)E=:G.
\]

All training observations, residuals, and updates depend only on $A(0)U$,
the hidden initialization, and the fixed data. Thus the training sigma-field
$\mathcal F$ in the candidate is independent of the standard Gaussian
matrix $G$. The active-coordinate training problem has the same initialized
sample Gram and canonical mobilities as the original problem. In particular
its real fitting theorem and real separate-budget argument can be run
without any first-weight restriction involving $G$.

Choose $E_n\in\mathcal F$ to require the strict training initialization
margins, the all-time real physical tube, and survival of its real training
budgets through $T_n$. Its probability tends to one. The rescaled budget
constants from the source suffice for the real problem as well. This does
not require a polynomial convergence rate for $\Pr(E_n^c)$.

On $E_n$, bounded real slopes, hidden operator norms and readout coordinates
give, for every real $G$ and every unit query,

\[
 \|k^{(\ell)}(t,v)\|_{2,n}\le CS,\qquad
 |k_i^{(\ell)}(t,v)|\le CS\sqrt n.
 \tag{A}
\]

Neither estimate uses a query maximum event or a query moment theorem.
They are the deterministic bounds needed for exceptional-event integration.

When a query-grid modulus needs $\|A(t)\|_{\rm op}/\sqrt n$, intersect
with a separate event $\|G\|_{\rm op}\le C\sqrt n$. This event may be
chosen with exponentially small failure at fixed $d-r$. Together with
the active-coordinate bound it supplies the full first-matrix bound.
It must not be silently included in $E_n$ when conditional Gaussian
rotation is later used. The candidate keeps it among the separate query
events, which is the correct arrangement.

## 3. The passive-query endpoint really needs only RMS

Delete neuron $i$ in layer $j<L$, retaining rectangular layers with the
original normalization $n$. The outgoing initialized column $x_i$ and,
for $j\ge2$, incoming initialized row transposed $y_i$ are independent
$N(0,I/n)$ roots after conditioning on retained initialization.

Let $d_v=\delta^{(j+1)}(t,v)$ at a zero-source cavity, and define
$B_v=D_\Theta d_v$ and $E_v=D_{e_v}d_v$, where $e_v$ is an
external preactivation in layer $j+1$. These are blocks of the augmented
Hessian of the unnormalized scalar output $nf(t,v)$. The full Hessian
formula is prior-source equation (11), with the passive query replacing
the training sample. Every curvature term is a contraction of one
diagonal $\operatorname{diag}(\phi_\ell''k^{(\ell)}(v))$ between
bounded forward derivative maps. Its normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm
is at most $C\|k^{(\ell)}(v)\|_{2,n}\le CS$.

The remaining mixed weight and readout blocks factor through a
width-dimensional vector space and have bounded operator norm. Hence their
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norms are bounded independently of width.
The readout mixed block need not be $O(S)$, and the candidate correctly
claims only

\[
 \|B_v\|_{2,n}\le C.
 \tag{B}
\]

For $E_v$ both arguments are external-preactivation variations, so there
are no mixed weight or readout terms. It has the form

\[
 E_v=\sum_{\ell\ge j+1}
 T_\ell^\top\operatorname{diag}(\phi_\ell''k^{(\ell)}(v))T_\ell,
 \qquad \|T_\ell\|_{\rm op}\le C.
\]

Taking the trace and bounding each squared row norm of $T_\ell$ by its
operator norm squared gives
$|\operatorname{tr}E_v|/n\le C\sum_\ell\|k^{(\ell)}(v)\|_{1,n}
\le CS$. This proves both candidate estimates (11) without a query
budget or a query coordinate cap.

The training endpoint $B_a$ and each residual-Hessian insertion have the
higher Schatten bounds from the augmented training Hessian. They use
training budgets only. For a term with $h\ge1$ Hessians between $B_v$
and $B_a^\top$, choose exponents

\[
 2,\underbrace{2(h+1),\ldots,2(h+1)}_{h+1\text{ training factors}}.
\]

Their reciprocal sum is one, including exactly the normalization $1/n$.
The positive-real base propagators are contractions. Time-simplex
integration therefore gives candidate equation (13).

For the summation, put $D=D_H/\eta$ in this paragraph. The budget part
of its $h$th term is bounded explicitly by

\[
 2eCSD\sqrt{2\mathcal B}\,
       (4eS^2D)^h(h+1).
\]

Indeed the split contributes $2^h$, the factor inside the bracket is
$2SD(h+1)$, and
$(h+1)^{h+1}/h!\le e^{h+1}(h+1)$. The existing condition
$16eS^2D\sqrt{2\mathcal B}\le1$ makes the series converge with a
width-independent constant. The constant part sums to at most
$CA_H e^{2SA_H}$. For $h=0$ use both Hilbert--Schmidt endpoints.
Thus the state trace is bounded independently of the query cap.

Multiplication by its exterior residual activity gives $CS$. The direct
external trace gives $CS$ as above, and the adaptive-residual rank-one
trace retains its $n^{-1+0.001}$ factor up to logarithms. No query
higher moment was inserted into this argument.

## 4. Complete passive backward graph at a deletion

The direct sources must be distinguished according to which observable
is evaluated. At a passive query the full forward computation has source

\[
 e_v=x_i h_i^{(j)}(v)+\text{learned-column remainder}
\]

at layer $j+1$. Its lower backward computation has source

\[
 q_v=y_i\delta_i^{(j)}(v)+\text{learned-row remainder}
\]

at layer $j-1$, when $j\ge2$. Both are required for a comparison of the
complete query backward graph. They are observable sources and do not
enter the training vector field. The candidate includes both.

In contrast, the upper response $d_v=\delta^{(j+1)}(v)$ depends on the
retained state and the query forward source $e_v$, but not on the lower
reverse observable source $q_v$. Thus its first variation is precisely

\[
 d_v^{\rm full}-d_v^0
   =B_vV+E_v x_i h_i^{(j)}(v)+o_{\ell^2}(1).
 \tag{C}
\]

The reverse *training* source still contributes to $V$ and therefore
produces the centered $x_i$--$y_i$ bilinear form after pairing (C) with
$x_i$. Absence of a direct $q_v$ term in (C) does not omit that effect.

Here is the local graph check under the imposed query cap
$M_n=C_*S\sqrt{\ell_n}$.

1. The query forward and downward backward recursions use the same
   primitives as the training graph: bounded scalar gates, coordinate
   products, and matrix actions. The query supplies no residual or force.
   The true retained parameter variation remains the training variation.
2. With deterministic training and query controls fixed, every first
   variation is linear in the independent omitted roots. Query backward
   parameter derivatives have operator norm bounded by a fixed polynomial
   in $1+M_n$; this follows either from the downward recursion or the
   augmented Hessian formula.
3. The control amplitudes are bounded by fixed powers of $\ell_n$.
   The exact physical forward speed is $O(S\rho)$ in normalized RMS.
   Backward differentiation on the query cap is
   $O(\rho(1+SM_n))$ in normalized RMS. Coordinate Lipschitz constants
   are therefore at most $\sqrt n$ times a fixed polylogarithm. The
   learned row and column sources retain their $n^{-1/2}$ factor.
4. The linear variation operator is at most
   $n^{0.001}\operatorname{polylog}(n)\le n^{0.005}$ eventually.
   Gaussian coordinate and quadratic-form tails at $n^{-0.1}$ then
   have exponent $n^{0.79}$, while the control net has logarithm
   $n^{0.625}\operatorname{polylog}(n)$. Adding finitely many query
   control families does not change that strict comparison.
5. For the sphere grid, fixed-order input derivatives have polynomial
   bounds on a first-matrix norm event. A sufficiently fine polynomial
   grid therefore controls off-grid errors. Only one query's finite
   control family is needed at a grid point; taking the union over the
   polynomial number of points adds $O(\log n)$ to the log cardinality.
   It does not form a simultaneous continuum of uncontrolled histories.

For the nonlinear graph, split each node change into its Gaussian linear
part and a remainder. Products of two linear changes use one coordinate
norm $n^{-0.1}$ and one Euclidean norm $n^{0.01}$, giving $n^{-0.09}$.
Mixed linear/remainder products use the coordinate norm, and the pure
remainder product is quadratic. A reference query carrier multiplies these
terms by at most a polylogarithm. Changed hidden matrices retain the explicit
$n^{-1/2}$ scaling. These are exactly the rules of the current-study
augmented-graph lemma, now applied also in the downward passive recursion.

The independent incoming-row probes are the same bounded lower derivative
maps applied to $y_i$. Their variation has the prior
$n^{2(0.01)-0.1}=n^{-0.08}$ scale. Adding a passive lower forward map
does not change this calculation. No additional query residual Taylor term
exists. At the remainder cap $n^{-0.04}$, the largest terms are still
$n^{-0.08}$ before the $n^{0.001}$ propagator and logarithms; they are
$o(n^{-0.04})$ afterward. Thus the local comparison supplies
coordinate-small passive carrier differences and the ordinary Euclidean
upper-response comparison used by (C).

This checks the new observable extension from the underlying primitives;
it does not infer it just from a previously proved training maximum.

Pairing (C) with $x_i$ now gives exactly the state trace in Section 3,
the direct query trace, the centered cross-root term and the vanishing
rank-one term. The learned outgoing-column increment has norm
$CS^2/\sqrt n$, and its pairing with $d_v$ costs $CS^3$ by (A).
Consequently

\[
 |k_i^{(j)}(t,v)-x_i^\top d_v^{-i}(t)|\le CS+o(1)
 \tag{D}
\]

holds uniformly on common prefixes. Its nonvanishing coefficient is
independent of $C_*$. Constants multiplying vanishing local errors may
depend on $C_*$, which only changes the eventual width.

## 5. Query-cap survival and polynomial exceptional probabilities

Training initialization transfers to singleton cavities deterministically
on the strict full training event: removing one bounded activation changes
the normalized subsequent features by $O(n^{-1/2})$, and deleting a row
or column cannot increase the initialized operator norms. This argument
uses no passive matrix. The separate training budgets transfer by

\[
 \mathcal H_a^{-i}\le
 e^{\eta\varepsilon_n/S}\mathcal H_a+O(1/n)
 <2\mathcal B
\]

on the common prefix. For query caps, the new coordinate-small comparison
similarly gives

\[
 \sup_{v,t,\ell,\text{retained }i}|k_i^{(\ell),-j}(t,v)|
 \le C_*S\sqrt{\ell_n}+o(1)
 <2C_*S\sqrt{\ell_n}.
\]

Thus a cavity cannot hit either doubled cap first. All such statements
are prefix comparisons, not conditioning events for Gaussian laws.

Freeze a cavity reference at its own training/query stops and set it to
zero when its own physical initialization conditions fail. Its entire
coefficient field is a function of retained initialization alone and is
independent of the omitted outgoing root. At each fixed $t,v$, the
conditional variance of $x_i^\top d_v^{-i}(t)$ is at most $C^2S^2$.
Time and query grid moduli are polynomial as above. For any fixed $M>0$,
a Gaussian threshold $C_M S\sqrt{\ell_n}$ makes the union over this
polynomial grid, neurons and layers fail with probability at most
$C_M n^{-M}$. Choose $C_*$ with a strict margin above this threshold
and (D)'s fixed shift. Equation (D) then strictly improves the full
query cap.

There is no circular constant choice. The training variational exponent,
activity restriction and asymmetric trace bound do not depend on $C_*$.
The query cap enters only fixed-polylogarithmic local coefficients and
off-grid bounds. Their polynomial exponents and the Gaussian entropy
comparison remain unchanged; enlarging $C_*$ merely enlarges the width
at which those inequalities hold.

For clarity, the probability assertion actually needed and established is
the following. For each fixed $M$, there is an event $\Omega_{n,M}$ on
which the query cap and (D) hold through $T_n$ whenever $E_n$ holds,
such that

\[
 \Pr(E_n\cap\Omega_{n,M}^{\,c})\le C_M n^{-M}.
 \tag{E}
\]

The independent passive first-matrix norm failures can be added to the
right-hand side with exponentially small probability. The conditional
local Gaussian event has superpolynomial failure and is integrated over
retained configurations, with the zero-reference convention on failed
cavity starts. On a successful full start those cavity starts have the
strict inherited margins, so this convention does not alter the prefix
comparison.

Equation (E) does **not** assert $\Pr(E_n^c)\le C_Mn^{-M}$ and does
not require it. No moment estimate conditions an omitted root on $E_n$.
This is the essential distinction in the candidate's probability passage.

The auxiliary cap and threshold in this argument may be chosen separately
for each fixed moment order. They are not runtime state, and they do not
alter the common training event $E_n$, the labels, or the budget constants.
The resulting width threshold may depend on that fixed order.

## 6. Fixed-query moments and the all-time extension

Fix $p\ge2$, $t\le T_n$, a unit query $v$, and a coordinate/layer.
Choose $M>p/2+2$ in (E). On the good event, absorb (D)'s $o(1)$
into $CS$ at sufficiently large width; $S>0$ is fixed. Raising the
pathwise bound to the $p$th power gives

\[
 \mathbf1_{E_n\cap\Omega_{n,M}}|k_i^{(j)}(t,v)|^p
 \le C_p\mathbf1_{E_n\cap\Omega_{n,M}}
       \bigl(|x_i^\top d_v^{-i}(t)|^p+S^p\bigr).
\]

Remove the indicators from this nonnegative upper bound before taking
conditional expectation with respect to the omitted root. The frozen
coefficient has normalized RMS at most $CS$ for every retained
configuration, including the zero-reference case. Gaussian moments then
bound this contribution by $C_pS^p$.

On the complement use (A) and (E):

\[
 \mathbb E\bigl[
 \mathbf1_{E_n\cap\Omega_{n,M}^{\,c}}|k_i^{(j)}(t,v)|^p
 \bigr]\le C_p S^p n^{p/2-M}.
\]

Averaging over coordinates proves candidate equation (19), uniformly
in the fixed $t,v$. No independence between neuron coordinates and no
common-cavity empirical-moment expansion is needed. In particular the
slowly vanishing probability of $E_n^c$ never gets multiplied by
$n^{p/2}$: it is absent from this expectation.

The top carrier is already bounded by $BS$. For the post-horizon tail,
differentiate the ordinary query recursion, using only (A), bounded
gates and mixers, and the uniform real forward speed $O(S\rho)$.
This gives a deliberately coarse normalized RMS speed
$C(1+\sqrt n)\rho$. After integration and conversion to a coordinate
norm, the tail is at most

\[
 CSn e^{-\kappa T_n}\le CSn^{-7},
 \qquad \kappa=\lambda/4,\quad
 T_n=32\lambda^{-1}\ell_n.
\]

This estimate is uniform in the unit query and every real $G$ on $E_n$.
It does not reuse the finite-horizon query cap after $T_n$. Adding it
to the finite-time moments proves the all-time version of (19), including
the endpoint.

## 7. Check of the downstream use and verdict limits

The new moment statement is strong enough for the next step; a supremum
inside its expectation is unnecessary. The deterministic gate estimate

\[
 \|\phi'(z)-\phi'(z')\|_{4,n}
 \le C\|z-z'\|_{2,n}^{1/2}
\]

follows by combining its pointwise boundedness and Lipschitz bounds.
A changed gate paired with a reference carrier therefore needs only a
counting-four norm. Probability Hölder uses a larger but fixed carrier
moment, available from Section 6. The possibly dependent passive matrix
factor is handled by the other Hölder factor; no independence of it
and the carrier is required.

The deterministic clock $\tau=1-e^{-\kappa t}$ avoids root-dependent
time indexing. Conditional on $\mathcal F$ the gradient identity
$\nabla_G f=n^{-1}\delta^{(1)}(E^\top v)^\top$ is exact because
training does not depend on $G$. Gaussian rotation can be integrated
over $\mathcal F$ with $\mathbf1_{E_n}$ because that indicator is
independent of the rotating passive Gaussian matrices. The fixed-moment
increment estimate therefore follows with the candidate's one-half
power. Choosing a fixed $p>2(d+1)$ makes the net sum
$\sum_j2^{-j/2+j(d+1)/p}$ finite, without a width-dependent logarithm.
Parameter convergence and bounded output permit passage to the endpoint.

The conditional mean is a proof object only. The runtime uses the same
realized network restricted to $V+\operatorname{span}\{e\}$ and then
the preexisting autonomous compression theorem. In those orthonormal
coordinates the initialized first matrix has the exact canonical law,
the training Gram and dynamics are unchanged, and the fixed radial query
map plus its $dr+O(d+r)$ storage are counted. Nothing in the insertion
lemma requires storing a conditional expectation, a dense trained matrix,
or a trajectory table.
