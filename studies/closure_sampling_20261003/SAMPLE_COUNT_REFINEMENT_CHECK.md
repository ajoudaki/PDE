## 1. Initialized Gram geometry

The recursively defined Hilbert map has inner products equal to the
initialized covariance recursion. It introduces no population-training
premise. The isonormal map is bounded from its Hilbert domain to every
finite Gaussian \(L^p\), so derivatives along sphere angles are legitimate
Hilbert derivatives, including at singular intermediate covariances.

In the ordered Faà di Bruno formula, the coefficient is \(k!/p!\)
times the sum over positive ordered compositions. Cauchy's derivative
estimate for the activation cancels exactly this \(p!\). The \(L^2\)
norm of a product of \(p\) correlated Gaussian derivatives is at most
\((2p)^{p/2}\) times their standard deviations, by Hölder; independence
of those derivatives is not assumed.

The elementary inequality
\(\prod_i j_i!\le k!/p!\), for \(\sum_i j_i=k\), absorbs that Gaussian
factor up to \(C^k\). The derivative bound \(C^k(k!)^{3/2}\) is therefore
preserved under another hidden layer, rather than acquiring an extra
factorial order with depth. Pure derivatives in one angle suffice:
integrate a Fourier coefficient in a coordinate of maximal frequency.
Optimizing the derivative order gives \(\exp(-c|k|_\infty^{2/3})\).

The real rank of the degree-\(J\) Fourier truncation is at most
\((2J+1)^{d-1}\); conjugate frequency pairs contribute two real
coefficient vectors for two frequencies. Uniform Hilbert approximation
controls the trace of the positive Gram remainder. If the approximation
rank is at most \(m/2\), projection onto its sample-space nullspace gives
\[
\gamma(m-R)\le \operatorname{tr}(P Q)
=\operatorname{tr}(P(Q-Q_J))\le m\epsilon_J^2.
\]
Hence \(\gamma\le2\epsilon_J^2\), with no spurious factor \(m\).
Choosing \(J\asymp m^{1/(d-1)}\) proves the count/gap relation.

For the capped lower bound \(\bar\gamma=\min(1,\gamma)\),
\(\lambda=\min(1,\gamma/m)\ge\bar\gamma/m\). Substitution into the
new sufficient label range \(Y\le c\lambda\) yields precisely exponent
\(3(d-1)/2\), with no extra arbitrary epsilon. This is a sufficient
corollary using fixed-dimensional initialization geometry. It is not a
larger numerical threshold than the same dataset's actual \(c\lambda\).
