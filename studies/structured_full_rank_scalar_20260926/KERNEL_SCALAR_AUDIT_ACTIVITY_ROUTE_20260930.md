## 1. Independent scaling check

Write alpha=2/m. Use the candidate's initial p_a,q_a,H_a,d_a and set

\[
 \gamma_{ab}=H_b\odot d_a,\qquad
 \beta_{ab}=q_a\odot W_0^T\gamma_{ab}.
\]

The initial feature tangent generated by a readout equal to H_b has
first-weight component beta_ab u_a^T and middle-weight component
gamma_ab p_a^T. The training metric for the latter component contains
an additional factor 1/n. Its scalar Gram is consequently

\[
 S_{(a,b),(c,d)}
 =(u_a\cdot u_c)\frac{\beta_{ab}^T\beta_{cd}}n
   +\frac{(\gamma_{ab}^T\gamma_{cd})(p_a^Tp_c)}{n^2}.
\]

This matches equation (7), including both normalization factors. In the
block population the second term is

\[
 \mathbb E[k^{-1}\gamma_{ab}^T\gamma_{cd}]
 \;\mathbb E[k^{-1}p_a^Tp_c],
\]

not the expectation of their within-block product. Learned middle
connections couple different blocks, which is why the two normalized
layer contractions factor in the limit. Both factors are Gram matrices;
their product entrywise is the Gram of their tensor-product features.
The actual transpose of G appears in beta, so the construction preserves
the initial forward/backward dependence.

The leading readout is c1=-alpha sum_b z_b H_b. Since z'=r and
J_ab'=r_a z_b, direct substitution gives exactly the candidate's w2,
A2 and W2, all with the positive alpha-squared coefficient for the
parameter changes. In residual-equation normalization, the quadratic
kernel correction is

\[
 \alpha^3\sum_{bc}J_{bc}
   \{S_{(a,q),(b,c)}+S_{(q,a),(b,c)}\}
 +\alpha^3\sum_{db}z_dz_b S_{(q,d),(a,b)}.
\]

The candidate uses an unscaled kernel and multiplies it by alpha in the
residual equation, so its alpha-squared M and N are consistent. Confusing
these conventions would cause a factor 2/m error; no such error is
present.

The positive completion also has the correct orientation: M_qa is
the inner product of initial H_q with the quadratic change in H_a.
Therefore the first-order change in its Gram is M+M^T, and
M^T K0^-1 M completes precisely that cross-Gram. Its order is R^4.

## 2. Uniform Gaussian remainder check

Let R=int_0^t ||r||_2 and g=1+||G||F, with R≤1. All constants in this
section may depend on fixed m,k and initialization moment bounds.
The following estimates are valid for arbitrary integrable controlled
residual paths; they do not assume the path is already close to the
aggregate solution.

The exact residual factorization gives pointwise |c|≤CR and |A|≤CR².
Normalized memory has bounded entries. In the backward field the initial
block contributes G^T delta2=O(Rg), while the learned global correction
is O(R³), because its shared contraction pairs A=O(R²) with delta2=O(R).
Thus |w-w0|≤CR²g. It follows that

\[
 |h_1-p|\le CR^2g,
 \qquad |h_2-H|\le CR^2g^2.
\]

The forward learned correction is O(R²); the extra g in the second bound
comes from G(h1-p). The exact normalized-memory average gives
|B/L-p|≤CR³g. No Taylor expansion in L itself is needed.

Integrating c' against the feature difference gives
|c-c1|≤CR³g². Bounded second derivatives of tanh then imply

\[
 |\delta_2-\delta_{2,1}|\le CR^3g^2,\qquad
 |\delta_1-\delta_{1,1}|\le CR^3g^3.
\]

In the second estimate, the possible terms are an initial transpose times
the delta2 error, a changed first activation derivative times a readout
of size R followed by one transpose, and the learned middle correction
of size R³. Integration against the residual gives the candidate's
|A-A2|≤CR⁴g² and |w-w0-w2|≤CR⁴g³.

For completeness, the local forward preactivation has an initial-block
part G h1 and a learned part built from A and the averaged contraction
of B/L with h1. Subtracting its quadratic expression leaves O(R⁴g⁴):
one extra G multiplies the w remainder, and the quadratic Taylor
remainder of h1 costs O(R⁴g²) before that multiplication. In the learned
part, A-A2 is O(R⁴g²), while a feature-contraction change of order R²
multiplies A2=O(R²). The normalized-memory change of order R³
multiplies A2 and only contributes at order R⁵. Applying tanh once more
therefore gives |h2-H-h2,2|≤CR⁴g⁴, as claimed.

These estimates are uniform over |u|≤1: the input enters through vectors
of norm at most one, and every activation derivative used here is
uniformly bounded. They do not require a bound on the largest Gaussian
block in a finite realization. Averaging the bounds uses moments of g,
and the stated eighth moment controls the squared g⁴ remainder. Thus
unbounded Gaussian marks do not invalidate this source estimate.

Substitution into the three tangent-Gram terms gives

\[
 K_*=K_0+M+M^T+N+O(R^4).
\]

For instance, delta1 is itself O(Rg) and its approximation error is
O(R³g³), so the Gram difference is O(R⁴) after averaging; an uncontrolled
supremum over G is unnecessary. Likewise the readout-feature Gram
remainder is bounded by the fourth-order feature remainder and the square
of the quadratic feature change.

In the exact P1 innovation, E_ab=O(R²), D_ab=O(R²), and V_ab=O(R³).
Hence its size is at most C(R⁴+R⁵)||r||. Combining it with the completed
kernel's O(R⁴) alteration proves the residual-relative source bound
C R⁴||r||. The same calculation with one query index proves its uniform
query analogue.

This verifies the distinction in the candidate: P1 and canonical gradient
dynamics agree through the cubic output term, but need not agree at the
quintic term. The special normalized-clock change B/L-p=O(R³) affects
the middle weights at order R⁵ and output at order R⁶. The different
replacement of the current feature by its memory average already yields
the order-R⁴ kernel lag and order-R⁵ output error. Those two order
statements are compatible.

## 3. All-time bootstrap and feedback absorption

The source estimate and the O(R²) kernel change imply, for both the exact
and reduced flows,

\[
 \dot r=-\alpha K_0r+e_0,\qquad
 |e_0|\le CR^2|r|.
\]

As long as R stays below a chosen fixed R0 with
CR0²≤lambda/m, differentiating |r| gives exponential decay with rate
lambda/m. Its activity integral is at most
m a ||ybar||/lambda. Choosing a sufficiently small that this is less
than R0/2 rules out the first exit from R≤R0. The argument is uniform
in physical time, including the equality r=0 by continuity and uniqueness.

Now define z*,J* from the exact residual, and write
I(t)=int_0^t |r*-r|. Then

\[
 |z^*-z|\le I(t),\qquad
 |J^*-J|\le Ca I(t),\qquad
 |\widehat K(z^*,J^*)-\widehat K(z,J)|\le Ca I(t).
\]

The middle estimate follows by expanding r*z* - rz, using |z*|≤Ca
and int|r|≤Ca; the last follows from the linear J and quadratic z
terms, with the completion contributing a still smaller term. With
lambda1>0 a fixed coercivity constant for alpha Khat, the residual
error therefore obeys

\[
 |r^*(t)-r(t)|
 \le\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_1(t-s)}
       \{Ca I(s)+Ca^4\}\,|r^*(s)|\,ds.
\]

Integration over t≤T and nonnegativity of the integrands gives
I(T)≤Ca²I(T)+Ca⁵. For a sufficiently small the first term is absorbed.
The same integral formula bounds the supremum residual error by Ca⁵.
Thus the claimed fifth-order all-time output estimate is a propagation
theorem with a proved small source, not merely a formal Taylor expansion.

The loss estimate is also correct: the sum of the two residual norms is
O(a), while their difference is O(a⁵), so their squared-loss difference
is O(a⁶).

## 5. Remaining qualifications

1. At the initial audit stage, the exact Gaussian-block P1 population
   flow was accepted as the stipulated comparison object. The candidate
   does not itself give a
   complete existence/uniqueness construction for that flow with unbounded
   marks. Its source estimate is valid along every such solution. A
   standalone theorem package must supply or reference the population
   well-posedness result; this dependency was not audited here. In
   particular, moment bounds for remainders alone should not be presented
   as a separate uniqueness theorem for arbitrary initial laws. Section 7
   below subsequently closes this dependency for the prescribed Gaussian
   law, while retaining the restriction concerning arbitrary initial laws.

2. Exact contractions are the theorem's coefficients. To preserve an
   O(a⁵) implemented output error, leading Gram and leading query
   coefficient errors should be O(a⁴), while errors in S and its query
   versions should be O(a²), with constants incorporating the stability
   margin. These orders follow because their integrated output effects
   are respectively O(a delta0) and O(a³ deltaS). The computed K0 must
   remain positive definite, and any PSD-preserving completion must use
   the computed coefficients consistently. The note identifies this
   numerical budget but does not prove a particular quadrature algorithm
   attains it at a stated work cost.

3. The Monte Carlo scale O(a/sqrt(b)) and resulting a^-8 comparison
   are conditional cost reasoning, not a matching lower bound or a
   uniform-in-query sampling theorem established in the candidate.
   Nonzero variance, stability, coefficient accuracy, and the observable
   norm must be matched. This qualification does not change the finite
   aggregate state count or the analytic approximation theorem.

4. The result is perturbative: small amplitude, initial SPD Gram, fixed
   m and fixed block size. Its cubic feature-learning term is present,
   but feature movement is O(a²). It does not establish useful accuracy
   for the study's unit-label strong-feature-learning targets, a useful
   numerical threshold, or an arbitrary-accuracy hierarchy at fixed
   amplitude.

No hidden PSD assumption about the exact P1 flow, missing physical-time
factor, Gaussian maximum-block dependence, tensor-product normalization
error, or passive-decoder alias defect was found in the frozen candidate.

The uniform bounds on c,A,B and L follow from bounded activation and the
continuous residual fields, while the first-weight displacement grows at
most linearly in 1+||G||. At a restart time the local state Lipschitz bound
is C(1+||G||²): differentiation can introduce one forward G and one
backward G^T, but no higher power. It does not depend on the magnitude
of the evolved first weights, because activation derivatives are bounded.

The crucial direct-dependency check is correct. The new shared S field
depends only on the integrated local state. The new r,V fields also
depend instantaneously on the trial S through the second-layer
activation. Dependence through the trial L is delayed by its integral
of rho. There are no other instantaneous field edges. Thus the weighted
norm ||S||+eta||r,V|| converts the instantaneous S-to-r,V dependence
into a coefficient eta C2; choosing eta first and the interval length
second gives a strict contraction. An unweighted small-time estimate for
all fields would not suffice, but the written proof does not make that
mistake.

The local map preserves its closed field set: the output r,S,V are
bounded respectively by c plus labels, B, and the product A times c,
independently of the magnitude of the trial forward field. Strictly
larger trial bounds and a sufficiently short interval keep these bounds
inside the chosen set. Completeness in the continuous-curve supremum
norm and the displayed geometric contraction give a unique fixed point.

The Gaussian exponential moment formula in the proof is correctly
normalized for k² entries of variance 1/k. It controls the polynomial
factor times exp(C delta ||G||²) uniformly for a small fixed upper bound
on delta. Correlations of evolved local states with G introduce no new
measure: expectations remain over the original Gaussian seed labels.

The C1 regularity argument is also valid. The derivative of S uses
only B' and the first feature derivative, dominated by C(1+||G||).
Once S and L are C1, differentiation of the forward and backward output
contractions costs at most C(1+||G||²). These dominating functions have
finite Gaussian moments. Passive queries are computed after the training
fixed point and satisfy the same bounds uniformly over the unit disk.

Finally the a priori finite-T bounds make all restart radii and local
contraction constants uniform up to T. Iterating finitely many intervals
gives existence and uniqueness on that interval. Since T is arbitrary,
the object required by the small-amplitude all-time bootstrap is defined.
The phrase that L stays above one is interpreted as L≥1, including its
initial value and the stationary zero-label case; strict inequality is
neither needed nor generally true.
