# Smooth clipping: all-time root-width population theorem

2026-10-01. This is the coordinator's complete synthesis of the smooth
continuation. **Internally checked theorem in the probability formulations
(1)--(2); not a promoted manuscript or book result.** It establishes
the original study's fixed-confidence full-population rate. The stronger
all-initialization all-time mean-square assertion remains a separate open
tail question. Neither formulation compares with the unclipped population.

## Exact statement

Use exactly SMOOTH_SETUP.md: two hidden tanh layers, q=1 response memories,
fixed independent Gaussian initialized mixer and its actual transpose,
canonical independent Gaussian read-in, zero initial readout and values,
initial keys equal to initial training features, and residual-RMS clock
starting at one. Both recursive post-gate backward signals are clipped by
c_M(s)=M tanh(s/M), with any fixed M>0 independent of n. Residuals remain
outside the clip. There is no independently trained dense middle matrix.

Fix finite training inputs and a label vector with fixed RMS Y>0. Assume
the deterministic population initial readout-feature Gram is positive
definite. For Y below a positive threshold depending on the fixed data,
Gram margin and M, and for a probability law mu supported on a bounded
query set, the conclusion is the following. There are constants
C,c and n0, independent of width and physical time, such that for n>=n0,

\[
E\left[\int\sup_{t\ge0}
 |f_{n,M}(t,x)-f_{\infty,M}(t,x)|^2\,d\mu(x)
 \;\middle|\;\mathcal G_n\right]\le\frac Cn,
\qquad P(\mathcal G_n^c)\le Ce^{-cn}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here G_n is the original initialized fitting event and f_infinity,M is
the own deterministic smooth-closure population predictor. It is not
defined as a finite-width mean. Constants may depend on the fixed nonzero
label vector; no uniform claim as Y tends to zero is needed. Zero labels
give the stationary zero predictor exactly.

In particular, for every delta in (0,1), the actual random initialization
law, without conditioning, obeys

\[
P\left\{\left(\int\sup_{t\ge0}
 |f_{n,M}(t,x)-f_{\infty,M}(t,x)|^2\,d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}
 >\sqrt{C/\delta}\,n^{-1/2}\right\}
 \le\delta+Ce^{-cn}.
\tag{2}
\]

This is an all-time, fixed-confidence root-width theorem, including the
fitted endpoint. It has no additive Y^3, Y^5/M^2, or other width-independent
remainder. The labels and M stay fixed. It does not claim an unconditioned
all-time second moment over the exceptional initializations.

## 1. What smoothness supplies

The useful scalar map is the complete gated signal

\[
F(\alpha,p)=M\tanh(p\,\operatorname{sech}^2\alpha/M).
\tag{3}
\]

Every fixed-order mixed derivative is globally bounded. This uses tanh's
gate structure, not merely bounded derivatives of the cap. Powers of
p sech^2(alpha)/M generated by differentiating alpha are controlled by
the exponentially decaying positive derivatives of tanh. For an upper
signal with |w|<=CS, its pure-z derivatives additionally have size O(S):
they vanish at w=0 and their mixed w derivative is uniformly bounded.

These facts do not give a width-independent population L2 Hessian. The
proof instead uses Gaussian coordinate tangent moments and deterministic
absolute derivative tensors for scalar path functionals. Nor is the
residual-norm clock smooth at zero; it is used only through first
differences in the autonomous comparison.

The true prediction derivative contains p=w sech^2(z), whereas the
trained signal is d=c_M(p). Their difference is retained exactly. Smooth
clipping is never declared inactive. SMOOTH_ALLINIT_ROUTE shows that
dropping this distinction would leave a fixed-label error rather than
prove (1).

## 3. Forced finite populations satisfy a closed response law at root width

Fix any such deterministic history theta, including finitely many passive
query contractions if required. The two population statistics are the
covariances of lower h and upper d, and their responses to additive lower
and upper carrier perturbations. The candidate scalar fields are

\[
z=G^h+R_h d+\frac1m\sum_bv_bK_{ba},\qquad
p=G^d+R_d h+\frac1m\sum_bk_bV_{ba}.
\tag{5}
\]

The Gaussian histories have covariances E[h(t)h(s)] and E[d(t)d(s)].
The lower Gaussian history is independent of its initial first-layer row.
R_h is a causal response integral. R_d also has the instantaneous atom
E[partial_z c_M(w sech^2 z)] delta_t, in addition to its past response.
This is precisely the same-matrix transpose feedback; it is not discarded
as though the two Gaussian actions were independent.

The probabilistic source estimates are in SMOOTH_CAVITY_ROUTE:

- Removing one Gaussian row or column leaves a genuinely independent
  Gaussian vector when theta is fixed. Reinsertion has an ordinary
  Euclidean Taylor remainder O(n^(-1/2)) in every fixed moment. Each tangent
  coordinate has scale n^(-1/2); its squared local defect has scale n^(-1).
  Summing squared defects over n coordinates gives the claimed remainder.
- A separate tagged-forcing variation proves the same scale for response
  errors. It does not differentiate a bound on the state error. The paired
  row/column calculation identifies expected diagonal responses, including
  direct atoms, with responses of the scalar equations (5).
- Normalized response traces concentrate at root width by Hilbert--Schmidt
  first-difference estimates. Full-versus-deleted traces differ at root
  width. Covariance entries obey the corresponding estimates.
- Unconditioned forced Gaussian laws are valid: their response moments grow
  at most as a polynomial in ||W0|| times exp(C S(1+||W0||^2)). Gaussian
  operator tails give the required uniform moments. This avoids conditioning
  a removed row on the original fitting event.

SMOOTH_MEAN_MAP_ROUTE proves that the map sending input covariances and
responses to the new expectations in (5) is contractive for small fixed
activity. It proves continuum source-density convergence and separates
the instantaneous atom. Singular Gaussian history covariances are allowed.
Crucially its Section11 constructs deterministic entrywise derivative
majorants, including parameter-insertion tensors for response kernels.
These bounds allow sup_(t,s) E|error(t,s)| to be integrated directly.
They never replace it by the generally larger E sup_(t,s)|error(t,s)|.

Here is the combination. Take the full finite expected covariance/response
tuple, with an exponentially small admissible localization if needed.
For each fixed target and source, the cavity identities compare its output
to the single-neuron law at random cavity inputs with C/sqrt(n) error.
The deterministic entrywise majorants replace those inputs by the full
deterministic mean tuple using the centered and deleted-versus-full
estimates. This gives an approximate fixed point with defect C/sqrt(n)
in the mean-map norm. The contraction then places that tuple within
C/sqrt(n) of its unique fixed point. The amplitude normalization by S
causes no width dependence: the frozen total activity is bounded above
and below by positive constants times the fixed Y.

Random cavity initial covariances need not equal the deterministic C0.
The input domain accordingly allows any bounded admissible initial
covariance; the lower output resets it to C0. Constants for the deterministic
domain can be chosen to include the cutoff cavity tuples and their mean,
then activity is decreased to enforce contraction. The Gaussian cutoff
replacement errors are exponentially small. This embedding is part of
the combined bridge check, not an assumption of neuron independence.

The fixed point is the own forced common-action population. To identify it,
use the existing fixed-mesh Gaussian-program construction and common-action
ODE for prescribed theta. Finite-width bounded observables converge
qualitatively to that forced population on compact physical intervals.
The preceding quantitative identification gives the same observables at

This argument uses the original fixed Gaussian action and its adjoint;
it introduces no dense trained comparison. The two prescribed histories

## 4. An integrable velocity source closes the all-time argument

Uniform value comparison alone is insufficient for feedback restoration.
Append the passive lower observable q_a=dot h_a/rho for prescribed theta,
defined as zero at zero rho. Its exact formula is

\[
q_a=-\frac2m\sum_b\frac{r_b}{\rho}(u_a\cdot u_b)
       \operatorname{sech}^2(Au_a)
       F(Au_b,p_b).
\tag{6}
\]

The ratios are fixed bounded coefficients; they are never differentiated.
This is a bounded smooth observable of the lower state and carrier.
The reused action W0 q has a Gaussian representation jointly with W0 h,
with the covariances E[hq], E[qq], its past response, and its direct
current-carrier response atom. It has no feedback into the law map.

The actual forced prediction velocity is a bounded smooth upper functional
plus a term w sech^2(z) G^q, linear in a passive Gaussian coordinate.
Its derivative envelopes have uniformly bounded conditional Gaussian
moments. Thus the same covariance/response comparison proves

\[
|E\dot f_{n,M}^{\theta}(t,x)-\dot f_{\infty,M}^{\theta}(t,x)|
 \le C\rho(t)/\sqrt n.
\tag{7}
\]

There is no derivative taken of an approximation bound. The same bounded
lower observables give the contraction velocity
dot K_ba=rho E[(h_b-k_b)h_a]/tau+E[k_b dot h_a]. Hence both the mean
prediction velocity and mean K velocity have integrable root-width
consistency errors. Passive queries add a finite number of forward
observables; constants are uniform on their fixed bounded input set.

This uniformity applies to the statistical source estimates themselves.
One query adds one lower projection and its passive Gaussian forward
outputs. Every derivative/response estimate uses that input only through
its norm and its inner products with the fixed training inputs. The number
of added observables stays fixed. No positive Gram gap involving the query
is required, because Gaussian covariance interpolation permits singular
covariances. Thus on ||x||<=R the same constants apply, and integration
over a probability law supported there preserves the estimates.

Scalar freezing compares actual and forced velocities before division by
rho, so no ratio-difference estimate is needed. It supplies exactly the
statistical hypotheses (5)--(7) of SMOOTH_FEEDBACK_COMPLETION: root-width
K,V value errors, and integrable errors for rho, the residual derivative,
and the K derivative.

That note proves the remaining autonomous comparison on the common-action
spaces. Reconstruct the true moments from the forced population, control
its deviation from the supplied moments, and compare to the autonomous
population. If D is the state/clock distance and R the residual distance,
the equations give

\[
D(t)\le C\epsilon+C\int_0^t(\bar\rho D+R),\qquad
D^+R\le-\kappa R+C\rho_\infty D+e(t),
\quad\int e\le C\epsilon,\quad\epsilon=n^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]

Integrate the second inequality first and insert it into the first.
Gronwall depends on total residual activity, not elapsed physical time.
It gives sup D+integral R<=C/sqrt(n), including the fitted endpoint.
The query velocity and state comparisons consequently prove

\[
\left(\int\sup_t|m_{n,M}(t,x)-f_{\infty,M}(t,x)|^2d\mu(x)\right)^{1/2}
 \le C/\sqrt n.
\tag{9}
\]

Combining (9) with (4) proves (1); Markov and the initialization tail give
(2). Thus the mean bias is addressed directly, rather than bounded by
the total size of feature learning as in the earlier Y^3 estimate.
