## 1. Fixed contract and the needed falsifier

The target is the actual two-hidden-layer tanh q=1 system with cap **M=1**, canonical independent Gaussian initialization, zero readout and values, initial keys equal to initial first-layer features, and residual-RMS clock initialized at one. For fixed data and labels, set

\[
u_a=x_a/\sqrt d,\quad h_a=\tanh(Au_a),\quad
B=W_0+\frac1{mn}\sum_a v_a k_a^T,\quad
g_a=\tanh(Bh_a),\quad f_a=w^Tg_a/n.
\]

With \(r_a=f_a-y_a\), \(\rho=\|r\|_2/\sqrt m\), and \(C_1(s)=\max(-1,\min(1,s))\), the equations are

\[
\begin{gathered}
d_a=C_1(w\odot\operatorname{sech}^2(Bh_a)),\qquad
\ell_a=C_1(\operatorname{sech}^2(Au_a)\odot B^Td_a),\\
\dot w=-\frac2m\sum_a r_ag_a,\quad
\dot A=-\frac2m\sum_a r_a\ell_a u_a^T,\quad
\dot v_a=-2r_ad_a,\quad
\dot k_a=\frac\rho\tau(h_a-k_a),\quad \dot\tau=\rho.
\end{gathered}                                                   \tag{1}
\]

The transpose is the actual transpose of the forward matrix. Labels have fixed sufficiently small positive RMS \(Y\), and the initial population feature Gram has a fixed positive gap. Neither labels, cap, data, Gram margin, nor query law depends on width. The stationary case \(Y=0\) cannot supply a lower bound.

Let \(\mathcal G_n\) be the operator-norm and initial-Gram good event in the assigned notes. Write \(f_n\) and \(f_*\) for this finite system and its own clipped population. For a fixed query law \(\mu\), the intended lower bound would concern

\[
\left(\mathbb E\left[\int\sup_{t\ge0}|f_n(t,x)-f_*(t,x)|^2
 \,d\mu(x)\,\middle|\,\mathcal G_n\right]\right)^{1/2},          \tag{2}
\]

or a nonvanishing-probability version of the same population error. A lower bound \(c n^{-1/4}\), for example, would refute root width. An unconditional all-time moment dominated by \(\mathcal G_n^c\) would be a different conclusion.

On bounded query sets, the established centered bound is root width. Thus a lower bound larger than root width there must also lower-bound

\[
\left\|\mathbb E[f_n\mid\mathcal G_n]-f_*\right\|_{\mu,\infty}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

Indeed Minkowski bounds the RMS error above by the norm in (3) plus \(C_\mu n^{-1/2}\). Concentration therefore excludes a proposed random-sign fluctuation of size \(n^{-1/4}\) around the conditional center. It does not exclude deterministic bias of that size.

## 2. A clipping corner cannot amplify a coupled root-width error

For all real \(p,p',z,z'\), the exact inequality from the fitting note is

\[
|C_1(p\operatorname{sech}^2z)-C_1(p'\operatorname{sech}^2z')|
\le |p-p'|+2|z-z'|.                                             \tag{4}
\]

Consequently, for *any* coupling of random inputs,

\[
\|C_1(P_n\operatorname{sech}^2Z_n)
       -C_1(P\operatorname{sech}^2Z)\|_{L^2}
\le \|P_n-P\|_{L^2}+2\|Z_n-Z\|_{L^2}.                           \tag{5}
\]

This includes arbitrary atoms at either preclip threshold. In particular, an already established \(O(n^{-1/2})\) coupling cannot become an \(n^{-1/4}\) error through the hard-clipping value map. No threshold density or differentiability hypothesis enters (5).

The corner can prevent an improvement from root width to inverse width. For the scalar illustration \(P=1\), \(P_n=1+n^{-1/2}\xi\), \(Z_n=Z=0\), where \(\xi\) is uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\),

\[
\mathbb E C_1(P_n)-C_1(P)=-\frac1{2\sqrt n}.                     \tag{6}
\]

This example is only a sharpness check for (5), not a neural counterexample. It produces root width, not a slower rate. Atoms of the *clipped output* at \(\pm1\) are also insufficient: such atoms arise from whole saturated intervals and do not imply atoms of the preclip input at the corner.

For the actual model, the upper clip is inactive by the assigned small-label theorem:

\[
|w_i(t)\operatorname{sech}^2 z_{a,i}(t)|
\le 2Y/\lambda\le1.
\]

Only the lower clip could supply a new corner mechanism. Its vector field uses the clipping **value**; the exact prediction-velocity formula also uses \(\ell_a\), not the derivative of the clipping map. A proof that counts threshold-indicator changes must therefore explain how those changes yield an error in values despite (4). No such explanation was found. This exclusion is conditional on controlling the preclip inputs; it is not a proof of the missing Gaussian-reuse coupling.

## 3. Initial covariance degeneracy does not create quarter-width innovations

Here is a useful exact obstruction to a tempting square-root argument. Suppose \(V_i\in\mathbb R^q\) are iid, \(\mathbb E\|V_i\|^2<\infty\), and

\[
Q=\mathbb E[V_iV_i^T],\qquad Q_n=\frac1n\sum_i V_iV_i^T.
\]

If \(v\in\ker Q\), then

\[
0=v^TQv=\mathbb E[(v^TV_i)^2]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v^TV_i=0\text{ almost surely}.
\]

Taking a basis of the finite-dimensional kernel shows

\[
\ker Q\subseteq\ker Q_n\quad\text{almost surely}.               \tag{7}
\]

An exactly zero population covariance direction is therefore exactly zero empirically. It is incorrect in this situation to substitute a generic signed \(O(n^{-1/2})\) covariance fluctuation into a square root and claim an \(n^{-1/4}\) innovation.

This applies to any fixed list of initial first-layer features, including appended passive queries. It also propagates through the initial Gaussian tanh layer. To check this second statement, let \(Q_1\) and \(Q_{1,n}\) be the corresponding first-feature raw covariances and

\[
Q_2=\mathbb E[\tanh(Z)\tanh(Z)^T],\qquad Z\sim N(0,Q_1).
\]

For \(v\in\ker Q_2\), the continuous function \(z\mapsto v^T\tanh z\) vanishes \(N(0,Q_1)\)-almost surely. That Gaussian has full support on \(\operatorname{range}Q_1\): in an orthonormal eigenbasis, its positive-eigenvalue coordinates have everywhere-positive Gaussian densities. A continuous function vanishing almost surely consequently vanishes everywhere on that range. By (7),

\[
\operatorname{range}Q_{1,n}\subseteq\operatorname{range}Q_1.
\]

Conditional on the initialized first layer, every finite upper-preactivation row lies in \(\operatorname{range}Q_{1,n}\). Its tanh feature therefore also satisfies \(v^Tg_i=0\). Thus the zero directions of \(Q_2\) are retained by the upper empirical covariance as well.

A related elementary check concerns an approximate residual field \(\eta_n\). If a candidate coupling already gives

\[
\mathbb E\frac1n\|\eta_n\|_2^2\le C/n,
\]

then its root-mean-square size is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), not \(O(n^{-1/4})\). Calling its squared norm an arbitrary root-width covariance fluctuation discards precisely the positivity and squaring that determine its scale.

These statements concern initial iid covariances and already-coupled residuals. They do **not** prove that nearly dependent, dynamically adapted Gaussian histories enjoy a uniform coupling. Those histories remain a possible location for an unproved bias, but initial covariance singularity is not itself the requested counterexample.

## 4. Late physical time cannot supply an additional instability

On \(\mathcal G_n\), both the actual and population query velocities obey the assigned estimate

\[
|\partial_t f_n(t,x)|+|\partial_t f_*(t,x)|
\le C(1+\|u_x\|)Y e^{-\lambda t}.                              \tag{8}
\]

Set \(e_n=f_n-f_*\). For \(t\ge T\), integration gives

\[
|e_n(t,x)|\le |e_n(T,x)|
             +C(1+\|u_x\|)Y e^{-\lambda T}.
\]

For every fixed \(\mu\) of finite second moment, Minkowski therefore gives the pathwise good-event bound

\[
\|e_n\|_{\mu,\infty}
\le\|e_n\|_{\mu,T}+C_\mu Y e^{-\lambda T}.                      \tag{9}
\]

At \(T_n=(\log n)/(2\lambda)\), the remainder is root width. Any slower all-time error must already be visible by this logarithmic horizon; it cannot first arise from substantial additional motion arbitrarily late in training. This does not turn a fixed-horizon population theorem into a logarithmic-horizon theorem, since its constants may depend on the horizon.

The all-time initialization stability in the concentration note gives another scoped exclusion. Two good trajectories at the **same width** remain within a fixed constant times their normalized initial discrepancy. Thus a root-width perturbation for which such a same-width coupling has actually been constructed cannot undergo a macroscopic symmetry-breaking amplification. This is not a comparison between width \(n\) and the population, because that coupling is the missing part of the general problem.

## 5. An actual-model theorem for orthogonal passive queries

Let

\[
U=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_m\}\subseteq\mathbb R^d.
\]

For every \(x\) with \(u_x\in U^\perp\),

\[
f_*(t,x)=0\quad(t\ge0),\qquad
\mathbb E\left[\sup_{t\ge0}|f_n(t,x)|^2\mid\mathcal G_n\right]
\le \frac{C Y^2}{n}.                                           \tag{10}
\]

The conditional statement is for widths with \(\Pr(\mathcal G_n)>0\), in particular all sufficiently large widths under the stated population Gram margin. The constant is independent of the magnitude of \(x\). Consequently, if any fixed probability law \(\mu\) is supported on this orthogonal subspace, then

\[
\mathbb E\left[\int\sup_{t\ge0}|f_n(t,x)-f_*(t,x)|^2d\mu(x)
 \middle|\mathcal G_n\right]\le \frac{C Y^2}{n}.                \tag{11}
\]

No moment of that particular query law is required. If \(U=\mathbb R^d\), this special case contains only the zero query and is correspondingly trivial; the nontrivial case is \(\dim U<d\).

### 5.1 Independence retained by the exact dynamics

Let \(\mathcal F=\sigma(A_0P_U,W_0)\), where \(P_U\) is orthogonal projection. Equation (1) changes every row of \(A\) only in \(U\). All training features and all other evolving state variables are therefore measurable functions of \(\mathcal F\). The good event is also \(\mathcal F\)-measurable.

For \(u_x\perp U\),

\[
Au_x=A_0u_x\quad\text{for all time},\qquad
H:=\tanh(A_0u_x).
\]

The independent Gaussian first rows imply that the coordinates of \(H\) are independent, symmetric, bounded by one, and independent of \(\mathcal F\). Conditional on \(\mathcal F\), the actual query trajectory is exactly

\[
F_t(H)=\frac1n w(t)^T\tanh(B(t)H).                             \tag{12}
\]

It is odd in \(H\), so its conditional mean is zero at every time. This proves zero conditional bias relative to zero without claiming that trained rows are independent.

### 5.2 Deterministic velocity envelopes on the good event

Write \(S(t)=\int_0^t\rho\), \(D=K_0+2\), and \(E=\dot B\). The assigned fitting proof gives, for every good realization,

\[
S\le Y/\lambda\le1,\quad \|B\|_{\rm op}\le D,\quad
\|w\|_\infty\le2S,\quad\|\dot w\|_\infty\le2\rho.
                                                               \tag{13}
\]

In addition to \(\|E\|_{\rm op}\le8S\rho\), the reconstruction implies the induced supremum-norm estimate

\[
\sup_{h\in[-1,1]^n}\|Eh\|_\infty\le8S\rho.                    \tag{14}
\]

For completeness, \(\|k_a\|_\infty\le1\), \(\|\dot k_a\|_\infty\le2\rho\),

\[
\frac1m\sum_a\|\dot v_a\|_\infty\le4S\rho,
\qquad
\frac1m\sum_a\|v_a\|_\infty\le2S^2.
\]

For \(E=(mn)^{-1}\sum_a(\dot v_a k_a^T+v_a\dot k_a^T)\), multiplication by \(|h_j|\le1\) gives \(4S\rho+4S^2\rho\le8S\rho\), proving (14). The same bounds give the operator estimate by normalized outer-product norms. This uses the actual low-rank derivative of \(B\).

Put \(\psi=\operatorname{sech}^2\). The exact velocity of (12), as a smooth function of an arbitrary fixed \(h\in[-1,1]^n\), is

\[
a_t(h)=\frac1n\{\dot w^T\tanh(Bh)
                   +w^T[\psi(Bh)\odot Eh]\}.                  \tag{15}
\]

For constants depending on \(D\) but not on \(n,t,x\),

\[
\sup_h\|\nabla_h a_t(h)\|_2\le C\rho(t)/\sqrt n,\qquad
\sup_h|\partial_{jj}a_t(h)|\le C\rho(t)/n
\quad(1\le j\le n).                                           \tag{16}
\]

Here is a direct verification. With \(z=Bh\),

\[
\nabla_h a_t=\frac1n\left\{
B^T[\dot w\odot\psi(z)+w\odot\psi'(z)\odot Eh]
+E^T[w\odot\psi(z)]\right\}.
\]

Use \(|\psi|\le1\), \(|\psi'|\le2\), (13)–(14), and the operator bounds. Each bracket has Euclidean norm at most \(C\sqrt n\rho\) or \(2S\sqrt n\), giving the first estimate. Also,

\[
\partial_{jj}a_t=\frac1n\sum_i\{
\dot w_i\psi'(z_i)B_{ij}^2
+w_i\psi''(z_i)(Eh)_iB_{ij}^2
+2w_i\psi'(z_i)B_{ij}E_{ij}\}.
\]

The elementary derivative formula for \(\psi\) gives \(|\psi''|\le6\). Each column of \(B\) has norm at most \(D\), each column of \(E\) has norm at most \(8S\rho\), and (14) bounds the remaining coordinate factor. Cauchy–Schwarz in the last sum proves the second estimate in (16).

### 5.3 Product-coordinate variance and the temporal supremum

For independent coordinates \(H_j\), let \(H^{(j)}\) replace coordinate \(j\) by an independent copy. The elementary replacement-variance inequality is

\[
\operatorname{Var}a(H)\le\frac12\sum_j
 \mathbb E[(a(H)-a(H^{(j)}))^2].                               \tag{17}
\]

One proof exposes coordinates successively and writes the centered function as its orthogonal martingale differences. Conditional Jensen bounds the square of each difference by the expected conditional variance obtained by resampling that coordinate while all others are fixed. For a real random variable \(V\) and its conditionally independent copy \(V'\), that variance equals \(\tfrac12\mathbb E[(V-V')^2]\). Summing the orthogonal differences yields (17).

Since \(|H_j-H'_j|\le2\), Taylor's formula in that coordinate and (16) give

\[
|a_t(H)-a_t(H^{(j)})|
\le2|\partial_j a_t(H)|+2C\rho/n.
\]

Squaring, summing, and applying both parts of (16) proves

\[
\operatorname{Var}_H a_t(H)\le C\rho(t)^2/n.                    \tag{18}
\]

The function (15) is odd in \(h\): tanh is odd, \(\psi\) is even, and \(Eh\) is odd. Thus \(\mathbb E_Ha_t(H)=0\). Since \(F_0=0\), the pathwise inequality

\[
\sup_t|F_t(H)|\le\int_0^\infty|a_s(H)|\,ds
\]

and Minkowski's integral inequality give, for every fixed good training environment,

\[
\left(\mathbb E_H\sup_t|F_t(H)|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\int_0^\infty\sqrt{\mathbb E_Ha_t(H)^2}\,dt
\le\frac C{\sqrt n}\int_0^\infty\rho(t)dt
\le\frac{CY}{\lambda\sqrt n}.                                 \tag{19}
\]

This bound is uniform over all product symmetric laws on \([-1,1]^n\), including the actual \(\tanh(\|u_x\|Z_i)\) law. Average over the conditional good training environments. The assigned qualitative population convergence at every fixed query and (19) identify the deterministic population limit as zero. This proves (10). Tonelli's theorem then proves (11); correlations between predictions at different queries do not enter the integral of squared suprema.

Thus both symmetry breaking and large query magnitudes are rigorously excluded as slower-rate mechanisms in this special case. This proof is not an arbitrary time-dependent-feature substitute: (12) follows from the exact orthogonal invariance of (1), and (14) uses its exact memory reconstruction.

## 6. What finite-second-moment query tails do and do not permit

For general queries, the good-event and population readout bounds give the uniform estimate

\[
\sup_{t,x}|f_n(t,x)-f_*(t,x)|\le 4Y/\lambda.                    \tag{20}
\]

For \(R>0\), their contribution outside \(\|u_x\|\le R\) is consequently at most

\[
\frac{4Y}{\lambda}\sqrt{\mu\{\|u_x\|>R\}}.
\]

If \(\mu\) has finite second moment, then

\[
R^2\mu\{\|u_x\|>R\}
\le\int_{\{\|u_x\|>R\}}\|u_x\|^2d\mu(x)\longrightarrow0.
\]

In particular, queries of norm greater than \(\sqrt n\) contribute \(o(n^{-1/2})\) to the error norm. A slower rate cannot be attributed solely to this extreme tail.

More generally, if a proposed finite/population state coupling supplied a discrepancy \(D_n\) with

\[
\sup_t|f_n(t,x)-f_*(t,x)|\le C(1+\|u_x\|)D_n,
\qquad \|D_n\|_{L^2(\mathcal G_n)}\le Cn^{-1/2},
\]

then integrating its square immediately gives root width for every finite-second-moment query law. Linear amplification by query magnitude cannot overcome that moment assumption. The actual general state coupling has not been established, so this is a conditional exclusion, not a general theorem about all tails.

The concentration route's fourth-moment requirement comes from its quadratic query-velocity sensitivity bound. Failure of that particular estimate to integrate under a second moment is not an actual-model lower bound. A valid tail-based counterexample would still need fixed queries with a proved systematic error and fixed masses obeying the second-moment constraint; none was found here.
