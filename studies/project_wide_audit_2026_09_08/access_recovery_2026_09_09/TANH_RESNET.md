## 3. Task16: tanh corrections and surviving proof skeleton

### 3.1 Frozen contract and finite identities

This is the older **order-one stored Gaussian readout** model, not the modern tiny-readout source. In normalized vector space \(H_n\), the initialized connectors have entries \(N(0,1/n)\), and input coordinates and readout coordinates are independent order-one Gaussians. The input, both hidden matrices and readout are all trained. Put

\[
x_1=\tanh u,\quad x_2=\tanh(G_1x_1),\quad
x_3=\tanh(G_2x_2),\quad f=\langle A,x_3\rangle_n,
\]
\[
B_3=D_3A,\quad Q_2=G_2^*B_3,\quad B_2=D_2Q_2,
\quad Q_1=G_1^*B_2,\quad B_1=D_1Q_1.
\]

With \((b\otimes x)v=b\langle x,v\rangle_n\), feature-ascent gradients are \(A'=x_3,u'=B_1,G_2'=B_3\otimes x_2,G_1'=B_2\otimes x_1\). Hence

\[
K_n=\|x_3\|_n^2+\|B_3\|_n^2\|x_2\|_n^2
+\|B_2\|_n^2\|x_1\|_n^2+\|B_1\|_n^2.
\]

For full squared loss, \(\dot f=2\eta eK_n\) and \(\dot e=-2\eta eK_n\). The finite metric differentiation, rank-one normalization, real transposes, residual sign preservation and compact-time finite-flow bounds check. Bounded activations bound the readout first, then the upper learned trace norm, upper operator action, lower cotangent, and lower learned trace norm. This establishes finite global continuation, not source convergence.

The activated coordinate \(Y=\tanh u\) is admissible only with \(|Y|\le1\); canonical restart additionally requires \(|Y|<1\) almost surely and \(\operatorname{artanh}Y\in L^2\). This is not a vector field on arbitrary ambient \(L^2\) inputs. The final Euler-domain repair uses
\(\max_{|y|\le1}(1-y)(1+y)^2=32/27\), together with a residual multiplier restricted to \([1/2,1]\). It concerns the specified auxiliary clipped construction; clipping does not become raw GF/GD merely by naming a residual clock.

## 4. Scalar-particle residual theorem: retained, with exact scope

For fixed samples and fixed-dimensional raw parameter \(\theta\), the architecture is

\[
x_{a,\ell+1}=x_{a,\ell}+\frac1{nL}\sum_i\Phi(\theta_{\ell i},x_{a,\ell}),
\qquad \dot\theta=-\eta nL\nabla_\theta E,
\quad E=\frac1{2B}\sum_a(f_a-y_a)^2.
\]

The residual stream here is scalar. The Gaussian variables, when used, are raw finite-dimensional particle parameters, not dense central-limit-scaled inter-hidden-layer matrices. The general assumptions bound \(\Phi(\theta,0)\), \(\Phi_x\), and parameter derivatives and relevant Lipschitz constants on bounded state strips uniformly in \(\theta\). Squashed amplitude/slope/bias satisfy these assumptions even under unbounded Gaussian raw initialization; an unsquashed arbitrary neural feature need not.

The full proof skeleton survives reinspection:

1. Direct differentiation gives the scaled particle gradient with the **next-depth** adjoint \(r_{\ell+1}\). The adjoint recursion, kernel and batch normalization agree.
2. Uniform state/adjoint/velocity bounds follow from the hypotheses, first for finite depth and then for the depth ODE. They are independent of width and depth.
3. Couple two parameter-law profiles in the integrated metric \(d(\rho,\nu)=\int_0^1W_1(\rho_s,\nu_s)ds\). Forward and backward integral equations produce a velocity estimate Lipschitz in parameter and in this metric. The complete measurable profile space and characteristic fixed point yield a unique global evolution. The source explicitly handles measurable depth representatives and continuous reconstructed readouts; it does not identify arbitrary point values of an \(L^1\) equivalence class.
4. Finite empirical laws are exact characteristic solutions of the finite-depth population system. Coupling gives a pathwise width error \(C_T\delta_{n,L}\), with \(\delta_{n,L}=L^{-1}\sum_\ell W_1(\rho^n_{0,\ell},\rho_0)\).
5. Depth-Lipschitz control and comparison of both the forward scheme and exact discrete adjoint give \(C_T/L\). Thus the joint error is \(C_T(\delta_{n,L}+L^{-1})\).
6. Under iid depth-constant initialization in \(\mathcal P_1\), \(\mathbb E\delta_{n,L}=\mathbb E W_1(\rho^n_0,\rho_0)\to0\), independently of \(L\). Every joint sequence \(n,L\to\infty\) follows, without imposing a numerical width rate. Readout stability gives the stated output/kernel/loss conclusions.

This is a **global-in-training-time population well-posedness result and a compact-horizon approximation theorem**. It is not approximation uniformly on all training time, universal asymptotic fitting, or a simultaneous training-step/GD limit. The \(1/L\) is depth discretization error. At fixed depth \(L=3\), width tends to the depth-three **finite-depth population system**; one cannot discard the nonvanishing \(1/L\) error and call that the continuous-depth limit.

The history also clarifies two points absent or understated in the earlier master:

- **Centered Gaussian nonfreezing witness.** For \(\Phi=\tanh\alpha\,\tanh(\tanh\omega\,x+\tanh\beta)\), centered independent Gaussian raw coordinates give \(\mathbb E\Phi(\theta,x)=\mathbb E\Phi_x(\theta,x)=0\). Thus initially \(x(s)=x_0\), the scalar depth adjoint is one, and \(f(0)=x_0\). Nevertheless \(K(0)=\mathbb E\|\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta,x_0)\|^2>0\), including at \(x_0=0\), because the bias derivative is nonzero on a positive-probability set. For a single target \(y\ne x_0\), \(E'(0)=-\eta(x_0-y)^2K(0)<0\). Zero initial averaged residual field therefore does not mean frozen training. This does not prove \(K'(0)\ne0\), nontriviality for every batch, or that every Gaussian law becomes non-Gaussian immediately. See task16 feedback at 2967 and final at 3000.
- **Chaos corollary.** Feedback at 2973 compares interacting particles with independent same-initialization population characteristics. The uniform velocity estimate gives trajectory discrepancy bounded by \(C_T\delta_{n,L}\) in the stated averaged coupling, hence convergence for fixed finite collections using exchangeability, and an additional \(O(1/L)\) depth error where applicable. Do not replace the general \(\mathcal P_1\) sampling error by a universal \(n^{-1/2}\) Wasserstein rate.

The finite-moment obstruction in the general-activation document concerns an exact, state-universal encoding by a fixed finite family of **linear moments** over the stated laws, with an infinite-rank feature family. It does not prohibit arbitrary finite encodings, a special orbit, or approximate moment schemes.

\[
z_\ell=W_\ell h_{\ell-1}/n,\qquad
h_\ell=h_{\ell-1}+\alpha L^{-1}\tanh z_\ell,
\qquad f=n^{-1}c^Th_L.
\]

Initialize \(W_{\ell,ij}=w_0(s_\ell,u_i,u_j)+G_{\ell,ij}\), where the microscopic \(G_{\ell,ij}\) are standard Gaussian and \(w_0\) is a regular coherent kernel, possibly infinite rank. \[
P_{\ell-1}^a=P_\ell^a+\alpha L^{-1}(W_\ell/n)^TR_\ell^a,
\qquad \dot W_\ell=-\alpha\sum_aR_\ell^a(h_{\ell-1}^a)^T,
\]
\[
E'=-\frac1{Ln^2}\sum_\ell\|\dot W_\ell\|_F^2.
\]

These identities pass. The norm is of the full-batch sum, not the sum of squared per-example gradients. A per-example adjoint terminating at \(c\) instead keeps \(q_a\) outside the training equation; the two conventions must not be mixed.
