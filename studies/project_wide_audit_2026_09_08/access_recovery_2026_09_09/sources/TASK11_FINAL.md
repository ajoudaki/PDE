Yes. For the final formulation—where the \(\lambda_n^{(\ell)}\) need not be normalized—the answer is affirmative.

Crucially, the Tensor Programs theorem is used only for fixed auxiliary Euler meshes. Continuous time and exact GD are obtained by separate, dimension-uniform deterministic estimates.

## The witness

Take

\[
\phi(x)=\arctan x,
\qquad
q(x)=\phi'(x)=\frac1{1+x^2},
\]

and

\[
\sigma_{1,n}^2=1,\qquad
\sigma_{2,n}^2=\frac1n,\qquad
\sigma_{3,n}^2=\frac1{n^4},
\]

\[
\lambda_n^{(1)}=n,\qquad
\lambda_n^{(2)}=1,\qquad
\lambda_n^{(3)}=\frac1n,
\qquad
\eta_{0,n}=\frac1{n^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\eta_{0,n}\lambda_n^{(1)}=n^{-1},\quad
\eta_{0,n}\lambda_n^{(2)}=n^{-2},\quad
\eta_{0,n}\lambda_n^{(3)}=n^{-3},
\]

so every actual parameter step tends to zero.

Use normalized inner products

\[
\langle u,v\rangle_n=\frac1n u^\top v,\qquad
\|u\|_n^2=\langle u,u\rangle_n,
\]

and define

\[
A=W^{(1)},\qquad B=W^{(2)},\qquad C=nW^{(3)}.
\]

Thus \(C_j(0)\sim N(0,n^{-2})\), and

\[
H=\arctan A,\qquad
S=BH,\qquad
G=\arctan S,\qquad
D=Cq(S),
\]

\[
f=\langle C,G\rangle_n,\qquad e=f-1.
\]

For vectors \(u,v\), write

\[
(u\otimes v)x=u\langle v,x\rangle_n.
\]

## 1. Exact finite-width gradient flow

A direct differentiation gives

\[
\dot A=-2e\,q(A)B^*D,
\]

\[
\dot B=-2e\,D\otimes H,
\]

\[
\dot C=-2e\,G.
\]

Here \(B^*=B^\top\) with respect to the normalized inner products.

The layerwise kernels are exactly

\[
K_n^{(1)}
=
\|q(A)B^*D\|_n^2,
\]

\[
K_n^{(2)}
=
\|H\|_n^2\|D\|_n^2,
\]

\[
K_n^{(3)}
=
\|G\|_n^2.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dot f=-2eK_n,\qquad
\dot L=-4e^2K_n,
\qquad
K_n=\sum_{\ell=1}^3K_n^{(\ell)}.
\]

The hidden velocities are

\[
V_n^{(1)}{}^2
=
4e^2K_n^{(1)}
\]

and

\[
\dot S
=
-2e\left[
\|H\|_n^2D
+
B\big(q(A)^2B^*D\big)
\right],
\]

so

\[
V_n^{(2)}{}^2
=
4e^2
\left\|
\|H\|_n^2D
+
B\big(q(A)^2B^*D\big)
\right\|_n^2.
\]

All three blocks therefore appear in the exact output dynamics.

## 2. The autonomous limiting flow

Introduce

\[
F(a)=a+\frac{a^3}{3},\qquad X=F(A),\qquad J=F^{-1}.
\]

Since

\[
F'(a)=1+a^2=\frac1{q(a)},
\]

the first equation becomes

\[
\dot X=-2eB^*D.
\]

Moreover, \(J\) is globally \(1\)-Lipschitz, and

\[
x\longmapsto \arctan J(x),\qquad
x\longmapsto q(J(x))
\]

are bounded Lipschitz functions.

The limiting state consists of two probability algebras with Hilbert spaces

\[
\mathcal H_1=L^2(\Omega_1),\qquad
\mathcal H_2=L^2(\Omega_2),
\]

a current bounded operator

\[
B(t):\mathcal H_1\to\mathcal H_2,
\]

and current fields

\[
X(t)\in\mathcal H_1,\qquad C(t)\in\mathcal H_2.
\]

Set

\[
A=J(X),\quad H=\arctan A,\quad
S=BH,\quad G=\arctan S,\quad
D=Cq(S),
\]

\[
f=\langle C,G\rangle_{\mathcal H_2},\qquad e=f-1.
\]

The limiting autonomous equations are

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\dot X&=-2eB^*D,\\
\dot B&=-2e\,D\otimes H,\\
\dot C&=-2eG .
\end{aligned}}
\]

The initial conditions are

\[
A(0)\sim N(0,1),\qquad X(0)=F(A(0)),\qquad C(0)=0,
\]

and \(B(0)=\Gamma\), where \(\Gamma\) is the limiting Gaussian action generated by the iid \(N(0,1/n)\) matrices.

This system uses only the current \(B,X,C\). It contains no prescribed output trajectory, past history, or finite parameter array, so it is autonomous and restartable.

## 3. Construction of the Gaussian action

For a fixed rational Euler mesh \(\delta\) with finitely many steps,

\[
B^r
=
B^0-2\delta\sum_{s<r}e^sD^s\otimes H^s.
\]

Therefore, for any vector \(v\),

\[
B^rv
=
B^0v
-
2\delta\sum_{s<r}e^sD^s\langle H^s,v\rangle_n,
\]

and similarly

\[
(B^r)^*w
=
(B^0)^*w
-
2\delta\sum_{s<r}e^sH^s\langle D^s,w\rangle_n.
\]

Thus a fixed mesh uses only finitely many applications of \(B^0\), \((B^0)^*\), coordinatewise nonlinearities, and empirical moments.

This is precisely within the finite-program scope of [Tensor Programs IV, Definition 7.1, Setup 7.2 and Theorem 7.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.14522v3#page=22). The theorem is not being applied to \(T/\eta_{0,n}\) steps. Its pseudo-Lipschitz hypotheses hold because:

- \(F\) has polynomial growth;
- \(J,\arctan\circ J,q\circ J\) are globally Lipschitz or bounded Lipschitz;
- products involving the adaptive scalar \(e\) are pseudo-Lipschitz;
- \(B^0_{ji}\sim N(0,1/n)\);
- \(C^0=\gamma/n\) is a standard Gaussian vector times a scalar converging to zero.

The rank-stability-free pseudo-Lipschitz master theorem underlying this is [Tensor Programs III, Theorem E.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10685v3#page=33).

Take a countable language containing all rational meshes and a dense countable family of bounded Lipschitz probes. The fixed-program theorem gives consistent joint empirical laws and moments for every finite sublanguage. A countable intersection and Kolmogorov extension produce \(\Omega_1,\Omega_2\).

On the dense probe subspaces define

\[
\Gamma[p]=[B^0p],\qquad
\Gamma^\dagger[q]=[(B^0)^\top q].
\]

The Gaussian operator-norm tail estimate in [Tensor Programs III, Fact A.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10685v3#page=25) gives, with probability tending to one,

\[
\|B^0_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le M
\]

for a deterministic \(M\). Hence

\[
\|\Gamma p\|_2\le M\|p\|_2,
\]

so \(\Gamma\) extends to a bounded operator. Passing the exact finite identity

\[
\langle B_n^0p_n,q_n\rangle_n
=
\langle p_n,(B_n^0)^\top q_n\rangle_n
\]

shows

\[
\Gamma^\dagger=\Gamma^*.
\]

No cross-width operator-norm convergence is claimed or needed.

Writing \(B=\Gamma+P\), the learned part satisfies

\[
\dot P=-2eD\otimes H,\qquad P(0)=0,
\]

and is trace class because

\[
\|D\otimes H\|_1=\|D\|_2\|H\|_2.
\]

At every fixed mesh, the nonzero singular values of \(P\) are determined by the finite Gram matrices of the \(D^r\)'s and \(H^r\)'s, which converge under the fixed Tensor Program.

## 4. Well-posedness and mesh removal

Let \(a=\pi/2\). Since \(|H|,|G|\le a\),

\[
\|D\|_2\le\|C\|_2,\qquad
|e|\le a\|C\|_2+1.
\]

Also,

\[
\frac d{dt}\|C\|_2
\le 2a(a\|C\|_2+1),
\]

\[
\|\dot P\|_1
\le 2a|e|\|C\|_2,
\]

\[
\|\dot X\|_2
\le
2|e|(\|\Gamma\|+\|P\|_1)\|C\|_2.
\]

Pointwise,

\[
\|C(t)\|_\infty
\le
\|C(0)\|_\infty+2a\int_0^t|e(s)|\,ds.
\]

These give dimension-independent bounds on every compact interval.

On a bounded tube, the vector field is locally Lipschitz in

\[
\|(X,P,C)\|_{\mathcal E}
=
\|X\|_2+\|P\|_1+\|C\|_2.
\]

The only potentially delicate product obeys

\[
\|Cq(S)-\widetilde Cq(\widetilde S)\|_2
\le
\|C-\widetilde C\|_2
+
M_C\operatorname{Lip}(q)\|S-\widetilde S\|_2
\]

when both \(C,\widetilde C\) have the common \(L^\infty\) bound \(M_C\).

A formal Picard proof is obtained by clipping \(C\) only inside this product, solving the globally defined clipped equation, and then choosing the clipping level above the preceding a-priori envelope. This also proves uniqueness and restartability.

The same constants apply at finite \(n\). Therefore, if \(Y_n\) is the finite-width gradient flow and \(Y_n^\delta\) its Euler approximation,

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\left(
\|X_n-X_n^\delta\|_n
+
\|C_n-C_n^\delta\|_n
+
\|P_n-P_n^\delta\|_1
\right)
\le C_T\delta,
\]

uniformly in \(n\) on events whose probabilities tend to one. The identical estimate holds between the limiting Euler scheme and limiting flow.

Thus the order of limits is

\[
\text{fix }\delta
\;\longrightarrow\;
n\to\infty
\;\longrightarrow\;
\delta\downarrow0.
\]

The Tensor Programs theorem is never invoked at an \(n\)-dependent number of steps.

## 5. Exact GD versus gradient flow

Let

\[
R=B^*D.
\]

One exact first-layer GD update is

\[
A^+=A+s,\qquad
s=-2\eta_{0,n}e\,q(A)R.
\]

The \(B\)- and \(C\)-updates are exactly Euler updates of their transformed equations. For \(X=F(A)\),

\[
F(A+s)-F(A)-F'(A)s
=
As^2+\frac{s^3}{3}.
\]

Because \(F'(A)s=-2\eta_{0,n}eR\), this is exactly the sole consistency error.

On every a-priori tube,

\[
\|s\|_n\le C_T\eta_{0,n},
\qquad
\|s\|_\infty\le \sqrt n\,\|s\|_n,
\qquad
\|A\|_n\le\|X\|_n.
\]

Hence the per-step defect satisfies

\[
\left\|As^2+\frac{s^3}{3}\right\|_n
\le
C_T\bigl(\eta_{0,n}^2n+\eta_{0,n}^3n\bigr).
\]

After \(T/\eta_{0,n}\) steps, discrete Grönwall gives

\[
\sup_{t\le T}d_{\mathcal E}
\bigl(Y_n^{\rm GD}(t),Y_n^{\rm GF}(t)\bigr)
\le
C_T\bigl(
\eta_{0,n}
+\eta_{0,n}n
+\eta_{0,n}^2n
\bigr).
\]

For \(\eta_{0,n}=n^{-2}\), the right side is \(O(n^{-1})\).

This is the required joint width/discrete-time estimate.

## 6. Uniform integrability and path topology

The only unbounded weighted readouts are

\[
q(A)R,\qquad q(A)^2R.
\]

At each fixed mesh, the Tensor Program gives convergence of the empirical law and second moment of \(R\); hence the squared coordinates are uniformly integrable.

For the exact flow, use

\[
x^2\mathbf 1_{\{|x|>M\}}
\le
2y^2\mathbf 1_{\{|y|>M/2\}}
+
4(x-y)^2
\]

with \(x=R_n(t)\) and \(y=R_n^\delta(\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\delta)\). The uniform Euler error transfers square-uniform-integrability from the fixed mesh to the continuous flow. The GD-to-flow estimate transfers it again to exact GD.

If \(g\) is bounded Lipschitz, truncation then gives

\[
\|(g(A)-g(\widetilde A))\widetilde R\|_n^2
\le
\operatorname{Lip}(g)^2M^2\|A-\widetilde A\|_n^2
+
4\|g\|_\infty^2
\langle \widetilde R^2\mathbf1_{\{|\widetilde R|>M\}}\rangle_n.
\]

Therefore both \(q(A)R\) and \(q(A)^2R\) converge strongly in empirical \(L^2\), uniformly on compact time intervals. This passes \(K^{(1)}\), \(V^{(1)}\), and the \(B(q(A)^2R)\) term in \(V^{(2)}\). Every other term contains only bounded nonlinearities.

For path laws, if \(u_i,v_i\) have the same initial values,

\[
\frac1n\sum_i
\sup_{t\le T}|u_i(t)-v_i(t)|^2
\le
T\int_0^T
\|\dot u(t)-\dot v(t)\|_n^2\,dt.
\]

Together with fixed-grid \(W_2\) convergence, this proves

\[
\overline\mu_n^{(1)}
\longrightarrow
\overline\mu^{(1)},\qquad
\overline\mu_n^{(2)}
\longrightarrow
\overline\mu^{(2)}
\]

in \(W_2(C([0,T]))\), in probability, along the full sequence. It also proves convergence of the squared velocities in \(L^1([0,T])\), and uniform convergence of \(K_n,f_n,L_n\).

The diagonal argument is

\[
d(Y_n^{\rm GD},Y)
\le
d(Y_n^{\rm GD},Y_n^{\rm GF})
+d(Y_n^{\rm GF},Y_n^\delta)
+d(Y_n^\delta,Y^\delta)
+d(Y^\delta,Y).
\]

Choose \(\delta\) first and then \(n\). This establishes tightness, identification, uniqueness, uniform integrability, and vanishing discrete error jointly.

## 7. Strict activity and nonlinearity

Let

\[
A_0\sim N(0,1),\qquad h=\arctan A_0,\qquad
m=\mathbb E h^2>0.
\]

Then

\[
Z=\Gamma h\sim N(0,m).
\]

Put

\[
g=\arctan Z,\qquad
u=gq(Z),\qquad
\nu=\mathbb E u^2>0,\qquad
k_3=\mathbb E g^2>0.
\]

Let

\[
\xi=\Gamma^*u.
\]

Elementary Gaussian row conditioning gives

\[
\xi=\kappa h+\sqrt{\nu}\,\zeta,
\qquad
\zeta\sim N(0,1),\quad \zeta\perp A_0,
\qquad
\kappa=\mathbb E[u'(Z)].
\]

Consequently,

\[
d
:=
\mathbb E[q(A_0)^2\xi^2]
=
\nu\,\mathbb E q(A_0)^2
+
\kappa^2\mathbb E[q(A_0)^2h^2]
>0.
\]

Define the positive operator

\[
\mathcal R
=
mI+\Gamma M_{q(A_0)^2}\Gamma^*.
\]

Then

\[
\langle u,\mathcal Ru\rangle
=
m\nu+d>0,
\qquad
\|\mathcal Ru\|_2>0.
\]

The limiting solution has, in \(L^2\) and trace norm,

\[
C(t)=2tg+o(t),
\]

\[
D(t)=2tu+o(t),
\]

\[
B(t)-B(0)
=
2t^2u\otimes h+o_{\mathfrak S_1}(t^2),
\]

\[
A(t)-A_0
=
2t^2q(A_0)\xi+o_{L^2}(t^2),
\]

\[
S(t)-Z
=
2t^2\mathcal Ru+o_{L^2}(t^2).
\]

Therefore

\[
K^{(1)}(t)=4dt^2+o(t^2),
\]

\[
K^{(2)}(t)=4m\nu t^2+o(t^2),
\]

\[
K^{(3)}(t)
=
k_3+4(m\nu+d)t^2+o(t^2),
\]

and hence

\[
K(t)
=
k_3+8(m\nu+d)t^2+o(t^2).
\]

Similarly,

\[
V^{(1)}(t)^2
=
16dt^2+o(t^2),
\]

\[
V^{(2)}(t)^2
=
16t^2\|\mathcal Ru\|_2^2+o(t^2).
\]

The hidden displacements satisfy

\[
\|A(t)-A_0\|_2^2
=
4dt^4+o(t^4)>0,
\]

\[
\|S(t)-S(0)\|_2^2
=
4t^4\|\mathcal Ru\|_2^2+o(t^4)>0.
\]

Finally,

\[
f(0)=0,\qquad
f(t)=2k_3t+o(t),
\]

\[
L(t)=1-4k_3t+o(t)<1
\]

for sufficiently small \(t>0\).

At \(t=0\), both \(A_0\) and \(S_0=Z\) are nondegenerate full-support Gaussians. For any law \(\mu\) of positive variance,

\[
\mathcal N(\mu)
=
\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\arctan Z)
-
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(Z,\arctan Z)^2}
{\operatorname{Var}_\mu Z}.
\]

For a full-support Gaussian this is strictly positive, since otherwise \(\arctan z\) would agree with an affine function on all of \(\mathbb R\). This functional is \(W_2\)-continuous while the variance stays bounded away from zero. Hence both hidden-layer nonlinear residuals remain positive on a sufficiently short interval.

## Conclusion

There exists \(T_*>0\) such that, for every \(0<T<T_*\):

- the exact interpolated GD state converges jointly in probability along the full sequence;
- both hidden path laws converge in \(W_2(C([0,T]))\) to finite, nondegenerate laws;
- both hidden layers have positive finite velocity energy and nonzero macroscopic displacement;
- all three kernel blocks have positive finite time integrals;
- the activation remains genuinely nonlinear in both hidden layers;
- \(K(t)\) is finite, positive, and nonconstant;
- \(f(0)=0\ne1\) and \(L(T)<L(0)\);
- the limiting evolution is autonomous and restartable.

Moreover,

\[
\|B(t)-B(0)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=
2t^2\sqrt{m\nu}+o(t^2)>0,
\]

so the middle layer is moving at leading operator order; the construction is not a frozen-edge, NTK, linearized, or tangent-mark limit.

Thus the latest statement is resolved affirmatively. The earlier continuous-time Tensor Program invocation was indeed invalid; the correct proof uses Tensor Programs only at fixed meshes and supplies the continuous-time and exact-GD limits by independent uniform analysis.
