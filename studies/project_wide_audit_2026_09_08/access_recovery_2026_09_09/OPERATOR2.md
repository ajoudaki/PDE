## 6. Finite-state, Gaussian and dataset claims recovered from older turns

### 6.1 A valid finite-algebra obstruction, not a universal closure no-go

The complete historical review at record 24734 contains the elementary finite-dimensional algebra argument. If a finite-dimensional unital linear subspace `A` of continuous functions on a connected domain is closed under pointwise multiplication, then each `g in A` satisfies a nonzero polynomial relation: the powers `1,g,g^2,...` are linearly dependent. Its continuous image lies in the finite root set of that polynomial and is therefore a singleton. Thus `A` consists only of constants.

This excludes that specific nontrivial pointwise-function algebra. It does not exclude finitely many infinite-dimensional fields or operators, a finite-dimensional nonlinear manifold, a state-specific parametrization, or every moment closure. The master's separate bounded-contraction representation theorem and deep-linear operator positive must not be collapsed into this different elementary statement.

The associated natural-coordinate argument is also local and specific. For invertible correlation matrix `R`, trying to flatten the full frame by `D Psi=diag(1/phi'(U)) R^-1` requires equality of mixed partial derivatives. On an open set where `phi'` is nonzero and `g=1/phi'` varies, it demands `g'(U_k)(R^-1)_(ka)=0` for `a != k`. This blocks that exact frame flattening for genuinely coupled `R`; it does not prove that the actual single vector field has no useful coordinates, and does not cover singular `R` by writing its inverse.
