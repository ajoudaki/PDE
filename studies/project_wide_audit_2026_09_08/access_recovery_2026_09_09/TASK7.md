## 3. Claim ledger and proof-skeleton checks

### 3.1 Toy example: new full source coverage, not a new NN theorem

The toy takes iid initial pairs `(P,Z0)`, with `P∈{−1,1}` fixed during training, bounded activation `φ=arctan`, prediction the average of `Pφ(Z)`, and raw updates `Z+=−η P r φ′(Z)`. Its transformed flow has `X=F(Z)`, `F(z)=z+z³/3`, and `X′=−Pr`. Thus `X(t)=X0−Ps(t)` where the scalar clock satisfies

`s′=E[P φ(F⁻¹(X0−Ps))]−y`.

The right side is bounded and Lipschitz. For a fixed auxiliary Euler mesh the oracle trajectories are iid copies driven by the deterministic population clock. Separate bounded empirical residual and observable errors are controlled by a finite union of Hoeffding bounds; stability accumulates them. The raw-step transformation defect is width-independent here because the individual driving factor is bounded. Sending the auxiliary mesh to zero proves the toy result under `η_n→0`.

Corrections/limits:

- The later stronger condition `η_n√n→0` can be imposed on the toy but is unnecessary for this proof. The user’s historical `η=o(√n)` expression is not the NN’s sufficient `η=o(n^(−1/2))`. Neither a casual “necessary” in the side-chat nor agreement with the user proves necessity of either width power.
- A minor endpoint bookkeeping slip uses `M=ceil(T/δ)` but an `e^T−1` bound for all `m≤M`. Use `mδ≤T`, or enlarge the constant to `e^(T+δ)−1`. This does not change convergence.
- Literal independence applies to whole oracle trajectories across particles, not new independent samples at every time. The NN oracle is different because it reuses a Gaussian matrix and its transpose.
- The fourth attachment’s tanh vector/matrix system is a teaching example, not the stated arctan supervised network. Its bounded-state ODE/Euler estimates are sound; its brief “Gram entries converge, hence coefficients converge” sentence omits a rank/regularization argument. The later L2 finite-Gaussian lemma supplies that missing type of argument; the attachment alone is not a complete independent probabilistic theorem.

### 3.2 L2: unchanged accepted theorem, now exact review/supersession provenance

The final contract is one input and target both 1, two arctan hidden layers, independent first coordinates `N(0,1)`, middle entries `N(0,1/n)`, rescaled readout coordinates `N(0,n^(−2))`, output `n^(−1) Cᵀh2`, loss `r²`, and raw GD with rates proportional to `(n,1,n)` in these coordinates. Its sufficient regime is `η_n√n→0`; `η_n=n^(−2)` is an example. Earlier raw-readout variance `n^(−4)` and rescaled variance `n^(−2)` are consistent under `C=n W_raw`; order-one rescaled readout is a different contract.

The final proof skeleton was checked against its actual text:

1. The natural coordinate cancels the first activation derivative **exactly for flow**, not for raw GD. The cubic identity gives accumulated defect `C_T(η_n+η_n√n+η_n²n)` and explicitly covers interpolation of raw parameters.
2. At a fixed auxiliary mesh, learned matrices unroll into initial Gaussian action plus finite rank-one memory. The oracle borrows all scalar feedback coefficients: residuals and both families of cross-time overlaps. Its stored finite memory matrix does **not** exactly produce its oracle nodes; equation(30) displays the empirical-minus-population overlap defect.
3. The internal Gaussian lemma uses fixed globally Lipschitz instructions and iid finite-second-moment roots, including `F(G)`. Independent small input perturbations make finite Gram limits invertible. Conditional projected Gaussian averaging gives joint W2 convergence; a common-matrix, operator-norm stability comparison removes the perturbations. No continuity of a singular unperturbed pseudoinverse is assumed.
4. Countably many compatible generated coordinates produce two separate probability spaces, a bounded action and its actual adjoint. Passing the finite transpose identity is essential. Equal numeric neuron indices across layers are not paired.
5. The transformed vector field is Lipschitz on sets with an independent readout coordinate-supremum bound. Dissipation and triangular norm estimates give global finite-time continuation; the separate readout update closes the Euler/GD stopping argument.
6. Fixed-mesh convergence, uniform deterministic comparison, and then auxiliary-mesh removal identify the population flow. Compact-time W2 convergence gives uniform square-tail control for the backward field; clipping extends the result to kernel blocks, velocities and path laws.
7. Initial regression and adjunction give strictly positive coefficients for both hidden displacements/speeds and kernel growth at small positive time. Those activity conclusions are local in time, not a new theorem of global fitting for arbitrary labels/data.
