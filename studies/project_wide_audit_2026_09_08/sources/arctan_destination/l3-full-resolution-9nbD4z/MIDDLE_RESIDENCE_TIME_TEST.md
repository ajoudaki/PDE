# Positive mobility and temporal bounds do not control residence amplification

Status: candidate mathematical route test, not a canonical-network theorem.
This is an explicitly prescribed-input auxiliary middle-layer system.
Its mobility has the same positive scalar-plus-Gram form as the canonical
middle mobility, but is frozen; its query is prescribed, not generated by
the trained upper layers. No claim of canonical reachability, probability,
or population failure is made.

The inference tested is whether positive mobility, bounded normalized
preactivations and velocities, a query starting at zero, and bounded
normalized query derivative force short residence in positive middle
curvature or a bounded response amplitude. They do not, even when the
auxiliary system's actual response is used instead of a frozen coefficient.

All vector norms are ordinary Euclidean norms. The normalization is
written explicitly. Fix a horizon \(S>0\), an integer \(n\ge2\), and put
\[
 u=\frac{(0,1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt{n-1}},\qquad
 v=e_1+u,\qquad A=I+vv^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \lambda_n=\sqrt{\frac n{n-1}}.
\]
Here \(e_1\) is the first coordinate vector. Thus \(I\preceq A\preceq3I\);
in the orthonormal basis \(e_1,u\), its nontrivial block is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
In particular this test does not replace positive mobility by an
indefinite matrix or use an unbounded mobility norm.

Define, for \(0\le s\le S\),
\[
 \zeta(s)=1+\frac34\lambda_n s^2,\qquad
 q^{(2)}_1(s)=-\sqrt n\,s,\qquad
 q^{(2)}_j(s)=\lambda_n s[1+\zeta(s)^2]\quad(2\le j\le n).
 \tag{1}
\]
These are smooth prescribed functions of time, held fixed when taking
initial-state derivatives. The auxiliary ODE is
\[
 (z^{(2)})'=A\big[\phi'(z^{(2)})\odot q^{(2)}(s)\big],
 \qquad z^{(2)}(0)=(1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \phi(z)=\arctan z.
 \tag{2}
\]
For each \(n,S\), its vector field is globally Lipschitz in
\(z^{(2)}\) on this time interval: \(|\phi''|\le2\),
\(\|A\|_{\rm op}=3\), and \(\sup_{s\le S}\max_j|q^{(2)}_j(s)|<\infty\).
It is continuous in time and bounded in the state at each time.
The usual finite-dimensional Picard theorem therefore gives a unique
global solution on the interval. No width-uniform Lipschitz constant
is asserted.

## An actual stationary rare coordinate

The solution of (2) is exactly
\[
 z^{(2)}_1(s)=1,\qquad z^{(2)}_j(s)=\zeta(s)\quad(j\ge2).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed its gated query has first coordinate
\(-\sqrt n\,s/2\) and every other coordinate \(\lambda_n s\).
Its projection on \(u\) is therefore \(\sqrt n\,s\).
Applying the displayed two-by-two block of \(A\) gives zero in
coordinate one and \(3\sqrt n\,s/2\) in the \(u\) direction.
The latter is exactly the derivative of the remaining coordinates
in (3), since \(\zeta'(s)=3\lambda_n s/2\). The initial values match,
and uniqueness proves (3).

Every bound
\[
 \sup_{0\le s\le S}
 \left\{\frac{\|z^{(2)}(s)\|_2}{\sqrt n},
 \frac{\|(z^{(2)})'(s)\|_2}{\sqrt n},
 \frac{\|q^{(2)}(s)\|_2}{\sqrt n},
 \frac{\|(q^{(2)})'(s)\|_2}{\sqrt n}\right\}\le C_S
 \tag{4}
\]
holds with a constant independent of \(n\); also \(q^{(2)}(0)=0\).
For explicit verification, let
\(Z_S=1+3\sqrt2 S^2/4\). The four bounds follow respectively from
\[
 |z^{(2)}_j|\le Z_S,\qquad
 \frac{\|(z^{(2)})'\|_2}{\sqrt n}=\frac32s,
\]
\[
 \frac{\|q^{(2)}\|_2^2}{n}
   =s^2\{1+[1+\zeta^2]^2\},
\]
\[
 \frac{\|(q^{(2)})'\|_2^2}{n}
   =1+\{1+\zeta^2+2s\zeta\zeta'\}^2,\qquad
 |\zeta'|\le3\sqrt2 S/2.
 \tag{5}
\]
The activation is bounded by \(\pi/2\), its derivative by one,
and the gated query and activation velocity inherit the corresponding
bounds from (4). Thus bounded temporal query regularity is genuinely
present; a discontinuous or rapidly oscillating query is not being used.

Since \(\phi''(z)=-2z/(1+z^2)^2\), the actual curvature coefficients are
\[
 q^{(2)}_1(s)\phi''(z^{(2)}_1(s))=\frac{\sqrt n}{2}s,
 \qquad
 q^{(2)}_j(s)\phi''(z^{(2)}_j(s))
   =-\frac{2\lambda_n s\zeta(s)}{1+\zeta(s)^2}
   =:b(s)\quad(j\ge2).
 \tag{6}
\]
Consequently \(-\lambda_n s\le b(s)\le0\), whereas the rare
coordinate stays at its positive curvature for the entire interval:
\[
 \int_0^S
  [q^{(2)}_1(s)\phi''(z^{(2)}_1(s))]_+\,ds
     =\frac{\sqrt n\,S^2}{4}.
 \tag{7}
\]
For every fixed \(c>0\), the empirical mean of the exponentials
of these positive-curvature integrals is at least
\(n^{-1}\exp(c\sqrt n S^2/4)\), which diverges.
The mean of the integrals themselves is just \(S^2/(4\sqrt n)\);
small average curvature does not exclude this effect.

## Actual response growth, including the coupled bulk

Differentiate (2) with respect to its initial state while keeping
the prescribed query (1) fixed. Its genuine propagator satisfies
\[
 P'=A\operatorname{diag}
       \big(q^{(2)}\odot\phi''(z^{(2)})\big)P,\qquad P(0)=I.
 \tag{8}
\]
The plane spanned by \(e_1,u\) is invariant. Writing
\(P(s)e_1=x(s)e_1+y(s)u\), its exact equations are
\[
 x'=\sqrt n\,s\,x+b(s)y,\qquad
 y'=\tfrac12\sqrt n\,s\,x+2b(s)y,\qquad (x(0),y(0))=(1,0).
 \tag{9}
\]
The solution remains in the cone \(x>0,\ 0\le y\le x/2\).
One way to verify this without assuming positivity is to set
\(r=y/x\) as long as \(x>0\). It obeys
\[
 r'=\sqrt n\,s(1/2-r)+b(s)(2r-r^2).
 \tag{10}
\]
At \(r=0\) this is nonnegative; at \(r=1/2\) it is \(3b/4\le0\).
Existence and uniqueness for this scalar smooth equation preserve
the interval \([0,1/2]\). Solving
\(x'=(\sqrt n\,s+b r)x,\ x(0)=1\), then setting \(y=rx\),
gives a positive solution of (9) on the entire interval.
This also proves that no zero of \(x\) was silently divided by.

It follows from (6) and this cone that
\[
 x'(s)\ge(\sqrt n-\lambda_n/2)s\,x(s),\qquad
 \|P(s)e_1\|_2\ge x(s)\ge
 \exp\left(\frac{\sqrt n-\lambda_n/2}{2}s^2\right).
 \tag{11}
\]
The actual coupled response, not just the rare scalar diagonal,
therefore has exponential-in-\(\sqrt n\) amplification. In particular
for each fixed \(s>0\),
\[
 \frac1n\operatorname{Tr}(P(s)P(s)^{\mathsf T})
 \ge\frac1n\exp\big((\sqrt n-\lambda_n/2)s^2\big)\longrightarrow\infty .
 \tag{12}
\]
This is consistent with bounded squared logarithms divided by \(n\);
it is not a counterexample to any earlier logarithmic response bound.

There is also a zero-initial-response version with a controlled,
independent Gaussian source. Let \(\xi\sim N(0,1)\), independent of
the deterministic path, and solve
\[
 w'=A\operatorname{diag}
       \big(q^{(2)}\odot\phi''(z^{(2)})\big)w+\xi e_1,
 \qquad w(0)=0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The source has \(\mathbb E\|\xi e_1\|_2^2=1\), equivalently
\(n\mathbb E[\|\xi e_1\|_2^2/n]=1\).
Write \(w=\xi(Xe_1+Yu)\). The deterministic equations for \(X,Y\)
are (9) with \(+1\) in the first equation and zero initial values.
The closed cone \(X\ge0,\ 0\le Y\le X/2\) is invariant:
on \(Y=0\) its derivative is \(\sqrt n\,sX/2\ge0\);
on \(Y=X/2\),
\[
 (Y-X/2)'=3b(s)X/4-1/2\le0;
\]
and at the vertex the forcing points into the cone. These
inward boundary inequalities for a locally Lipschitz linear ODE,
or their first-exit proof, give the claimed invariance.
Consequently, putting \(a_n=\sqrt n-\lambda_n/2>0\),
\[
 X'\ge a_n sX+1,\qquad
 X(S)\ge\int_0^S
     \exp\big(a_n(S^2-t^2)/2\big)\,dt
 \ge\frac S2\exp(3a_nS^2/8).
 \tag{14}
\]
Hence
\[
 n\,\mathbb E\left[\frac{\|w(S)\|_2^2}{n}\right]
 =X(S)^2+Y(S)^2
 \ge\frac{S^2}{4}\exp(3a_n S^2/4)\longrightarrow\infty .
 \tag{15}
\]
The source bound alone, even with this actual auxiliary propagator
and deterministic coefficients independent of the Gaussian source,
therefore does not close a response-amplitude estimate.

## Exact scope of the failed inference

The coherent bulk cancels the rare coordinate's primal velocity in (3),
but does not cancel its transverse amplification in (9). Positive
mobility and bounded query velocity do not prevent this distinction.
This defeats a residence-time argument relying only on (4), zero
initial query, bounded activation, and positive bounded mobility.

This system is not asserted to be the canonical trained architecture:
no \(W^{(2)},W^{(3)},W^{(4)}\) satisfying their actual update equations
have been constructed, and (1) is not their backpropagated query.
The initial data are deterministic and do not have the prescribed
independent Gaussian law. The test gives no failure probability,
no actual-network response lower bound, and no counterexample to
the all-finite-time joint MF/GF/GD theorem. A valid continuation route
must use further trained dynamical or probabilistic information.
No computation or numerical experiment was used.
