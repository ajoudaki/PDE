# Exact raw-cubic depth-2 recurrence

## 1. Model and feature flow

For one input and two equal-width hidden layers, take

\[
X_j=U_j^3,
\qquad
Z_i=n^{-1/2}\sum_jW_{ij}X_j,
\qquad
f_n=n^{-1}\sum_iA_iZ_i^3.
\]

The scaled directional operator
\(D_n=n\nabla f_n\cdot\nabla\) is differentiation along
\(\dot\theta=n\nabla f_n\).  If

\[
B_i=A_iZ_i^2,
\qquad
R_j=n^{-1/2}\sum_iW_{ij}B_i,
\]

then direct differentiation of the original finite-width network gives

\[
\dot A=Z^3,
\qquad
\dot W=3n^{-1/2}BX^\top,
\qquad
\dot U=9U^2\odot R.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus \(d^k f_n(\theta(t))/dt^k|_{t=0}=D_n^kf_n\).  There is no
quadratic-specific closure in (1.1): for the cubic activation it is enough to
retain \(U\) and form \(X=U^3\) exactly.

## 2. Integrating the repeated matrix

Integrating \(W\) in (1.1) before taking the width limit yields

\[
W(t)=W_0+\frac3{\sqrt n}\int_0^tB(s)X(s)^\top\,ds.
\]

Consequently,

\[
Z(t)=n^{-1/2}W_0X(t)
+3\int_0^tB(s)\langle X(s),X(t)\rangle_n\,ds,
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
R(t)=n^{-1/2}W_0^\top B(t)
+3\int_0^tX(s)\langle B(s),B(t)\rangle_n\,ds.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equations (2.1)--(2.2) keep every finite-width low-rank memory term.  Treating
the two appearances of \(W_0\) as independent would incorrectly discard the
transpose responses described next.

## 3. Chronological Gaussian detransposition

At a fixed Taylor order, the width limit has two scalar laws:

- the column law, generated by \(U_0\sim N(0,1)\) and fresh centered Gaussian
  variables \(\xi_k\);
- the row law, generated by \(A_0\sim N(0,1)\) and fresh centered Gaussian
  variables \(\eta_k\).

The base variables are independent of all innovations.  At chronological
order \(k\), exact Wick--Stein peeling gives

\[
E[\eta_k\eta_j]=E[X_kX_j],
\qquad
n^{-1/2}W_0X_k
=\eta_k+\sum_{j<k}E[\partial_{\xi_j}X_k]B_j,
\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
E[\xi_k\xi_j]=E[B_kB_j],
\qquad
n^{-1/2}W_0^\top B_k
=\xi_k+\sum_{j\le k}E[\partial_{\eta_j}B_k]X_j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The strict inequality in (3.1) follows because \(X_k\) only uses transpose
innovations through order \(k-1\); \(B_k\) already contains the current
forward innovation, hence the non-strict inequality in (3.2).  Every
expectation in (3.1)--(3.2) is a finite polynomial Gaussian moment and is
evaluated exactly by Wick recurrence.

## 4. Ordinary Taylor assembler

Write every feature as \(G(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}G_kt^k\).  From (1.1),

\[
U_k=\frac9k[t^{k-1}]U^2R,
\qquad
A_k=\frac1k[t^{k-1}]Z^3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Set \(X_k=[t^k]U^3\) and \(B_k=[t^k]AZ^2\).  Expanding the two Volterra
terms in (2.1)--(2.2) gives

\[
Z_k=\widehat Z_k+
3\sum_{p+q+r+1=k}\frac{B_pE[X_qX_r]}{p+q+1},
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
R_k=\widehat R_k+
3\sum_{p+q+r+1=k}\frac{X_pE[B_qB_r]}{p+q+1},
\tag{4.3}
\]

where the hatted terms are (3.1)--(3.2).  The schedule

\[
(U_k,A_k)\to X_k\to Z_k\to B_k\to R_k
\]

is chronological and therefore never solves an implicit equation.

The output is

\[
F^{(k)}(0)=k!\,E\,[t^k](AZ^3).
\tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. Derivative-normalized assembler

The second route stores \(G^{(k)}(0)\), not \(G_k\).  It independently
assembles every product with binomial rules.  In particular,

\[
U^{(k)}=9\,(U^2R)^{(k-1)},
\qquad
A^{(k)}=(Z^3)^{(k-1)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(p+q+r+1=k\), conversion of a Volterra summand from ordinary
coefficients to derivatives produces the integer weight

\[
3\frac{k!}{p!q!r!(p+q+1)}
=3\binom{k}{r}\binom{p+q}{p}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This route uses (5.1)--(5.2) directly and never divides its final output by
factorials.  Agreement with the ordinary route therefore tests every
factorial, binomial, and Volterra denominator independently.

## 6. Exact low-order audit

At initialization,

\[
E[X_0^2]=E[U_0^6]=15,
\qquad
Z_0\sim N(0,15),
\qquad
E[B_0^2]=E[Z_0^4]=675.
\]

The readout, matrix, and bottom-feature gradient blocks in \(F'(0)\) are

\[
E[Z_0^6]=50\,625,
\]

\[
9E[B_0^2]E[X_0^2]=91\,125,
\]

\[
81E[U_0^4]E[R_0^2]=164\,025.
\]

Their sum is \(305\,775\).  The exact third- and fifth-order values also
match the older, activation-generic compilers before the new orders are
accepted.

Finally, reflecting \(A\mapsto-A\) makes \(f_n\) odd and flips parity on
each application of \(D_n\).  Symmetry of the Gaussian readout proves the
even-order zeros at every finite width, independently of the Gaussian-program
limit.
