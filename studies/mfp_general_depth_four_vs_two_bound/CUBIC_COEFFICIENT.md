# Candidate step-doubling factor and its unresolved bridge

The direct, unconditional definition of the coefficient is (4.1) in
`COMPILER_AND_CONSTANTS.md`.  This note analyzes the sharper candidate
identity

\[
 \kappa_{42,\phi,L}=6\kappa_{21,\phi,L}                 \tag{0.1}
\]

The universal gradient-Euler algebra and the activation recursions below
are correct.  However, an independent audit found that Lemma 1.1 has not
been extended from a fixed query history to the whole changing-Gram
response functor.  Therefore (0.1) is **conditional/open**, is not used in
`THEOREM.md`, and must not be cited as a proved consequence of the
width-first DAG.  No finite-width learning-rate jet is used here.

Throughout this note \(d=\mathbb E\phi'(G)^2>0\).  The constant branch is
treated at the end.

## 1. Finite cylindrical gradient intertwining

The only structural input needed for (0.1) is the following finite lemma.

**Missing Lemma 1.1 (Price-adjoint intertwining).**  Fix finitely many population
parameter directions \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) at initialization.  Expose, for
each reused matrix, the finitely many forward and transpose queries needed
to evaluate all mixed directional derivatives generated by these
directions through order three.  The inverse-free response DAG for this
finite collection is \(C^3\) at its singular initialization covariance and
defines a scalar cylindrical potential \(\Phi(\tau)\).  Its first
directional derivative is

\[
 D\Phi[v]=\langle {\cal G},v\rangle_{\cal P},            \tag{1.1}
\]

where \({\cal P}\) is the constant mean-field parameter metric and

\[
 {\cal G}=(X_L, C_L\otimes X_{L-1},\ldots,
 C_2\otimes X_1, C_1)                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

is the population back-propagated gradient.

*Fixed-history calculation.*  The singular Price lemma in
`COMPILER_AND_CONSTANTS.md`, applied
to this finite enlarged query collection, proves \(C^3\) regularity and
commutation of its mixed derivatives.  It remains to verify (1.1) across a
reused matrix.

For one connector, let \(x\) be a lower query, \(c\) its upper cotangent,
and \(\dot x\) a directional perturbation.  Write the local forward and
transpose response forms as

\[
 \dot y=I(\dot x)+\rho c+\dot Kx+K\dot x,
 \qquad r=J(c)+\sigma x+K^Tc.                            \tag{1.3}
\]

Here \(I(\dot x)\) and \(J(c)\) are the fresh forward and transpose
Gaussian actions; \(K\) denotes the parameter-direction kernel, not a
cotangent Gram.  Put

\[
 q=\mathbb E[x\dot x],\quad k=\mathbb Ec^2,\quad
 \rho=\mathbb E[\partial_\chi\dot x],\quad
 \sigma=\mathbb E[\partial_\xi c].                      \tag{1.4}
\]

Gaussian integration by parts, valid also for the singular joint law by
the regularization proof of the Price lemma, gives

\[
 \mathbb E[cI(\dot x)]=\sigma q,
 \qquad \mathbb E[J(c)\dot x]=\rho k.                   \tag{1.5}
\]

Substitution in (1.3) yields the exact local adjoint identity

\[
 \mathbb E[c\dot y]
 =\mathbb E[r\dot x]+\langle c\otimes x,\dot K\rangle.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

For vector histories, (1.6) holds componentwise and the cross-response
terms pair in the same way.  Applying it successively from the top layer
to the bottom cancels every internal forward/cotangent pairing.  The
remaining boundary and parameter terms are exactly (1.1)--(1.2).

The calculation through (1.6) proves adjointness for one fixed finite
history.  It does **not** yet prove that adjoining mixed derivative queries
commutes with every changing covariance, response expectation, and old
history query.  That response-functor compatibility is the missing part of
the lemma; the following conclusions are conditional on it.

The temporal population DAG is the Euler recursion for (1.2).  Indeed,
after \(s\) steps the connector displacement is

\[
 h\sum_{r<s}C_{\ell,r}\otimes X_{\ell-1,r}.             \tag{1.7}
\]

Its forward and transpose contractions are respectively

\[
 h\sum_{r<s}Q_{\ell-1,rs}C_{\ell,r},\qquad
 h\sum_{r<s}K_{\ell,rs}X_{\ell-1,r},                   \tag{1.8}
\]

and the initialization-matrix actions produce exactly the \(\rho\)- and
\(\sigma\)-response terms.  The readout and first-layer updates agree
identically.  Induction in time and layer therefore identifies the
nodewise Price jets of the temporal DAG with the jets of cylindrical
gradient Euler.

## 2. Universal gradient-Euler algebra

At initialization write

\[
 g={\cal G}(0),\qquad H=D{\cal G}(0),\qquad A=Hg,
 \tag{2.1}
\]

\[
 B=D^2{\cal G}(0)[g,g],\qquad C=HA,                     \tag{2.2}
\]

and define

\[
 {\mathsf H}_L=\|A\|_{\cal P}^2,\qquad
 {\mathsf S}_L=D^3\Phi(0)[g,g,g].                       \tag{2.3}
\]

Lemma 1.1 and symmetry of the cylindrical mixed derivatives give

\[
 D\Phi[C]=D^2\Phi[g,A]={\mathsf H}_L,\qquad
 D\Phi[B]={\mathsf S}_L.                                \tag{2.4}
\]

Let \(\theta_k(h)\) be the displacement after \(k\) Euler steps.  Three
direct differentiations of

\[
 \theta_{s+1}=\theta_s+h{\cal G}(\theta_s)              \tag{2.5}
\]

give

\[
 \theta_k'(0)=kg,\qquad
 \theta_k''(0)=k(k-1)A,                                 \tag{2.6}
\]

\[
 \theta_k'''(0)=k(k-1)(k-2)C
 +\frac{k(k-1)(2k-1)}2B.                                \tag{2.7}
\]

The third-order chain rule, followed by (2.4), yields

\[
 \boxed{
 F_{k,L}^{(3)}(0)=
 2k(k-1)(2k-1){\mathsf H}_L
 +\frac{k(4k^2-3k+1)}2{\mathsf S}_L.}                  \tag{2.8}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed integer \(r\ge1\),

\[
 \frac{F_{2r,L}^{(3)}(0)-8F_{r,L}^{(3)}(0)}6
 =r(2r-1)\left(2{\mathsf H}_L+\frac12{\mathsf S}_L\right).
 \tag{2.9}
\]

The case \(r=1\) is the two-versus-one coefficient

\[
 \kappa_{21,\phi,L}=2{\mathsf H}_L+\frac12{\mathsf S}_L. \tag{2.10}
\]

Taking \(r=2\) would prove (0.1) under Missing Lemma 1.1.  Notice that
(2.9) is only a cubic-coefficient
identity; the time-uniform fifth-order theorem is a separate question.

## 3. Explicit activation-integral recursion

Write, for \(G\sim N(0,1)\),

\[
 g=\phi(G),\quad p=\phi'(G),\quad q=\phi''(G),
 \quad t=\phi'''(G),                                    \tag{3.1}
\]

and define the nine activation moments

\[
 \begin{gathered}
 d=\mathbb Ep^2,\qquad u=\mathbb Ep^4,
 \qquad v=\mathbb E[gq],\qquad m=\mathbb E[gp^2q],\\
 \rho=\mathbb E[pt],\qquad s=\mathbb Eq^2,
 \qquad j=\mathbb E[p^3t],\qquad e=\mathbb E[p^2q^2],
 \qquad \ell=\mathbb E[g^2p^2].                        \tag{3.2}
 \end{gathered}
\]

All are finite under the assumed envelope.  Put

\[
 \Theta_0=1,\qquad \Theta_a=1+d\Theta_{a-1},\qquad
 b_a=d^{L-a},\qquad \pi_a=db_a.                         \tag{3.3}
\]

Initialize \(V_0=M_0=J_0=0\).  For \(1\le a\le L\), set

\[
 V_a=dV_{a-1}+\Theta_{a-1}^2b_au,                       \tag{3.4}
\]

\[
 M_a=vV_{a-1}+\Theta_{a-1}^2b_am+(d+v)M_{a-1},          \tag{3.5}
\]

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_a={}&3\Theta_{a-1}V_{a-1}\rho
 +3\Theta_{a-1}^3b_aj\\
 &+3\Theta_{a-1}M_{a-1}(\rho+s)
 +d(J_{a-1}+3M_{a-1}).                                  \tag{3.6}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{{\mathsf S}_L=J_L+3M_L.}                       \tag{3.7}
\]

For the Hessian-square term, initialize

\[
 \beta_{L+1}=0,\qquad\gamma_{L+1}=1                   \tag{3.8}
\]

and, for \(a=L,L-1,\ldots,1\), set

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta_a={}&b_aV_{a-1}s
 +3\Theta_{a-1}^2b_a^2e+d\beta_{a+1}
 +\gamma_{a+1}^2\ell\\
 &+2\Theta_{a-1}\gamma_{a+1}b_am,                      \tag{3.9}
 \end{aligned}
\]

\[
 \gamma_a=\pi_a+\Theta_{a-1}b_a(\rho+s)
 +\gamma_{a+1}(v+d).                                    \tag{3.10}
\]

Finally,

\[
 \boxed{{\mathsf H}_L=V_L+\beta_1+
 \sum_{a=2}^L(\beta_a+\pi_aV_{a-1}).}                  \tag{3.11}
\]

We justify every recursion.  Along the straight cylindrical line
\(\theta_0+hg\), let \(P_a=X_a'(0)\).  The fresh forward innovation and
the learned-plus-response term give the local representation

\[
 \dot Z_a=\sqrt{V_{a-1}}E_a+\Theta_{a-1}R_ap,\qquad
 P_a=p\dot Z_a,                                         \tag{3.12}
\]

where \(Z_a,E_a,R_a\) are independent,
\(Z_a,E_a\sim N(0,1)\), and \(\mathbb ER_a^2=b_a\).  Thus
\(\mathbb EP_a^2=V_a\), proving (3.4).

Let

\[
 M_a=\mathbb E[X_a(0)X_a''(0)],
 \qquad
 J_a=b_a^{-1}\mathbb E[R_aX_a'''(0)].                  \tag{3.13}
\]

For \(a=L\), \(b_L=1\) and \(R_L=A\).  Differentiating
\(X_a=\phi(Z_a)\), using

\[
 X_a''=q(\dot Z_a)^2+p\ddot Z_a,\qquad
 X_a'''=t(\dot Z_a)^3+3q\dot Z_a\ddot Z_a+pZ_a^{(3)},  \tag{3.14}
\]

and inserting the response derivative supplied by (1.5) gives the five
surviving independent-Gaussian contractions

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{quantity}&\text{contribution at layer }a\\ \hline
 M_a&vV_{a-1}+\Theta_{a-1}^2b_am+(d+v)M_{a-1}\\
 J_a&3\Theta_{a-1}V_{a-1}\rho
 +3\Theta_{a-1}^3b_aj
 +3\Theta_{a-1}M_{a-1}(\rho+s)
 +dJ_{a-1}+3dM_{a-1}.
 \end{array}                                             \tag{3.15}
\]

Terms containing a single centered \(E_a\) or \(R_a\) vanish; the
remaining contractions are exactly the moments in (3.2).  This proves
(3.5)--(3.6).  At the top,

\[
 \left.\frac{d^3}{dh^3}\right|_{0}
 \mathbb E[(A+hX_L(0))X_L(h)]
 =J_L+3M_L,                                              \tag{3.16}
\]

which proves (3.7).

For (3.9)--(3.11), let \(T_a\) be the tangent of the cotangent
\(C_a\), and let the tangent of its incoming carrier have the local form

\[
 \sqrt{\beta_{a+1}}B_a+\gamma_{a+1}g.                  \tag{3.17}
\]

Using (3.12), direct differentiation gives

\[
 T_a=\sqrt{\beta_{a+1}}B_ap+\gamma_{a+1}gp
 +\sqrt{V_{a-1}}R_aE_aq
 +\Theta_{a-1}R_a^2pq.                                 \tag{3.18}
\]

The four primitive variables are independent.  Squaring (3.18) gives
exactly (3.9).  Differentiating the reused-matrix response in the common
forward source gives

\[
 \mathbb E\partial_{Z_a}T_a
 =\gamma_{a+1}(v+d)+\Theta_{a-1}b_a(\rho+s);            \tag{3.19}
\]

the learned transpose term contributes \(\pi_a\), proving (3.10).
Moreover \(\mathbb E[C_aT_a]=\mathbb E[X_aP_a]=0\).  Hence the tangent
norms of the top-weight, first-layer, and matrix-gradient blocks are,
respectively,

\[
 V_L,\qquad\beta_1,\qquad
 \beta_a+\pi_aV_{a-1}\quad(2\le a\le L).              \tag{3.20}
\]

Summing them proves (3.11).

Equations (3.2)--(3.11) are a terminating recursion involving only
one-dimensional Gaussian activation integrals.  In particular,

\[
 \boxed{\text{conditionally:}\quad
 \kappa_{21,\phi,L}=2{\mathsf H}_L+\frac12{\mathsf S}_L,
 \qquad
 \kappa_{42,\phi,L}=12{\mathsf H}_L+3{\mathsf S}_L.}   \tag{3.21}
\]

For \(L=3\), run (3.3)--(3.11) for exactly three forward and three
backward indices; this gives

\[
 \kappa_{42,\phi,3}=12{\mathsf H}_3+3{\mathsf S}_3.    \tag{3.22}
\]

As a check, for \(\phi(x)=x\), the recursion gives

\[
 {\mathsf S}_3=0,\qquad {\mathsf H}_3=40,\qquad
 \kappa_{21,\phi,3}=80,\qquad
 \kappa_{42,\phi,3}=480.                               \tag{3.23}
\]

The activation recursions in this section were independently reconstructed
and pass the \(L=1\) and expanded \(L=2\) checks.  What remains open is
their identification with the direct temporal coefficient, not their
internal algebra.

If \(d=0\), continuity and Gaussian full support give
\(\phi\equiv\pm1\).  Then \(F_{k,L}(h)=kh\), so both coefficients and
both discrepancies are zero.
