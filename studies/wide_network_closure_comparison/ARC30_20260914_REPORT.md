# Thirty-degree arcs: actual wide networks versus observable closures

The requested extension is complete: eight actual GPU network trajectories and
eight closure trajectories through physical time 40, with both full hidden
activation Gram matrices and loss saved at 206 times. Loss remains reasonably
close in absolute error, while hidden geometry agrees less well than in the
earlier five-degree experiment. At the original integration resolution,
increasing closure order improves the second-layer Gram error but worsens the
first-layer error. Thus the earlier improvement across every Gram observable
does not extend to this broader geometry.

Finer order-5 integration improves the Gram comparison, bringing maximum
training-panel errors to 0.01924 and 0.01952. The final quadrature refinement
still exceeds the predeclared numerical diagnostic cutoff for both Grams.
These are internally checked finite empirical comparisons; the experiment does
not establish the accuracy of an ideally integrated or infinite-order closure.

## Input geometry and theoretical scope

The [frozen plan](ARC30_20260914_PLAN.md) interprets 30 degrees as the maximum
absolute deviation of the **actual sampled directions** from each reference
center. The normalized input is \(u=x/\sqrt 2\). There are eight points around
\(e_1=(1,0)\) with target +1 and eight around \(e_2=(0,1)\) with target −1,
all with training weight 1/16. For \(j=0,\ldots,7\),

\[
z_j=\frac{2j-7}{7}\tan(\pi/12),\qquad
U(z)=\left(\frac{1-z^2}{1+z^2},\frac{2z}{1+z^2}\right).
\]

The first cluster is \(U(z_j)\); the second is its quarter-turn. The sampled
angles reach −30° and +30°, and 60° and 120°, respectively. This preserves the
earlier relative stereographic spacing, rather than equal angular spacing.
The previous actual endpoints were approximately ±5.01019°.

The task still has simple labels and is linearly separable by \(u_1-u_2\).
Its minimum signed distance to the unit-normal separator is
\(\sin(15^\circ)=0.2588190451\). Cross-cluster input inner products now range
from −0.8660254 to +0.8660254: training pairs need not be close to orthogonal.

For H4, the reference centers are orthogonal and each labeled cluster must be
within the theorem's very small supported neighborhood of its center. H4 does
not require every pair of training inputs to be approximately orthogonal;
within-cluster pairs are strongly correlated. The established
[H4 scope](../../docs/README.md) is summarized near
the C-H4 established-scope paragraph of the documentation. Its numerical
support radius is much smaller than these resolved arcs. Both the earlier
five-degree case and this thirty-degree case are exploratory uses of the
equations outside that stated time-40 support neighborhood. Neither experiment
enlarges the proved theorem.

## Measurements and fixed comparison

The bias-free two-hidden-layer tanh network, initialization variances, output
scaling, gradient-flow mobilities and simultaneous Heun update are unchanged
from the [original network experiment](WIDE_GPU_20260914_REPORT.md). No parameters
were fitted to observed network trajectories. All matching network initial
Gaussian draw hashes agree with the earlier arc runs before training.

For layer \(\ell=1,2\), the saved observable is

\[
G_\ell(t;a,b)=\frac{1}{n}\,h_\ell(t,u_a)^\top h_\ell(t,u_b).
\]

The closure uses its corresponding population-weighted activation product.
Input weights do not enter individual Gram entries. Each saved array has shape
\((206,2,144,144)\): the 16 training inputs followed by the unchanged 128-point
passive circle. Full cross-panel blocks are retained. The passive circle never
feeds training. Gram accumulation uses float64 even for float32 network runs.

The reference \(\bar G_\ell\) is the arithmetic mean of three networks of width
8192, seeds 11, 29 and 47. On a panel of \(m\) equally weighted inputs, the
reported matrix error is

\[
E_\ell=\max_{t\in\mathcal T}
\left[\frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{a,b=1}^{m}
\bigl(G^{\rm closure}_\ell(t;a,b)-\bar G_\ell(t;a,b)\bigr)^2\right]^{1/2},
\qquad |\mathcal T|=206.
\]

We also compare \(\Delta G_\ell(t)=G_\ell(t)-G_\ell(0)\), subtracting each
method's own initial Gram. This is a change of activation Gram, not the Gram
of neuron displacement. Loss is the unhalved training mean squared residual;
its error is the maximum saved-time absolute difference from the **mean of
the three network losses**, not the loss of their mean predictions. All maxima
below concern the saved grid, not continuous-time bounds.

## Numerical uncertainty and interpretation

The frozen descriptive convention calls Gram errors ≤0.02 close, errors >0.05
material discrepancies, and intermediate values inconclusive. Loss uses 0.05
and 0.10. These are absolute empirical conventions, not accuracy guarantees.
Numerical refinement discrepancies have a separate cutoff of 0.002.

| Control | Largest G/ΔG discrepancy over layers and panels | Maximum loss difference |
|---|---:|---:|
| Network half-step | 0.000005275 | 0.000005960 |
| Network float64 comparison | 0.000000220 | 0.000000045 |
| N3 half-step | 0.000000457 | 0.000001179 |
| N3 Q/P doubling | 0.004463 | 0.005663 |
| N5 Q/P 1024/512 → 2048/1024 | 0.015786 | 0.002761 |
| N5 Q/P 2048/1024 → 4096/2048 | 0.003489 | 0.001568 |
| N5 finest half-step | 0.000000485 | 0.000001205 |

Time and precision effects are small at the checked configurations.
Quadrature remains the unresolved numerical effect. For the finest N=5,
both G panels in both layers and both layer-2 ΔG panels remain unresolved
under their relevant controls; layer-1 ΔG passes on both panels. Finest N=5
loss and secondary prediction pass the declared diagnostics. Baseline N5
must also account for the larger preceding quadrature change; its time
diagnostic is transferred from the finest resolution, not a matching baseline
half-step. N3 uses its own controls. N1 has no dedicated closure control.
No N3 control is used as an N5 bound. Refinement differences are diagnostics,
not rigorous error bounds, and these checks do not identify a convergence rate.

The maximum width-mean discrepancy across G/ΔG panels is 0.005839. Maximum
seed-pair discrepancy is 0.007105 at width8192 and 0.012400 at width2048.
Three seeds give a spread diagnostic, not a confidence interval or proof of
an infinite-width value. The fixed-order improvement cannot be isolated
from numerical and finite-width effects under the frozen attribution gates.
The evidence supports approximate finite-resolution tracking with mixed
order trends, not a failure theorem for the limiting construction.

## Execution, verification and reproduction

All six primary networks used widths 2048/8192, seeds 11/29/47, float32
and h=0.01. The controls were width8192 seed11 h=0.005 and width2048 seed11
float64 h=0.01. Both RTX3090 GPUs were used with one worker per device;
TF32 and reduced-precision accumulation were disabled. PyTorch was
2.9.0+cu130. Closures used float64, h=0.005, the unchanged order-dependent
ridge and the fixed menu in the plan; N3 baseline and finest N5 also used
h=0.0025 controls. NumPy was 1.26.4. No scientific runs failed, were retried,
or were added after outcomes were observed.

Summed GPU-worker time was 224.64 seconds and closure-worker time 351.84
seconds. Both campaigns completed within the frozen resource limits; exact
resource totals, source hashes, output hashes and commands are in the
[final verification](../../data/generated/wide_network_closure_comparison/ARC30_20260914_v1/final_verification.json)
and [campaign record](../../data/generated/wide_network_closure_comparison/ARC30_20260914_v1/arc30_campaign.json).
All 262 earlier generated files and 18 frozen earlier study sources retain
their bytes. The README is the existing administrative record; sources remain
flat in this study and generated products stay in its assigned data directory.

All 16 trajectories pass the recorded shape, finite-value, symmetry, bound,
PSD-checkpoint, trace/RMS, loss/prediction, input/time, initialization and
provenance checks. An independent checker computed the principal 32 Gram
