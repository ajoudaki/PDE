## Question, scope and interpretation

H1: the computed trajectories and closure-vs-network Gram discrepancies become
approximately stationary over consecutive substantial late-time windows.
H0: at least one observable continues to drift at the declared resolution and
horizon. Numerical or resource failure is inconclusive about eventual settling.
This is finite empirical evidence, not a proof of convergence to an equilibrium,
all-time approximation, or a unique hidden representation. Existing H4 support
and finite-horizon claims do not cover this broader law at arbitrary long times.

Preserve the exact two-hidden-layer bias-free tanh model, working normalized
inputs u=x/sqrt(2), binary labels, equal training weights, initialization,

## Metrics and stopping rule

Use the existing matrix RMS ||A||F/m on each panel (m=16 or128).
For every individual run, inspect its loss and both Grams on both panels.
For each closure inspect G and DeltaG error curves against both width2048 and
width8192 three-seed means, where DeltaG subtracts each method's own t0 Gram.
Keep the frozen initial network Gram baseline and circle prediction diagnostics.

At each common stage T>=160, test BOTH windows [T/2,3T/4] and[3T/4,T].
All conditions must hold on both windows:

- Every run: loss range <= max(1e-4, .05 * mean window loss).
- Every run/layer/panel: twice the maximum matrix RMS distance to its middle
  saved matrix <= .005. By the triangle inequality this upper-bounds every
  pairwise saved-matrix distance in that window; it cannot hide joint drift
  merely because prediction errors are constant.
- Every closure/width/layer/panel for G andDeltaG: RMS-error range <=.002.

If all pass, stop with "apparent settling at these tolerances"; no asymptotic
claim. If any fail, proceed to the next declared doubling stage. Stop at10240
or a resource/numerical gate even if curves remain unsettled. Do not loosen
thresholds, omit an inconvenient run, or invent a follow-up branch after results.
Report each failed condition and the observed variations. Also report fractional
loss changes, endpoint Gram changes, endpoint errors and times, so absolute
smallness does not conceal continued relative improvement at very small loss.
