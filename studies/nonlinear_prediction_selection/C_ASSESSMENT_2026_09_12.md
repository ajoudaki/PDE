## A gives better analytic control, not a small computational state

C.4.9's important contribution for C is its integrated-control source tube.
Within its exact comparison to the complete reference history, absolute source
mass controls full Gaussian query tails and reached-state regularity without
constants growing merely with elapsed physical time. The derivative estimates
bound query variation by the instantaneous control mass. These are useful
interfaces for deciding time resolution and propagating a numerical discrepancy.

There are two qualifications. The source tube has a small integrated deviation
budget from the reference, not unrestricted long-time validity. And the proof
retains every old source and its named response coefficients. Bounded total
coefficients and Gaussian tails do not by themselves bound the number of modes,
the dimension of a joint integration problem, or the error from deleting history.
A's one-reference Osgood comparison can propagate a discrepancy only after a
candidate construction has produced it in the required sense. Applying it to
new numerical states remains a mathematical obligation.

This prevents both misleading conclusions: A neither solves C by writing a
two-by-two projected equation nor shows C impossible because the state is large.
The anchor Gram inverse is two-by-two; the state used to evaluate it still
contains the full first rows, trained operator increment, readout and reused
Gaussian action/adjoint.

## Existing compression results give components with strict boundaries

Finite-controls §13 gives exact trained-memory identities and a small temporal
cover of supplied query curves. It does not show how the approximating system
generates those curves from the retained information. The Gaussian Q5/G package
does contain genuine adaptive query constructions; it would be inaccurate to
dismiss the whole package as frozen-path work. However, those constructions
retain width-dependent vectors, trained matrices and large transcripts. Their
model is three-hidden-layer arctan in feature time. Q7 compares two identically
clipped systems; growing the cap does not identify the uncut population flow.

A now supplies stronger reached-query tails and regularity for its particular
tanh histories. That makes some formerly conditional estimates more plausible
components of C, after the model and algorithm hypotheses are checked. It does
not convert the older algorithms into an inexpensive population solver. The
leading constructive question remains whether the approximation's own Gaussian
and response errors can be controlled at useful finite cost.

## The smallest complete C target

The new task should produce one end-to-end solver for the original C.4.7 GF
through 40, from canonical initialization, on an explicitly represented family
that includes nonatomic correlated laws. The input family needs a checkable
admission condition and must remain fixed while accuracy is refined. Law
quadrature is legitimate, with its error composed with the established law
continuity; solving a dense network on that quadrature law is not a new route.

Its theorem must identify the limit of its actual numerical refinements with
that population flow and control whole-circle prediction uniformly over the
time interval. It must also cover the hidden and response observations needed
to identify the evolution and justify continuation from a saved finite state.
Full ambient operator-norm convergence is not imposed. Costs must include all
retained history, field coordinates, Gaussian integration, covariance solves,
conditioning, time steps and numerical precision.

A finite covariance matrix or a sampled ensemble of a derived population process
is not automatically a disguised network. It can qualify if it has a derived
causal law, controlled approximation error and counted cost, without trainable
all-to-all raw neural connections. Conversely, writing one density with one
coordinate per source can conceal a prohibitive numerical problem. The prompt
leaves representation choice open while fixing these information constraints.

For practical relevance, evaluate a complete bound on a meaningful nonlinear
learned trajectory at a declared useful accuracy and feasible resource budget.
It is not necessary to resolve the effect of every arbitrarily tiny law
perturbation. It is necessary to report whether the practical example resolves
such an effect, and not to let an accuracy-dependent shrinking family make
returning the reference prediction a purported general solver.

This remains an ambitious research target. Effective admission and resource
bounds may be difficult because current constants can be highly conservative.
A convergence theorem with only astronomical resources would be valuable
partial progress, but would not meet C's intended usable-computation goal.

## Why the first theorem should not absorb A's entire learning horizon

A's slow equation starts at the fitted reference endpoint and approximates
original-mixture GF only in its proved small-contamination limit, away from
slow time zero. A solver for that equation alone would not approximate the
original flow to arbitrary accuracy at fixed positive contamination.

A certified acceleration would additionally require computing the reference
endpoint and retained Gaussian history, controlling the initial layer and
finite-contamination bias, and justifying its numerical continuation. A gives
interfaces for this analysis, but no evaluated practical bias bound. Adding
all of these requirements to C's already new representation problem would
couple two difficult achievements without necessity.

The first C result therefore retains the existing nonatomic physical-time-40
scope. A's longer learning episode is a valuable next benchmark or extension;
matching the learning scales of B and later milestones remains the roadmap's
explicit common-family obligation. Completing this first C package alone does
not promise computation at every subsequent learning scale.

## Broader exploration and calibration

Keep the solver's equations usable beyond the certified family wherever they
remain defined. This preserves the user's second purpose: investigating whether
theorems underestimate the actual range of nonlinear population learning.
Such investigation needs independent refinement of numerical error sources
and finite-network comparisons, with failed refinements and conditioning failures
reported. Results outside the certified scope remain exploratory. Preparation
of that reusable interface and protocol belongs to C; training campaigns require
separate authorization.

My confidence has increased in the available stability tools, not in an already
identified cheap closure. C is a complementary research direction with a
substantial representation bottleneck. The strategically useful early decision
is to exhibit finite causal variables and a bound for the error they actually
generate before investing in a large solver. A new GF-to-GD branch is not
warranted simply because this representation problem is hard; a pivot would
need a concrete advantage on that same problem.
