## 1. Actual evolving-kernel comparison

The kernel identity (2.1) correctly includes the block mobilities inside the
tangent kernel and the loss factor $2/m$ outside it. At zero readout every
hidden contribution vanishes, giving exactly the last-feature Gram in (2.2).
This does not freeze the subsequent hidden motion, and the candidate does not
make that substitution.

For Section 3 write $a=2\lambda/m$ and $b=2\mu/m$. The homogeneous evolution
generated by $-(2/m)K(t)$ contracts by $e^{-a(t-u)}$, since symmetry and the
uniform lower eigenvalue bound give

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\|v(t)\|_2^2
 \le-2a\|v(t)\|_2^2.
\]

The residual difference $e=r-s$ satisfies the displayed forced equation with
the correct sign and zero initial condition. Its time-integrated bound is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^\infty\|e(t)\|_2\,dt
 &\le\frac{2\delta_{\mathrm{train}}}{m}
     \int_0^\infty\|s(u)\|_2
           \int_u^\infty e^{-a(t-u)}\,dt\,du\\
 &\le\frac{2\delta_{\mathrm{train}}}{m}
       \frac{m}{2\lambda}
       \frac{m\|y\|_2}{2\mu}
 =\frac{m\delta_{\mathrm{train}}\|y\|_2}{2\lambda\mu}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Multiplication by the prediction factor $2/m$ cancels the remaining $m$.
Adding the cross-kernel contribution gives precisely

\[
 \sup_{t,x}|f(t,x)-g(t,x)|
 \le\frac{\|y\|_2}{\mu}
       \left(\delta_{\mathrm{cross}}
             +\frac{B\delta_{\mathrm{train}}}{\lambda}\right).
\]

There is no singularity when $\lambda=\mu$, because the proof integrates the
convolution directly rather than dividing by $\lambda-\mu$. If $y=0$, the
bound is zero, consistently with the equilibrium trajectories. If both kernel
errors vanish, the output difference vanishes. Positivity of both gaps is
essential, and is explicitly stated. The bound is asymmetric only because the
proof contracts the $K$ equation and estimates the residual of the $G$
equation; this is valid, not a missing symmetric factor.

The endpoint assertion also follows: uniformly in $x$,

\[
 |\partial_t f(t,x)|
 \le\frac{2B\|y\|_2}{m}e^{-2\lambda t/m},\qquad
 |\partial_t g(t,x)|
 \le\frac{2(B+\delta_{\mathrm{cross}})\|y\|_2}{m}e^{-2\mu t/m}.
\]

Both upper bounds have finite integrals, hence make the prediction functions
uniformly Cauchy in time. The candidate correctly requires the evolving gaps
and both kernel errors uniformly in time and over the required cross-inputs.
It does not infer them from the initialized Gram gap or initialized
concentration. Supplying a reference kernel externally is expressly not
claimed to give an autonomous surrogate.

## 2. Tightness and the tolerance scale

The candidate distinguishes two assumptions correctly. Boundedness in
probability of the scalar trajectory norms $\|Z_n\|$ suffices for (4.1) when
$A_n\to\infty$. Compact containment of the random **functions** is stronger
and is what gives a finite spatial-and-temporal net at each fixed normalized
tolerance. A norm bound alone would not give that compactness in an
infinite-dimensional function space; Section 4 does not make that inference.

The fixed-confidence covering argument is valid and gives a covering
cardinality independent of $n$, with the dimension and confidence allowed to
affect the cardinality. It does not provide a way to compute or store the
trajectory centers, and the candidate says this explicitly. Quantitative
failure rates are also correctly withheld: boundedness in probability does
not imply probability $1-n^{-10}$ at a prescribed logarithmic threshold.

## 3. Fluctuation truncation and finite precision

The degree choice in Section 5 is conservative and valid. Put

\[
 s_n=\log\frac{2C_d}{1-e^{-\rho_d}}
           +(b+c)\log\log(en).
\]

If $s_n>0$, then $\rho_dp_n\ge s_n$, and the geometric tail in (5.2) is
at most $\tfrac12e^{-\rho_d}(\log(en))^{-b}$, hence no larger than the
claimed half-tolerance. If $s_n\le0$, then $p_n=0$ and the same bound follows
from $e^{s_n}\le1$. Thus the maximum with zero in the degree definition does
not leave an uncovered small-$n$ case. For $n\ge1$,
$\log\log(en)\ge0$ as required by the subsequent elementary inequality.

The polynomial restriction kernel and dimension formula (5.3) are correct.
The proof uses both signs of the last sphere coordinate over an open ball;
this is why a vanishing remainder has both coefficient polynomials zero.
The separate $d=1$ statement correctly avoids an inappropriate large-degree
dimension asymptotic for a two-point sphere.

The inequality

\[
 (1+u)^q\le q^qe^{1-q}e^u\quad(q\ge1,\ u\ge0)
\]

has its maximum ratio at $u=q-1$ and includes $q=1$. Hence the fixed-$d$
rewriting as $O_d(\log(en))$ is mathematically sound. The rapidly growing
constant and lack of a joint dimension-width claim are disclosed.

For the orthonormal degree-at-most-$p_n$ basis, the function
$\sum_jY_j(x)^2$ is constant because rotation preserves that space and its
inner product, and rotations act transitively on the sphere. Its integral is
$N$, so the constant is $N$. If every coefficient error is bounded by
$\tau/(2N)$, where $\tau=(\log(en))^{-b}$, the error vector has Euclidean norm
at most $\tau/(2\sqrt N)$. Its evaluation pairing with
$(Y_1(x),\ldots,Y_N(x))$ is therefore at most $\tau/2$, as claimed.

For a more explicit precision count, let
$R_n=C_d(\log(en))^c/(1-e^{-\rho_d})$. A coefficient in $[-R_n,R_n]$ can
be rounded with error at most $\tau/(2N)$ using no more than

\[
 \left\lceil\log_2\left(2+\frac{4R_nN}{\tau}\right)\right\rceil
\]

bits; a grid with adjacent spacing at most $\tau/(2N)$ suffices. Since
$N=O((1+\log\log(en))^{d-1})$, this is
$O_{d,C_d,\rho_d,b,c}(1+\log\log(en))$ bits per coefficient. Multiplying by
$N$ yields the claimed upper bit order. The special case $b=c=0$ can have
bounded degree and fewer bits; the candidate's bound remains a valid, loose
upper bound. This accounts only for the instantaneous quantized fluctuation
vector. Section 5 explicitly excludes a claim that this vector has a closed
autonomous evolution or that its basis and deterministic reference can be
evaluated with that same total memory.

## 4. Small-ball and deterministic-family entropy claims

The finite-storage statements in Section 6 are correct for deterministic
rules with all instance-specific information counted. One codeword cannot
approximate two targets separated by more than twice the error. For a random
target, the successful event lies in at most $2^{B_n}$ balls, giving exactly
$2^{B_n}q_n\ge1-\delta_n$.

For (6.1), orthogonal projection of the snapshot contracts the supremum norm
to the Euclidean coefficient norm, so a successful trajectory approximation
implies a Gaussian coefficient vector in a ball of radius $A_n$. Its density
is bounded by $(2\pi)^{-D/2}\prod_j\sigma_j^{-1}$, while that ball has volume
$\pi^{D/2}A_n^D/\Gamma(D/2+1)$. Their product gives the exact displayed
constant, including the factor $2^{D/2}$. For $D=1$ it reduces to
$\sqrt{2/\pi}\,A_n/\sigma_1$, which is the interval-length times density
bound. The cap by one is necessary and correctly present. A zero variance
must be removed from the selected coordinates, which is ensured by the
stated strict positivity of all $\sigma_j$.

This calculation assumes exactly independent Gaussian coefficients; no
central limit theorem for the actual network is silently used. The candidate
correctly notes that a fixed-dimensional limit theorem does not establish
the bound for a growing number of modes. Worst-case Gaussian support is not
used as a probability lower bound.

For the deterministic-family covering argument, if its parameter domain lies
in $[-R,R]^p$, divide that cube into cells of side at most
$\varepsilon/(2C\sqrt p)$ and choose one parameter representative from each
nonempty cell. The trajectory diameter of a cell is at most
$\varepsilon/2$ by (6.2). This gives at most
$(1+C'/\varepsilon)^p$ centers with $C'$ depending on $R,C,p$. Combining
this cover with a common compact fluctuation set, using half the target
tolerance for each contribution, gives (6.3). Uniform tightness over the
parameter domain is needed for that common compact set and is explicitly
assumed. The resulting statement concerns covering cardinality, not cheap
decoding or an admissible codebook of trajectories.

## 5. Remaining qualifications and minor precision notes

No material mathematical correction was found. The following small
clarifications would make the conditional statements more literal; they do
not alter their content or turn them into results for the actual network.

- The degree definition uses $\log C_d$, so it should take $C_d>0$. If
  $C_d=0$, all fluctuation modes vanish and the zero representation handles
  that separate case immediately.
- The $O_d$ notation in the final per-coefficient and total-bit statements of
  Section 5 should retain the already fixed dependencies on
  $C_d,\rho_d,b,c$, as in the explicit count above. The earlier degree-count
  statement already lists them.
- The growing-horizon comparison in Section 7 uses $A\ge0$ and $\gamma>0$.
  For the stated $T=O(\log n)$ conclusion, $A$ and $\gamma$ must be independent
  of $n$, with $A>0$ for the nontrivial case. The claim is the horizon needed
  to make that particular exponential upper bound root-width small; faster
  actual convergence could need less time.
- In the scalar self-variability counterexample, the displayed event uses
  $C>0$. Constants $C\le0$ fail immediately whenever the numerator is
  positive. Thus the advertised lack of a finite pathwise comparison remains
  correct, including that edge case.
- The entire activation $e^{z^2}$ is outside the task's bounded-derivative
  class. The candidate already identifies it only as an illustration that
  analyticity by itself is insufficient, not as a counterexample within the
  requested model. That distinction should remain in any synthesis.

The unresolved scientific premises are exactly the ones the candidate lists:
whole-sphere and all-time fluctuation control, quantitative confidence and
dimension dependence, an autonomous deterministic-limit construction, and
actual learned-trajectory richness for any impossibility theorem. This check
validates the conditional calculations; it does not supply those premises.
