Yes. We should define \(W^{(3)}\) from the beginning to mean the rescaled output weight used by the proof. There is then no raw output-weight symbol and no bar notation anywhere.

The factor \(1/n\) in the output and the initialization and update of \(W^{(3)}\) are adjusted accordingly.

## Complete finite-width setup

For width \(n\), the evolving parameters are

\[
z_k^{(1)}\in\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
W_k^{(2)}\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},
\qquad
W_k^{(3)}\in\mathbb R^n.
\]

Here \(W_k^{(3)}\) always denotes the rescaled output weight. It is the only output-weight variable used anywhere below.

The forward pass is

\[
h_k^{(1)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_k^{(1)}\right),
\]

\[
z_k^{(2)}
=
W_k^{(2)}h_k^{(1)},
\]

\[
h_k^{(2)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_k^{(2)}\right),
\]

\[
f_{n,k}
=
\frac1n
\bigl(W_k^{(3)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(2)},
\]

where

\[
\phi(s)=\arctan s
\]

acts coordinatewise.

The target is \(1\), so

\[
r_{n,k}=f_{n,k}-1,
\qquad
L_{n,k}=r_{n,k}^2.
\]

The initialization is

\[
z_{0,i}^{(1)}
\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1),
\]

\[
W_{0,ji}^{(2)}
\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
N\left(0,\frac1n\right),
\]

\[
W_{0,j}^{(3)}
\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
N\left(0,\frac1{n^2}\right),
\]

with all initial quantities independent.

Thus \(W_0^{(3)}\) is already written in its rescaled coordinates. Its coordinates vanish as \(n\to\infty\), so the population oracle sets

\[
W_0^{(3)}=0.
\]

The difference between the small random finite initialization and zero is handled later by the stability comparison.

Define the residual-free backpropagation derivatives by

\[
\delta_k^{(\ell)}
:=
n\frac{\partial f_{n,k}}
        {\partial z_k^{(\ell)}}.
\]

For the second hidden layer,

\[
\delta_k^{(2)}
=
W_k^{(3)}
\odot
\phi'\!\left(z_k^{(2)}\right).
\]

Backpropagating through \(W_k^{(2)}\) and then through the first activation gives

\[
\delta_k^{(1)}
=
\phi'\!\left(z_k^{(1)}\right)
\odot
\bigl(W_k^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_k^{(2)}.
\]

The corresponding loss derivatives are

\[
\frac{\partial L_{n,k}}
     {\partial z_k^{(\ell)}}
=
\frac{2r_{n,k}}{n}\delta_k^{(\ell)}.
\]

For \(\phi(s)=\arctan s\), define

\[
F(s)=s+\frac{s^3}{3},
\qquad
x_k^{(1)}
=
F\!\left(z_k^{(1)}\right).
\]

Since

\[
F'(s)=1+s^2=\frac1{\phi'(s)},
\]

the first-layer activation derivative cancels in the evolution of \(x_k^{(1)}\).

With Euler step \(\Delta\), the exact induced updates in the rescaled coordinates are

\[
x_{k+1}^{(1)}
=
x_k^{(1)}
-
2\Delta r_{n,k}
\bigl(W_k^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)},
\]

\[
W_{k+1}^{(2)}
=
W_k^{(2)}
-
\frac{2\Delta r_{n,k}}{n}
\delta_k^{(2)}
\bigl(h_k^{(1)}\bigr)^\top,
\]

\[
W_{k+1}^{(3)}
=
W_k^{(3)}
-
2\Delta r_{n,k}h_k^{(2)}.
\]

The last equation is already the update for the rescaled output weights \(W_k^{(3)}\). No conversion to or from another output-weight variable is needed.

The population oracle is analyzed for fixed \(\Delta\). For a fixed time horizon \(T\),

\[
N=\left\lceil\frac T\Delta\right\rceil
\]

is fixed while \(n\to\infty\). Therefore only finitely many forward and transpose calls must be controlled.

## The first forward call

At initialization,

\[
h_0^{(1)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_0^{(1)}\right),
\]

and

\[
z_0^{(2)}
=
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}.
\]

Conditional on \(h_0^{(1)}\),

\[
z_{0,j}^{(2)}
=
\sum_{i=1}^n
W_{0,ji}^{(2)}h_{0,i}^{(1)}
\]

is Gaussian with variance

\[
\operatorname{Var}
\left(
z_{0,j}^{(2)}
\mid h_0^{(1)}
\right)
=
\frac1n
\sum_{i=1}^n
\left(h_{0,i}^{(1)}\right)^2
=
\frac{\|h_0^{(1)}\|_2^2}{n}.
\]

Different second-layer coordinates \(j\) use different rows of \(W_0^{(2)}\), so they are conditionally independent.

The initial empirical law gives

\[
\frac{\|h_0^{(1)}\|_2^2}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
\left(H_0^{(1)}\right)^2
\right].
\]

Therefore the coordinates of \(z_0^{(2)}\) behave empirically like

\[
Z_0^{(2)}
\sim
N\left(
0,
\mathbb E\left[
\left(H_0^{(1)}\right)^2
\right]
\right).
\]

The second-layer activation is

\[
h_0^{(2)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_0^{(2)}\right).
\]

This is a coordinatewise function and introduces no new matrix randomness.

In the oracle,

\[
W_0^{(3)}=0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
f_0=0,
\qquad
r_0=-1,
\qquad
\delta_0^{(2)}=0.
\]

The first update gives

\[
x_1^{(1)}=x_0^{(1)},
\]

\[
W_1^{(2)}=W_0^{(2)},
\]

and

\[
W_1^{(3)}
=
2\Delta h_0^{(2)}
=
2\Delta\phi\!\left(z_0^{(2)}\right).
\]

It follows that

\[
h_1^{(1)}=h_0^{(1)},
\qquad
z_1^{(2)}=z_0^{(2)},
\qquad
h_1^{(2)}=h_0^{(2)}.
\]

The next second-layer backpropagation derivative is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_1^{(2)}
&=
W_1^{(3)}
\odot
\phi'\!\left(z_1^{(2)}\right)\\
&=
2\Delta\,
\phi\!\left(z_0^{(2)}\right)
\odot
\phi'\!\left(z_0^{(2)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\delta_1^{(2)}
=
2\Delta\,
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_0^{(2)}}
\frac12
\left\|
\phi\!\left(z_0^{(2)}\right)
\right\|_2^2.
\]

The population residual used by the oracle at this step is

\[
r_1
=
2\Delta\,
\mathbb E\left[
\phi\!\left(Z_0^{(2)}\right)^2
\right]
-1.
\]

## The first transpose call

The first nontrivial transpose operation is

\[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

This is not multiplication by an independent Gaussian matrix because

\[
\delta_1^{(2)}
\]

depends on

\[
z_0^{(2)}
=
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}.
\]

For any nonzero vector \(b\), define the ordinary orthogonal projection

\[
P_{b^\perp}
=
I-\frac{bb^\top}{b^\top b}.
\]

For row \(j\) of \(W_0^{(2)}\), the previous forward call supplies the constraint

\[
W_{0,j:}^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}
=
z_{0,j}^{(2)}.
\]

Conditioning the Gaussian row on this equation gives

\[
\left(W_{0,j:}^{(2)}\right)^\top
\overset d=
\frac{
z_{0,j}^{(2)}
}{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}
h_0^{(1)}
+
\widetilde W_{0,j:}^{(2)\top},
\]

where, conditionally,

\[
\widetilde W_{0,j:}^{(2)\top}
\sim
N\left(
0,
\frac1nP_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
\right).
\]

Multiplying row \(j\) by \(\delta_{1,j}^{(2)}\) and summing gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
\overset d={}&
\frac{
\bigl(z_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
}{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}
h_0^{(1)}
\\
&+
\frac{\|\delta_1^{(2)}\|_2}{\sqrt n}
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
\gamma^{(1)},
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\gamma^{(1)}\sim N(0,I_n)
\]

is independent of the previously constructed vectors.

The first term is the response forced by the old forward call:

\[
\frac{
\bigl(z_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
}{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}
h_0^{(1)}.
\]

The second term is the genuinely new Gaussian part:

\[
\frac{\|\delta_1^{(2)}\|_2}{\sqrt n}
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
\gamma^{(1)}.
\]

The response is necessary because of the exact transpose identity

\[
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
=
\bigl(z_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

The new Gaussian term is perpendicular to \(h_0^{(1)}\), so it contributes zero to the left-hand side. The response term supplies the required overlap exactly.

The induction already knows

\[
\frac{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
\left(H_0^{(1)}\right)^2
\right],
\]

\[
\frac{
\bigl(z_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
Z_0^{(2)}\delta_1^{(2)}
\right],
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_1^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
\left(\delta_1^{(2)}\right)^2
\right].
\]

Therefore the corresponding population adjoint action is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^*
\delta_1^{(2)}
={}&
\frac{
\mathbb E\left[
Z_0^{(2)}\delta_1^{(2)}
\right]
}{
\mathbb E\left[
\left(H_0^{(1)}\right)^2
\right]
}
H_0^{(1)}
\\
&+
\sqrt{
\mathbb E\left[
\left(\delta_1^{(2)}\right)^2
\right]
}
\,\Gamma^{(1)},
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\Gamma^{(1)}\sim N(0,1)
\]

is independent of the previous first-layer population coordinates.

The finite coordinates of

\[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
\]

are not claimed to be iid. The conclusion is that their joint empirical averages with all previous first-layer coordinates approach the corresponding population expectations.

## The next forward call

The first-layer transformed coordinate now moves according to

\[
x_2^{(1)}
=
x_1^{(1)}
-
2\Delta r_1
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

Then

\[
z_2^{(1)}
=
F^{-1}\!\left(x_2^{(1)}\right),
\]

and

\[
h_2^{(1)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_2^{(1)}\right).
\]

Each coordinate of \(h_2^{(1)}\) is a deterministic scalar function of previously controlled first-layer coordinates. Therefore its joint empirical behavior follows from the existing induction hypothesis.

The next difficult operation is

\[
W_0^{(2)}h_2^{(1)}.
\]

It is not an independent Gaussian multiplication because \(h_2^{(1)}\) depends on

\[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

At this point the matrix history contains both

\[
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}
=
z_0^{(2)}
\]

and

\[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

Conditioning \(W_0^{(2)}\) simultaneously on both observations gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_0^{(2)}
\overset d={}&
\frac{
z_0^{(2)}
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top
}{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}
\\
&+
\frac{
\delta_1^{(2)}
\left[
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
\right]^\top
}{
\bigl(\delta_1^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
}
\\
&+
P_{(\delta_1^{(2)})^\perp}
\widetilde W_0^{(2)}
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(\widetilde W_0^{(2)}\) is a fresh independent Gaussian matrix with entries \(N(0,1/n)\).

The first term retains

\[
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}
=
z_0^{(2)}.
\]

The second retains

\[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}.
\]

The final term is invisible to both previously inspected matrix directions.

Multiplying by \(h_2^{(1)}\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
W_0^{(2)}h_2^{(1)}
\overset d={}&
\frac{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_2^{(1)}
}{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_0^{(1)}
}
z_0^{(2)}
\\
&+
\frac{
\left[
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
\right]^\top
h_2^{(1)}
}{
\bigl(\delta_1^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
}
\delta_1^{(2)}
\\
&+
\frac{
\left\|
P_{(h_0^{(1)})^\perp}
h_2^{(1)}
\right\|_2
}{
\sqrt n
}
P_{(\delta_1^{(2)})^\perp}
\gamma^{(2)},
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\gamma^{(2)}\sim N(0,I_n)
\]

is fresh and independent of the previous history.

The first response term comes from the component of \(h_2^{(1)}\) parallel to \(h_0^{(1)}\). Since

\[
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}
=
z_0^{(2)},
\]

that component must be mapped to the corresponding multiple of \(z_0^{(2)}\).

The second response term is forced by

\[
\bigl(\delta_1^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
W_0^{(2)}h_2^{(1)}
=
\left[
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_1^{(2)}
\right]^\top
h_2^{(1)}.
\]

The first response term supplies part of the required overlap. The second response term supplies the remainder.

The fresh Gaussian term is perpendicular to \(\delta_1^{(2)}\), so it does not disturb this exact constraint.

Every coefficient in this formula is an empirical contraction of previously controlled vectors. For example,

\[
\frac{
\bigl(h_0^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_2^{(1)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
H_0^{(1)}H_2^{(1)}
\right],
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_1^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
\left(\delta_1^{(2)}\right)^2
\right].
\]

Therefore all response coefficients and Gaussian variances converge to their population values.

## The exact induction hypothesis

The first-layer collection now contains quantities such as

\[
z_0^{(1)},
\qquad
h_0^{(1)},
\qquad
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top\delta_1^{(2)},
\qquad
x_2^{(1)},
\qquad
h_2^{(1)}.
\]

For every fixed polynomially bounded function \(\psi\), the induction asserts

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\psi\Bigl(
&z_{0,i}^{(1)},
h_{0,i}^{(1)},
\left[
(W_0^{(2)})^\top\delta_1^{(2)}
\right]_i,
x_{2,i}^{(1)},
h_{2,i}^{(1)},
\ldots
\Bigr)
\\
\longrightarrow\;
\mathbb E\,\psi\Bigl(
&Z_0^{(1)},
H_0^{(1)},
(W_0^{(2)})^*\delta_1^{(2)},
X_2^{(1)},
H_2^{(1)},
\ldots
\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The second-layer collection contains quantities such as

\[
z_0^{(2)},
\qquad
h_0^{(2)},
\qquad
W_1^{(3)},
\qquad
\delta_1^{(2)},
\qquad
W_0^{(2)}h_2^{(1)}.
\]

Its empirical statement is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n
\psi\Bigl(
&z_{0,j}^{(2)},
h_{0,j}^{(2)},
W_{1,j}^{(3)},
\delta_{1,j}^{(2)},
\left[
W_0^{(2)}h_2^{(1)}
\right]_j,
\ldots
\Bigr)
\\
\longrightarrow\;
\mathbb E\,\psi\Bigl(
&Z_0^{(2)},
H_0^{(2)},
W_1^{(3)},
\delta_1^{(2)},
W_0^{(2)}H_2^{(1)},
\ldots
\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The first-layer index \(i\) and second-layer index \(j\) label different neuron populations. There is no artificial coordinatewise pairing between them.

Polynomially bounded means that, for some fixed constants \(C\) and \(q\),

\[
|\psi(s)|
\le
C\left(1+\|s\|_2^q\right).
\]

Taking

\[
\psi(s)=s^2
\]

gives empirical second-moment convergence.

Taking

\[
\psi(s,t)=st
\]

gives contractions such as

\[
\frac{
\bigl(h_r^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(1)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
H_r^{(1)}H_k^{(1)}
\right],
\]

\[
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_r^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
\delta_r^{(2)}\delta_k^{(2)}
\right],
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\bigl(W_k^{(3)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(2)}
}{n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E\left[
W_k^{(3)}H_k^{(2)}
\right].
\]

The last convergence is exactly the convergence of the oracle prediction.

Higher powers provide the moment control needed to prevent a small number of unusually large coordinates from dominating these averages.

The induction also maintains stable linear relations among the vectors previously supplied to \(W_0^{(2)}\) and \((W_0^{(2)})^\top\).

This is necessary because simultaneous Gaussian conditioning produces finite Gram matrices with entries such as

\[
\frac{
\bigl(h_r^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_s^{(1)}
}{n}
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_r^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_s^{(2)}
}{n}.
\]

If a limiting Gram matrix is singular, entrywise convergence alone does not guarantee convergence of its inverse or pseudoinverse. The proof must distinguish a genuinely new direction from a direction that is already represented by previous ones.

For example,

\[
h_1^{(1)}=h_0^{(1)},
\]

so

\[
W_0^{(2)}h_1^{(1)}
=
W_0^{(2)}h_0^{(1)}
=
z_0^{(2)}
\]

exactly. This repeated source direction creates no fresh Gaussian component.

More generally, the induction proves:

- a genuinely new source direction has a positive limiting distance from the old source span and therefore a positive Gaussian innovation variance;
- a direction with zero limiting distance is eventually an exact combination of the retained core directions;
- the relevant Gram ranks and pseudoinverses therefore stabilize.

Thus the induction maintains both joint empirical-moment convergence and stable linear relations.

Coordinatewise operations are easy to add. For example,

\[
h_k^{(\ell)}
=
\phi\!\left(z_k^{(\ell)}\right),
\]

\[
W_{k+1}^{(3)}
=
W_k^{(3)}
-
2\Delta r_kh_k^{(2)},
\]

and

\[
\delta_k^{(2)}
=
W_k^{(3)}
\odot
\phi'\!\left(z_k^{(2)}\right)
\]

are coordinatewise functions of already controlled second-layer vectors, with deterministic oracle coefficient \(r_k\). They introduce no new matrix randomness.

The difficult operation is a new call to

\[
W_0^{(2)}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top.
\]

Conditioning on every previous forward and transpose call gives a finite response made from old network vectors plus Gaussian randomness projected away from all previously inspected directions.

To prove the enlarged empirical-average property, the new error is divided into:

\[
\text{fresh Gaussian empirical average}
-
\text{its conditional expectation},
\]

\[
\text{conditional expectation using finite-width contractions}
-
\text{the same expression using population contractions},
\]

and

\[
\text{empirical average of the resulting function of old coordinates}
-
\text{its population expectation}.
\]

The first difference vanishes by a sufficiently high-moment law for the fresh Gaussian vector.

The projected Gaussian coordinates are dependent, but only finitely many directions are removed. For example,

\[
\frac1n
\left\|
\left(
I-P_{(\delta_1^{(2)})^\perp}
\right)\gamma^{(2)}
\right\|_2^2
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Thus the projection is negligible at the level of empirical averages while remaining essential for preserving the exact previous matrix constraints.

The second difference vanishes because every finite-width contraction, response coefficient, and Gaussian variance converges.

The third difference vanishes by the previous induction hypothesis because it involves only previously controlled coordinates.

No central limit theorem is used. The proof establishes that the unscaled empirical errors tend to zero; it does not identify their \(\sqrt n\)-scaled fluctuations.

What is needed is:

- convergence of all required polynomial moments;
- sufficiently high moments for the conditional Gaussian sampling argument;
- stable ranks when a limiting Gram matrix is singular.

It is not necessary for every constructed vector to remain sub-Gaussian.

## The oracle and the trained matrix

Iterating the second-layer matrix update gives

\[
W_k^{(2)}
=
W_0^{(2)}
-
\frac{2\Delta}{n}
\sum_{r<k}
r_r\,
\delta_r^{(2)}
\bigl(h_r^{(1)}\bigr)^\top.
\]

Therefore

\[
W_k^{(2)}h_k^{(1)}
=
W_0^{(2)}h_k^{(1)}
-
2\Delta
\sum_{r<k}
r_r\,
\delta_r^{(2)}
\frac{
\bigl(h_r^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(1)}
}{n},
\]

and

\[
\bigl(W_k^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)}
=
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)}
-
2\Delta
\sum_{r<k}
r_r\,
h_r^{(1)}
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_r^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)}
}{n}.
\]

The rescaled output weights satisfy

\[
W_k^{(3)}
=
W_0^{(3)}
-
2\Delta
\sum_{r<k}
r_rh_r^{(2)}.
\]

In the oracle,

\[
W_0^{(3)}=0,
\]

so

\[
W_k^{(3)}
=
-2\Delta
\sum_{r<k}
r_rh_r^{(2)}.
\]

The population oracle supplies the deterministic values

\[
\mathbb E\left[
H_r^{(1)}H_k^{(1)}
\right],
\]

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\delta_r^{(2)}\delta_k^{(2)}
\right],
\]

and

\[
r_k
=
\mathbb E\left[
W_k^{(3)}H_k^{(2)}
\right]-1.
\]

The finite oracle uses those deterministic population values in its instructions. Its only matrix-random operations are finitely many calls to

\[
W_0^{(2)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\bigl(W_0^{(2)}\bigr)^\top.
\]

The reused-Gaussian induction proves that

\[
\frac{
\bigl(h_r^{(1)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(1)}
}{n}
-
\mathbb E\left[
H_r^{(1)}H_k^{(1)}
\right]
\longrightarrow0,
\]

\[
\frac{
\bigl(\delta_r^{(2)}\bigr)^\top
\delta_k^{(2)}
}{n}
-
\mathbb E\left[
\delta_r^{(2)}\delta_k^{(2)}
\right]
\longrightarrow0,
\]

and

\[
\frac{
\bigl(W_k^{(3)}\bigr)^\top h_k^{(2)}
}{n}
-
\mathbb E\left[
W_k^{(3)}H_k^{(2)}
\right]
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The actual finite Euler network uses its own empirical residuals and contractions. It is compared with the oracle afterward by a deterministic stability estimate.

Thus the proof separates the reused-Gaussian empirical-law argument from the stability argument for empirical feedback.

\qquad

\qquad
W^{(3)}(t).

The derived population quantities are

Z^{(1)}

F^{-1}\!\left(X^{(1)}\right),
\]

\[
H^{(1)}
=
\phi\!\left(Z^{(1)}\right),
\]

\[
Z^{(2)}
=
W^{(2)}H^{(1)},
\]

\[
H^{(2)}
=
\phi\!\left(Z^{(2)}\right),
\]

\[
f
=
\mathbb E\left[
W^{(3)}H^{(2)}
\right],
\]

\[
r=f-1,
\]

and

\[
\delta^{(2)}
