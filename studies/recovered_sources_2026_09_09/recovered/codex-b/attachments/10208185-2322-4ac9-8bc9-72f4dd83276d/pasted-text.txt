# Headline result

Let the initial particle pairs

\[
(p_i,z_{i,0}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
\]

be independent copies of a random pair \((P,Z_0)\), where \(P\in\{-1,1\}\). The label \(p_i\) never changes; only \(z_{i,k}\) is trained.

Define

\[
\phi(z)=\arctan z,
\qquad
f_{n,k}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n p_i\phi(z_{i,k}),
\qquad
r_{n,k}=f_{n,k}-y.
\]

Exact finite-width GD is

\[
z_{i,k+1}
=
z_{i,k}
-
\eta_n p_i r_{n,k}\phi'(z_{i,k}).
\]

Represent it in continuous training time \(t=k\eta_n\), holding each iterate constant until the next update. Linear interpolation gives the same limit.

Assume only

\[
\eta_n\longrightarrow0.
\]

The limiting population state is the distribution

\[
\mu_t=\operatorname{Law}(P,X(t)),
\]

where

\[
F(z)=z+\frac{z^3}{3},
\qquad
X(0)=F(Z_0),
\qquad
Z(t)=F^{-1}(X(t)).
\]

Given the current population law \(\mu_t\), compute its residual

\[
r(t)=\mathbb E[P\phi(Z(t))]-y,
\]

and evolve each population particle by

\[
\frac{dX(t)}{dt}=-P\,r(t).
\]

This is autonomous: the current law determines the current residual, and the residual determines the next movement. No externally supplied trajectory or past history is needed once the current law is known.

For every fixed \(T\) and every fixed bounded function \(\psi(p,z)\) that is Lipschitz in \(z\),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
}
\]

This is uniform in time for each fixed \(\psi\). Choosing

\[
\psi(p,z)=p\phi(z)
\]

gives convergence of the prediction, and therefore of the residual and normalized loss.

---

## 1. Raw finite GD versus transformed finite flow

Let

\[
q(z)=\phi'(z)=\frac1{1+z^2},
\qquad
X=F(z)=z+\frac{z^3}{3}.
\]

Since

\[
F'(z)=1+z^2=\frac1{q(z)},
\]

the raw continuous gradient flow

\[
\dot z_i=-p_i r_nq(z_i)
\]

becomes exactly

\[
\dot X_i=-p_i r_n.
\]

Thus the nonlinear coordinate change is exact in continuous time.

It is not exact for discrete GD. Let

\[
v_i=-p_i r_n.
\]

One GD step is

\[
z_i^+=z_i+\eta_nq(z_i)v_i.
\]

Because \(F\) is cubic,

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(z_i^+)
={}&F(z_i)+\eta_n v_i
\\
&+\eta_n^2z_iq(z_i)^2v_i^2
+\frac{\eta_n^3}{3}q(z_i)^3v_i^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first two terms are transformed Euler. The remaining terms are the nonlinear-coordinate defect.

Set

\[
R=\frac{\pi}{2}+|y|.
\]

Since \(|r_n|\le R\), the one-step coordinate defect is at most

\[
\eta_n^2
\left(R^2+\frac{R^3}{3}\right).
\]

The exact continuous flow also differs from one ideal Euler step by at most

\[
\frac R2\eta_n^2.
\]

If \(E_k\) is the largest transformed-coordinate difference between GD and the finite flow at time \(k\eta_n\), then

\[
E_{k+1}
\le
(1+\eta_n)E_k
+
\eta_n^2
\left(
R^2+\frac{R^3}{3}+\frac R2
\right).
\]

Because \(E_0=0\), discrete Gronwall gives

\[
\max_{k\eta_n\le T}E_k
\le
C_1(T,y)\eta_n,
\]

where \(C_1(T,y)\) does not depend on \(n\).

Therefore

\[
\text{raw finite GD}
\approx
\text{finite transformed flow}
\]

whenever \(\eta_n\to0\).

## 2. Replace the finite flow by coarse Euler

Choose an auxiliary proof mesh \(\delta>0\), unrelated to \(\eta_n\).

Finite transformed Euler is

\[
X^n_{i,m+1}
=
X^n_{i,m}-\delta p_i r^n_m,
\]

where

\[
r^n_m
=
\frac1n\sum_j
p_j\phi\!\left(F^{-1}(X^n_{j,m})\right)-y.
\]

The transformed vector field is bounded by \(R\) and is 1-Lipschitz in the largest coordinate difference. Ordinary Euler analysis therefore gives

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\max_i
|X_i^{n,\mathrm{flow}}(t)-X_i^{n,\mathrm{Euler}}(t)|
\le
C_2(T,y)\delta,
\]

uniformly in \(n\).

Thus the actual GD trajectory is within

\[
C_1(T,y)\eta_n+C_2(T,y)\delta
\]

of coarse finite Euler.

The purpose of \(\delta\) is that the number of coarse updates

\[
M=\left\lceil\frac T\delta\right\rceil
\]

is fixed while \(n\to\infty\).

---

## 3. Pop Euler and the finite oracle

Pop Euler evolves one representative random particle:

\[
r_m
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
P\phi(F^{-1}(X_m))
\right]-y,
\]

\[
X_{m+1}=X_m-\delta P r_m.
\]

The finite oracle starts from the same finite initialization as finite Euler but uses the pop residual:

\[
\bar X_{i,m+1}
=
\bar X_{i,m}-\delta p_i r_m.
\]

The oracle also has an empirical residual,

\[
\bar r^n_m
=
\frac1n\sum_i
p_i\phi(F^{-1}(\bar X_{i,m}))-y,
\]

but it does not use this number in its update.

Let

\[
E_m
=
\max_i|X^n_{i,m}-\bar X_{i,m}|.
\]

Subtracting the finite-Euler and oracle updates gives

\[
E_{m+1}
\le
E_m+\delta|r^n_m-r_m|.
\]

Insert \(\bar r^n_m\):

\[
|r^n_m-r_m|
\le
|r^n_m-\bar r^n_m|
+
|\bar r^n_m-r_m|.
\]

Because \(\phi\circ F^{-1}\) is 1-Lipschitz,

\[
|r^n_m-\bar r^n_m|\le E_m.
\]

Hence

\[
E_{m+1}
\le
(1+\delta)E_m
+
\delta|\bar r^n_m-r_m|.
\]

Since \(E_0=0\),

\[
\max_{m\le M}E_m
\le
(e^T-1)
\max_{m\le M}|\bar r^n_m-r_m|.
\]

The finite/oracle difference is therefore controlled entirely by the oracle’s residual sampling error.

---

## 4. Oracle sampling versus pop Euler

The pop residuals \(r_0,\ldots,r_{M-1}\) are deterministic once the initial population law is fixed.

Each complete oracle trajectory is therefore a deterministic function of its own initialization:

\[
(p_i,\bar X_{i,0})
\longmapsto
(p_i,\bar X_{i,0},\ldots,\bar X_{i,M}).
\]

Because the initial pairs are independent copies, the complete oracle trajectories are independent copies of the pop Euler trajectory.

At every step \(m\),

\[
p_i\phi(F^{-1}(\bar X_{i,m}))
\]

are therefore independent copies of

\[
P\phi(F^{-1}(X_m)).
\]

Hoeffding’s inequality and a union bound give, with probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\),

\[
\max_{m\le M}|\bar r^n_m-r_m|
\le
\pi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\max_{m\le M}E_m
\le
(e^T-1)\pi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
}.
\]

There is a second, distinct sampling error. Let \(\psi\) be the observable in the headline result, and define the length of its range by

\[
B_\psi=\sup\psi-\inf\psi.
\]

The same argument gives, with probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\),

\[
\max_{m\le M}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i
\psi(p_i,\bar Z_{i,m})
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z_m)]
\right|
\le
B_\psi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
},
\]

where

\[
\bar Z_{i,m}=F^{-1}(\bar X_{i,m}),
\qquad
Z_m=F^{-1}(X_m).
\]

The first sampling error controls how residual error changes the finite dynamics. The second directly controls the empirical \(\psi\)-avg.

For the whole trajectory, define

\[
\Gamma_i
=
(p_i,\bar Z_{i,0},\ldots,\bar Z_{i,M}).
\]

These are independent copies of

\[
\Gamma=(P,Z_0,\ldots,Z_M).
\]

Thus, for every integrable trajectory observable \(\Psi\),

\[
\frac1n\sum_i\Psi(\Gamma_i)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E[\Psi(\Gamma)].
\]

No independence across time is asserted or needed; independence is across complete particle trajectories.

## 5. Remove the pop Euler mesh

The pop flow is

\[
\dot X(t)=-P r(t),
\qquad
r(t)=\mathbb E[P\phi(F^{-1}(X(t)))]-y.
\]

To prove existence and uniqueness concretely, write

\[
X(t)=X_0-Ps(t).
\]

Then the scalar accumulated residual satisfies

\[
\frac{ds(t)}{dt}
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
P\phi(F^{-1}(X_0-Ps(t)))
\right]-y.
\]

The right-hand side is bounded by \(R\) and is 1-Lipschitz in \(s\). Therefore this scalar ODE has a unique global solution. This proves that the pop flow is well-defined.

Pop Euler is ordinary Euler for the same scalar equation. If \(s_m\) is its discrete accumulated residual, then

\[
\max_{m\delta\le T}|s_m-s(m\delta)|
\le
\frac R2(e^T-1)\delta.
\]

Because

\[
X_m=X_0-Ps_m,
\qquad
X(t)=X_0-Ps(t),
\]

the same estimate holds for \(X_m-X(m\delta)\). Holding Euler constant between grid points adds at most \(R\delta\). Therefore

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
|X^{\mathrm{pop\ Euler}}(t)-X^{\mathrm{pop\ flow}}(t)|
\le
C_2(T,y)\delta.
\]

No oracle is needed here: pop Euler and pop flow already live on the same population state and use the same vector field.

---

## 6. Add the five errors

At any time \(t\), the triangle inequality gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
\\
&\le
|\text{finite GD avg}-\text{finite flow avg}|
&&\text{(actual step)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{finite flow avg}-\text{finite Euler avg}|
&&\text{(finite mesh)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{finite Euler avg}-\text{oracle avg}|
&&\text{(feedback)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{oracle avg}-\text{pop Euler avg}|
&&\text{(sampling of }\psi\text{)}
\\
&\quad+
|\text{pop Euler avg}-\text{pop flow avg}|
&&\text{(pop mesh)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Suppose \(\psi\) is \(L_\psi\)-Lipschitz in \(z\). Since \(F^{-1}\) is 1-Lipschitz, the five terms are bounded respectively by

\[
L_\psi C_1(T,y)\eta_n,
\]

\[
L_\psi C_2(T,y)\delta,
\]

\[
L_\psi(e^T-1)\pi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
},
\]

\[
B_\psi
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
},
\]

and

\[
L_\psi C_2(T,y)\delta.
\]

These estimates hold simultaneously for all \(t\le T\), with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{t\le T}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
\le{}&
L_\psi C_1(T,y)\eta_n
\\
&+2L_\psi C_2(T,y)\delta
\\
&+
\left[L_\psi(e^T-1)\pi+B_\psi\right]
\sqrt{
\frac{\log(4(M+1)/\alpha)}{2n}
}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves the headline result as follows.

Given an error tolerance \(\varepsilon>0\) and failure probability \(\alpha>0\):

1. Choose \(\delta\) small enough that the two mesh errors are below \(\varepsilon/3\).
2. Keep \(\delta\) fixed, so \(M=\lceil T/\delta\rceil\) is fixed.
3. Choose \(n\) large enough that \(\eta_n\) and both sampling terms are below the remaining error.

Then

\[
\Pr\left(
\sup_{t\le T}
\left|
\frac1n\sum_i\psi(p_i,z_i^{\mathrm{GD}}(t))
-
\mathbb E[\psi(P,Z(t))]
\right|
>\varepsilon
\right)
\le\alpha.
\]

Since \(\alpha\) is arbitrary, this is uniform compact-time convergence in probability.

“For every bounded Lipschitz \(\psi\)” means that the argument applies to each fixed \(\psi\). A finite collection of observables can be handled simultaneously by another finite union bound.

For the toy model, the step-size requirement is simply

\[
\eta_n\to0.
\]

The full neural-network proof has the raw-GD estimate

\[
C_T\left(
\eta_n+\eta_n\sqrt n+\eta_n^2n
\right),
\]

so its sufficient criterion is

\[
\eta_n\sqrt n\to0.
\]

The audited theorem’s choice \(\eta_n=n^{-2}\) is one convenient instance of this criterion, not the only possible one.
