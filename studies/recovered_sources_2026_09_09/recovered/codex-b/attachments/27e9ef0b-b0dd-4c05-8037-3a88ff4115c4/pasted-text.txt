Uniqueness means the Stieltjes moment problem is determinate: there is exactly one nonnegative measure \(\rho\) on \([0,\infty)\) satisfying
\[
\mu_n=\int_0^\infty \lambda^n\,\rho(d\lambda)
\quad\text{for every }n.
\]A standard sufficient condition is the Stieltjes–Carleman condition
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_n^{-1/(2n)}=\infty.}
\]If this series diverges, the representing measure—assuming it exists—is unique. For example, an all-order bound of the form
\[
\mu_n\le C^n(2n)!
\]would imply Carleman and therefore uniqueness.
An equivalent operational picture uses the rational hierarchy: uniqueness holds when the limiting Gaussian lower and zero-Radau upper Stieltjes transforms coincide. In an indeterminate problem, those two subsequences can converge to distinct extremal transforms representing different measures with the same moments.
Important qualifications:
All-order Hankel positivity establishes existence, not uniqueness.
Failure of Carleman’s condition does not prove nonuniqueness; it merely makes that sufficient criterion unavailable.
The six known moments \(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_5\) cannot decide uniqueness. Many measures can share a finite moment prefix.
The canonical zero-radius result rules out a compactly supported representing measure, but it does not rule out Carleman determinacy.
So after proving all-order positivity, the next uniqueness obligation would be an all-order growth estimate for \(\mu_n\), ideally strong enough to establish the displayed Carleman divergence.

Ignoring the DMFT report entirely, the shallow model teaches something quite clean:
\[
\boxed{\text{compact dynamics do not imply Stieltjes dynamics}.}
\]That is actually encouraging for the broader compression question, while being fatal to Stieltjes as its universal explanation.
For the shallow network, every neuron has the explicit two-variable characteristic flow
\[
(a(s),v(s))=\Phi_s(a_0,v_0),
\]and the mean output is represented as
\[
F(s)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}
a_s(a_0,v_0)v_s(a_0,v_0)^2\,
\rho_0(da_0\,dv_0).
\]The width \(n\) has disappeared. The entire population dynamics are encoded by:
a fixed two-dimensional characteristic map \(\Phi_s\);
a fixed initial law \(\rho_0\);
one fixed-dimensional integral.
That is a genuine form of width-independent compression.
Yet after changing from feature time \(s\) to output coordinate \(y=F(s)\), the induced kernel
\[
K(y)=F'\!\left(F^{-1}(y)\right)
\]does not have the positive-resolvent representation
\[
\frac{K(\sqrt{x})-K(0)}{x}
=\int_0^\infty\frac{\rho(d\lambda)}{1+\lambda x}.
\]So the implication
\[
\text{compact characteristic representation}
\Longrightarrow
\text{positive Stieltjes spectral representation}
\]is false.
This tells us that Stieltjes was never the essence of compression. It was one unusually attractive sufficient mechanism because it would have supplied, all at once:
positive spectral weights;
stable real poles;
nested Padé/Gaussian/Radau approximations;
monotone upper and lower bounds;
a natural finite rational ODE hierarchy.
The negative Hankel determinant removes all those automatic guarantees. It does not impose a lower bound on state dimension and does not rule out:
signed rational approximations;
nonlinear finite-dimensional ODEs;
characteristic or transport representations;
finite auxiliary memory variables;
matrix-valued or non-self-adjoint spectral descriptions;
quadrature of a low-dimensional latent integral.
A two-state nonlinear ODE can easily produce a non-Stieltjes output. Therefore leaving the Stieltjes cone is not the same as leaving the finite-dimensional world.
There is, however, an important distinction between three kinds of “compactness”:
Notion	Shallow model status
Fixed-dimensional characteristic equations	Exact
Width-independent integral over a fixed latent space	Exact, wherever the population integral is well defined
Closed autonomous ODE for \(F\) or the loss alone	Not obtained
Finite \(D(\varepsilon,T)\) approximation of the integral	Plausible/provable under suitable boundedness and regularity
Horizon-independent \(D(\varepsilon)\) valid globally	Not established


The integral still retains a continuum of initial conditions. Numerically replacing it by \(M\) quadrature particles produces a finite system of dimension \(O(M)\), with \(M\) independent of network width but generally dependent on accuracy and horizon. Proving that \(M(\varepsilon,T)\) remains controlled is the real approximation problem.
The Gaussian version also warns us that an explicit formula is not automatically a global theorem: the characteristic denominators can vanish, so tail integrability and positive-time well-posedness must be proved separately. Stieltjes positivity would have helped with global control, but its failure means those estimates must come directly from the dynamics.
I would therefore reformulate the broader conjecture as follows:
For an appropriately regular class of mean-field neural systems, every accuracy \(\varepsilon>0\) and finite horizon \(T\) admit an architecture-derived, autonomous, restartable surrogate of finite complexity \(D(\varepsilon,T)\), independent of network width and not fitted to the target trajectory, that approximates the declared observables uniformly on \([0,T]\).

The stronger claim
\[
D=D(\varepsilon)\quad\text{independent of }T
\]should be a separate long-horizon conjecture requiring an additional stability, dissipation, or plateau theorem.
The shallow example updates the program this way:
Falsified: Stieltjes positivity is a universal organizing principle for compressed neural training.
Established in the shallow model: low-dimensional characteristic structure can exist without Stieltjes structure.
Still open: whether this characteristic compression can be converted into certified finite autonomous approximations uniformly on long horizons.
Still open and substantially harder: whether deeper networks admit a fixed-dimensional latent description, or whether depth creates irreducible response/history variables.
Methodological lesson: derive compression from invariants, characteristics, conditional laws, and stability first; classify the resulting function afterward. Do not choose Stieltjes—or another attractive function class—as the foundational assumption.
So the shallow counterexample does not undermine the deepest compression conjecture. It tells us that the conjecture, if true, must be broader and more dynamical than a positive spectral moment theory.
