Yes. For a carefully chosen residual mean-field architecture, the continuous-training-time program can be proved completely, including a joint width-and-depth limit uniform on every compact training interval.

This is a genuine positive theorem, but it is not a theorem for the earlier dense Gaussian-matrix \(\mu\)P network. The residual particle architecture removes the adaptive reused-transpose mechanism that blocked the dense \(H=3\) proof.

## 1. Frozen architecture

Fix a batch \((\xi_b,y_b)_{b=1}^B\subset\mathbb R^2\), with \(B=O(1)\). Let

\[
\sigma(x)=\tanh x
\]

and use raw trainable parameters

\[
\theta=(\alpha,\omega,\beta)\in\mathbb R^3.
\]

Define one residual neuron by

\[
\Phi(\theta,x)
=
\tanh(\alpha)\,
\tanh\!\left(\tanh(\omega)x+\tanh(\beta)\right).
\]

Thus the activation is strictly nonlinear, while the effective amplitude, slope, and bias are smoothly bounded. The raw parameters themselves remain unconstrained and are trained by ordinary Euclidean gradient flow.

For depth \(L\), width \(n\), and \(h=L^{-1}\), define

\[
X^{n,L}_{b,0}=\xi_b,
\]

\[
X^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}
=
X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
+
\frac1{nL}
\sum_{i=1}^n
\Phi\!\left(\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}\right),
\qquad 0\le \ell<L.
\]

This is a post-nonlinearity residual block: the nonlinear branch is evaluated first and then added to the identity skip. Every residual block is trained.

The output and MSE are

\[
F_b^{n,L}=X^{n,L}_{b,L},
\qquad
\mathcal E^{n,L}
=
\frac1{2B}\sum_{b=1}^B(F_b^{n,L}-y_b)^2.
\]

Initialization is independent across \((\ell,i)\):

\[
\theta_{\ell i}(0)\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\rho_0,
\qquad
\rho_0=N(m,I_3),
\]

with a nonzero shift \(m\) chosen below to avoid symmetry-induced triviality.

The correct joint mean-field/depth gradient scaling is

\[
\dot\theta_{\ell i}
=
-\eta nL\,\nabla_{\theta_{\ell i}}\mathcal E^{n,L}.
\]

The factor \(n\) is the usual mean-field width scaling. The additional factor \(L\) compensates for the residual factor \(1/L\). With only the \(n\)-scaling, training freezes as \(L\to\infty\).

## 2. Exact finite-width identities

Set

\[
A^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\partial_x\Phi\!\left(
\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
\right).
\]

Define the output sensitivity

\[
R^{n,L}_{b,L}=1,
\]

\[
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
=
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}
\left(1+\frac1L A^{n,L}_{b,\ell}\right).
\]

An exact chain-rule calculation gives

\[
\nabla_{\theta_{\ell i}}F_b^{n,L}
=
\frac1{nL}
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}
\nabla_\theta\Phi\!\left(
\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
\right).
\]

Therefore the exact particle flow is

\[
\dot\theta_{\ell i}
=
-\frac{\eta}{B}
\sum_{b=1}^B
(F_b^{n,L}-y_b)
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}
\nabla_\theta\Phi\!\left(
\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}
\right).
\]

The occurrence of \(R_{\ell+1}\), rather than \(R_\ell\), is essential: layer \(\ell\) first perturbs \(X_{\ell+1}\).

The properly scaled kernel is

\[
K^{n,L}_{bc}
=
nL\sum_{\ell,i}
\nabla_{\theta_{\ell i}}F_b^{n,L}
\cdot
\nabla_{\theta_{\ell i}}F_c^{n,L}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
K^{n,L}_{bc}
=
\frac1L\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}R^{n,L}_{c,\ell+1}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
&\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell})\\
&\cdot
\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{c,\ell}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\dot F_b^{n,L}
=
-\frac{\eta}{B}
\sum_{c=1}^B
K^{n,L}_{bc}(F_c^{n,L}-y_c).
\]

This kernel is positive semidefinite, and

\[
\dot{\mathcal E}^{n,L}
=
-\frac{\eta}{L}
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\left|
\frac1B\sum_b
(F_b^{n,L}-y_b)
R^{n,L}_{b,\ell+1}
\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta_{\ell i},X^{n,L}_{b,\ell})
\right|^2.
\]

All these identities are exact at finite \(n,L\).

## 3. The continuous-depth mean-field program

Let \(\rho_t(s)\in\mathcal P_1(\mathbb R^3)\) denote the parameter law at depth \(s\in[0,1]\) and training time \(t\).

At each \(t\), first perform the forward depth sweep

\[
\partial_s x_b(s,t)
=
\int
\Phi(\theta,x_b(s,t))\,\rho_t(s,d\theta),
\qquad
x_b(0,t)=\xi_b.
\]

Then perform the backward sweep

\[
-\partial_s r_b(s,t)
=
r_b(s,t)
\int
\partial_x\Phi(\theta,x_b(s,t))
\,\rho_t(s,d\theta),
\qquad
r_b(1,t)=1.
\]

Set

\[
f_b(t)=x_b(1,t),
\qquad
p_b(s,t)
=
\frac1B r_b(s,t)(f_b(t)-y_b).
\]

The training velocity is

\[
v_t(s,\theta)
=
-\eta\sum_{b=1}^B
p_b(s,t)\,
\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta,x_b(s,t)).
\]

Finally, evolve the parameter law by

\[
\partial_t\rho_t(s)
+
\nabla_\theta\cdot\bigl(\rho_t(s)v_t(s,\cdot)\bigr)
=0,
\qquad
\rho_0(s)=\rho_0.
\]

This is the gradient-flow master program:

1. forward depth ODE;
2. backward depth adjoint;
3. current-time parameter transport.

It is autonomous and restartable. It retains the entire current depth-indexed law but no training history. The law becomes non-Gaussian immediately; no Gaussian or finite-moment closure is being claimed.

Its kernel is

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{bc}(t)
=
\int_0^1
r_b(s,t)r_c(s,t)
\int
&\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta,x_b(s,t))\\
&\cdot
\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta,x_c(s,t))
\,\rho_t(s,d\theta)\,ds .
\end{aligned}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dot f_b(t)
=
-\frac{\eta}{B}
\sum_{c=1}^B K_{bc}(t)(f_c(t)-y_c).
\]

## 4. Global well-posedness

Since \(|\Phi|\le1\),

\[
|x_b(s,t)|\le |\xi_b|+1.
\]

On this automatically bounded feature range,

\[
|\Phi|+|\partial_x\Phi|
+\|\nabla_\theta\Phi\|
+\|\nabla_\theta^2\Phi\|
+\|\partial_x\nabla_\theta\Phi\|
+\|\nabla_\theta\partial_x\Phi\|
\le C_\Phi.
\]

The constant is global in the raw parameters because every raw parameter appears through a \(\tanh\).

Moreover,

\[
|r_b(s,t)|
=
\exp\!\left(
\int_s^1
\int\partial_x\Phi(\theta,x_b(u,t))
\,\rho_t(u,d\theta)\,du
\right)
\le e.
\]

Hence \(p_b\), \(v_t\), and the spatial Lipschitz constant of \(v_t\) are bounded on every compact training horizon.

For two depth profiles \(\rho,\widetilde\rho\), define

\[
D(\rho,\widetilde\rho)
=
\int_0^1W_1(\rho(s),\widetilde\rho(s))\,ds.
\]

Forward and backward Gronwall estimates give

\[
\max_b\|x_b-\widetilde x_b\|_{L^\infty_s}
+
\max_b\|r_b-\widetilde r_b\|_{L^\infty_s}
\le C\,D(\rho,\widetilde\rho).
\]

Therefore

\[
|v[\rho](s,\theta)-v[\widetilde\rho](s,\widetilde\theta)|
\le
C\left(
|\theta-\widetilde\theta|
+
D(\rho,\widetilde\rho)
\right).
\]

Solving the characteristic ODE

\[
\dot\Theta_s(t,a)
=
v_t(s,\Theta_s(t,a))
\]

and setting

\[
\rho_t(s)=\Theta_s(t,\cdot)_\#\rho_0
\]

gives a contraction on a sufficiently short training interval. The same interval length works after every restart because the constants above are uniform. Iteration proves:

> For every \(T<\infty\), the continuous-depth program has a unique characteristic solution
> \[
> \rho\in C\!\left([0,T];
> L^1([0,1];\mathcal P_1(\mathbb R^3))\right).
> \]
> It is global, autonomous, and restartable.

No compact-support assumption is required.

## 5. Width convergence, uniformly in depth

For a depth-\(L\) measure profile write

\[
d_L(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\nu)
=
\frac1L
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
W_1(\mu_\ell,\nu_\ell).
\]

The residual factors \(1/L\) imply the uniform estimates

\[
\max_{\ell,b}|X_b^\ell-\widetilde X_b^\ell|
\le C\,d_L(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\nu),
\]

\[
\max_{\ell,b}|R_b^\ell-\widetilde R_b^\ell|
\le C\,d_L(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\nu).
\]

Hence the layer velocities satisfy

\[
|v_\ell[\boldsymbol\mu](\theta)
-v_\ell[\boldsymbol\nu](\widetilde\theta)|
\le
C\left(
|\theta-\widetilde\theta|
+d_L(\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\nu)
\right),
\]

with \(C\) independent of \(L\).

Transport an optimal coupling of two initial profiles by their respective characteristic flows. Gronwall gives

\[
d_L(\boldsymbol\mu_t,\boldsymbol\nu_t)
\le
e^{C_Tt}
d_L(\boldsymbol\mu_0,\boldsymbol\nu_0),
\qquad 0\le t\le T,
\]

again with no \(L\)-dependence.

Let

\[
\widehat\rho^{\,n,L}_{\ell,0}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\theta_{\ell i}(0)}.
\]

Then, pathwise,

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
d_L(\widehat{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\,n,L}_t,
\boldsymbol\rho^L_t)
\le
e^{C_TT}
\frac1L\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
W_1(\widehat\rho^{\,n,L}_{\ell,0},\rho_0).
\]

Because Gaussian empirical measures converge in \(W_1\),

\[
q_n
:=
\mathbb E W_1\!\left(
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{Z_i},\rho_0
\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

Therefore, for every deterministic sequence \(L=L_n\),

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
d_{L_n}(
\widehat{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\,n,L_n}_t,
\boldsymbol\rho^{L_n}_t)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

There is no required relation between \(L_n\) and \(n\).

## 6. Depth convergence

The continuous solution has a canonical depth-Lipschitz representative:

\[
W_1(\rho_t(s),\rho_t(u))
\le C_T|s-u|.
\]

This follows because \(x_b,r_b,p_b\), and therefore \(v_t(s,\theta)\), are Lipschitz in \(s\).

The finite-depth population program is the forward/backward Euler discretization of the continuous-depth program. Its one-step forward and backward defects are \(O(L^{-2})\). A coupled characteristic comparison gives

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\frac1L
\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
W_1(\rho^L_{\ell,t},\rho_t(\ell/L))
\le\frac{C_T}{L},
\]

together with

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\max_{\ell,b}
\left(
|X^L_{b,\ell}(t)-x_b(\ell/L,t)|
+
|R^L_{b,\ell}(t)-r_b(\ell/L,t)|
\right)
\le\frac{C_T}{L},
\]

and

\[
\sup_{t\le T}\max_{b,c}
|K^L_{bc}(t)-K_{bc}(t)|
\le\frac{C_T}{L}.
\]

## 7. Joint theorem

Define the random initialization discrepancy

\[
\delta_{n,L}
=
\frac1L\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
W_1(\widehat\rho^{\,n,L}_{\ell,0},\rho_0).
\]

For every \(T<\infty\), there is \(C_T<\infty\), independent of \(n,L\), such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{t\le T}\Bigg[
&
\frac1L\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}
W_1\!\left(
\widehat\rho^{\,n,L}_{\ell,t},
\rho_t(\ell/L)
\right)\\
&+
\max_{\ell,b}
|X^{n,L}_{b,\ell}(t)-x_b(\ell/L,t)|\\
&+
\max_b|F_b^{n,L}(t)-f_b(t)|
+
\max_{b,c}|K^{n,L}_{bc}(t)-K_{bc}(t)|
\Bigg]
\le
C_T\left(\delta_{n,L}+\frac1L\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(\mathbb E\delta_{n,L}=q_n\to0\), for every pair of sequences

\[
n_k\to\infty,\qquad L_k\to\infty,
\]

with no relation between their rates,

\[
\sup_{t\le T}
\left(
\max_b|F_b^{n_k,L_k}(t)-f_b(t)|
+
|\mathcal E^{n_k,L_k}(t)-\mathcal E(t)|
+
\max_{b,c}|K^{n_k,L_k}_{bc}(t)-K_{bc}(t)|
\right)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

This is exact continuous-training-time convergence; no Euler discretization in training time is used.

## 8. Nontriviality

The scaling permits, but does not automatically guarantee, nonlazy motion. Symmetric initialization or already-fitted labels can make the velocity zero.

An explicit nontrivial choice is

\[
B=1,\qquad \xi_1=1,\qquad y_1=0,
\qquad
\rho_0=N((2,2,0),I_3).
\]

For this distribution,

\[
\int\Phi(\theta,1)\,\rho_0(d\theta)>0,
\]

so the continuous-depth forward flow is not the identity. Moreover, the initial residual is nonzero and

\[
\int_0^1\int
\left|
p_1(s,0)\nabla_\theta\Phi(\theta,x_1(s,0))
\right|^2
\rho_0(d\theta)\,ds>0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\dot{\mathcal E}(0)<0,
\]

and the parameter law moves by \(O(t)\) in training time. This is genuinely feature-learning, not a lazy or linearized limit. The map \(x\mapsto\Phi(\theta,x)\) also has nonzero second derivative on a positive-\(\rho_0\) set.

## Scope

This proves the requested gradient-flow mean-field-program theorem for a particular strictly nonlinear residual architecture:

- arbitrary fixed batch size;
- every residual block trained;
- Gaussian raw initialization;
- nontrivial continuous-depth limit;
- exact continuous gradient flow;
- uniform convergence on every compact training interval;
- joint \(n,L\to\infty\) with no rate relation.

It does not prove the corresponding theorem for dense CLT-scaled Gaussian matrices. The decisive simplification is structural: each residual block is an empirical mean-field vector field, so its dependence on a layer law is Lipschitz in Wasserstein distance. There is no adaptively reused dense transpose and hence no weighted-Wishart co-localization obstruction.
