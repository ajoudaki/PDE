# Independent full-coefficient check of the p6/p7 candidate

## Frozen comparison design

This phase starts after `p7_jet_oracle.py` and its two successful Gaussian
fixture checks were frozen. The candidate is the independently derived
`P7_DERIVATION.md` in this study, once its author freezes that report. No
candidate expression was used to implement or validate the oracle.

The target is the complete middle-matrix coefficients `[t^7]A` and
`[t^8]A`, including all lower label degrees generated by moving output
residuals. Supporting comparisons are the displayed lower activation,
upper preactivation, and readout-times-gate coefficients through the
required orders. All comparisons are finite algebra: candidate pairings
are replaced explicitly by arithmetic means, and operator actions use the
same initialized matrix and transpose as the oracle.

Two fixture classes are fixed before checking candidate formulas:

1. A width-eight fixture with exact initial lower and upper Grams `vI`
   and `tau I`, constructed by the already available
   `p45_jet_check.ideal_initial(8201)`. Its remaining initialized operator
   component is generic. Full empirical cubic output feedback is retained;
   Gram isotropy alone is not assumed to impose further label symmetries.
2. A width-sixteen fixture with rows `h=r_j*(+/-1,+/-1)` for each of
   `r=(0.2,0.35,0.5,0.65)`, with diagonal middle entries
   `(0.8,1.0,1.2,1.4)` repeated on the corresponding four sign rows.
   Thus `H=tanh(A_0 h)` has the same sign symmetries, both initial Grams
   are exactly scalar, and coordinate interchange also preserves the
   fixture. This separately checks any population parity/swap collection
   without treating an arbitrary exact-Gram fixture as symmetric.

The primary comparison uses exact bivariate monomial bookkeeping, not
label interpolation. Every nonzero total label degree is checked
separately; numerical evaluations at `(0.7,-1.1)` and `(-0.6,0.9)` provide
an additional readout of the same fixed coefficient tables. The normalized
absolute discrepancy threshold is `3e-12` with scale
`max(1,max(abs(reference)))`; nonfinite values fail. Numerical initial Gram
errors must be below the same threshold.

No rank conclusion can be inferred when the finite width is smaller than
the candidate generator count. The check must therefore compare the
actual coefficient formulas or their specified reconstructions, not merely
observe that a numerically full-rank candidate table spans a small matrix.
It tests neither Gaussian population moment formulas nor convergence of a
finite-carrier implementation. No GPU use or training is authorized here.
Stop once both fixed fixtures pass, or report the first unresolved
coefficient discrepancy; changes responding to a failed candidate must be
reported as corrections rather than silently counted as an initial pass.

## Complete coefficient comparison: PASS

The frozen candidate was `P7_DERIVATION.md`, SHA256
`5800550c92ddf66e58729d2464e5116a65a49d8e9f3e7566dfa58a738a052884`.
Its equations (2)--(14) were implemented directly in
`p7_candidate_check.py`, SHA256
`1d25ff00c3d1e4955e42008f60996614900c22af9bafe7020cfabdfd9f7db11b`.
That implementation uses ordinary bivariate coefficient polynomials and
actual finite matrix actions/pairings; it does not query oracle coefficients
when constructing candidate coefficients.

For both fixed fixtures, every compared bivariate coefficient passes:

| Quantity | Compared time powers |
|---|---|
| Lower activation `h` | 0 through 6 |
| Upper preactivation `z` | 0 through 6 |
| Positive residual `b=y-f` | 0 through 6 |
| Readout times upper gate `u=c*(1-H^2)` | 0 through 7 |
| Actual middle matrix `A` | 0 through 8 |

The middle coefficients are reconstructed by the complete candidate
convolution

```
A_q = (1/q) sum_a sum_(i+j+k=q-1, j>=1) b_(a,i) (u_(a,j) outer h_(a,k))/n.
```

The highest normalized absolute discrepancy across both fixtures, all
individual monomial coefficients, and the two declared numerical label
evaluations is `1.942890293094024e-16`. In particular, `A_7` retains label
degrees `2,4,6`; `A_8` retains `2,4,6,8`; `h_6` retains `2,4,6`; and
`u_7` retains `1,3,5,7`. No moving residual term or lower label degree was
discarded. No candidate correction was necessary.

The generic fixture has additional empirical beta monomials, for example
`[y1^2 y2] beta_1 = 0.06416304420259739` and
`[y2^3] beta_1 = 0.009166149171799635`. Its PASS therefore checks the full
formula with generic empirical cubic feedback, not merely its simplified
population label structure.

The predefined sign/swap fixture has the common diagonal/off-diagonal
structure, but its coefficients happen to be equal:
`beta_a=0.05971641449484514*y_a*(y1^2+y2^2)`. This radial special case
does not by itself test the candidate's nonradial extra factor collection.
The exact symbolic check below covers that case without replacing or
searching over the predefined finite fixtures.

## Exact four-plus-two collection: PASS

An independent coefficient calculation was constructed from equations
(3)--(5), treating `H1,H2,F11,F12,F21,F22` as independent formal variables
and imposing only `d_a=1-H_a^2`, `e_a=-2H_a d_a`. It did not read the
derivation agent's rank-check implementation. All arithmetic was rational.

Let the population-form feedback coefficients be

```
beta_1 = alpha*y1^3 + eta*y1*y2^2
beta_2 = alpha*y2^3 + eta*y2*y1^2.
```

They decompose as `eta*beta_radial+(alpha-eta)*beta_nonradial`, with
`beta_radial,a=y_a*(y1^2+y2^2)` and
`beta_nonradial=(y1^3,y2^3)`. The M and P formulas are linear in beta.
The exact checks establish:

- For radial beta, every M and P coefficient is a rational linear
  combination of the eight K3 coefficients.
- For nonradial beta, the eight K3 coefficients together with M pivots
  `(a,j)=(0,1),(0,2),(1,3),(1,4)` have rank 12 and reconstruct all twelve
  M coefficients with exactly zero residual.
- Adding P pivots `(a,j)=(0,1),(1,4)` gives rank 14 and reconstructs all
  twelve P coefficients with exactly zero residual.

The probe index in this paragraph is zero-based; each `j` multiplies
`y1^(5-j)y2^j`. These are exactly the candidate's four M and two P
pivots, translated from its one-based probe indexing.

When `alpha!=eta`, subtracting the radial K3 contributions from actual M
pivots and dividing by `alpha-eta` recovers the nonradial pivots. The
same argument then applies to P. When `alpha=eta`, all M and P columns
already lie in the K3 span. Thus these identities prove the stated
collection for every population-form pair `(alpha,eta)`; they do not
rely on numerically finding an invertible small-width feature matrix.

The candidate's raw P fields are `tau P` at time seven. Multiplying the
complete P collection identity by `tau` preserves its span containment;
the numerical raw scales `6!` and `7!` are nonzero scalar multipliers
and also preserve containment. The actual finite candidate comparison
retains its specified factors before any ridge normalization.

Together with the complete convolution check, the collection validates a
sufficient nested list of `(14,28)` factors through middle power seven
and `(26,46)` through middle power eight, conditional on the population
identities used in the candidate. It does not prove that these are minimal
actual Gaussian spans, that Gaussian moment reductions are correct, that
the finite-carrier dictionary matches finite jets exactly, or that the
population has the required eighth-order time regularity.
