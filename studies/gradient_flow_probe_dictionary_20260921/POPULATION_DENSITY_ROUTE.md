# Population density route: what the derivative factors actually generate

This is a scoped independent proof attempt for the natural all-order continuation of the implemented derivative-factor dictionary. It uses only DERIVATION.md, FACTORIZATION.md, ORDER_COUNTS.md, P45_DERIVATION_ROUTE.md, P7_DERIVATION.md, P7_DICTIONARY_SPEC.md, SINGLE_SAMPLE_GLOBAL_BOUND.md and established C.4.7.8–10. The mathematical and conjecture-investigation skills and their applicable references were read. No training, numerical experiment, other study, prior verdict or other agent's draft was used.

The target is convergence, after population width, of the actual whole-middle replacement with the prescribed ridge, for one training sample and preferably uniformly over physical time and the input circle. This note does **not** establish that target. It proves a positive seed-information result and an exact Gaussian innovation obstruction to the simplest span argument. Neither conclusion is a claim that the all-order dictionary fails.

## 1. Precise spaces and the first enrichment

Use the normalized probes and notation of P7_DERIVATION.md:

\[
g_1,g_2\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}\sim N(0,1),\quad
h_a=\tanh g_a,\quad \ell_a=1-h_a^2,
\]
\[
Y_a=W_0h_a,\quad H_a=\tanh Y_a,\quad d_a=1-H_a^2,
\quad U_{ab}=d_aH_b.
\]

The two upper variables are independent centered Gaussians of variance
\(v=E[h_a^2]>0\). Let

\[
L_{ab}=\ell_a^2W_0^*U_{ab},\qquad
F_{ab}=vU_{ab}+W_0L_{ab}.
\]

The implemented p3 lower span is generated by \(h_a,L_{ab}\), and its upper span is

\[
V_{2,3}=\operatorname{span}\{U_{ab},\text{all binary cubic label coefficients of }K_{3,a}\},
\]

where

\[
S=\sum_b y_bH_b,\quad
R_a=\frac{y_a}{2}\sum_b y_bF_{ab},\quad
J=\sum_a y_ad_aR_a,
\quad K_{3,a}=d_aJ/3+S\phi''(Y_a)R_a.
\]

Nonzero raw column scales do not change these spans. Denote by
\(\mathcal H_Y=L^2(\sigma(Y_1,Y_2))\) the upper subspace of functions of the initial upper preactivations only. This is a subspace of the full upper population space, which also contains subsequent Gaussian source coordinates.

## 2. Initial seeds are already measurable from the first factors

**Proposition 1.** Up to completion by null sets,

\[
\sigma(U_{11},U_{12},U_{21},U_{22})=\sigma(Y_1,Y_2).
\]

The forward containment follows from the definition. For the reverse containment, outside the events \(H_2=0\) and \(H_1^2=H_2^2\), put

\[
r=U_{11}/U_{12}=H_1/H_2,
\qquad R=U_{12}/U_{22}=(1-H_1^2)/(1-H_2^2).
\]

Since \(r^2-R=(r^2-1)/(1-H_2^2)\), the denominator is nonzero on this event and

\[
H_2^2=\frac{1-R}{r^2-R},\qquad
H_2=\operatorname{sign}(U_{12})\sqrt{\frac{1-R}{r^2-R}},
\qquad H_1=rH_2.
\]

Both exceptional events have probability zero because \((H_1,H_2)\) has a continuous density on \((-1,1)^2\). Finally \(Y_a=\operatorname{arctanh}(H_a)\). This proves the proposition. Likewise, \(h_1,h_2\) determine \(g_1,g_2\) by inverse tanh, so every initial lower input feature \(\tanh(g\cdot x)\) is measurable from the initial lower columns.

Consequently the absence of explicitly retained \(Y_a,H_a\) is not an information-loss argument against the frozen *joint laws*. It remains a material issue for the frozen *linear spans* used by the middle action. The rational reconstruction just displayed is not a permitted linear middle contraction. Appending nonlinear functions of the initial factors would be a changed dictionary and is not performed here.

## 3. A positive definite innovation survives the p3 enrichment

**Proposition 2.** For the actual initialized Gaussian action,

\[
V_{2,3}\cap\mathcal H_Y=\operatorname{span}\{U_{11},U_{12},U_{21},U_{22}\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus none of \(H_1,H_2,Y_1,Y_2\) belongs to \(V_{2,3}\).

The proof keeps both orientations of the same initialized operator. C.4.7.8 (H6), specialized to these queries, gives

\[
W_0^*U_{ab}=\zeta_{ab}+\sum_j h_j\mu_{ab,j},
\quad \mu_{ab,j}=E[\partial_{Y_j}U_{ab}],
\]

where \(\zeta=(\zeta_{ab})\) is a centered Gaussian vector independent of \(g\), with covariance

\[
\Gamma_{ab,cd}=E[U_{ab}U_{cd}].
\]

This covariance is strictly positive definite. A zero-variance linear combination would vanish on the open square of possible \((H_1,H_2)\). Writing the four functions as

\[
H_1-H_1^3,\quad H_2-H_1^2H_2,\quad
H_1-H_1H_2^2,\quad H_2-H_2^3
\]

shows that their four distinct cubic monomials force all four coefficients to vanish.

The book source rule (H6) is stated for bounded action operands, so its use on \(L_{ab}\) requires a limit argument. First construct the two initial forward queries \(Y_a=W_0h_a\) and then all four bounded-operand reverse queries \(Q_{ab}=W_0^*U_{ab}\). For \(R\ge1\), define the bounded lower initialized programs

\[
L_{ab,R}=\ell_a^2R\tanh(Q_{ab}/R).
\]

Since \(|R\tanh(Q_{ab}/R)|\le|Q_{ab}|\) and \(Q_{ab}\in L^2\), dominated convergence gives \(L_{ab,R}\to L_{ab}\) in lower \(L^2\). Boundedness of the actual operator gives \(W_0L_{ab,R}\to W_0L_{ab}\) in upper \(L^2\). Now append all four forward calls \(W_0L_{ab,R}\), without appending further reverse calls. The named reverse source derivatives, with covariances and response coefficients frozen as required by (H6), are

\[
\partial_{\zeta_{cd}}L_{ab,R}
=\mathbf1_{(a,b)=(c,d)}\ell_a^2\operatorname{sech}^2(Q_{ab}/R).
\]

Thus the only reverse response term of this forward call is

\[
W_0L_{ab,R}=\xi_{ab,R}+k_{ab,R}U_{ab},\qquad
k_{ab,R}=E[\ell_a^2\operatorname{sech}^2(Q_{ab}/R)].
\]

There are no extra sequential response terms: the only preceding reverse inputs in this union are the four \(U_{cd}\), and previous appended forward calls do not enter a forward response sum. Dominated convergence gives \(k_{ab,R}\to E[\ell_a^2]\), because the integrand is bounded by one. Consequently \(\xi_{ab,R}\) converges in upper \(L^2\) to \(\xi_{ab}:=W_0L_{ab}-E[\ell_a^2]U_{ab}\). Each finite vector \((Y,\xi_R)\) is centered Gaussian by the bounded-program rule. Its covariance converges by lower \(L^2\) convergence of the operands. Its upper \(L^2\) limit \((Y,\xi_L)\) is therefore the centered Gaussian vector with that limiting covariance: convergence in probability gives convergence of characteristic functions, whose Gaussian formulas converge with the covariance matrices. This verifies the needed unbounded-operand extension without presuming it in (H6).

The resulting upper forward sources \(\xi_L\), jointly with \(Y=\xi_h\), therefore have covariance blocks

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_L)=\operatorname{Gram}(L),\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_L,Y)=\big(E[L_{ab}h_j]\big),\quad
\operatorname{Cov}(Y)=vI_2.
\]

The response part of each query is \(E[\ell_a^2]U_{ab}\), hence a function of \(Y\). Conditioning the joint Gaussian source on \(Y\) therefore yields

\[
F_{ab}=m_{ab}(Y)+Z_{ab},
\tag{2}
\]

where \(m_{ab}\) is measurable, \(Z\) is centered Gaussian independent of \(Y\), and

\[
\Sigma:=\operatorname{Cov}(Z)
=\operatorname{Gram}((I-P_h)L).
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(P_h\) is the lower orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{span}(h_1,h_2)\). All these fields have every finite moment: they are finite polynomials in finitely many Gaussian sources with bounded scalar gate factors. The required expectations and conditional covariances are therefore finite.

There is a useful quantitative strict positivity. For any fixed real coefficient array \(t=(t_{ab})\), the lower variable
\(\sum_{ab}t_{ab}L_{ab}\), conditionally on \(g\), has random part
\(\sum_{ab}t_{ab}\ell_a^2\zeta_{ab}\). Subtracting any element of \(\operatorname{span}(h_1,h_2)\) changes only its conditional mean. The conditional-variance identity thus gives

\[
t^T\Sigma t
\ge E\!\left[\sum_{ab,cd}t_{ab}\ell_a^2\Gamma_{ab,cd}t_{cd}\ell_c^2\right]
\ge \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)E[\ell_1^4]\,|t|^2.
\tag{4}
\]

Both constants in the last product are positive. In particular \(\Sigma\) is strictly positive definite.

Every constant linear combination of the cubic \(K_{3,a}\) label coefficients can be written

\[
\sum_{ab}r_{ab}(Y)F_{ab},
\]

where each \(r_{ab}\) is a polynomial in \(H_1,H_2\). This follows directly from the displayed formulas for \(R,J,K_3\); no conditional expectation has been taken to construct a column. Hence every \(v_3\in V_{2,3}\) has the form

\[
v_3=c\cdot U+r(Y)\cdot F.
\]

If \(v_3\in\mathcal H_Y\), (2) implies

\[
0=\operatorname{Var}(v_3\mid Y)=r(Y)^T\Sigma r(Y).
\]

By (4), \(r(Y)=0\) almost surely. Therefore \(v_3=c\cdot U\), proving (1). The converse inclusion in (1) was immediate from the definitions.

For \(H_a\), membership in \(\operatorname{span}U\) would be a polynomial identity on \((-1,1)^2\). Comparing the four cubic monomials again forces all coefficients to zero, incompatible with the nonzero linear target. For \(Y_a\), every fixed linear combination of the bounded \(U\)'s is bounded, while \(Y_a\) is a nondegenerate Gaussian with unbounded support. This proves the last assertion.

The point is stronger than a count of columns: the new p3 columns introduce genuine upper randomness without improving the exact span of initial-upper-only functions. A Gaussian-chaos proof cannot simply replace the new columns by their conditional means.

## 4. A uniform obstruction for arbitrarily enriched first-response coefficients

The previous argument has a stronger consequence than the finite p3 statement. Allow every measurable coefficient array \(r(Y)\) for which the displayed field is square integrable, and consider the enlarged family

\[
\mathcal A=\{c\cdot U+r(Y)\cdot F:c\in\mathbb R^4\}.
\]

This is much larger than the p3 span. It also contains the upper feedback additions M and P of P7_DERIVATION.md, because those fields are linear in the \(F_{ab}\) with coefficients depending only on \(Y\). Define the positive weight

\[
\rho(Y)=\frac{1}{1+m(Y)^T\Sigma^{-1}m(Y)}.
\]

**Proposition 3.** For every \(T\in\mathcal H_Y\),

\[
\inf_{v\in\mathcal A}\|T-v\|_2^2
=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R^4}E[\rho(Y)(T-c\cdot U)^2].
\tag{6}
\]

For each of \(T=H_1,H_2,Y_1,Y_2\), the right side is strictly positive.

To prove the identity, fix c and abbreviate \(D=T-c\cdot U\). Independence in (2) gives the conditional error

\[
(D-r^Tm)^2+r^T\Sigma r.
\]

Its unique pointwise minimizer is

\[
r_*(Y)=\frac{D\,\Sigma^{-1}m}{1+m^T\Sigma^{-1}m},
\]

as is checked either by differentiating this positive quadratic form or by substitution and completing the square. The minimum is \(\rho D^2\). This minimizing field is admissible: both
\((r_*^Tm)^2\le D^2\) and \(r_*^T\Sigma r_*\le D^2/4\), so \(r_*^TF\) is square integrable. Integration and then minimization in c prove (6).

The finite-dimensional span of U is closed in the weighted Hilbert space \(L^2(\rho\,dP)\), and \(\rho>0\) almost surely. Membership in that span would therefore be the same almost-sure equality already excluded in Proposition 2. Thus its weighted distance from each stated target is positive. No explicit numerical value of the distance is asserted.

Accordingly, arbitrary coefficient enrichment of the *linear first-response sector* cannot recover the missing initial seeds. An affirmative all-order proof has to exploit a different mechanism, such as the nonlinear response products and further Gaussian queries appearing from K5 onward. Proposition 3 does not bound those later sectors and does not refute the actual full dictionary.

There is a parallel lower statement. The four \(L_{ab}\), conditionally on g, form a nondegenerate Gaussian vector because their covariance is the positive diagonal gate matrix times \(\Gamma\) times that same matrix. Replacing \((Y,U,F)\) by \((g,h,L)\) in the conditional quadratic minimization proves a positive weighted distance from any target \(\tanh(g\cdot x)\) outside \(\operatorname{span}(h_1,h_2)\), even after arbitrary g-dependent coefficient enrichment linear in L. If \(x_1x_2\ne0\), that target is outside the span: an almost-sure identity would hold everywhere by continuity, whereas its mixed second derivative is

\[
-2x_1x_2\tanh(g\cdot x)\operatorname{sech}^2(g\cdot x),
\]

which is not identically zero; the mixed derivative of a linear combination of \(\tanh g_1,\tanh g_2\) is zero. Again, later nonlinear reverse-source products are not covered by this lower obstruction.

The lower estimate can be made uniform in g within this enlarged first-response sector. Write \(\mu\) for the 4-by-2 matrix \((\mu_{ab,j})\), and \(D_g\) for the diagonal 4-by-4 matrix with entries \(\ell_a^2\). Its conditional mean and covariance are \(D_g\mu h\) and \(D_g\Gamma D_g\), respectively. Consequently

\[
\rho_g=\frac{1}{1+(\mu h)^T\Gamma^{-1}(\mu h)}
\ge \frac{1}{1+2\|\Gamma^{-1/2}\mu\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}=:\rho_*>0.
\]

For \(h_x=\tanh(g\cdot x)\), therefore,

\[
\inf_{c,r}\|h_x-c\cdot h-r(g)\cdot L\|_2^2
\ge\rho_*\left(E[h_x^2]-\frac{E[h_xh_1]^2+E[h_xh_2]^2}{v}\right)>0
\]

whenever \(x_1x_2\ne0\). The infimum allows every square-integrable resulting field with measurable coefficients r. The last positivity follows from the nonmembership just proved and the Gram matrix \(vI_2\). This uniform bound is for the specified first-response enlargement, not for the complete order-p dictionary.

## 5. What an all-order triangularity argument must still prove

Let \(V_{2,\infty}=\overline{\bigcup_p V_{2,p}}\) for the natural nested continuation, and let \(C_Yv=E[v\mid Y]\). For any seed target \(T\in\mathcal H_Y\) and any dictionary vector \(v\), orthogonality of conditional expectation gives the exact decomposition

\[
\|T-v\|_2^2
=\|T-C_Yv\|_2^2+E[\operatorname{Var}(v\mid Y)].
\tag{5}
\]

Thus an all-order seed-density proof must construct actual **linear combinations of retained columns** for which the conditional-mean error and the surviving-innovation variance both tend to zero. Approximation of the conditional means alone is insufficient. At p3, (4) quantifies the innovation penalty. At larger orders, new source directions appear and old-source polynomial products occur; (4) does not supply an order-independent lower bound for their combined cancellation problem.

Even proving \(V_{2,p}\cap\mathcal H_Y=\operatorname{span}U\) at every finite p would not settle the closure. For example, in a Hilbert space with orthonormal vectors \(e_0,e_1,\ldots\), each finite span of \(e_0+e_j\), \(1\le j\le N\), intersects \(\operatorname{span}(e_0)\) only at zero, but

\[
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N(e_0+e_j)\longrightarrow e_0.
\]

Conversely the span of \(e_0+2^je_j\) stays a positive distance from \(e_0\): the vector \(e_0-\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}e_j\) is a nonzero orthogonal separator. The sizes and joint covariance of the successive innovations decide which behavior occurs. Nonzero conditional variance at every finite order is not an infinite-order counterexample.

The lower initial-input problem has the same form with \(C_g=E[\cdot\mid g_1,g_2]\): to recover \(h_x=\tanh(g\cdot x)\), one needs both approximation of its conditional mean and cancellation of reverse-source fluctuations in actual retained linear combinations. The initial columns generate the required sigma algebra, but do not by themselves establish those two bounds.

## 6. Route status and exact remaining obligation

The finite-order propositions are proved directly from the permitted Gaussian source law, including its response terms. The route has not proved nor disproved density of the infinite derivative spans. It has ruled out two shortcuts:

1. Treating absent initial preactivation columns as an information-theoretic obstruction; the first factors already determine those preactivations measurably.
2. Treating conditional means of derivative columns as if they belonged to their linear span; Proposition 2 supplies an explicit counterexample in the actual population construction.

The next decisive mathematical object is the noise-versus-mean quadratic form (5) for the complete order-p derivative factors. A positive order-independent separator for a required target would refute the corresponding span-density bridge. A construction driving both terms to zero, together with the same result for off-axis initial lower features and the needed action/update fields, would support the missing convergence bridge in SINGLE_SAMPLE_GLOBAL_BOUND.md. Neither a vanishing ridge nor finite Taylor coefficient containment establishes that construction.

No new seeds, operator words, angular probes, nonlinear polynomial enrichment or dense initialized background have been added to the proposed model.
