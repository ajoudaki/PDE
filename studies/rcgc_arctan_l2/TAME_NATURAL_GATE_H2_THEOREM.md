# One-action theorem for every tame natural gate

**Status:** proved after three independent hostile reconstructions.

## 1. Theorem

Let \(\phi\) be any tame natural gate in
[the frozen activation interface](../rcgc_program_history/TAME_NATURAL_GATE_CLASS.md). On
\(\mathbb R^n\), use

\[
 \langle v,w\rangle_n=n^{-1}v^{\mathsf T}w .
\]

Let \(A_0,u_0\) have iid standard Gaussian coordinates, let
\(G_0\) have iid \(N(0,1/n)\) entries, and make these three sources
independent. For

\[
 f_n(A,u,G)
 =\left\langle A,\phi\!\left(G\phi(u)\right)\right\rangle_n ,
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

train \(A,u\) in their normalized Hilbert metrics and \(G\) in the
ordinary Frobenius metric. Fix \(y_\star\in\mathbb R\) and
\(\eta\ge0\), and use full squared-loss flow. Then the calculus constructs a deterministic
immutable pointed Gaussian action and a unique global autonomous limiting
IDE. For every finite physical horizon \(T\),

\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 \bigl(
 |f_n-f|+|K_n-K|+|e_n-e|+|e_n^2-e^2|
 \bigr)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0 .                     \tag{1.2}
\]

The theorem applies to every member of the infinite family

\[
 \phi_m(x)=\int_0^x\frac{du}{1+u^{2m}},\qquad
 m\in\mathbb N,\quad m\ge1,
\]

and its \(m=1\) specialization is the canonical depth-two arctangent
model.

The probability quantifiers are as follows. For every fixed gate in the
possibly uncountable tame class, the convergence event may depend on that
gate. For the displayed countable integer family, one may take a single
probability-one event by intersection over \(m\in\mathbb N\). No uniform
event over the full uncountable activation class is claimed.

Besides (1.2), convergence of the training state means convergence of every
fixed bounded-Lipschitz compiled empirical observable and joint law. Finite
and limiting parameter fields live in different spaces: no cross-width
\(L^2\) or trace-norm subtraction of parameter states is asserted.

## 2. Exact compiler output

Put \(d=\phi'\), define

\[
 \Theta(x)=\int_0^x\frac{du}{d(u)},\quad
 r=\Theta(u),\quad \iota=\Theta^{-1},\quad
 \psi=\phi\circ\iota,\quad c=d\circ\iota .              \tag{2.1}
\]

The NaturalCoordinate rule gives the exact current fields

\[
 X=\psi(r),\quad Z=GX,\quad Y=\phi(Z),\quad
 B=A\,d(Z),\quad Q=G^*B                                  \tag{2.2}
\]

and exact feature flow

\[
 \boxed{A'=Y,\qquad r'=Q,\qquad G'=B\otimes_n X.}       \tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed, the original bottom equation is
\(u'=d(u)G^*B\), while \(\Theta'(u)=1/d(u)\). Also

\[
 \psi'(r)
 =\phi'(\iota(r))\iota'(r)=c(r)^2.                     \tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently the exact raw readouts are

\[
 f_n=\langle A,Y\rangle_n,                              \tag{2.5}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 K_n=\langle Y^2\rangle_n
 +\langle B^2\rangle_n\langle X^2\rangle_n
 +\langle c(r)^2Q^2\rangle_n=f_n'.}                    \tag{2.6}
\]

No activation-specific identity other than the generic natural-coordinate
definition has been used.

## 3. Immutable one-action source

### Lemma 3.1 (one projective source from fixed programs)

Let \(\mathscr P\) be the countable two-sorted language generated by
independent row and column Gaussian marks \(a_0,u_0\), a Ginibre letter
\(g\) and its formal adjoint \(g^*\), rational constants, the coordinate
maps declared by the tame-gate interface, Lipschitz saturations at integer
levels, finite scalar combinations, and normalized empirical moments. Close
\(\mathscr P\) under rational linear combinations and finite parallel
joins. Scalar-parametrized coordinate maps and tests are required to be
pseudo-Lipschitz jointly in their coordinate and scalar arguments.

For every fixed finite program in this language, Tensor Programs III,
Theorem E.15, applies after taking \(a_0,u_0\) as the jointly Gaussian input
G-vars and creating \(r_0=\Theta(u_0)\) as the first coordinatewise program
line. (The pair \((u_0,r_0)\) is not jointly Gaussian and is not declared as
such.) All later program nonlinearities and tests are pseudo-Lipschitz of
finite degree, and the initial matrix has variance \(1/n\). The theorem
gives the joint almost-sure limit of all program empirical moments without a
rank-stability hypothesis and with genuine forward/transpose reuse. The
exact invocation boundary is audited in
[the fixed-mesh source audit](../rcgc_program_history/audits/TENSOR_PROGRAM_FIXED_MESH_AUDIT.md).

Take the simultaneous probability-one event over \(\mathscr P\) (and,
later, rational meshes). Any finite family of program fields occurs in one
parallel-join program, so its **joint** empirical law converges there.
Projection of a joined finite-width program onto a subfamily is exact;
hence the limiting finite-dimensional laws are projectively consistent.
The countable Kolmogorov realization gives row and column probability
algebras \((\Omega_R,\mathcal F_R,\mathbb P_R)\) and
\((\Omega_C,\mathcal F_C,\mathbb P_C)\), not merely unrelated Hilbert
pairings. Set \(H_R=L^2(\Omega_R)\), \(H_C=L^2(\Omega_C)\). The realized
program algebra retains coordinatewise multiplication, bounded Nemytskii
maps, and the Gaussian mark \(a_0\), which are needed by \(A\,d(Z)\) and
the Osgood estimate.

Quotient the rational span of program fields by equality in \(L^2\). On
these dense quotient algebras set

\[
 \Gamma[p]=[gp],\qquad \Gamma^\dagger[q]=[g^*q].
\]

Linearity follows from the exact finite program identities. On the common
Bai--Yin event, the inequality
\(\|G_{0,n}p_n\|_n\le\|G_{0,n}\|_{\rm op}\|p_n\|_n\)
passes jointly for every program representative and gives
\(\|\Gamma[p]\|_2\le2\|[p]\|_2\), and similarly for
\(\Gamma^\dagger\). It also proves that a null representative has a null
image, so the maps are well defined on the quotient. Passing the exact
finite identity

\[
 \langle G_{0,n}p_n,b_n\rangle_n
 =\langle p_n,G_{0,n}^*b_n\rangle_n
\]

for every pair of program representatives gives
\(\Gamma^\dagger=\Gamma^*\) on the dense algebras. Both maps therefore
extend uniquely and adjointly to all of \(H_C,H_R\). Thus

\[
 \mathfrak G=(H_R,H_C;a_0,r_0,\Gamma)                  \tag{3.1}
\]

is an immutable, deterministic pointed-action source. This construction
depends on the activation interface but not on the label, learning rate, or
future trajectory. Its logical order is fixed-program joint laws, then the
projective source/action, then the Banach ODE below, and only afterward the
Euler identification of that ODE; no trajectory is used to define the
source. \(\square\)

## 4. Autonomous limiting IDE and a-priori bounds

Let \(q\in\mathfrak S_1(H_C,H_R)\), \(G=\Gamma+q\), and interpret (2.2)
using coordinate functional calculus. The compiled feature IDE is

\[
 \boxed{A'=Y,\qquad r'=Q,\qquad q'=B\otimes X,\qquad q(0)=0.} \tag{4.1}
\]

Here
\(\|B\otimes X\|_1=\|B\|_2\|X\|_2\).
Writing \(M_\phi=\|\phi\|_\infty\) and
\(M_d=\|d\|_\infty\), every solution on \([-S,S]\) obeys

\[
 \|A(s)\|_2\le\|a_0\|_2+M_\phi |s|,                    \tag{4.2}
\]

\[
 \|q(s)\|_1
 \le M_\phi M_d
 \left(\|a_0\|_2|s|+\frac{M_\phi}{2}s^2\right),         \tag{4.3}
\]

\[
 \|r(s)\|_2
 \le\|r_0\|_2+
 \int_0^{|s|}\|G(\sigma)\|_{\rm op}
 M_d\{\|a_0\|_2+M_\phi\sigma\}\,d\sigma .              \tag{4.4}
\]

Most importantly,

\[
 |A(s)-a_0|\le M_\phi|s|\quad\text{pointwise}.          \tag{4.5}
\]

These bounds hold identically at finite width. They prevent finite
feature-time escape and preserve a translated Gaussian envelope for the
only unbounded multiplier \(A\).

### Lemma 4.1 (cutoff-uniform slab envelope)

Let \(a_{0,M}=\operatorname{clip}(a_0,[-M,M])\). For every fixed \(S\),
all level-\(M\) solutions constructed below satisfy

\[
 \sup_M\sup_{|s|\le S}
 \bigl(\|A_M\|_2+\|B_M\|_2+\|q_M\|_1+
       \|Q_M\|_2+\|r_M\|_2\bigr)\le C_S.             \tag{4.6}
\]

Indeed, \(\|a_{0,M}\|_2\le\|a_0\|_2\), so (4.2)--(4.3) are uniform in
\(M\). Then
\(\|B_M\|_2\le M_d\|A_M\|_2\),
\(\|Q_M\|_2\le(\|\Gamma\|+\|q_M\|_1)\|B_M\|_2\), and
integration of \(r_M'=Q_M\) gives the remaining bound. The identical
calculation holds at finite width on a common source-operator-norm event.
Thus every coefficient except the explicitly exposed multiplier cutoff has
a compact-slab bound independent of \(M\). \(\square\)

## 5. Well-posedness module

First clip \(a_0\) at level \(M\). Fix a slab \([-S,S]\), choose
\(R_{M,S}>M+M_\phi S\), and in the occurrence of \(A\) inside \(B\)
temporarily replace \(A\) by the pointwise map

\[
 \chi_{M,S}(A)=\operatorname{clip}(A,[-R_{M,S},R_{M,S}]).
\]

This distinction is essential: multiplication
\((A,Z)\mapsto A\,d(Z)\) is not locally Lipschitz
\(L^2\times L^2\to L^2\) on an unrestricted \(L^2\) ball. The map
\(\chi_{M,S}:L^2\to L^2\) is one-Lipschitz and has a fixed
\(L^\infty\) bound. Hence, on the Banach space

\[
 H_R\oplus H_C\oplus\mathfrak S_1(H_C,H_R)
\]

the resulting ambient vector field is locally Lipschitz:
\(\phi,d,\psi\) are globally Lipschitz, multiplication by
\(\chi_{M,S}(A)\) is bounded, \(G\) acts boundedly, and the rank-one map
satisfies the trace-norm identity above. Picard--Lindelöf gives its unique
solution. Equation (4.5), applied from the level-\(M\) seed, shows
\(|A(s)|\le M+M_\phi S<R_{M,S}\), so the auxiliary clip is inactive
throughout the slab. The solution therefore solves the original
level-\(M\) equation. Bounds (4.2)--(4.4) continue it globally by
overlapping slabs.

For uncut solutions the sole non-Lipschitz multiplier difference is

\[
 A\,d(Z)-\widetilde A\,d(\widetilde Z)
 =(A-\widetilde A)d(Z)
 +\widetilde A\{d(Z)-d(\widetilde Z)\}.                \tag{5.1}
\]

The precise Gaussian-multiplier lemma used here is the following. If
\(a_0\) is standard Gaussian, \(\|w\|_\infty\le H\), and
\(0<\delta=\|w\|_2\le H\), then Gaussian tail splitting (equivalently,
Hölder with \(p\asymp\log(eH/\delta)\)) gives

\[
 \|a_0w\|_2
 \le C_H\delta\sqrt{\log(eH/\delta)}.                \tag{5.2}
\]

The \(L^\infty\) hypothesis is essential. It applies with
\(w=d(Z)-d(\widetilde Z)\), because this difference is bounded and its
\(L^2\) norm is at most \(L_d\|Z-\widetilde Z\|_2\). Thus the resulting
modulus \(\omega(\delta)=\delta\sqrt{\log(e/\delta)}\) satisfies
\(\int_{0^+}du/\omega(u)=\infty\).
Using (4.5), the state distance

\[
 \Delta=\|A-\widetilde A\|_2+\|r-\widetilde r\|_2+
          \|q-\widetilde q\|_1
\]

satisfies on every a-priori ball

\[
 \Delta(s)\le\Delta(0)+C_S\int_0^{|s|}
 \{\Delta(\sigma)+\omega(C_S\Delta(\sigma))\}\,d\sigma.
                                                               \tag{5.3}
\]

Bihari--Osgood proves uniqueness and continuous dependence in the
Gaussian-envelope class. For cutoffs \(M'\ge M\), decompose the multiplier
difference using the smaller field \(A_M\). Lemma 4.1 makes every other
coefficient uniform in the cutoff, and direct Grönwall gives

\[
 \sup_{|s|\le S}D_{M,M'}(s)
 \le C_Se^{C_SM}\|a_{0,M'}-a_{0,M}\|_2,              \tag{5.4}
\]

where \(D_{M,M'}\) includes \(A,r,q,B,Q\). The Gaussian clipping tail is
\(O((1+M)e^{-M^2/4})\), uniformly as \(M'\to\infty\), so the cutoff
solutions are Cauchy on each fixed slab. Their restrictions agree on
overlapping slabs by cutoff uniqueness; taking \(M\to\infty\) separately
on each slab and gluing those restrictions produces the unique global
solution of (4.1). Since the immutable \(a_0\) remains in the marked state
and \(A(s)-a_0\) stays bounded, this solution class is closed under restart.
The exponent is linear in \(M\): every other state/action coefficient has
an \(L^2\)-based bound independent of \(M\), and the only \(M\)-dependent
Lipschitz term is
\(\|A_M\{d(Z)-d(\widetilde Z)\}\|_2
\le(M+C_S)L_d\|Z-\widetilde Z\|_2\).
An untracked \(e^{C_SM^2}\) estimate would not suffice.

The chain rule first holds at cutoff. Strong \(L^2\)/trace-norm convergence
and bounded convergence for \(c(r)Q\) then pass (2.5)--(2.6) to the uncut
solution. Hence \(f\in C^1\), \(K\) is finite and continuous, and \(f'=K\).

## 6. Width limit at fixed cutoff

Fix \(M,S\) and an operator-norm initialization event
\(\|G_{0,n}\|_{\rm op}\le L\). Its complement has probability tending to
zero. Unroll explicit Euler with a fixed mesh \(h\). The exact SourceSplit
rule gives at line \(k\)

\[
 G^kX^k=G_0X^k+\sum_{\ell<k}hB^\ell
             \langle X^\ell,X^k\rangle_n,              \tag{6.1}
\]

\[
 (G^k)^*B^k=G_0^*B^k+\sum_{\ell<k}hX^\ell
             \langle B^\ell,B^k\rangle_n.              \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus every fixed mesh is one finite two-sided source program, so the source
theorem identifies all of its fields and moments. Use on \([-S,S]\) the
same auxiliary current-\(A\) clip \(\chi_{M,S}\) from Section 5; the
a-priori estimate proves it inactive for both exact and sufficiently fine
Euler paths. On this genuinely Lipschitz ambient cutoff field the five
elementary estimates for \(X,Z,B,Q\) and
\(B\otimes X\) have constants depending only on \(M,S,L\), never on width.
The ordinary local-defect plus discrete-Grönwall proof therefore yields

\[
 \sup_{|s|\le S}d(U_{n,M}(s),U^h_{n,M}(s))
 \le C(M,S,L)h,                                        \tag{6.3}
\]

and the same estimate separately in the limiting source space. Finite and
limiting states are never subtracted in one norm: their fixed Euler
program observables are the bridge supplied by the source theorem. This
proves uniform compact-feature-time convergence of the state signatures and
of \(f\) after the ordered limits \(n\to\infty\), then \(h\downarrow0\).

### Raw-kernel certificate

The only unbounded raw term not continuous under mere state \(L^2\)
convergence is \(c(r)^2Q^2\). At a fixed auxiliary mesh \(\bar h\), the finite-program
theorem, applied to \(Q^4\), gives

\[
 \max_{k:|k\bar h|\le S}
 \langle |Q^{\bar h}_{n,M}(k\bar h)|^4\rangle_n
 \le C_{\bar h,M,S}
\]

with probability tending to one. For arbitrary scalar fields \(q,v\),

\[
 q^2\mathbf1_{\{|q|>R\}}
 \le4(q-v)^2+2v^2\mathbf1_{\{|v|>R/2\}}.               \tag{6.4}
\]

Use \(q=Q_{n,M}(s)\) and the nearest-mesh
\(v=Q^{\bar h}_{n,M}(s)\). Estimate (6.3), including its uniform-time control of
\(Q\), and Markov's inequality give

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{
\sup_{|s|\le S}\left\langle Q_{n,M}(s)^2
\mathbf1_{\{|Q_{n,M}(s)|>R\}}\right\rangle_n
>4C_{M,S}^2\bar h^2+\frac{8C_{\bar h,M,S}}{R^2}+\delta
\right\}=0                                             \tag{6.4a}
\]

for every \(\delta>0\).

For a prescribed error, first choose \(\bar h\) so the first term is small;
at that fixed auxiliary mesh, choose \(R\) so the second is small.
Equivalently, send \(R\to\infty\) at fixed \(\bar h\) and then
\(\bar h\downarrow0\). The possible explosion of
\(C_{\bar h,M,S}\) is harmless.
This proves square uniform integrability for the exact continuous
\(Q_{n,M}\), uniformly in \(s\). A node-only Euler estimate would not
suffice; the uniform-time estimate in (6.3) is essential.

To pass the varying bounded multiplier, truncate \(Q\) at level \(R\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\{c(r)-c(r^h)\}Q^h\|_2^2
 &\le L_c^2R^2\|r-r^h\|_2^2\\
 &\quad+\langle (Q^h)^2\mathbf1_{\{|Q^h|>R\}}\rangle .
\end{aligned}                                          \tag{6.5}
\]

Use the Lipschitz saturation \(T_R(Q)\) when invoking the fixed-program
source theorem; the indicator occurs only in the deterministic tail
inequality. The comparison mesh here is a **second** mesh \(h\), chosen only
after \(\bar h\) has established UI and after the tail level \(R\) is fixed.
For the bounded observable
\(g_R(r,Q)=c(r)^2T_R(Q)^2\), the within-space exact/Euler error is
\(O(C_Rh)\). Choose \(h\) accordingly and invoke TP with fixed
\(M,R,h\), then take \(n\to\infty\). This two-mesh order avoids any
circular competition between \(C_{\bar h}/R^2\) and \(C_Rh\). It proves
uniform convergence of the third term in (2.6). The other two terms use
bounded \(X,Y,d\) and the cutoff \(A\).
Therefore, at fixed \(M\),

\[
 \sup_{|s|\le S}\{|f_{n,M}-f_M|+|K_{n,M}-K_M|\}
 \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.                            \tag{6.6}
\]

No Tensor Program theorem with a growing number of mesh lines has been
invoked.

## 7. Cutoff removal and physical time

Compare the uncut trajectory with the level-\(M\) trajectory by placing
the bounded \(A_M\) in the multiplier slot:

\[
 A\,d(Z)-A_Md(Z_M)
 =(A-A_M)d(Z)+A_M\{d(Z)-d(Z_M)\}.                      \tag{7.1}
\]

Ordinary Grönwall on the common a-priori event gives, uniformly in width,

\[
 \sup_{|s|\le S}
 \{d(U_n,U_{n,M})+\|Q_n-Q_{n,M}\|_2\}
 \le C_Se^{C_SM}\|A_{0,n}-A_{0,n}^{(M)}\|_2.           \tag{7.2}
\]

The Gaussian tail beats \(e^{C_SM}\), so (7.2) vanishes in the ordered
limit \(n\to\infty\), then \(M\to\infty\), and likewise in the population
source. Applying (6.4) transfers square uniform integrability from a fixed
large cutoff to the uncut \(Q_n\). A second use of (6.5), now comparing
\(r_n\) and \(r_{n,M}\), passes the weighted square. Thus

\[
 \sup_{|s|\le S}\{|f_n(s)-F(s)|+|K_n(s)-K(s)|\}
 \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.                            \tag{7.3}
\]

The epsilon order is explicit: choose \(M\) to control
uncut-versus-cutoff; at that fixed \(M\), use one auxiliary fixed mesh to
establish square UI (call it \(\bar h\)); choose a saturation level \(R\);
choose the distinct final comparison mesh \(h\); and only then send
\(n\to\infty\) through one fixed
Tensor Program. No diagonal choice \(M_n,R_n,h_n\) is used or implied.

Finally ScalarLossClock gives

\[
 \dot s=2\eta\{y_\star-F(s)\},\qquad F'=K\ge0.          \tag{7.4}
\]

The residual sign is preserved and
\(|s(t)|\le2\eta|y_\star|t\). At finite width,
\(s_n'=2\eta\{y_\star-F_n(s_n)\}\) and
\(|s_n(t)|\le2\eta|y_\star-F_n(0)|t\). Since the initial row mark is
independent of the bounded top feature,
\(\mathbb E f_n(0)^2\le M_\phi^2/n\), so all clocks lie in one compact
feature interval with high probability. Uniform scalar-ODE stability and
(7.3) prove (1.2).

The final physical IDE is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \dot A&=2\eta eY,&
 \dot r&=2\eta eQ,&
 \dot q&=2\eta e\,B\otimes X,\\
 \dot e&=-2\eta eK,&
 f&=\mathbb E[AY]=y_\star-e .
\end{aligned}}                                         \tag{7.5}
\]

It is autonomous in the current marked action state
\((A,r,q,e;\mathfrak G)\); the mesh, cutoff, and proof clock do not appear
in the final system.
