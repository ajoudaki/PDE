# Program Contract: Causal Flow Peeling Calculus

## Status and purpose

This is the frozen non-vacuity contract for the Causal Flow Peeling Calculus (CFPC).  It specifies the finite-width model, the limiting object, the required convergence, and the conditions under which a proposed calculus counts as a genuine reduction.  It is a specification, not a theorem.

The first nonlinear target is the one-sample, three-hidden-layer arctangent feature flow.  The contract deliberately permits an infinite-dimensional limiting state, but only when that state has finitely many **types**, a local autonomous evolution rule, and computably convergent finite truncations.  Calling an entire path, an unbounded response registry, or the original sequence of finite networks a “state” does not satisfy the contract.

## 1. Finite-width reference model

For each width `n`, put

\[
H_{\ell,n}=\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
\langle v,w\rangle_n=\frac1n v^\top w,
\qquad
\|v\|_n^2=\langle v,v\rangle_n.
\]

The adjoint of a matrix for these equal normalizations is its ordinary transpose.  For `b,x in H_n`, define the normalized rank-one map

\[
(b\otimes_n x)v=b\langle x,v\rangle_n.
\]

Fix a hidden depth `L >= 1`.  The trainable state is

\[
Y_n=(u,A,G_1,\ldots,G_{L-1}).
\]

For `z_1=u`, the forward pass is

\[
x_1=\phi(z_1),
\qquad
z_{\ell+1}=G_\ell x_\ell,
\qquad
x_{\ell+1}=\phi(z_{\ell+1}),
\]

and the scalar predictor is

\[
f_n=\langle A,x_L\rangle_n.
\]

The reverse pass is

\[
b_L=A\odot\phi'(z_L),
\qquad
r_\ell=G_\ell^*b_{\ell+1},
\qquad
b_\ell=\phi'(z_\ell)\odot r_\ell
\quad(1\leq \ell<L).
\]

In raw feature time the exact finite-width ascent flow is

\[
\dot A=x_L,
\qquad
\dot G_\ell=b_{\ell+1}\otimes_n x_\ell,
\qquad
\dot u=b_1.
\tag{F}
\]

For a unit-norm single input this is the corresponding scaled parameter gradient.  A scalar loss multiplier produces only a scalar time change on any interval where that time change is well defined; the calculus must first prove (F), then may transfer it to a specified loss.

The raw optimizer-metric tangent kernel is

\[
\Theta_n=
\|x_L\|_n^2
+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}
\|b_{\ell+1}\|_n^2\|x_\ell\|_n^2
+\|b_1\|_n^2.
\tag{K}
\]

The initialization is

\[
G_\ell(0)=\Gamma_\ell,
\qquad
(\Gamma_\ell)_{ij}=n^{-1/2}g_{\ell,ij},
\qquad
g_{\ell,ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\]

with the source matrices independent across layers and independent of exchangeable/iid seed coordinates for `u(0)` and `A(0)`.  The principal calibration uses centered unit-variance Gaussian seeds.  Any extension to other seeds must state its moment and independence assumptions.

The source `Gamma_l` and its transpose are two orientations of the **same** random matrix.  Replacing a transpose reuse by an independent Gaussian copy changes the model and is forbidden.

## 2. Concrete activation and admissible extensions

The principal nonlinear activation is

\[
\phi(s)=\arctan s,
\qquad
\phi'(s)=\frac1{1+s^2}.
\]

The calculus may exploit boundedness, smoothness, analyticity on the real axis, the rational differential chain generated by `(s-i)^{-1}` and `(s+i)^{-1}`, or explicit derivative majorants.  It may not assume a finite Hermite expansion or an unproved finite-dimensional moment closure.

The one-hidden-layer generic-activation gate uses an explicitly stated admissible class.  At minimum, its assumptions must ensure existence and uniqueness of the coordinate flow, propagation of the moments needed by (K), and continuity of the predictor and kernel.  Stronger smoothness assumptions are permitted when they are used by a reusable unary-functional-calculus rule and not inserted solely to reproduce a known answer.

## 3. What constitutes the limiting state

Let `A_L` be a typed observable algebra generated by:

- current vector fields at each layer;
- current learned inter-layer morphisms and their adjoints;
- one immutable Gaussian source generator per inter-hidden layer, with forward and transpose orientations tied together;
- normalized inner products and rank-one maps;
- coordinatewise activation/differential-chain operations;
- the finite response, influence, or marked-edge types required by the calculus.

A valid limiting state at time `t` consists of:

1. finitely many named current-time species in a complete graded space;
2. a positive normalized character `chi_t` evaluating closed observables;
3. fixed source generators shared by every time and every observable;
4. a local vector field or derivation `F_infinity` constructed from the calculus operations;
5. a projective family of finite truncations whose error tends to zero by a proved, computable certificate.

The state may contain infinitely many grades or diagram coefficients.  It may not contain an arbitrary function of two times, the full path prefix, one species for every past gradient step, or an oracle whose value is defined as “the limit of the desired finite-width quantity.”

The limiting equation must be autonomous and restartable:

\[
\dot {\mathcal S}(t)=\mathcal F_\infty(\mathcal S(t)),
\qquad
\mathcal S(0)=\mathcal S_0,
\tag{IDE}
\]

in a stated complete topology.  Integral notation is acceptable, but the future after time `t` must be determined by `S(t)` and the fixed source law, not by an unstated history.

## 4. Required theorem for the arctangent target

For hidden depth `L=3` and every finite horizon `T` on which the limiting solution is certified, the calculus must establish all of the following.

### C1. Existence and uniqueness

The autonomous IDE has a unique solution on `[0,T]` in the certified state class.  For arctangent, a global-in-feature-time theorem is preferred; if only a local theorem is proved, the continuation criterion must be explicit and independently verifiable.

### C2. Finite-width well-posedness and uniform envelope

With probability tending to one, the finite flow exists on `[0,T]` and remains in a width-uniform envelope strong enough for every product and observable used below.  Bare normalized `L^2` bounds do not suffice when the proof uses multiplication or activation-gated adaptive transpose signals.

### C3. Single-source compact-time state convergence

For every fixed finite family of typed observables `P` in the dense calculus algebra and every required finite moment order,

\[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left|
\operatorname{ev}_{n,t}(P)-\chi_t(P)
\right|\longrightarrow0
\]

in the stated probabilistic mode, with uniform integrability whenever products are passed to the limit.  All observables are evaluated using one joint source construction; convergence of separately recoupled marginals is insufficient.

### C4. Predictor and raw-kernel convergence

In particular,

\[
\sup_{t\le T}|f_n(t)-f_\infty(t)|\to0,
\qquad
\sup_{t\le T}|\Theta_n(t)-\Theta_\infty(t)|\to0,
\]

in probability and in every `L^p` explicitly claimed.  The formula for `Theta_infinity` must be obtained by evaluating (K) in the limiting state, not introduced independently.

### C5. Quantitative completion certificate

There must be a finite approximation `S^(N,h)` and an error decomposition of the form

\[
\operatorname{Err}_{n,N,h}(T)
\le
C_T\bigl(
\varepsilon_{\rm init}(n,N)
+\varepsilon_{\rm width}(n,N)
+\varepsilon_{\rm trunc}(N)
+\varepsilon_{\rm time}(h,N)
\bigr),
\tag{E}
\]

or a demonstrably equivalent certificate, where each term is proved from local calculus rules, the limits can be taken in a stated order, and the right side tends to zero.  A first-order Euler argument must exhibit an `O(h^2)` local consistency defect and stable accumulation to `O(h)` global error, or use another integrator with its corresponding orders.

### C6. Projective and restart consistency

Truncations and time meshes must converge to one state.  In particular, changing the mesh may not silently resample Gaussian innovations, and restarting a truncation at an intermediate time must have a defect that vanishes with the truncation.

### C7. Depth recursion

Adding a hidden layer may enlarge a finite type signature or introduce another source color, but may not require a qualitatively new convergence oracle.  The same inference rules and master estimates must cover all fixed depths for which their explicit continuation bounds hold.

### C8. Effective computability

For fixed `T`, tolerance `epsilon`, depth, and activation certificate, the calculus must in principle generate a finite system and a checkable bound below `epsilon`.  “In principle” permits impractically large enumeration; it does not permit an undecided infinite hierarchy or a truncation tail asserted without a modulus.

## 5. Mandatory calibration ladder

No depth-three nonlinear claim is admissible until the same implementation and certificate system recovers, without importing tailored proof identities:

1. linear hidden depth `L=1`;
2. linear hidden depth `L=2`;
3. linear hidden depth `L=3`;
4. generic admissible activation at `L=1`;
5. arctangent activation at `L=2`.

For deep linear ascent, claims are restricted to compact intervals before the maximal solution time unless global existence is separately proved.  Passing a rung means more than writing its formal limiting equations: it requires the finite-width-to-limit bridge and the appropriate specialization of (E).

Fixed finite-input extensions are pressure tests, not substitutes for the single-input target.  Input labels become a finite color set and the theorem must remain stable at singular Gram matrices; no inverse-Gram lower bound may be imposed merely to avoid identical or symmetric inputs.

## 6. What counts as a genuine machinery reduction

A new lemma counts as progress only if:

- its hypotheses are expressed in locally propagated grades/certificates;
- each hypothesis has a concrete recursive verification route;
- its conclusion eliminates an infinite family of adaptive contractions, response chains, or time steps at once;
- its proof does not invoke the desired compact-time convergence or an equivalent tail/stability assertion;
- the resulting subproblem is structurally simpler, for example a finite graph grammar, a scalar majorant system, or a standard Banach-space theorem with all assumptions checked.

Examples of non-reductions include:

- “assume the adaptive Gaussian query remains asymptotically Gaussian”;
- “assume all response norms stay bounded” without a closed majorant;
- “assume every unpromoted diagram has a width deficit” without a finite grammar or induction proving preservation;
- “complete the diagrams in a suitable norm” without proving that source action, nonlinear composition, multiplication, and time evolution are continuous in that norm;
- a conditional theorem whose certificate is exactly the original target restated in new notation.

## 7. Decisive falsifiers and rollback rules

The current machinery candidate is rejected or substantially redesigned if any of the following is established on an earlier rung:

1. the proposed graded norm is infinite already for `arctan(Z)` or loses all positive radius under the one-color `L=2` flow;
2. a fixed low-grade observable changes by order one as the truncation cutoff increases;
3. an unrepresented zero-width-defect cumulant or response primitive is generated;
4. the finite-width query rule needs a tail bound not implied by the propagated certificate;
5. the local width error lacks the time-step factor needed for stable accumulation;
6. the restart or semigroup defect does not vanish;
7. the `L=2` arctangent rung requires a model-specific conserved quantity or transform absent from the calculus rules;
8. the depth-three extension creates a new response species at every order with no proved summation or tail estimate.

Failure of a particular calculus is not evidence that the network limit is false.  It is evidence that the proposed completion mechanism has failed its contract.

## 8. Current claim level

Only the finite-width identities (F) and (K), the program contract, and the separation of proof obligations are asserted here.  No CFPC completion theorem or nonlinear depth-three limit is currently claimed.
