# Conditional query anti-concentration without oddness

Scoped proof route, 2026-10-11. This note proves a conditional
anti-concentration statement for initialized-feature interpolation at an
orthogonal query. It does not prove the requested lower bound for the fully
trained endpoint under the original label cap. Its main point is that two
independent copies of the unused Gaussian query recover anti-concentration
conditional on the **entire active initialization**, even though the available
central limit theorem conditions only on lower layers.

The scientific inputs were `paper/compact.tex`, `paper/compact_fitting.tex`,
`ODD_DEPTH_ENDPOINT.md`, `LINEAR_RESPONSE_ROUTE.md`, and `POSITIVE_ROUTE.md`,
read in full. The rigorous-math and canonical-notation skills, including the
neural-network reference, were applied. No other study, experiment, or paper
dependency was consulted. All constants below concern fixed data, activations,
labels, and depth; no labels shrink with width.

## 1. The conditional law being studied

Use the canonical width-
\(n\) network
\[
h^{(1)}(x)=\phi_1(W^{(1)}x/\sqrt d),\qquad
h^{(\ell)}(x)=\phi_\ell(W^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)}(x)),\qquad
f_n(x)=w^\top h^{(L)}(x)/n.
\]
Here \(m,L\ge2\), all training inputs satisfy \(\|x_a\|=\sqrt d\), and
all data and activations are fixed before width increases.
First-layer entries are independent standard Gaussians; hidden entries have
variance \(1/n\); all blocks are independent. The activations satisfy the
strip analyticity and bounded-derivative assumptions in `paper/compact.tex`.
Training directions \(v_a=x_a/\sqrt d\) span a proper subspace \(S\), and
the fixed unit query \(v\) lies in \(S^\perp\). The final population training
Gram is \(Q=Q^{(L)}\succ0\), and \(y\ne0\).

Let \(\mathcal A_n\) be the sigma field generated by
\(W^{(1)}_0|_S,W^{(2)}_0,\ldots,W^{(L)}_0\). It determines the complete
training trajectory. In particular the initialized training-feature matrix
and its minimum-norm interpolating readout are
\[
B_0=[h^{(L)}_0(x_1),\ldots,h^{(L)}_0(x_m)]/\sqrt n,
\qquad
u_*=B_0(B_0^\top B_0)^{-1}y.
\]
The inverse exists with probability tending to one. On its complement assign
the frozen predictor any value. Put
\[
e_n=\|u_*\|,\qquad e_0^2=y^\top Q^{-1}y>0,
\qquad
F_n(Z)=u_*^\top h^{(L)}_0(Z)/\sqrt n.
\tag{1}
\]
Here the query recursion starts from
\(h^{(1)}_0(Z)=\phi_1(Z)\), with
\(Z=W^{(1)}_0v\sim N(0,I_n)\) independent of \(\mathcal A_n\).
Initialization Gram convergence gives \(e_n\to e_0\) in probability.

Introduce an independent copy \(Z'\) of \(Z\), using the **same** active
initialization and the same readout in both \(F_n(Z)\) and \(F_n(Z')\).
These are auxiliary independent draws of the unused block at the same query.
They need not be realizable as two orthogonal directions in the original
input space. In particular this construction does not assume
\(\dim S^\perp\ge2\).

## 2. The paired population coefficient

Define scalar diagonal and cross moments recursively by
\[
q_0=1,\quad c_0=0,\qquad
q_\ell=\mathbb E\phi_\ell(\sqrt{q_{\ell-1}}G)^2,
\qquad
c_\ell=\mathbb E[\phi_\ell(U)\phi_\ell(V)],
\tag{2}
\]
where \(G\sim N(0,1)\) and \((U,V)\) is centered Gaussian with variances
\(q_{\ell-1}\) and covariance \(c_{\ell-1}\). The augmented population
Gram for the training coordinates and the two query copies has both
training-query columns equal to \(c_\ell\mathbf1\), both query diagonals
equal to \(q_\ell\), and query-query entry \(c_\ell\). This follows at the
first layer from independence of the query columns; the Gaussian-row
recursion then preserves those equalities.

Every activation is nonconstant under \(\gamma=\lambda_{\min}(Q)>0\).
Indeed, a constant activation would make all training features identical
from that layer onward, leaving the final Gram with rank at most one. Also
\(q_\ell>c_\ell\ge0\) at every layer. For nonnegativity, the normalized
Hermite expansion at a nonnegative correlation gives
\(c_\ell=\sum_{k\ge0}a_k^2(c_{\ell-1}/q_{\ell-1})^k\ge0\).
For strict inequality, start with \(q_0>c_0\), and note that the Gaussian
pair in (2) has positive-definite covariance. If its two activated values
were equal almost surely, full support and continuity would force the
activation to be constant. Thus
\(2(q_\ell-c_\ell)=\mathbb E(\phi_\ell(U)-\phi_\ell(V))^2>0\).

Let \((X,U,V)\) be the final-layer Gaussian preactivation vector, where
\(X\in\mathbb R^m\) contains the training coordinates and \(U,V\) the two
query coordinates. Its covariance is the layer-\((L-1)\) augmented Gram.
Define
\[
H=(\phi_L(X_a))_{a=1}^m,\qquad
T=H^\top Q^{-1}y,\qquad
V_{\rm pair}
=\mathbb E[(\phi_L(U)-\phi_L(V))^2T^2].
\tag{3}
\]
The mean of \((\phi_L(U)-\phi_L(V))T\) is zero, because the two population
cross columns coincide. All of its moments are finite by linear activation
growth and Gaussian moments. In particular (3) is its variance.

The coefficient is strictly positive. To verify this without assuming a
nonsingular training covariance at layer \(L-1\), set
\[
A=(U+V)/2,\qquad B=(U-V)/2,
\qquad \sigma^2=(q_{L-1}-c_{L-1})/2>0.
\]
The Gaussian variable \(B\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\) is independent of \((X,A)\):
its covariance with every training coordinate vanishes because the two
cross columns agree, and its covariance with \(A\) vanishes because the two
query variances agree. This independence also holds when \((X,A)\) has
singular covariance, by factorization of the joint Gaussian characteristic
function. Moreover \(A\) has positive variance
\((q_{L-1}+c_{L-1})/2\).

The real analytic nonzero function \(\phi_L'\) has isolated zeros, so
\(\phi_L'(A)\ne0\) almost surely. Also
\(\mathbb ET^2=e_0^2>0\). For each \(a\) with \(\phi_L'(a)\ne0\), the
function \(b\mapsto\phi_L(a+b)-\phi_L(a-b)\) has nonzero derivative at
zero, and hence has positive squared expectation under the Gaussian law of
\(B\). Conditioning on \((X,A)\) proves
\[
V_{\rm pair}>0.
\tag{4}
\]

An explicit positive lower expression is available from the real second
derivative bound \(|\phi_L''|\le\beta\). If
\(|b|\le|\phi_L'(a)|/(2\beta)\), the derivative on the interval from
\(a-|b|\) to \(a+|b|\) retains its sign and has magnitude at least
\(|\phi_L'(a)|/2\). Therefore
\[
V_{\rm pair}\ge
\mathbb E\left[
 T^2\phi_L'(A)^2\,
 \mathbb E_B\left(B^2\mathbf1_{
 |B|\le|\phi_L'(A)|/(2\beta)}\right)
\right]>0.
\tag{5}
\]
This bound depends only on the displayed population law and activation. It
does not assert a lower bound in terms of \(q_L\) and the upper envelope
alone.

## 3. Annealed paired spread gives fully conditional anti-concentration

For every fixed \(c>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\Pr\{|F_n(Z)-F_n(Z')|\le c/\sqrt n\}
\le2\Phi(c/\sqrt{V_{\rm pair}})-1,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function. The probability
in (6) averages over both the active initialization and the two independent
unused query columns.

Here are the conditional central limit theorem details relevant to (6).
Let \(S_n\) be the empirical layer-\((L-1)\) augmented Gram for training,
query \(Z\), and query \(Z'\). Conditional Gaussian-row averaging and
continuity of Gaussian activation expectations imply
\(S_n\to S_0\) in probability, with \(S_0\) the population augmented
Gram. The proof applies to these auxiliary query columns exactly as to a
finite ordinary query panel. At the top layer, condition on all preceding
layers and both query columns. The neuron rows are then independent with
the Gaussian transform law determined by \(S_n\).

For a covariance \(S\) near \(S_0\), let \(Q(S)\) be its top-feature
training Gram and \(d(S)\) the difference of its two top-feature
training-query columns. The corresponding conditional population predictor
difference is
\(d(S)^\top Q(S)^{-1}y\). At \(S_0\), \(d(S_0)=0\), and the first-order
empirical interpolation influence is precisely
\((\phi_L(U)-\phi_L(V))T\). In general the influence is
\[
\left[\phi_L(U)-\phi_L(V)-d(S)^\top Q(S)^{-1}H\right]
 H^\top Q(S)^{-1}y.
\]
The inverse is uniformly bounded near \(S_0\). Gaussian moments and linear
growth uniformly bound the third absolute moments of these influences, and
their variances converge to \(V_{\rm pair}\). Expanding the inverse around
\(Q(S)\) leaves an \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})\) interpolation remainder.
The characteristic-function expansion
\(\mathbb E e^{it\Psi/\sqrt n}
=1-t^2\mathbb E\Psi^2/(2n)+O(n^{-3/2}\mathbb E|\Psi|^3)\)
therefore proves, along every deterministic \(S_n\to S_0\), a centered
normal limit with variance \(V_{\rm pair}\).

Convergence to a continuous normal law is uniform over interval endpoints:
a finite partition into normal quantiles bounds the distribution-function
error between partition points and in the tails. It is also locally uniform
as \(n\to\infty,S\to S_0\), since failure would give a deterministic
convergent subsequence contradicting the preceding argument. The
conditional population centering can thus be left uncontrolled. A centered
normal has maximal mass in the centered interval among intervals of fixed
length, so averaging the conditional interval bound proves (6). This step
does **not** condition on \(\mathcal A_n\); that stronger conditioning is
obtained next by a separate argument.

Write \(\nu_n\) for the conditional law of \(F_n(Z)\) given
\(\mathcal A_n\), and put
\[
b_n(r)=\Pr\{|F_n(Z)-F_n(Z')|\le2r\mid\mathcal A_n\}.
\]
For any interval \([a-r,a+r]\), independence of \(Z,Z'\) conditional on
\(\mathcal A_n\) gives
\[
\nu_n([a-r,a+r])^2\le b_n(r).
\tag{7}
\]
The bound remains true after taking the supremum over \(a\). For any
\(0<\delta<1\), choose
\[
c_\delta=\frac{\delta^3\sqrt{2\pi V_{\rm pair}}}{16}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The density bound for the normal law and (6) imply
\[
\limsup_n\mathbb E b_n(c_\delta/\sqrt n)
\le \frac{4c_\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi V_{\rm pair}}}
=\delta^3/4.
\]
Consequently this expectation is at most \(\delta^3/2\) for every sufficiently
large individual width. Markov's inequality and (7) show
\[
\Pr_{\mathcal A_n}\left\{
 \sup_{a\in\mathbb R}
 \Pr_Z\{|F_n(Z)-a|\le c_\delta/\sqrt n\mid\mathcal A_n\}
 \le\delta
\right\}\ge1-\delta/2.
\tag{9}
\]
Equation (9) is the promised anti-concentration conditional on all active
training initialization. It is uniform over arbitrary
\(\mathcal A_n\)-measurable centers, including the conditional mean. It
requires no assertion that top neurons remain independent after conditioning
on that sigma field. Singular empirical training Grams only add a vanishing
failure probability; assigning the frozen predictor arbitrarily there was
already allowed in (6).

For example, on the event in (9),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z(F_n\mid\mathcal A_n)
\ge(1-\delta)c_\delta^2/n.
\tag{10}
\]
This follows by taking \(a=\mathbb E_ZF_n\) in (9) and integrating only
outside the displayed interval.

## 4. What is still needed at the actual endpoint

Suppose a separate argument gives, on an active event of probability tending
to one, a centered endpoint comparison
\[
\left\|R_n-\mathbb E_ZR_n\right\|_{L^2_Z}
\le\frac{D e_n}{\sqrt n}(Y/\lambda)^2,
\qquad
R_n(Z)=f_n(\infty,\sqrt d\,v)-F_n(Z),\quad
\lambda=\gamma/m.
\tag{11}
\]
This note does not prove (11) for nonodd lower layers. Absolute means of
\(R_n\) need not be small. They can be absorbed into the arbitrary center
in (9).

For every center \(a\), use the triangle inequality on the event
\(|R_n-\mathbb E_ZR_n|\le c_\delta/(2\sqrt n)\). Equations (9), (11),
and conditional Chebyshev yield
\[
\sup_a\Pr_Z\left\{
 |f_n(\infty,\sqrt d\,v)-a|\le\frac{c_\delta}{2\sqrt n}
 \mid\mathcal A_n\right\}
\le\delta+\frac{4D^2e_n^2(Y/\lambda)^4}{c_\delta^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
The same upper bound controls the conditional small-ball probability for
the difference from an independent trained run: condition additionally on
that run's full value and then average. Thus (11) would transfer the frozen
bound if its right side were sufficiently small relative to
\(\sqrt{V_{\rm pair}}/\sqrt n\). Since \(e_n\to e_0\), the dimensionless
comparison parameter is
\[
\frac{D e_0(Y/\lambda)^2}{\sqrt{V_{\rm pair}}}.
\tag{13}
\]
Positivity of \(V_{\rm pair}\) alone does not bound (13). In particular,
making \(c_\delta\) small to obtain higher conditional confidence makes
the allowed remainder smaller too.

## 5. The diagonal lower bound does not control this comparison

Here is a family entirely within the nonodd, nonaffine, unbounded activation
class. Fix \(L\ge2\), take \(m=2,d=3\), training directions \(e_1,e_2\),
and orthogonal query \(e_3\). At every layer let
\[
\phi_\varepsilon(z)=1+\varepsilon(z+\tanh z),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1/10.
\tag{14}
\]
These functions are nonodd, nonaffine, unbounded, holomorphic on
\(|\operatorname{Im}z|<1\), and have uniformly bounded first and second
derivatives on the half strip. The common envelope can be chosen as
\(\beta=16\): \(|\operatorname{sech}^2(x+iy)|\le1/\cos^2(1/2)\), and
\(|\tanh(x+iy)|\le1\) for \(|y|\le1/2\), which bounds the derivatives
of (14) below 16. Also \(1+|\phi_\varepsilon(0)|=2\).

The input coordinates in this example are mutually orthogonal. Their
population feature Gram at every layer is equicorrelated, with diagonal
\(q_\ell\) and off-diagonal \(c_\ell\) from (2). Since
\(z+\tanh z\) is odd and every preactivation is centered Gaussian,
\(\mathbb E\phi_\varepsilon(Z)=1\), and hence \(q_\ell\ge1\).
Thus \(q_L\ge\beta^{-40L}\) holds with a very large margin. Section 2
gives \(\gamma_\varepsilon=q_L-c_L>0\), and for sufficiently small
\(\varepsilon\), \(\lambda_\varepsilon=\gamma_\varepsilon/2\le1\).

Choose equal labels
\(y=(Y_\varepsilon,Y_\varepsilon)^\top\), with
\(Y_\varepsilon=\lambda_\varepsilon16^{-30L}>0\). Each member of this
family has labels fixed independently of width and satisfies the original
cap exactly. The label amplitude varies only between different activation
problems. For these labels,
\[
\frac{V_{\rm pair}}{e_0^2}
=\frac{\mathbb E[(H_*-H_*')^2(H_1+H_2)^2]}{2(q_L+c_L)}.
\tag{15}
\]
At every fixed depth, \(q_\ell,c_\ell\to1\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This follows inductively from the Gaussian
square-root coupling and the uniform linear-growth bound. In the same
coupling the top features converge to one in every fixed finite
\(L^p\), so the numerator of (15) tends to zero, while its denominator
tends to four. Therefore
\[
V_{\rm pair}/e_0^2\longrightarrow0,
\qquad V_{\rm pair}>0\text{ for every fixed }\varepsilon>0.
\tag{16}
\]
Hence the original diagonal condition and upper activation envelope cannot
replace the comparison parameter (13) by a fixed positive lower scale.
This is an obstruction to transferring an **absolute** centered-error
estimate with only its envelope constant. It is not a counterexample to the
actual endpoint lower bound. A sharper estimate tracking the same small
activation variation could still succeed.

There is a related valid quantitative observation. Let \(K_L(t)\) be the
population scalar kernel for two unit inputs of inner product \(t\).
Its convergent power expansion has nonnegative coefficients: at each layer
the Hermite expansion has nonnegative coefficients in the preceding
correlation, and composition preserves this property. Therefore
\(Q-c_L\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\succeq0\), with diagonal \(q_L-c_L\).
Let \(P\) project onto \(\mathbf1^\perp\). Then
\[
(m-1)\gamma\le\operatorname{tr}(PQ)
=\operatorname{tr}(P(Q-c_L\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top))
\le m(q_L-c_L),
\]
so \(q_L-c_L\ge\gamma/2\) for \(m\ge2\). This controls the unweighted
paired feature variance. It does not by itself control the weighted
quantity (3), or remove the comparison requirement (13).

## 6. A full conditional covariance lower bound is false

The tempting direct endpoint route would seek
\(\operatorname{Cov}_Z(h^{(2)}(Z))\succeq cI_n\), with a fixed \(c>0\),
uniformly over sufficiently small trained perturbations of the second
matrix. For general nonodd activations this already fails at initialization,
where the perturbation is zero.

Take two hidden layers and
\[
\phi_1(z)=1+\varepsilon(z+\tanh z),\qquad
\phi_2(z)=\log(1+e^z),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1/10.
\tag{17}
\]
Both are nonodd, nonaffine, and unbounded. For \(|\operatorname{Im}z|<1\),
\(1+e^z\) has positive real part, so its principal logarithm is
holomorphic. Writing \(r=e^{\operatorname{Re}z}\) and
\(t=\operatorname{Im}z\),
\[
|\phi_2'(z)|^2=\frac{r^2}{1+2r\cos t+r^2}\le1,
\qquad
|\phi_2''(z)|=\frac r{1+2r\cos t+r^2}
\le\frac1{2(1+\cos t)}.
\]
These verify the full-strip first derivative bound and half-strip second
derivative bound directly. The preceding checks for \(\phi_1\) show that
the pair admits the paper's common choice \(a=1,\beta=16\).

Condition on the active initialization and write \(W=W^{(2)}_0\), with
row \(w_i^\top\), row sum \(b_i=\sum_jW_{ij}\), and independent
\(Z_j\sim N(0,1)\). Put \(\eta(z)=z+\tanh z\). The row query
preactivation is exactly
\[
W_i\phi_1(Z)=b_i+\varepsilon\sum_jW_{ij}\eta(Z_j).
\]
Oddness of \(\eta\), the bound \(|\eta(z)|\le2|z|\), and the elementary
Gaussian exponential moment give, for every real \(t\),
\[
\mathbb E e^{t\varepsilon\eta(G)}
=\mathbb E\cosh(t\varepsilon\eta(G))
\le\mathbb E\cosh(2t\varepsilon G)
=e^{2\varepsilon^2t^2}.
\]
Since \(\log(1+e^x)\le e^x\) for real \(x\), independence of the
coordinates proves the deterministic conditional bound
\[
\operatorname{Var}_Z(h^{(2)}_{0,i}(Z))
\le\mathbb E_Z h^{(2)}_{0,i}(Z)^2
\le\exp(2b_i+8\varepsilon^2\|w_i\|^2).
\tag{18}
\]

Under Gaussian initialization the \(b_i\) are independent standard normal
variables. Their minimum is at most \(-\sqrt{\log n}\) with probability
tending to one. For a direct check, integrate the standard normal density
over \([a,a+1/a]\), with \(a=\sqrt{\log n}\), to obtain
\(\Pr\{G\ge a\}\ge C n^{-1/2}/\sqrt{\log n}\) for a fixed \(C>0\).
Thus \(n\Pr\{G\ge a\}\to\infty\), and independence proves the claim.
Also \(\max_i\|w_i\|\le2\) with probability tending to one: the
chi-square exponential moment gives
\(\Pr\{\|w_i\|^2>4\}\le e^{-n}2^{n/2}\), and a union bound over
\(n\) rows tends to zero. Intersecting these two events in (18) yields
\[
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\operatorname{Cov}_Z(h^{(2)}_0(Z))\right)
\le\min_i\operatorname{Var}_Z(h^{(2)}_{0,i}(Z))
\le e^{-2\sqrt{\log n}+32\varepsilon^2}
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{19}
\]
No independence between a row's sum and norm was used.

This example satisfies the other endpoint assumptions. Use the two
orthogonal training directions and orthogonal query from Section 5. The
first-layer training covariance has diagonal
\(q_1=1+\varepsilon^2\mathbb E\eta(G)^2>1\) and off-diagonal one,
so the Gaussian entering the second layer has full support. A linear
combination of its two softplus coordinates cannot vanish identically
unless both coefficients vanish; varying one coordinate at a time proves
this. Thus the final training Gram has \(\gamma>0\). Its equal-diagonal
form and the real 1-Lipschitz property of softplus give
\(\gamma=q_2-c_2\le q_1-c_1\le4\varepsilon^2\), so
\(\gamma/m\le1\). Jensen's inequality gives
\(q_2\ge(\mathbb E\phi_2(\sqrt{q_1}G))^2\ge(\log2)^2\), which
implies the required diagonal lower bound. Choose any fixed positive
label amplitude below \((\gamma/m)16^{-60}\).

Equation (19) rules out the full-matrix bound even on the zero-perturbation
member of a proposed uniform neighborhood. It does **not** rule out a
lower bound for the covariance quadratic form evaluated at the actual
fitted readout. Such a proof would need to show that sufficient readout
energy remains in neurons with substantial query variance, and that
covariances with the remaining neurons cannot cancel that contribution.
Neither step is supplied by the full-matrix argument refuted here.

The proved output is (9): a positive, width-independent scale for
anti-concentration conditional on the complete active initialization, valid
at arbitrary fixed depth without oddness or bounded activation values. The
unresolved endpoint obligations are a suitable nonodd centered comparison,
and a relative bound that makes its error smaller than the conditional
query variation under the original label cap. No endpoint theorem is
claimed here.
