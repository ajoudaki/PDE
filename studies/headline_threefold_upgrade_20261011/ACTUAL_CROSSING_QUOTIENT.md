# Analytic zero sets sharpen the crossing conclusion

Scoped continuation by uniform_time_scope, 2026-10-11.
ACTUAL_TIME_BRIDGE.md remains unchanged. This note proves an additional
conditional statement, not nonexactness of any particular compressor.

## 1. Statement and exact remaining condition

Use precisely the two-layer canonical example of ACTUAL_TIME_BRIDGE.md:
\(m=d=L=2\), normalized training inputs \(e_1,e_2\), normalized passive
input \(v_*=(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2\), labels \(Y(1,-1)\), and activations
\(\phi(z)=z+\tanh z\), \(\psi(z)=\tanh z\). The physical passive input is
\(x_*=\sqrt2v_*\). The fixed \(Y>0\) is the admissible choice in that note.

For every width \(n\) and fixed time \(t>0\), the actual prediction
\(f_n(t,x_*)\) has a non-atomic law under its canonical Gaussian
initialization.

Let \(f_{{\rm comp},n}(t,x_*)\) be any random compressed predictor determined
by the primary initialization and possibly additional randomness. Require
the second dense initialization to be independent of the joint sigma-field
generated by the primary initialization and all compressor randomness;
separate marginal independence would not suffice. Suppose its sample paths are real
analytic on an open interval containing \([1,2]\). Define
\[
 e_n(t)=f_{{\rm comp},n}(t,x_*)-f_n(t,x_*),\qquad
 g_n(t)=f_n(t,x_*)-\widetilde f_n(t,x_*),
\]
and let \(N_n\) be the event that \(e_n\) is not identically zero on
\((1,2)\). Then
\[
 \Pr\{N_n,\ \exists t\in(1,2):
                  e_n(t)=g_n(t)=0\}=0.                    \tag{1}
\]
On \(N_n\), every interior crossing of the dense difference therefore
has nonzero compressed error, almost surely. On the strict sign-change
event in ACTUAL_TIME_BRIDGE.md, this implies
\[
 \sup_{\substack{1<t<2\\g_n(t)\ne0}}
       \frac{|e_n(t)|}{|g_n(t)|}=+\infty
 \quad\hbox{almost surely on }N_n.                         \tag{2}
\]
Thus the strengthening no longer depends on any convention for \(0/0\).
If additionally \(\Pr(N_n^c)\to0\), the positive-probability actual crossing
proved in the frozen candidate yields a probability bounded away from zero
of the genuine divergence (2).

The nonexactness hypothesis \(N_n\), and analytic well-posedness of the
specific compressed construction, are explicit conditions. Neither is
assumed proved for the current Taylor construction by this note.

## 2. Actual finite-time flow is analytic in its initialization

Write \(A=W^{(1)}\), \(B=W^{(2)}\), \(w(0)=0\), and, for \(a=1,2\),
\[
 P_a=\phi(Ae_a),\quad Z_a=BP_a,\quad H_a=\psi(Z_a),\quad
 f_a=n^{-1}w^\top H_a,\quad c_a=y_a-f_a .
\]
The exact canonical flow is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot A_{:a}
 &=c_a\,\phi'(A_{:a})\odot B^\top[w\odot\psi'(Z_a)],\\
 \dot B&=n^{-1}\sum_{a=1}^2c_a[w\odot\psi'(Z_a)]P_a^\top,\\
 \dot w&=\sum_{a=1}^2c_aH_a .
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{3}
\]
The vector field is real analytic on the entire real parameter space.

For arbitrary finite real initial \(A_0,B_0\), not only Gaussian typical
points, the flow exists for every finite positive time. Indeed, decreasing
squared loss gives
\[
 \|c(t)\|_2\le\sqrt2Y,\quad \sum_a|c_a(t)|\le2Y,
 \quad \|w(t)\|_\infty\le2Yt .
\]
Here \(|\psi|,|\psi'|\le1\), \(|\phi'|\le2\), and
\(|\phi(z)|\le2|z|\) on the real line. For
\[
 M_A(t)=\|A(t)\|_{\rm F}/\sqrt n,\qquad
 M_B(t)=\|B(t)\|_{\rm F},
\]
equation (3) consequently gives the upper norm-derivative bounds
\[
 D^+M_A(t)\le8Y^2t\,M_B(t),\qquad
 D^+M_B(t)\le8Y^2t\,M_A(t).                                 \tag{4}
\]
For the first, bound the column sum using
\(\|B\|_{\rm op}\le M_B\) and \(\|w\|_n\le2Yt\).
For the second use the outer-product identity
\(\|bc^\top/n\|_{\rm F}=\|b\|_n\|c\|_n\) and
\(\max_a\|P_a\|_n\le2M_A\).
Gronwall yields
\[
 M_A(t)+M_B(t)
 \le [M_A(0)+M_B(0)]e^{4Y^2t^2}.                            \tag{5}
\]
Together with the readout bound this rules out finite-time escape.

The local analytic-dependence theorem for an analytic finite-dimensional
ODE now applies along every finite trajectory. Its hypotheses here are:
an open real parameter domain, a real-analytic vector field, local
uniqueness, and a solution remaining in a compact subset on the finite
time interval. Analytic local solution maps can be composed over finitely
many neighborhoods covering that trajectory. It follows that
\[
 (A_0,B_0)\longmapsto f_n(t,x_*)                            \tag{6}
\]
is real analytic at every real initial point for each fixed \(t>0\).
The same theorem makes each actual sample path real analytic in time on
every open positive-time interval.

## 3. An explicit nonconstant-initialization witness at every time

Fix \(a>0\). Consider the deterministic initial parameters
\[
 (A_0)_{11}=a,\qquad (B_0)_{11}=\varepsilon ,
\]
with every other entry of \(A_0,B_0\) and all entries of \(w(0)\) zero.
These points need not have positive Gaussian probability; they witness that
the globally analytic map (6) is not constant.

Only the first neuron of each hidden layer and the first readout coordinate
can become nonzero. This is an invariant subsystem: inactive first-layer
rows have zero corresponding \(B\)-columns; inactive upper rows and readout
coordinates have zero updates; the remaining \(B\)-entries are outer products
with inactive lower features or inactive readout coordinates.
Write its two lower weights as \(a_1(t),a_2(t)\), its scalar hidden mixer
as \(b(t)\), and its readout as \(w(t)\). Initially
\[
 a_1(0)=a,\quad a_2(0)=0,\quad b(0)=\varepsilon,\quad w(0)=0 .
\]

At \(\varepsilon=0\) the trajectory is stationary and every output is zero.
Differentiate this scalar subsystem with respect to \(\varepsilon\) at zero.
The first variations of both \(a_1,a_2\) vanish, because their updates
contain the product \(bw\). Since \(\psi'(0)=1\), the remaining first
variations \(b_1,w_1\) solve exactly
\[
 \dot b_1=\frac{Y\phi(a)}n\,w_1,\qquad
 \dot w_1=Y\phi(a)b_1,\qquad b_1(0)=1,\quad w_1(0)=0 .
\]
For \(k=Y\phi(a)/\sqrt n>0\), their solution is
\[
 b_1(t)=\cosh(kt),\qquad w_1(t)=\sqrt n\,\sinh(kt).
\]
Analytic parameter dependence therefore gives, at every fixed \(t>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b(t)&=\varepsilon\cosh(kt)+O(\varepsilon^2),\\
 w(t)&=\varepsilon\sqrt n\,\sinh(kt)+O(\varepsilon^2),\\
 a_1(t)&=a+O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 a_2(t)=O(\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
At the passive input the active lower preactivation is
\((a_1+a_2)/\sqrt2\). Consequently
\[
 f_n(t,x_*)
 =\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\sqrt n}\,
   \phi(a/\sqrt2)\sinh(kt)\cosh(kt)+O(\varepsilon^3).
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
The displayed coefficient is strictly positive for every \(n\ge1,t>0\).
Thus (6) is nonconstant for every such \(n,t\), whereas its value at
\(\varepsilon=0\) is zero. No width-dependent label choice or frozen
hidden-weight replacement was used.

## 4. Atomlessness and the conditional zero-set argument

A nonconstant real-analytic scalar function on connected Euclidean space
has Lebesgue-null level sets. A short justification is useful here.
Subtract a putative level value. At every zero, some finite-order Taylor
derivative is nonzero; otherwise the function vanishes on a neighborhood
and hence everywhere by analytic continuation on the connected domain.
Choose a direction in which a derivative of that finite order is nonzero.
It stays nonzero on a sufficiently small neighborhood. Along every line
parallel to that direction, the one-dimensional function has at most that
many zeros in the neighborhood, by repeated Rolle's theorem.
Fubini shows its local zero set has measure zero. A countable neighborhood
cover proves the global assertion.

The canonical Gaussian law of \((A_0,B_0)\) has a full Lebesgue density,
and the variance rescaling in \(B_0\) is nonsingular. Equations (6)--(7)
therefore imply
\[
 \Pr\{f_n(t,x_*)=z\}=0
 \quad\hbox{for every fixed }t>0,\ z\in\mathbb R.             \tag{8}
\]

Condition now on the primary initialization and all randomness used to
construct its compressed predictor. On \(N_n\), the fixed analytic function
\(e_n\) has a discrete, hence countable, zero set \(Z_n\) in \((1,2)\).
For each of these conditionally fixed times \(s\), independence and (8)
give
\[
 \Pr\{\widetilde f_n(s,x_*)=f_n(s,x_*)
       \mid\hbox{primary and compression}\}=0.
\]
Taking the countable union over \(s\in Z_n\) proves (1).
The usual measurability requirement is only that the random predictors
are jointly measurable with continuous sample paths. If desired, the
isolated zeros can be measurably enumerated by their unique occurrence in
closed rational subintervals; each zero has such an isolating interval.

Finally, on the strict sign-change event, \(g_n\) is not identically zero
and has an interior zero \(\tau\in(1,2)\). Its analyticity makes that zero
isolated with finite order. By (1), \(e_n(\tau)\ne0\) on \(N_n\) almost
surely. Continuity of \(e_n\) and \(g_n\), approaching \(\tau\) through
nearby nonzero values of \(g_n\), proves (2). In particular, deleting all
zero-denominator times does not make the local supremum finite.

## 5. Claim boundary

This note closes the actual fixed-time atomlessness obligation and proves
the conditional analytic-numerator strengthening. It does not establish
that the current selected Taylor predictor is nonexact at this test input,
or that it has an analytic sample path for all required states.
If those two construction-specific properties hold with probability
tending to one, the candidate crossing theorem gives a convention-independent
counterexample to its uniform same-time ratio. Without verifying them, the
unconditional conclusion remains the actual dense crossing theorem and
the failure of every strictly positive simultaneous denominator bound.
