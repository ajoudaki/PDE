# Restoring self-gates on every learned backward interaction

This theorem is a further step beyond the all-supplied-gate oracle and the
top-gate restoration. Every layer now uses its own backward activation gate
on its learned interaction. Only the initialized backward branches continue
to receive the dense gate. At zero initial readout the top gate is entirely
self-generated. Residuals, the old clock, all histories and all learned
matrices are generated by the system itself.

The result is uniform-in-width O(P^-1) trajectory and test-prediction error
for arbitrary fixed depth and arbitrary finite training inputs, under the
same RMS/operator initial bounds as the earlier oracle. It does not assume
coordinatewise bounds on the initialized readout or on backpassed Gaussian
fields. The dense limit's existence/regularity remains the given premise.

## 1. Construction

Use the paper's canonical tanh flow, L>=2 hidden layers, samples
q_a=x_a/sqrt(d), and initialized matrices W_(0,l) and readout w_0.
All neuron spaces carry probability measures. Vector norms are population
L2, or finite-width RMS; hidden increments use Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
equal to ordinary Frobenius norm at width n. A tensor u tensor v means
g -> u E[v g], represented at width n by uv^T/n.

Write the learned middle increments and learned readout as

\[
 A_\ell=W_\ell-W_{0,\ell},\qquad v=w-w_0.
\]

The dense trajectory supplies D_(l,a)^D(t)=diag tanh'(z_(l,a)^D(t)).
The oracle computes its own forward responses with its complete current
operators W_0+bar A and readout w_0+bar v. Let
bar D_(l,a)=diag tanh'(bar z_(l,a)). Define its backward responses by

\[
 \bar\delta_{L,a}=D_{L,a}^D w_0+\bar D_{L,a}\bar v,
 \tag{1}
\]
\[
 \bar\delta_{\ell,a}
  =D_{\ell,a}^D W_{0,\ell+1}^*\bar\delta_{\ell+1,a}
    +\bar D_{\ell,a}\bar A_{\ell+1}^*\bar\delta_{\ell+1,a},
 \qquad \ell<L.
 \tag{2}
\]

This is an explicit split at each initialized/learned branch; it is not the
usual backpropagation of the oracle's own predictor. The top dense gate is
unused when w_0=0. Every lower dense gate is used only on the W_0 branch.

The oracle uses its own residual bar r_a=bar f_a-y_a, residual RMS bar rho,
and old clock bar tau=1+integral bar rho. First layer and readout follow the
canonical updates with these residuals and (1)--(2). For each hidden link,
the raw Legendre moments store bar h_(l-1,a) and
bar b_(l,a)=(bar r_a/bar rho)bar delta_(l,a) in its own clock, using a constant
initial forward prefix and zero backward prefix. Their reconstruction is

\[
 \bar A_\ell=-\frac2m\sum_a\int_0^{\bar\tau}
   (\Pi_P\bar b_{\ell,a})\otimes(\Pi_P\bar h_{\ell-1,a})\,d\xi.
 \tag{3}
\]

Only the known gates on the initialized branches are external inputs.
All raw ODEs are well-defined at zero residual because their insertion
sources are bar rho times forward responses and bar r times backward responses.

At the dense physical state, bar D=D^D and the two terms of (2) recombine
into D^D W^*delta. Likewise (1) recombines into D_L^D w.
The dense trajectory therefore solves exactly the uncompressed vector
field associated with (1)--(2).

## 2. Statement and explicit bounds

Fix T and assume a regular dense flow on [0,T]. Let

\[
 X=\max_a\|q_a\|,\quad Y=(m^{-1}\sum_a y_a^2)^{1/2},\quad
 R_0=\|w_0\|_2,\quad K_{0,\ell}=\|W_{0,\ell}\|_{\rm op}.
\]

The initial first layer may be any admissible population row field.
No L-infinity assumption on w_0 is made. Use the distance

\[
 d(\bar\theta,\theta)=
 \|\bar W_1-W_1\|_{\rm row,L^2}
 +\sum_{\ell=2}^L\|\bar W_\ell-W_\ell\|_{\rm HS}
 +\|\bar w-w\|_2.
 \tag{4}
\]

For every P>=1, the system (1)--(3) exists uniquely on [0,T] and

\[
 \sup_{t\le T}d(\bar\theta_P(t),\theta_D(t))
 \le\frac{C_{\rm comp}e^{L_TT}}{\sqrt{P(P+1)}}.
 \tag{5}
\]

All constants below depend only on T, fixed data/depth, R_0 and K_(0,l).
They are independent of P and neuron-space dimensions.

The unchanged readout update gives

\[
 \frac d{dt}\|w\|_2^2
 =Y^2-\frac4m\sum_a(f_a-y_a/2)^2\le Y^2 .
\]

Thus both dense and oracle states obey

\[
 R=\sqrt{R_0^2+TY^2},\quad Q=R+Y,\quad S=TQ,\quad A=1+S,
\]
\[
 \|w\|_2\le R,\qquad \rho\le Q,\qquad
 \int_0^T\rho\,dt\le S,\qquad
 \|w-w_0\|_\infty\le2S.
 \tag{6}
\]

The last inequality is pointwise integration of |dot w|<=2rho, and is
valid even if w_0 itself is unbounded pointwise.

Define top-down

\[
 \beta_L=R_0+2S,\qquad
 J_\ell=2\sqrt{AS}\,\beta_\ell,\qquad
 K_\ell=K_{0,\ell}+J_\ell,\qquad
 \beta_{\ell-1}=K_\ell\beta_\ell.
 \tag{7}
\]

Then ||bar A_l||_op<=J_l, ||bar W_l||_op<=K_l and
max_a||bar delta_(l,a)||_2<=beta_l. At the top use (1) and (6).
Given beta_l, projection contraction in (3) bounds its learned operator
by J_l. Equation (2) then bounds the next backward response by
(K_(0,l)+J_l)beta_l=beta_(l-1). This remains valid even though the two
backward branches have different gates. The dense states satisfy the same
bounds, since their exact learned operator has norm at most 2S beta_l.
The first-layer increment is at most 2SX beta_1.

## 3. Key new fact: the dense learned adjoint has bounded outputs

The dense learned operator has its exact history representation

\[
 A_\ell^D(t)
 =-\frac2m\sum_b\int_0^t
       r_b^D(s)\,\delta_{\ell,b}^D(s)
          \otimes h_{\ell-1,b}^D(s)\,ds .
 \tag{8}
\]

For any v in the output-layer L2 space,

\[
 (A_\ell^D(t))^*v
 =-\frac2m\sum_b\int_0^t
     r_b^D(s)\,h_{\ell-1,b}^D(s)
        \langle\delta_{\ell,b}^D(s),v\rangle\,ds .
\]

Each coordinate of h is bounded by one. Cauchy--Schwarz in the neuron
population, followed by m^{-1}sum |r_b|<=rho_D, gives

\[
 \|(A_\ell^D(t))^*v\|_\infty
 \le 2S\beta_\ell\|v\|_2 .
 \tag{9}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \|(A_{\ell+1}^D(t))^*
                \delta_{\ell+1,a}^D(t)\|_\infty
 \le 2S\beta_{\ell+1}^2.
 \tag{10}
\]

This is derived from the exact dense history, not assumed. It uses no
Gaussian independence and no maximum of a backpassed initialized field.
Only the dense learned adjoint needs (9); no P-uniform L2-to-L-infinity
bound for the oracle's reconstructed adjoint is asserted or used.

## 4. Existence of the moment oracle

Population local existence needs care because the self-gate in (2) multiplies
an L2 field. It can be proved by an inactive truncation of the forward moments,
rather than assuming global Hilbert-space Lipschitz regularity.

For the raw forward moment
H_(l-1,a,k)=integral_0^tau h_(l-1,a)(xi) p_k(xi/tau) dxi, every coordinate
obeys

\[
 |H_{\ell-1,a,k}|
 \le \int_0^\tau|p_k(\xi/\tau)|d\xi
 \le\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{2k+1}}\le\frac A{\sqrt{2k+1}} .
 \tag{11}
\]

The middle inequality uses Cauchy--Schwarz and the exact Legendre norm.
The bound holds on every regular interval, independently of all backward
updates, since all actual forward activations are bounded by one.

To construct a locally Lipschitz auxiliary raw ODE, temporarily:

- Clip v=w-w_0 inside the self-gated term in (1) to [-2S-1,2S+1].
- In the self-gated learned-adjoint term in (2) only, replace each forward
  moment H_(l-1,a,k) by its coordinatewise clipping at
  A/sqrt(2k+1)+1. Keep the original moments for forward reconstruction.

Indeed the learned-adjoint action is the finite sum

\[
 \bar A_\ell^*\delta
 =-\frac2{m\tau}\sum_{a,k<P}(2k+1)\,
       H_{\ell-1,a,k}\langle B_{\ell,a,k},\delta\rangle.
\]

After clipping, each H factor is bounded pointwise and Lipschitz in L2.
The product of a varying bounded self-gate with that factor is locally
Lipschitz in L2; the other factors are scalar inner products. Induction
through the finitely many layers establishes local Lipschitz regularity
of the entire finite-order raw moment system. The initialized branch is
a fixed bounded operator followed by a prescribed bounded multiplication
operator. Dense L2-continuous fields supply strongly continuous time
dependence on every fixed L2 operand.

The readout energy and clock bounds (6) still hold for this auxiliary
system. The forward moments still have their integral representations, so
(11) still holds. Both clippings therefore stay inactive. On every regular
interval the actual bounds (7) and the raw moment integral bounds apply.
For a fixed P the resulting state ball has finite uniform local Lipschitz
and velocity bounds, which give continuation to T. Any solution of the
unclipped system has the same inactive bounds, proving uniqueness as well.
At zero residual all raw and outer updates vanish, and the state is stationary.

## 5. Accumulated compression error

Let G_D^split(t,theta) be the uncompressed update defined by (1)--(2),
using the state's own residual. The oracle satisfies exactly

\[
 \dot{\bar\theta}=G_D^{split}(t,\bar\theta)+E,\quad E_1=E_w=0,
\]
\[
 E_\ell=\frac{2\bar\rho}{m}\sum_a
   (\bar b_{\ell,a}-\bar b_{\ell,a}^*)
       \otimes(\bar h_{\ell-1,a}-\bar h_{\ell-1,a}^*).
 \tag{12}
\]

This is the paired-moment identity and does not require a gradient
interpretation for the supplied backward signals. For each history,
D_v=||v-Pi_Pv||^2 satisfies dot D_v=bar rho||v-v^*||^2. Its initial error
is zero on the specified prefixes. Thus

\[
 \int_0^T\|E_\ell\|_{\rm HS}dt
 \le\frac2m\sum_a\sqrt{D_{b,\ell,a}D_{h,\ell-1,a}} .
\]

The forward derivative estimate remains unchanged because it needs only
the response bounds (7), not derivatives of backward gates. Explicit
width- and order-independent constants are

\[
 Z_1=4SX^4\beta_1^2,\qquad
 Z_\ell=3\left[4S\beta_\ell^2+
      (K_\ell^2+2A^2\beta_\ell^2)Z_{\ell-1}\right].
\]

Here Z_l bounds the sample-averaged squared forward derivative integrated
in the oracle's own clock. The H1 Legendre bound gives a squared forward
tail at most A^2 Z_l/[4P(P+1)]. The backward history mass is at most
S beta_l^2. The endpoint norm P/sqrt(tau) yields
integral bar rho||E_l/bar rho||_HS^2 <=2A^2 beta_l^2 Z_(l-1),
which closes the displayed forward derivative recursion by the chain rule.
Consequently

\[
 \int_0^T\sum_{\ell=2}^L\|E_\ell\|_{\rm HS}dt
 \le\frac{C_{\rm comp}}{\sqrt{P(P+1)}},\qquad
 C_{\rm comp}=A\sum_{\ell=2}^L\beta_\ell\sqrt{S Z_{\ell-1}} .
 \tag{13}
\]

No smooth-backward-history or matching-clock assumption enters (13).

## 6. One-reference stability with all learned self-gates

We need comparison only with the dense reference; uniform Lipschitz control
between arbitrary population states is neither used nor claimed.
Let d denote (4), evaluated against that reference. Forward preactivation
and activation errors are at most U_l d, where

\[
 U_1=X,\qquad U_\ell=K_\ell U_{\ell-1}+1 .
\]

At the top, the initialized readout term cancels. With v_D=w_D-w_0,

\[
 \bar\delta_L-\delta_L^D
   =\bar D_L(\bar w-w_D)
      +(\bar D_L-D_L^D)v_D .
\]

Since ||v_D||_infinity<=2S and |tanh''|<=2,

\[
 \|\bar\delta_{L,a}-\delta_{L,a}^D\|_2
 \le V_Ld,\qquad V_L=1+4S U_L.
 \tag{14}
\]

For a lower layer, subtraction of (2) gives exactly

\[
 \bar\delta_\ell-\delta_\ell^D
 =\bigl[D_\ell^D W_{0,\ell+1}^*
            +\bar D_\ell\bar A_{\ell+1}^*\bigr]
                       (\bar\delta_{\ell+1}-\delta_{\ell+1}^D)
 +\bar D_\ell(\bar A_{\ell+1}-A_{\ell+1}^D)^*
                       \delta_{\ell+1}^D
 +(\bar D_\ell-D_\ell^D)(A_{\ell+1}^D)^*
                       \delta_{\ell+1}^D.
 \tag{15}
\]

The first bracket has operator norm at most K_(l+1). The second term is
bounded by beta_(l+1)||bar W_(l+1)-W_(l+1)^D||_HS. By (10), the last
term is at most 4S beta_(l+1)^2||bar z_l-z_l^D||_2. Thus

\[
 V_\ell=K_{\ell+1}V_{\ell+1}+\beta_{\ell+1}
                  +4S\beta_{\ell+1}^2U_\ell,\qquad
 \max_a\|\bar\delta_{\ell,a}-\delta_{\ell,a}^D\|_2\le V_\ell d .
 \tag{16}
\]

These coefficients are finite at fixed depth and independent of width/P.

At a common residual, the canonical velocity differences have sum norm at
most rho Lambda d, where

\[
 \Lambda=2\left[
  XV_1+\sum_{\ell=2}^L(V_\ell+\beta_\ell U_{\ell-1})+U_L\right].
\]

The residual difference is bounded by C_f d, with C_f=1+R U_L.
Changing the residual at fixed responses costs at most
C_r=2[X beta_1+sum_(l=2)^L beta_l+1] times its sample RMS. Therefore

\[
 \|G_D^{split}(t,\bar\theta)-G_D^{split}(t,\theta_D)\|_{\rm sum}
 \le L_T d,\qquad L_T=Q\Lambda+C_r C_f .
 \tag{17}
\]

Every estimate involving a varying gate used the dense learned carrier
bound (10), not a bound on an arbitrary oracle carrier.
The dense trajectory satisfies dot theta_D=G_D^split(t,theta_D).
Subtracting this equation from (12), integrating, and using (17) yields
d(t)<=L_T integral_0^t d+integral_0^t sum||E_l||. The scalar integrating
factor gives (5) from (13).

## 7. Consequences and the one remaining kind of gate forcing

For a test input x repeat the forward recurrence with U_1=||x||/sqrt(d).
Then

\[
 |\bar f_P(t,x)-f_D(t,x)|
 \le[1+R U_L(\|x\|/\sqrt d)]\,d(\bar\theta_P(t),\theta_D(t)).
\]

This proves O(P^-1) uniform prediction error on every bounded input set,
and L2(nu) error for test distributions with finite second input moment.
The difference of test RMSEs against one common target is bounded by this
prediction discrepancy. If dense finite-width approximation has error a_n(T),
the coupled width-n oracle has error at most a_n(T)+C_T/P against the
population predictor, on the stated common initial bounds.

To restore full self-backpropagation, replace D_l^D by bar D_l also on
the initialized branches. The additional gate products, after an exact
one-reference subtraction, are

\[
 (\bar D_{\ell,a}-D_{\ell,a}^D)
      W_{0,\ell+1}^*\delta_{\ell+1,a}^D
       \quad(\ell<L),\qquad
 (\bar D_{L,a}-D_{L,a}^D)w_0 .
 \tag{18}
\]

Terms propagating backward differences have bounded operator coefficients,
so (18) isolates the new reference forcing. At w_0=0 the top term vanishes.
For bounded nonzero w_0 it is controlled by its sup norm, as in the complete
top-gate theorem. Initialized Gaussian operators generally supply only an
L2 bound for W_0^*delta, not the L-infinity estimate (9). Thus (18) remains
uncontrolled in the full unrestricted closure theorem.

This is a strict reduction of the oracle intervention: previously all
backward gates were supplied; now self-gates are restored on every learned
interaction and, at zero readout, entirely at the top. The unresolved
feedback is localized to the initialized branches. This does not remove
the exact W_0 actions from the model or claim the autonomous theorem.
