# Harmonic signed terms near complete lower collapse

Frozen scoped theoretical note, 2026-09-19. Scientific input scope: the supervisor's model statement only. No other study, established source, earlier finding, or numerical experiment was consulted. This note uses direct calculations; no specialized external theorem is invoked. Results are internally derived, not independently reviewed or promoted.

## Outcome and scope

The first nonzero signed Taylor term is harmonic in every lower-field plane obtained by varying the same scalar profile in the two physical coordinates. This statement concerns the signed correlation, not necessarily the leading term of the full loss. Under explicit nondegeneracy, the cubic and quintic cases give arbitrarily close lower-loss states.

Neither harmonicity of that term nor its combination with odd predictions and a positive squared-loss remainder implies a null attraction basin in the ambient gradient flow. An explicit analytic polynomial squared loss below has all those properties and an open attraction basin. It is a proof-route counterexample, **not** a realization in the supplied tanh population model. An actual open basin in that model is not established here.

The missing model-specific implication is therefore substantive: one would have to exclude stabilizing higher-order motion along directions where the leading harmonic polynomial degenerates, and justify conclusions for the full physical flow rather than a frozen slice. The canonical degree-2/3 mark construction supplies the boundedness, positive Gram matrix and nonatomicity used below, but these calculations do not use its polynomial structure to close that implication.

## Exact setup

Write the lower and upper probability spaces as \(\Omega _1,\Omega _2\). The lower field is \(w\in L^\infty(\Omega _1;\mathbb R^2)\); the upper field is \(c\in L^\infty(\Omega _2)\). The fixed marks are \(b_1\in L^\infty(\Omega _1;\mathbb R^k)\), \(b_2\in L^\infty(\Omega _2;\mathbb R^\ell)\), and \(M\in\mathbb R^{\ell\times k}\). Let

\[
a(v)=\mathbb E_1[b_1\tanh(w\cdot v)],\qquad
F(w;v)=\mathbb E_2[c\tanh(b_2^TMa(v))].
\]

There are finitely many \(v_i\in S^1\), positive \(\mu_i\), and real labels \(y_i\). Put

\[
s_i=\mu_i y_i,\quad T_r=\sum_i s_i v_i^{\otimes r},\quad
L=\sum_i\mu_i(F(w;v_i)-y_i)^2,\quad L_0=\sum_i\mu_i y_i^2.
\]

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, Taylor expansions below hold at \(w=0\) with \(c,M\) fixed. They are expansions in the \(L^\infty\) norm. All remainders follow from finite Taylor expansion of tanh and bounded marks, fields, and matrix entries; differentiation and expectation interchange because the corresponding local Taylor remainders are uniformly bounded.

Define

\[
B w=\mathbb E_1[b_1w^T]\in\mathbb R^{k\times2},\quad
d=\mathbb E_2[c b_2],\quad q=M^Td,\quad g=b_1^Tq.
\]

At complete lower collapse, every prediction is zero. Derivatives with respect to \(c,M\) vanish there, and the lower first variation is

\[
DL(0)[h]=-2\sum_i s_i\mathbb E_1[g h]\cdot v_i
=-2\mathbb E_1[g h]\cdot T_1.
\]

Thus \(T_1=0\) makes every such collapsed state stationary, independently of the physical metric in the other blocks.

## Taylor structure and the exact harmonic identity

For fixed \(c,M\), \(F(-w;v)=-F(w;v)\). Its Taylor expansion has only odd degrees:

\[
F(w;v)=F_1(w;v)+F_3(w;v)+F_5(w;v)+\cdots.
\]

Each \(F_r\) is homogeneous of degree \(r\) in \(w\) and of degree \(r\) in \(v\). Indeed, every term is a linear combination of integrals of products of exactly \(r\) factors \(w(\omega)\cdot v\), with coefficients independent of \(v\). Products generated by the upper tanh have the same property.

Suppose \(m\ge3\) is odd and \(T_1=T_3=\cdots=T_{m-2}=0\). Then

\[
C_r(w):=\sum_i s_iF_r(w;v_i)=0\quad(r<m),\qquad
P_m(w):=-2C_m(w).
\]

For every bounded real profile \(\eta\), every bounded field \(w_0\), and \(\xi\in\mathbb R^2\),

\[
\Delta_\xi P_m(w_0+\eta\xi)=0. \tag{1}
\]

To verify (1), apply two \(\xi\)-derivatives to a product of \(m\) factors of the form \((w_0(\omega)+\eta(\omega)\xi)\cdot v_i\). Summing the two physical coordinate derivatives contracts the two removed \(v_i\) factors to \(\lVert v_i\rVert^2=1\). Every resulting term is therefore a polynomial of degree \(m-2\) in \(v_i\), multiplied by coefficients independent of \(i\). Summing against \(s_i\) gives zero by \(T_{m-2}=0\). This proof allows factors belonging to different lower particles, so upper-layer cross terms are included.

The same calculation gives the familiar trace identity

\[
\operatorname{tr}T_m=T_{m-2},\qquad
\Delta_\xi T_m[\xi^{\otimes m}]=m(m-1)T_{m-2}[\xi^{\otimes(m-2)}].
\]

Thus the first nonzero odd signed moment gives a harmonic homogeneous polynomial on \(\mathbb R^2\). Nonzero \(T_m\) does **not** by itself guarantee nonzero \(P_m\): network coefficients can annihilate it. For example, if \(M=0\) and remains fixed, every prediction vanishes for every \(w\).

The full loss is

\[
L-L_0=-2\sum_i s_iF(w;v_i)+\sum_i\mu_iF(w;v_i)^2. \tag{2}
\]

The final term in (2) can precede degree \(m\), is nonnegative, and generally is not harmonic. Hence describing the *leading loss variation* as harmonic without specifying the slice and the first nonzero degree is incorrect.

## The complete cubic expansion

Let \(h(v,\omega_2)=b_2(\omega_2)^TM(Bw)v\). Direct substitution of \(\tanh z=z-z^3/3+O(z^5)\) gives

\[
F_1(w;v)=\mathbb E_1[g w]\cdot v,
\]

\[
F_3(w;v)=-\frac13\left(
\mathbb E_1[g(w\cdot v)^3]
+\mathbb E_2[c\,h(v,\cdot)^3]
\right). \tag{3}
\]

When \(T_1=0\), the quadratic term of the loss is

\[
L_2(w)=(\mathbb E_1[g w])^TS(\mathbb E_1[g w]),\qquad
S=\sum_i\mu_i v_iv_i^T,
\]

and its cubic term for fixed \(c,M\) is

\[
P_3(w)=\frac23\sum_i s_i\left(
\mathbb E_1[g(w\cdot v_i)^3]
+\mathbb E_2[c\,h(v_i,\cdot)^3]
\right). \tag{4}
\]

If \(S\) is positive definite and \(g\ne0\), the Hessian has exactly two positive lower-field directions, with kernel \(\mathbb E_1[g w]=0\); all \(c,M\) variations are in its kernel. On that kernel the cubic Taylor polynomial of the full joint loss is (4). Terms from varying \(c,M\) in the square have a factor \(F_1\) and vanish on the kernel. Terms from varying them in the signed degree-one prediction vanish identically because \(T_1=0\).

The kernel condition is isotropic in the two physical coordinates, so (1) continues to hold on its allowed paired planes. This is a statement about a Taylor polynomial, not a construction of an invariant center manifold or a theorem about its reduced dynamics.

## A nonzero harmonic descent slice at orders three and five

Assume the lower space is nonatomic, \(G=\mathbb E_1[b_1b_1^T]\) is positive definite, and \(q\ne0\). Then \(g\ne0\), since \(\mathbb E g^2=q^TGq>0\).

For every odd integer \(r\ge3\), there exists bounded \(\eta\) such that

\[
\mathbb E_1[b_1\eta]=0,\qquad \lambda_r:=\mathbb E_1[g\eta^r]\ne0. \tag{5}
\]

Here is an elementary construction, avoiding any vector-measure partition theorem. Choose a set on which \(g\) has a fixed sign and \(|g|\ge\epsilon>0\). Nonatomicity provides subsets \(A\) of arbitrarily small positive measure \(\delta\). Set

\[
m_A=\mathbb E_1[b_1 1_A],\qquad
\eta=1_A-b_1^TG^{-1}m_A.
\]

Its feature moment is zero. The subtracted term has \(L^\infty\) norm at most \(C\delta\), with \(C\) independent of \(A\). Expanding the \(r\)-th power gives

\[
\mathbb E_1[g\eta^r]=\mathbb E_1[g1_A]+O(\delta^2).
\]

The first term has absolute value at least \(\epsilon\delta\), so (5) holds for sufficiently small \(\delta\).

Take \(w=t\eta\xi\). Because \(B\eta=0\), the upper preactivation begins at order \(t^3\); its nonlinear cubic correction begins only at order \(t^9\). Therefore

\[
F(t\eta\xi;v)=-\frac13\lambda_3t^3(\xi\cdot v)^3
+\frac{2}{15}\lambda_5t^5(\xi\cdot v)^5+O(t^7).
\]

Consequently:

* If \(T_1=0\), \(T_3\ne0\), and \(\lambda_3\ne0\), then
  \[
  L(t\eta\xi)-L_0=\frac23\lambda_3T_3[\xi^{\otimes3}]t^3+O(t^5).
  \]
* If \(T_1=T_3=0\), \(T_5\ne0\), and \(\lambda_5\ne0\), then
  \[
  L(t\eta\xi)-L_0=-\frac4{15}\lambda_5T_5[\xi^{\otimes5}]t^5+O(t^6).
  \]

In either case choose \(\xi\) with the nonzero coefficient of the desired sign, using oddness under \(\xi\mapsto-\xi\). There are lower-loss states arbitrarily close to the collapsed state. Along any exact gradient flow with the physical positive metric, loss is nonincreasing. Such states cannot converge to any complete lower collapse whose loss is \(L_0\). This rules out attraction of an entire neighborhood. It does not rule out an open one-sided basin, a basin of positive measure, or attraction from a specified random initialization law.

For a concrete three-point example, take

\[
v_1=(-1/2,\sqrt3/2),\quad v_2=(-1/2,-\sqrt3/2),\quad v_3=(-1,0),
\quad\mu_i=1,\quad(y_1,y_2,y_3)=(1,1,-1).
\]

Then \(T_1=0\), and, writing \(\xi=(x,y)\),

\[
T_3[\xi^{\otimes3}]=\frac34(x^3-3xy^2).
\]

Even the feature-null slice need not have harmonic *leading full loss* at higher orders. For fourteen directions at angles \(j\pi/7\), labels \((-1)^j\), and unit weights, \(T_1=T_3=T_5=0\) while

\[
T_7[\xi^{\otimes7}]=\frac7{32}\operatorname{Re}(x+iy)^7.
\]

The tensor identities follow by expanding each odd power of cosine into its odd Fourier frequencies and summing the fourteen equally spaced roots. For a profile in (5) with \(r=3\), the full loss instead begins along the feature-null slice with

\[
L(t\eta\xi)-L_0=
\frac{\lambda_3^2}{9}t^6\sum_i\mu_i(\xi\cdot v_i)^6+O(t^7).
\]

For \(\xi\ne0\), this degree-six coefficient is positive, since the directions span \(\mathbb R^2\). Thus the first signed harmonic term of degree seven is preceded by a nonharmonic positive square.

## A genuine dynamical theorem for the homogeneous two-dimensional problem

For the isolated Euclidean gradient flow of a nonzero homogeneous harmonic polynomial \(H:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R\), of degree \(m\ge2\), the basin of zero has planar Lebesgue measure zero.

For completeness, in polar coordinates harmonicity gives

\[
H(r,\theta)=r^m(A\cos m\theta+B\sin m\theta).
\]

Indeed homogeneity writes \(H=r^mh(\theta)\), and the polar Laplacian gives \(h''+m^2h=0\). After a rotation and nonzero scale, \(H=\operatorname{Re}z^m\). Its harmonic conjugate \(K=\operatorname{Im}z^m\) satisfies \(\nabla H\cdot\nabla K=0\), verified by differentiating \(z^m\) in the two real coordinates. Hence \(K\) is constant along \(\dot z=-\nabla H\). A trajectory converging to zero must start on \(K=0\), a finite union of rays, which has area zero. On those rays the sign of \(H\) distinguishes radial approach from radial escape.

This theorem is useful only after proving that the intended dynamics reduces to this exact flow, or proving a suitable perturbation theorem for the actual reduction. Neither reduction is supplied by a loss restriction. In the present population model the field plane \(w=\eta\xi\) is generally not invariant: differentiating the lower cubic term produces a profile proportional to \(g\eta^2\), which need not be proportional to \(\eta\). The \(c,M\) blocks also generally move away from their frozen values. Therefore the preceding null-basin theorem cannot be applied to the full population flow by restricting its loss to that plane.

## Analytic squared-loss counterexample to the ambient inference

Use four Euclidean state variables \((x,y,u,v)\), and write

\[
r^2=x^2+y^2,\qquad \rho^2=u^2+v^2,\qquad P=x^3-3xy^2.
\]

Consider the analytic potential

\[
V=P+\frac14P^2+24\rho^4r^2+\rho^6. \tag{6}
\]

Its first nonzero homogeneous term is the harmonic cubic \(P\). It is harmonic on every paired isotropic plane

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y)+a\xi,\qquad (u,v)\mapsto(u,v)+b\xi
\]

for arbitrary real \(a,b\), because its plane Laplacian is \(a^2\Delta_{x,y}P=0\). Moreover (6) is an exact squared loss minus its value at zero: use the odd cubic prediction vector

\[
f=(-P/2,\sqrt{24}\rho^2x,\sqrt{24}\rho^2y,\rho^2u,\rho^2v),
\quad y_*=(1,0,0,0,0).
\]

Then \(\lVert f-y_*\rVert^2-1=V\). The zero entries of this illustrative target are not essential to the algebra: an orthogonal change of output coordinates gives a target with all entries nonzero and mixed signs while preserving the loss and oddness. This is still an auxiliary polynomial model, with no asserted representation by the assigned tanh architecture or its circle data.

Nevertheless the origin attracts the open set

\[
\mathcal U=\{0<\rho<1/2,\ 0<r<\rho^4\}. \tag{7}
\]

To prove this, use the exact Euclidean gradient flow \(\dot X=-\nabla V\). Euler's identity for the degree-three polynomial \(P\) gives, while \(r,\rho>0\),

\[
\dot r=-\frac{3P}{r}-\frac{3P^2}{2r}-48\rho^4r
\le3r^2-48\rho^4r,
\]

\[
\dot\rho=-96\rho^3r^2-6\rho^5.
\]

Thus inside the closure of (7), away from its zero-radius faces,

\[
\frac d{dt}\log\frac r{\rho^4}
\le3r-24\rho^4+384\rho^2r^2
\le-21\rho^4+384\rho^{10}
\le-15\rho^4<0.
\]

The last inequality uses \(\rho<1/2\), so \(384\rho^6\le6\). The ratio cannot cross its upper boundary, and \(\rho\) decreases. The faces \(r=0\) and \(\rho=0\) are invariant; uniqueness of the smooth ODE prevents a positive radius from reaching either in finite time. The trajectory stays bounded, hence exists for all positive time. Finally \(\dot\rho\le-6\rho^5\) implies \(\rho\to0\), and \(r<\rho^4\) implies \(r\to0\). This proves attraction from the nonempty open set (7), which has positive four-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

The mechanism is fully explicit: where the leading cubic vanishes to high order in the extra pair of variables, the degree-six square terms create transverse contraction strong enough to keep the trajectory in a shrinking tube. The origin remains a nonminimum because of the cubic. Consequently a nonzero harmonic leading term, odd prediction parity, and a positive even square remainder together do not imply a null basin in ambient dimensions.

## Status and exact remaining obligation

* **Established by direct calculation:** stationarity at \(T_1=0\); the harmonic identity (1); the cubic formulas (3)–(4); nonzero cubic/quintic descent slices under \(q\ne0\); the distinction between signed and full leading terms; the homogeneous planar null-basin theorem; and the open basin of the auxiliary analytic squared loss (6).
* **Refuted proof route:** infer a null basin of the full flow solely from harmonic leading signed variation, even after adding parity and squared-loss structure.
* **Not established:** any positive-measure basin for a nonoptimal collapsed state in the supplied population model; any null-basin theorem for that model; invariance of the displayed lower-field slices; or a finite-dimensional Lebesgue argument for a population initialization law.
* **Additional edge case:** mixed labels alone do not ensure a first nonzero odd signed tensor. Antipodal pairs carrying equal labels can make all odd tensors vanish, for example labels \(+1\) at \(\pm e_1\) and \(-1\) at \(\pm e_2\), with equal weights. Then every odd predictor has zero signed correlation. A nonzero first odd tensor is a separate hypothesis.
* **Bottleneck for this route:** prove a model-specific obstruction to the shrinking-tube mechanism in (6), using the complete loss and the physical metric after eliminating stable directions. Such an obstruction must control singular directions of the leading polynomial and motion in the other blocks. Harmonicity on slices supplies no such control by itself.

Route recommendation: retain the exact expansion and descent lemmas; close the unqualified harmonic-to-null-basin inference as falsified; leave the actual population-basin question open for a different mechanism or a model-specific reduction.
