## Independent derivations and adversarial attacks

### 1. Scaling, geometry and passive inputs

The finite gradient with respect to W1 carries the prediction factor `1/n`; mobility n cancels it. The connector's finite rank-one update retains `1/n`, and readout mobility n cancels its prediction normalization. With the mean loss, every update therefore has coefficient `-2/3`, exactly as in C4.17. The training labels are `1, cos(3pi/5), cos(3pi/5)` and the off-diagonal input correlations are `cos(pi/5), cos(pi/5), cos(2pi/5)`, agreeing with C4.3. The input Gram has rank two, but its three nonlinear ridge functions are independent.

The attack was that the passive circle might require an unproved zero-weight extension or an inverse of its singular augmented Gram. It does not: the first two training vectors span R2, giving C4.9 at every parameter state, including between raw-GD updates. The bounded coefficients and their angular derivatives give a common Lipschitz modulus through tanh, the bounded connector and readout pairing. Fixed finite passive probes therefore suffice. Taking the width limit for an angular net first, then refining the net, gives uniform angle/time convergence. Bounded predictions transfer it to the squared-error integral. This verifies the named continuum observable without a growing-design limit.

### 2. Unique matching and positivity of its denominator

For the frozen model, `r_g' = -(2/3)K r_g`, so

`L_g'(s) = -(4/9) r_g(s)^T K r_g(s) < 0`.

The residual never vanishes at finite s because the exponential is invertible and y is nonzero. The frozen loss has range `(0,L_g(0)]`. For the learned flow, C.1's energy identity and `||K(t)||_op <= B_K` give

`-(4/3)B_K L_f <= L_f' <= 0`.

Thus the learned loss stays strictly positive and within the frozen range. Combining its lower exponential bound with the frozen upper exponential bound yields `tau(t) <= B_K t/lambda_min(K)`, with the inequality in the direction used in C4.13. This establishes uniqueness and allows both clocks to remain inside the local interval. At zero both derivatives equal `-4B_0`; hence shortening the interval makes the inverse denominator uniformly nonzero. The attack of inferring finite-time interpolation from energy decrease alone is avoided.

### 3. Fourth moments and query chronology

The strongest possible obstruction is that a bounded L2 operator need not map a fixed L4 input into L4, so a generic operator-norm argument would not justify the second activation remainder. C.4 supplies the missing distributional information.

First reveal the lower Gaussian root and the three initial forward training calls, construct bounded U, and reveal the three reverse calls P. At this point A_x is measurable: its passive lower root is an exact linear combination of the revealed training roots, and it uses only the already revealed P. Its own forward answer and the passive upper answer Y_x have not been used to define it.

Projecting A_x onto the three training activations uses the fixed positive Q, and the reverse response uses the fixed positive V. C4.15 is then the allowed next forward conditional law. Its three summands have uniform fourth-moment bounds: a fixed Gaussian training tuple with bounded deterministic coefficients, bounded U with bounded deterministic coefficients, and a Gaussian innovation with uniformly bounded variance. No independence from Y_x or between different passive innovations is needed. M_x is bounded, so R_x^hid has the required uniform fourth moment. This closes the L4 obstruction for precisely the initialized directions used, without asserting an L4 operator norm or higher trained-path moments.

### 4. Actual-flow error, including moving residuals

Zero population readout and bounded tanh give `||W3(t)||_infinity = O(t)` from the readout integral. Hence both backward fields are O(t) in L2, both hidden parameter increments are O(t^2), and the predictions are O(t). Comparing the same integrals to their initial directions yields the three estimates in C4.18.

For the lower gate, boundedness and Lipschitz continuity give an L4 error of order t from an L2 preactivation error of order t^2. Holder against the *fixed* L4 field P_b then gives an L2 error O(t) in `delta1(t)/t - 2B`. Integration produces the O(t^3) remainders in C4.19. C4.20 is valid because one removes the unknown L2 error by Lipschitz continuity before Taylor-expanding only the fixed L4 direction. Applying it at both layers, with the connector product expanded, proves C4.21 uniformly on the circle. The product of the two O(t^2) increments is O(t^4) in the stated norms.

For the predictor difference, write `D3=W3-Wg3` and `F_x=H2_x(t)-H2_x(0)`. Exact subtraction gives C4.22 with the moving residual. Its output equation and `F_x=O(t^2)` first imply

`||D3(t)||_2 <= C integral_0^t ||D3(s)||_2 ds + C t^3`.

The contained Gronwall comparison gives D3=O(t^3). Reinsert it and `r_b=-y_b+O(t)` to obtain

`D3(t) = (4/3)t^3 sum_b p_b E_b + O_L2(t^4)`.

The term containing `f-g` integrates to O(t^4), so discarding it only after this estimate is legitimate. Pairing with the initialized activation and with `W3=2tS+O_L2(t^2)` yields

This independently reproduces J, including the readout correction. Both hidden contributions occur in R_x^hid. A finite formal jet was not used as a substitute for this strong trajectory estimate.

### 5. Clock cancellation and risk sign

Adjunction gives `E_2(S E_a)=sum_b p_b(G_ab D_ab+Q_ab V_ab)`. Thus

`p^T J_train=(16/3) mathcal A`, and `p^T a_train=2B_0`.

The Schur-product estimate gives positive `Q circ V` despite singular G; the other block is positive semidefinite. Hence `mathcal A>0`. Expanding the *mean* training loss gives

`L_f(t)-L_g(t)=-2 p^T J_train t^3+O(t^4)`.

Dividing by the initial frozen loss slope `-4B_0`, after first obtaining `tau-t=O(t^3)` by the mean-value bound, gives

`beta=(p^T J_train)/(2B_0)=8 mathcal A/(3B_0)>0`.

In particular `p^T(J_train-beta a_train)=0`. This is the relevant adversarial check against treating a positive training-speed gain as a population-risk sign. It cancels at equal training loss.

The matched predictor difference is `t^3(J-beta a)+O(t^4)`. In `R(g_tau)-R(f_t)`, its negative multiplies `g_tau+f_t-2 cos(3alpha)=-2 cos(3alpha)+O(t)`. The resulting sign and factor are exactly

`chi=2 integral cos(3alpha)(J-beta a) dmu`.

All error factors are uniformly bounded on the circle, so integration preserves O(t^4). The theorem permits chi=0. If an independent rigorous argument supplies chi nonzero, `Mt <= |chi|/2` proves C4.8. That logical implication is accepted; its premise is not supplied by this review or packet.

### 6. Explicit contraction and repeated/antipodal slots

For a bounded smooth upper function F, the simultaneous reverse law is a mean `sum_i H1_i E_2(partial_i F)` plus an innovation independent of the lower roots, with joint covariance `E_2(F_1 F_2)`. Conditioning on the lower roots in a product of two reverse answers gives precisely the two summands in Lambda. Dropping either the mean-product summand or any part of the full innovation second moment would change the coefficient. C4.28 retains both.

Adjunction independently gives

`E_2(F R_a^hid)=sum_b p_b[Q_ab E_2(F U_b)+G_ab Lambda_ab(F,U_b)]`.

Choosing F=U_x and F=H2_x phi'(Y_a) reproduces C4.29 and both explicit derivative formulas. There is no extra initialization variance in the trained rank-one term because all model variances are specified as one at population scale.

I explicitly attacked repeated and antipodal passive inputs. At a repeated slot, the lower and upper fields equal their training counterparts. At an antipodal slot, odd tanh gives `Z_x=-Z_a`, `H1_x=-H1_a`, `Y_x=-Y_a`, `H2_x=-H2_a`, while the gates are even. Consequently `A_x=-A_a`, `R_x^hid=-R_a^hid`, `E_x=-E_a`, and `J(x)=-J(x_a)`, as required by the network's exact oddness. Formal slots remain separate in the derivatives. At equality the covariance null relation is the matching difference of source inputs; at antipodality it is their sum. These are zero in L2, so the contracted response is unaffected by the null-space ambiguity. No inverse of the augmented covariance or the singular input Gram is invoked. Continuous covariance square roots and bounded integrands give a well-defined continuous angle dependence through these boundary cases.

### 7. Finite matching and limit order

Both finite comparisons keep the same actual Gaussian stored readout with entry variance `1/n^2`. C.1 treats it as a vanishing initialization perturbation; C.4 never replaces it by zero in the finite dynamics. Thus the finite hidden tangent blocks need not initially vanish, and no uniform finite-width cubic/sign statement near time zero follows.

The empirical frozen kernel tends to positive K. On an event whose probability tends to one, it is positive definite and the initial residual is nonzero. Frozen finite GF is strictly decreasing to zero. For raw GD, eventual `0<(2/3)eta_n lambda_i(K_n)<1` makes each nonzero eigendirection decrease strictly in magnitude; affine readout interpolation retains a positive decreasing factor inside each step. The corresponding loss is therefore continuously and strictly decreasing to zero, not merely decreasing at grid points.

For every fixed positive delta, the population learned loss on `[delta,T]` has a positive gap from both zero and its initial value. Uniform finite capture transfers those two gaps. The clock margin `tau(T)<=T_*/2` and the strict frozen slope keep finite matching times in a compact subset of the captured interval. If `e_f,e_g` are the two uniform loss errors there and c is the positive minimum of `|L_g'|`, the mean-value argument gives the concrete inverse bound

`sup_[delta,T] |tau_n-tau| <= (e_f+e_g)/c`.

This proves uniform convergence in probability of the clocks, and passive risk capture then proves convergence of matched risk differences. A separately proved sign transfers only on fixed `[delta,t0]`, where its limiting magnitude has a positive minimum. No rate, arbitrary shrinking delta_n, or unrestricted width/step/time interchange is claimed.
