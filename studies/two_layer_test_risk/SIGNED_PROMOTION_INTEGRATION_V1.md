## Notation, dependencies and mathematical scope

The proposal preserves the canonical stored-weight shapes, hidden-sum
normalization, readout factor `1/n`, variances `(1,1/n,1/n^2)`, raw mobilities
`(n,1,n)`, and residual `f-y`. Its mean loss uses `p=y/3`, as required by the
weighted C.3 correction, and does not inherit C.3's earlier summed-loss clock.
The training Gram is the original rank-two correlated Gram. It is neither
whitened nor inverted. The only exact positive Gram inverses in the analytic
argument concern the fixed training activation and response Grams.

Population pairings and adjoints have consistent types. `P,B,T,A` lie in the
first population and `S,U,M,R^hid,E` in the second. The risk `R` is explicitly
separated from `R^hid`. Lowercase local C++ arrays are explained as array
representations of the specified initialized population moments, not as a
new population convention. The bijection `1,2,3,x -> 0,1,2,3` is stated and
used consistently in all four tensor slots and upper arrays. The `T_bits`
tensor is distinguished from the initialized direction `T_x`.

The 60 new C4 equation tags are unique, do not collide with an old C4 tag, and
all explicit C4 parenthetical equation references resolve. An independent
scanner checked 19 local links in the new subsection and the complete changed
guides; all resolve within the edition. These links contain no fragments,
so there is no new fragment target to test. The supplied library checker
separately completed its entire available edition scan: 17 Markdown/Python
files, no boundary or local-link error. Its exclusion of C++ and its structural
scope are not treated as mathematical validation.

I checked these interfaces and implications in the full new proof:

- C.1 supplies a strong local flow, actual forward/adjoint probes and the
  vanishing-small-readout perturbation; the proposal does not infer a
  positive-time population theorem from finite jets.
- Passive circle inputs are exact first-layer linear combinations of two
  training directions. A finite angular net and uniform norm bounds extend
  the fixed-probe capture. Positive sample weights remain `1/3`.
- The strict activity hypotheses hold for bounded, smooth, nonconstant tanh,
  positive variances/mobilities, pairwise nonparallel inputs and nonzero labels.
  The rank-deficient input Gram does not invalidate positive `Q`, `K` or `V`.
- The additional fixed-direction fourth moments use the actual conditional
  forward/reverse law. They support the fixed-direction Taylor remainder;
  no ambient smoothness claim for a nonlinear map on all of L2 is imported.
- The moving residual is retained in the readout subtraction. The identities
  `p^T J=16 A/3`, `p^T a=2 B0`, and `beta=8 A/(3 B0)` have the correct factors
  for mean squared loss. Matching removes the training-speed component.
- The inverse-free contraction retains the full innovation second moment and
  both response mean factors. Formally separate singular passive slots are
  covered by the contained singular-Gram rule and the covariance interpolation
  estimate; the proposal does not differentiate a passive Cholesky factor.
- The risk sign is `R(g_tau)-R(f)`, consistent with a positive `chi`. The
  uniform fourth-order remainder and strict lower bound imply the stated
  sufficiently-small-positive-time inequality.
- Finite matching and sign transfer are restricted to fixed intervals bounded
  away from zero, with the actual common small random readout retained.
  There is no arbitrary shrinking-time, quantitative width, sample-size,
  later-time or universal generalization claim.

C.4 adds a risk comparison to C.3's feature-activity conclusion rather than
silently renaming activity as usefulness. The full arithmetic proof, operator
elimination and flow remainder are appropriately placed together with that
new theorem. I found no harmful duplication or incompatible claim in the
older material actually read. This is not a novelty or literature-exclusion
assessment.
