# Further compression starting from the response-memory population closure

## Scope and conclusion

This note treats the working order-\(P\) response-memory closure as the parent
model. It does not replace that
model by a frozen response dictionary, a temporal Taylor expansion, or a
trajectory-fitted scalar system. The questions are whether one can further
compress

1. the neuron populations into a finite set of aggregate observables;
2. the retained initialized operators \(W_0,W_0^\top\); and
3. the sample-indexed response histories so that state size need not grow with
   the number of training samples.

Here \(m\) denotes sample count and \(n\) denotes neuron width. The
fixed-kernel loss lower bound in section 2 concerns \(m\); it does **not**
obstruct an \(n\)-independent aggregate closure whose size may depend on
\(m\). The focused width-only analysis is in
[WIDTH_ONLY_POPULATION_COMPRESSION.md](WIDTH_ONLY_POPULATION_COMPRESSION.md).

The answers are different.

* A scalar aggregate hierarchy is principled, and this study already has a
  finite-horizon convergence theorem for a clipped version at fixed width,
  dataset and history order. It does not yet give a small or efficient scalar
  model. Generic scalar observables such as loss do not close by themselves.
* Redrawing \(W_0\) during training is not an approximation of the same
  network. It deletes correlations forced by reuse of one operator and its
  transpose. A shared, query-adaptive Gaussian action state is principled, but
  it has fixed size only when the encountered query/response system has a
  controlled effective rank. No such rank theorem is currently known.
* State independent of sample count is impossible uniformly over arbitrary
  finite datasets and labels. It is possible under input-space structure. For
  circle tasks, the cleanest next construction is a Fourier--Galerkin
  representation of the **sample-indexed fields of the successful population
  closure**. Its neuron-vector coefficients continue to evolve, so it does not
  freeze learned spatial directions. Under uniform input regularity and
  stability, its order depends on task bandwidth and accuracy rather than the
  raw number of samples.

The recommended order of work is therefore: compress the sample axis first,
keep the neuron population and a consistent \(W_0\) action, then test whether
the initialized-operator query transcript has low predictive rank. Full
population-to-scalar removal should remain a separate, more difficult axis.

## 1. The parent model and the three approximation axes

Fix width, data, depth and history order \(P\). Collect every current
neuron-vector response, history moment, outer weight and clock coordinate of
the response-memory closure into \(S\). With the realized initialized
operators and empirical training measure as fixed parameters, write

\[
 \dot S=F_{P,W_0,\nu_m}(S),
 \qquad
 \nu_m=\frac1m\sum_{a=1}^m\delta_{(x_a,y_a)}.
 \tag{1}
\]

This is the already implemented autonomous population closure. It preserves
the actual \(W_0\) and \(W_0^\top\), and its learned directions move because its
history moments are neuron vectors generated by its own current responses.

Any further reduction has three logically separate errors:

* aggregate error from replacing the neuron populations by finitely many
  contractions;
* operator error from replacing actions of \(W_0\) and \(W_0^\top\); and
* data-axis error from replacing the \(m\) sample-indexed fields or the measure
  \(\nu_m\).

History order \(P\) is a fourth, already analyzed approximation. Increasing
\(P\) cannot repair an aggregate, operator or data-axis defect.

## 2. When population observables form an autonomous scalar system

Let \(A(S)\in\mathbb R^K\) be the proposed aggregate state and suppose the
requested observable factors as \(O(S)=R(A(S))\).

### Exact projectability criterion

An exact autonomous law \(\dot q=G(q)\) on an admitted restart class exists
algebraically if and only if

\[
 A(S)=A(\widetilde S)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 DA(S)F_{P,W_0,\nu_m}(S)
 =DA(\widetilde S)F_{P,W_0,\nu_m}(\widetilde S).
 \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, if (2) holds, define

\[
 G(A(S))=DA(S)F_{P,W_0,\nu_m}(S).
 \tag{3}
\]

Condition (2) makes this definition single valued. Conversely, any identity
\(DA\,F=G\circ A\) implies (2). If the reduced ODE is uniquely well posed, the
chain rule proves that \(A(S(t))\) is its solution. Thus the exact question is
whether the chosen observables form an invariant algebra under the parent
generator, not whether the final quantity of interest happens to be scalar.

For approximate closure, choose a computable \(G_K\) and define the source
defect along the parent path by

\[
 \eta_K(t)=DA(S(t))F_{P,W_0,\nu_m}(S(t))-G_K(A(S(t))).
 \tag{4}
\]

If \(G_K\) is \(L_K\)-Lipschitz on a common region and initial aggregates
match, subtraction of the two integral equations gives

\[
 \|q_K(t)-A(S(t))\|
 \le \int_0^t e^{L_K(t-s)}\|\eta_K(s)\|\,ds.
 \tag{5}
\]

This separates production of omitted information from its amplification. A
small boundary term without a common stability bound is insufficient.

### The user's moment examples

The distinction is visible in the two scalar particle systems from the
question. If

\[
 \dot x_i=-\lambda\mu_1,\qquad
 \mu_p=\frac1n\sum_i x_i^p,
\]

then \(\dot\mu_1=-\lambda\mu_1\), so the mean closes by itself. If the second
example means

\[
 \dot x_i=-\lambda\mu_3,
\]

then the product rule gives

\[
 \dot\mu_p=-p\lambda\mu_{p-1}\mu_3.
\]

Consequently \((\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_3)\) is an exact closed system:

\[
 \dot\mu_1=-\lambda\mu_3,\qquad
 \dot\mu_2=-2\lambda\mu_1\mu_3,\qquad
 \dot\mu_3=-3\lambda\mu_2\mu_3.
\]

The superficially similar local law \(\dot x_i=-\lambda x_i^3\) behaves
differently:

\[
 \dot\mu_p=-p\lambda\mu_{p+2},
\]

so every finite moment list exposes a higher moment. Finite closure comes from
an invariant algebra of observables, not from polynomiality alone. The neural
population law contains local nonlinear gates, moving forward/backward fields
and shared operator actions, so it resembles the hierarchy-generating case
unless an additional invariant structure is proved.

### Why loss alone does not close

The obstruction is already visible in a fixed-kernel subproblem. Let a
residual vector obey

\[
 \dot r=-\frac2m\Theta r,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L=\frac1m r^\top r,
 \tag{6}
\]

where \(\Theta\) is symmetric positive semidefinite. Then

\[
 \dot{\mathcal L}=-\frac4{m^2}r^\top\Theta r.
 \tag{7}
\]

Two residuals of equal length can have different Rayleigh quotients, so they
have the same loss and different loss velocities. Adding spectral moments

\[
 \mu_j=\frac1m r^\top\Theta^j r
\]

gives the exact ladder

\[
 \dot\mu_j=-\frac4m\mu_{j+1}.
 \tag{8}
\]

It closes through the minimal polynomial of \(\Theta\). For a generic
\(m\times m\) kernel with \(m\) distinct eigenvalues, that can require \(m\)
independent quantities even though only loss is requested.

There is a sharper regular lower bound. In an eigenbasis of such a kernel,
put \(a_i=r_i(0)^2>0\). The future loss is

\[
 \mathcal L_a(t)=\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m
 a_i e^{-4\lambda_i t/m}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Suppose a \(K\)-dimensional autonomous system, with a differentiable
initialization map and sufficiently differentiable vector field and readout,
reproduces (9) for every \(a\) in an open positive set and is restartable from
its own state. The first \(m\) time derivatives at zero are

\[
 \mathcal L_a^{(j)}(0)=\frac1m\sum_i
 a_i(-4\lambda_i/m)^j,\qquad 0\le j<m.
 \tag{10}
\]

The matrix in (10) is Vandermonde and has rank \(m\). The same jet computed
from the reduced state factors through \(\mathbb R^K\), so its derivative has
rank at most \(K\). Hence \(K\ge m\). This is a lower bound for exact smooth
loss prediction over arbitrary restarts. It does not exclude approximation,
one fixed task, clustered spectra, or a structured low-dimensional residual
family.

In the nonlinear response-memory closure, the tangent kernel itself evolves
and the physical middle-weight reconstruction has a finite-\(P\) velocity
defect. Those effects add observables; they do not remove the fixed-kernel
obstruction.

### What the existing scalar hierarchy actually proves

Differentiating the parent closure's averages, shared-operator contractions
and requested outputs generates an exact countable hierarchy. For ordinary
output, mean and Gram observables, the reachable diagrams are forests; cyclic
diagrams can be discarded exactly. This is a real reduction of the formal
state.

At a finite diagram cutoff, deleting unresolved terms gives an autonomous
scalar ODE but does not make the deleted terms small. The unsaturated
construction has a proved common short-time interval and convergence as the
cutoff grows. The study's clipped construction goes further: for every fixed
finite width, dataset, history order, horizon and finite output list, it uses
an a priori parent envelope to clip each retained diagram at a size-dependent
bound. Every finite cutoff is then globally well posed, and its requested
outputs converge uniformly on the prescribed finite horizon to the parent
response-memory outputs as the aggregate cutoff tends to infinity.

This is a principled existence theorem for finite scalar approximation. It
also removes operational calls to \(W_0\) after the initial scalar
contractions have been computed. Its limitations are decisive for practical
compression:

* the cutoff bound can be enormous and depends on width, data, \(P\), horizon
  and the requested accuracy;
* no width-uniform or all-time estimate is established;
* a small unsaturated implementation already failed its output tests; and
* convergence of an exhaustive hierarchy does not imply that a small set of
  familiar moments closes.

The scalar route becomes genuinely economical only with additional structure,
such as one of the following:

1. a finite observable algebra invariant under the parent generator;
2. an invariant finite-dimensional family of joint neuron/operator laws;
3. a causal observable system with decaying Hankel or singular tail in a norm
   that controls the requested output; or
4. a quantitative hierarchy-tail estimate together with a propagated
   stability bound.

Gaussian initialization, exchangeability, a smooth activation and a scalar
output do not individually imply any of these properties. Moment-matched
representative neurons have the same issue: unless their autonomous motion
preserves every needed moment, periodically rematching them from the full
population is an oracle rather than a closure.

## 3. Width compression: a genuine exact obstruction

Here \(n\) is the neuron width. The sample-count argument in the first version
of this note concerned \(m\) and therefore did not answer the width question.
There is, however, a separate width obstruction. It already occurs with one
training sample, one trainable feature layer and no initialized middle matrix.

Consider the scalar-input tanh model

\[
 f_n(w)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\tanh w_i,\qquad
 r=f_n-y,\qquad \mathcal L=r^2.
 \tag{W1}
\]

Keep the unit readout fixed and train \(w\) with mobility \(n\). Since
\(\partial_{w_i}\mathcal L=2r(1-\tanh^2w_i)/n\), its canonical feature flow is

\[
 \dot w_i=-2r(1-\tanh^2w_i).
 \tag{W2}
\]

Define a local learning coordinate \(q\) by \(\dot q=-2r\). On any open time
interval where \(r\ne0\), \(q\) is a valid coordinate and

\[
 \frac{dw_i}{dq}=1-\tanh^2w_i.
\]

Put \(z_i=\tanh w_i\). Then

\[
 \frac{dz_i}{dq}=(1-z_i^2)^2,\qquad
 f_n=\frac1n\sum_i z_i.
 \tag{W3}
\]

Define polynomials

\[
 p_0(z)=z,\qquad
 p_{k+1}(z)=(1-z^2)^2p_k'(z).
 \tag{W4}
\]

Repeated differentiation of (W3) gives the exact output jets

\[
 \frac{d^kf_n}{dq^k}(0)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n p_k(z_i(0)).
 \tag{W5}
\]

The degree of \(p_k\) is \(3k+1\), with nonzero leading coefficient. Hence the
functions

\[
 p_0'(z),p_1'(z),\ldots,p_{n-1}'(z)
\]

are linearly independent: their degrees are \(0,3,\ldots,3(n-1)\). One can
therefore choose distinct \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\in(-1,1)\) such that the evaluation
matrix

\[
 \bigl[p_k'(z_i)\bigr]_{0\le k<n,\ 1\le i\le n}
 \tag{W6}
\]

is nonsingular. If every such evaluation determinant vanished, the functions
would be linearly dependent; choosing the points inductively proves the
contrapositive.

Now suppose a fixed \(K\)-dimensional smooth autonomous state \(a\) exactly
reproduced this output on an open set of neuron states for every width:

\[
 a(0)=E_n(w(0)),\qquad \dot a=G_n(a),\qquad f_n=H_n(a),
 \tag{W7}
\]

with smooth maps that may themselves depend on \(n\), but whose state
dimension \(K\) does not. Where \(H_n(a)-y\ne0\), the same state can be
parametrized by \(q\):

\[
 \frac{da}{dq}=\frac{G_n(a)}{-2(H_n(a)-y)}.
\]

Consequently the first \(n\) \(q\)-derivatives of the output are smooth
functions of the \(K\)-dimensional initial state \(E_n(w)\). Their map from
the neuron coordinates therefore has differential rank at most \(K\). But
(W5)--(W6), together with \(dz_i/dw_i=1-z_i^2>0\), show that the actual jet map
has rank \(n\) at the selected state. Thus

\[
 \boxed{K\ge n.}
 \tag{W8}
\]

Because \(n\) is arbitrary, there is no exact smooth scalar closure of fixed
dimension, uniform in width, on an open family of current neuron populations.
This is a nonlinear feature-learning obstruction with \(m=1\); it is unrelated
to storing an arbitrary label vector or to the initialized matrix.

The result is stronger than failure of raw power moments. It says that any
smooth finite encoder fails under these state-universal assumptions, because
the future output jets locally distinguish all \(n\) neuron coordinates. The
maintained observable-closure theory supplies an independent, more
architecture-oriented version: fixed finite lists of bounded-degree tensor
contractions with polynomial or analytic autonomous dynamics cannot close
uniformly in width on an open state set. Its proof generates connected
contraction graphs of unbounded complexity under repeated Lie derivatives.

### What this obstruction does and does not establish

It establishes a genuine impossibility for an **exact, fixed-dimensional,
regular, restartable, state-universal** scalarization. A full scalar closure
of the response-memory model advertised for arbitrary current populations
would include this kind of feature heterogeneity and cannot have dimension
independent of width.

It does not prove impossibility on one prescribed Gaussian-initialized
trajectory. A single trajectory can be encoded pathologically, or replayed
from time, which is why the study's closure contract requires one autonomous
rule over a restart family and excludes hidden trajectory data. Nor does the
argument prove that the canonically reached restart family of the present
deep Gaussian model contains an open set of all neuron states. Establishing
that stronger model-specific reachability claim remains open.

Most importantly, (W8) is not an approximation lower bound. In the toy model,
let \(\mu\) be the empirical law of the initial \(z_i\), and approximate it by
a \(K\)-atom measure \(\mu_K\). The characteristic flow defined by
\(dz/dq=(1-z^2)^2\) is Lipschitz on \([-1,1]\). Therefore, for bounded
\(|q|\),

\[
 |f_\mu(q)-f_{\mu_K}(q)|
 \le e^{C|q|}W_1(\mu,\mu_K).
 \tag{W9}
\]

A one-dimensional \(K\)-point quantization has
\(W_1(\mu,\mu_K)=O(K^{-1})\) under a bounded-domain construction. Evolving
those \(K\) representative neurons gives a finite scalar ODE whose size is
independent of the original width and whose factors move. Thus exact finite
closure is generically impossible while finite-accuracy width compression is
entirely possible. Adding \(\dot q=-2(f-y)\) and applying an ordinary
finite-horizon stability estimate transfers the approximation back to physical
time.

For the response-memory population closure, an analogous approximation needs
three additional results:

1. a width-uniform compactness or population-limit description of the joint
   neuron and history state;
2. a faithful population representation of the reused \(W_0,W_0^\top\)
   actions, including causal response correlations; and
3. a truncation or quantization error that remains small after nonlinear
   feedback on the desired finite horizon.

The current clipped forest theorem supplies finite scalar approximation for
each fixed \(n\), but its cutoff and constants may depend on \(n\). It is not
yet the required width-uniform theorem.

## 4. The initialized operator cannot be refreshed independently

Let \(W\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) have independent
\(N(0,1/n)\) entries. Even for deterministic queries, reuse leaves observable
correlations. For two forward queries \(q,s\),

\[
 \mathbb E[(Wq)_i(Ws)_j]
 =\delta_{ij}\frac{q^\top s}{n}.
 \tag{11}
\]

For a forward query \(q\) and reverse query \(p\),

\[
 \mathbb E[(Wq)_i(W^\top p)_j]
 =\frac{p_iq_j}{n}.
 \tag{12}
\]

Equation (11) records reuse across queries or times. Equation (12) records
that the reverse action is the transpose of the same matrix. Drawing an
independent matrix at each time makes the cross-time version of (11) zero.
Drawing independent forward and reverse matrices makes (12) zero. If queries
are themselves functions of earlier matrix actions, Gaussian conditioning
adds causal response terms, so one-time marginal matching is even less
sufficient.

A continuously redrawn \(W_0(t)\) would also make a zero-residual state move:
the learned moments would stop, while the reconstructed initialized part and
therefore the predictions would keep changing. With discrete redraws it is a
different stochastic training algorithm; in a continuous-time limit it needs
a separate noise scaling and is not the canonical gradient flow of a fixed
network. Fresh redraws are therefore unsuitable for the present target.

### Exact query-adaptive Gaussian realization

There is a useful alternative to materializing the matrix. Suppose the
algorithm has issued forward sources \(Q=(q_1,\ldots,q_k)\) and reverse
sources \(P=(p_1,\ldots,p_\ell)\), and has retained

\[
 U=WQ,\qquad V=W^\top P.
\]

Any valid transcript obeys

\[
 \ker Q\subseteq\ker U,qquad
 \ker P\subseteq\ker V,qquad
 P^\top U=V^\top Q.
 \tag{13}
\]

The last identity is the shared forward/transpose pairing. Gaussian linear
conditioning can reveal the action on a new query using the component already
fixed by this transcript plus one Gaussian innovation on the still-unseen
orthogonal block. Forward and reverse calls must condition on the same
transcript. This lazy construction has exactly the law obtained by drawing
the full Gaussian matrix first and revealing only the requested actions.

For transcript rank \(r\), storage is of order \(nr+r^2\), rather than
\(n^2\), and the query directions adapt to the current network. This is not a
frozen dictionary. It is exact in law for a finite query program.

The limitation is rank growth. A continuous nonlinear trajectory can generate
new query directions, and after \(n\) independent forward queries it can reveal
all columns of \(W\). Thus there is no universal exact fixed-rank action state
for arbitrary adaptive queries. The network's reachable query family may be
much smaller, but that is a property to prove or measure, not a consequence of
Gaussianity.

### What forms of \(W_0\) compression remain principled?

| Construction | Preserves the target? | What it achieves |
|---|---:|---|
| Counter-based pseudorandom seed, reused in both orientations | Up to the chosen finite-precision random generator | Reduces stored random entries, but an exact dense action still costs \(O(n^2)\). |
| Lazy shared Gaussian query transcript | Yes in distribution for the finite transcript | Cost scales with encountered query rank; exact size need not remain small. |
| Truncated adaptive transcript or causal balanced realization | Conditionally | Useful if discarded query/response singular values are small in a propagated norm. |
| Fixed low-rank SVD or fixed left/right factors | Generally no | Repeats the frozen-dictionary restriction; a Gaussian matrix has no small generic action rank. |
| Fast structured random transform | Changes the initialization law | Can reduce action cost, but needs a separate universality theorem before it approximates the Gaussian target. |
| Fresh Gaussian matrix at every step | No | Deletes reuse and transpose correlations and defines different dynamics. |

At the population level, the lazy transcript becomes a causal Gaussian
source/response system. A fixed-size realization would follow from a finite or
rapidly decaying causal predictive rank of its covariance and response
kernels, with coefficients computable from the current reduced state. The
study has derived this conditional realization, but has not established the
finite-rank hypothesis for nonlinear training.

This gives a qualified negative answer. The full action of a generic realized
\(W_0\) on arbitrary sources is not exactly compressible to a fixed small
state. Its action on the restricted query family generated by a task may be
approximately compressible, and the shared adaptive transcript is the right
object on which to test that claim.

## 5. Why arbitrary datasets cannot disappear from the state

For the current closure, a history coordinate such as
\(m_{\ell}^{(k)}(a,t)\) is a function of the training example \(a\). On an
empirical measure with \(m\) unrelated atoms, an arbitrary such function has
\(m\) degrees of freedom. Writing the training sums as integrals does not
remove those degrees of freedom:

\[
 \frac1m\sum_{a=1}^m r_a\,\delta_{\ell,a}h_{\ell-1,a}^\top
 =\int r(z)\delta_\ell(z)h_{\ell-1}(z)^\top\,d\nu_m(z).
 \tag{14}
\]

The fixed-kernel loss calculation in (6)--(10) already proves a generic
sample-count lower bound for exact smooth loss prediction over arbitrary
residual restarts. Arbitrary labels can carry \(m\) independent amplitudes.
No autonomous state whose regular dimension is independent of \(m\) can
reproduce all of them exactly. A positive result must therefore replace
"arbitrary dataset" by a class with bounded intrinsic complexity, or accept
an approximation of the empirical objective.

This is an exact obstruction to any universal reduction whose admitted family
includes the fixed-kernel regime. A matching \(m\)-dimensional lower bound for
only the precise nonlinear parent closure and only its canonically reached
restart set would require a separate reachability and jet argument; no such
stronger claim is made here.

This is not the same obstruction as the frozen neuron dictionary. It concerns
information in the task and its residual function. A method can allow every
neuron direction to move and still need \(m\) coefficients to remember an
arbitrary label vector.

## 6. The most natural sample-independent continuation: input modes

Assume inputs lie on the circle and write them as \(x(\theta)\). Instead of
storing a separate neuron vector for every sample, regard every current
forward, backward and history field as a function of \(\theta\), for example

\[
 h_1(\theta,t),\quad \delta_2(\theta,t),\quad
 m_{\ell}^{(k)}(\theta,t)\in\mathbb R^n.
\]

Expand the **input dependence** in Fourier modes:

\[
 h_1(\theta,t)\approx
 \sum_{|j|\le Q}\widehat h_{1,j}(t)e^{ij\theta},
 \tag{15}
\]

and do the same for every sample-indexed field. Each coefficient in (15) is
still a moving neuron vector. The history closure continues to generate new
neuron directions, and \(W_0\) is retained. This is fundamentally different
from a fixed left/right dictionary for the middle matrix.

For a uniform circle training law, the reconstructed learned correction also
has a direct mode factorization. Suppressing layer and clock constants, one
of its order-\(P\) terms has the form

\[
 \int_{S^1} b_k(\theta)a_k(\theta)^\top\,
                  \frac{d\theta}{2\pi}.
\]

With Fourier coefficients retained through \(|j|\le Q\), orthogonality gives

\[
 \int b_k(\theta)a_k(\theta)^\top\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
 \approx \sum_{|j|\le Q}
       \widehat b_{k,j}\widehat a_{k,j}^{\,*}.
 \tag{16}
\]

Here \(*\) is the conjugate transpose; a real sine/cosine representation gives
the equivalent real outer products. The learned correction therefore has rank
at most \(P(2Q+1)\) per link in this truncation, independently of \(m\). Its
factors are current Fourier coefficients of response memories, so their neuron
directions move throughout training.

The moment transport itself passes directly to these coefficients. For the
activity clock, write \(a_k(\theta)\) and \(b_k(\theta)\) for one link's
forward and backward moments. Their parent equations have the schematic exact
form

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \dot a_k(\theta)
 &=\rho h(\theta)-\frac{\rho}{L}
   \left[k a_k(\theta)+\sum_{j<k}(2j+1)a_j(\theta)\right],\\
 \dot b_k(\theta)
 &=r(\theta)\delta(\theta)-\frac{\rho}{L}
   \left[k b_k(\theta)+\sum_{j<k}(2j+1)b_j(\theta)\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \(\rho\) and \(L\) are shared scalars, Fourier transformation gives
the same triangular transport for every retained input mode, with sources
\(\widehat h_q\) and \(\widehat{r\delta}_q\). The current truncated network
produces those sources through a pseudospectral forward/backward pass. The
response-clock variant has the same structure with its declared shared clock
and weighted projection. Initialization transforms in the same way:
\(\widehat a_{0,q}(0)=\widehat h_q(0)\), with the other prescribed moment
coordinates zero. Thus this proposal is a direct change of representation of
the existing closure before input-mode truncation, rather than a new response
model.

There is an especially useful commutation identity:

\[
 \widehat{(W_0h)}_j=W_0\widehat h_j,
 \qquad
 \widehat{(W_0^\top\delta)}_j=W_0^\top\widehat\delta_j.
 \tag{17}
\]

Thus an order-\(Q\) input representation needs \(O(Q)\) initialized-operator
actions per field instead of \(m\). It does not yet remove the \(n^2\) matrix
storage, but it can remove sample count from both history memory and the number
of dense actions.

### Conditional approximation statement

A rigorous theorem should use a continuum training law on the circle, or a
separate controlled approximation of the empirical measure. One suitable
set of hypotheses on a fixed horizon is:

1. every parent forward, backward and history field is uniformly bounded in
   \(H^s(S^1;\mathbb R^n)\), for some \(s>1/2\);
2. the activation has enough bounded derivatives on the reached range for
   composition to preserve that Sobolev class;
3. the Galerkin and parent systems remain in a common bounded region on which
   the vector field is Lipschitz in the chosen norm; and
4. nonlinear products are projected consistently, with aliasing included in
   the truncation defect.

The Fourier projection satisfies

\[
 \|(I-\Pi_Q)g\|_{L^2(S^1;\mathbb R^n)}
 \le (Q+1)^{-s}\|g\|_{H^s(S^1;\mathbb R^n)}.
 \tag{18}
\]

Comparing the projected parent equation with the autonomous Galerkin equation
then gives, by the same integral stability argument as (5),

\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|S_Q(t)-S(t)\|
 \le C_T (Q+1)^{-s},
 \tag{19}
\]

with a constant determined by the regularity envelope and stability region.
Analytic dependence on a complex strip would replace the algebraic tail by an
exponential one. Equation (19) is a theorem template, not yet a proved result
for all activations and tasks in this study. Its main missing work is the
uniform spatial regularity of backward/history fields and containment of the
Galerkin solution.

On an equally spaced finite circle dataset, the same construction can be
stated with the discrete Fourier transform. A bound on the discrete
high-frequency tails, uniform in the grid size, replaces the continuum
Sobolev bound. Projected nonlinear products and their aliasing error must still
be controlled. This gives a direct route to approximating the empirical
full-batch closure without storing all \(m\) sample fields. Arbitrary
alternating labels have large discrete bandwidth, so this route correctly
predicts that their required \(Q\) may remain comparable to \(m\).

Smooth activations such as tanh, sigmoid, GELU and SiLU are the natural first
scope for this theorem. ReLU and SELU crossing their kink generally provide
less input regularity and therefore slower algebraic spectral decay; they are
not excluded, but they require a piecewise-regular estimate rather than an
analytic one.

For \(L\) learned links, order \(P\), width \(n\), and \(Q\) input modes, the
history state is then of order \(nLPQ\), independent of the number of samples
used to describe the underlying task. The dependence has moved to the
intrinsic input bandwidth. In higher input dimension the same idea uses
spherical harmonics, wavelets or another complete spatial basis, with the
usual dimension-dependent approximation rates.

A pseudospectral implementation would reconstruct the retained series on
\(O(Q)\) dealiasing nodes, evaluate activations and pointwise gates there, and
transform back with FFTs. Its sample-axis work is approximately
\(O(nLPQ\log Q)\), while applying a dense initialized operator to the mode
vectors costs \(O(n^2Q)\) per affected layer. Training integrals are obtained
from projected products or their zero mode. The original \(m\) examples may be
used once to form label/data coefficients; they need not remain evolving
state. These costs are schematic until the exact deep closure equations and
dealiasing rule are compiled.

### Empirical data and coresets

If the target remains the exact empirical objective, replacing \(\nu_m\) by
a weighted measure \(\widetilde\nu_Q\) changes the vector field. Let

\[
 F_{P,W_0,\nu}(S)=F_0(S)+\int G(S,z)\,d\nu(z).
\]

Suppose, on a common reached region \(\mathcal K_T\), a quadrature or coreset
satisfies

\[
 \sup_{S\in\mathcal K_T}
 \left\|\int G(S,z)\,d(\nu_m-\widetilde\nu_Q)(z)\right\|
 \le\varepsilon_Q.
 \tag{20}
\]

If the empirical vector field is \(L_T\)-Lipschitz there and the initial
states agree, then

\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|S_{\widetilde\nu_Q}(t)-S_{\nu_m}(t)\|
 \le
 \begin{cases}
 \varepsilon_Q(e^{L_TT}-1)/L_T,&L_T>0,\\
 T\varepsilon_Q,&L_T=0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{21}
\]

This follows directly by subtracting the two integral equations. It is the
right contract for a task coreset: the coreset must approximate the complete
class of evolving gradient sources, not only the initial loss or labels.
For this comparison, both dynamics are viewed on the same input-function
state space; only the measure used in their source integrals changes.

A size \(Q\) independent of \(m\) is possible when that source class has
bounded spatial smoothness, RKHS effective dimension, spectral bandwidth, or
another uniform complexity measure. For arbitrary alternating labels or
isolated outliers, \(Q\) can legitimately grow like \(m\). No method can make
that task information disappear while retaining a uniform exact guarantee.

Stochastic minibatching also removes full-dataset work per step, but it
changes deterministic gradient flow to a stochastic process. It is a valid
training method, not a deterministic compression theorem for (1).

### Dynamic low rank as a diagnostic and intermediate method

The sample-indexed fields can also be arranged as \(n\times m\) matrices and
approximated by moving low-rank factors. Allowing both factors to evolve avoids
the frozen-neuron-direction defect. It is useful for testing whether the
response-memory tensors have low numerical rank.

By itself, a factor on the sample side still stores \(O(mr)\) numbers. To
remove \(m\), that factor must be represented as a function of input, by modes
such as (15), or the data measure must be replaced by a controlled coreset.
Dynamic low rank is therefore a useful bridge and diagnostic, not a complete
sample-independent solution.

## 7. A combined finite-horizon error contract

Let

* \(E_{\rm mem}(P,T)\) be the dense-to-response-memory output error;
* \(\varepsilon_{\rm data}(Q,T)\) be the input-mode or coreset source defect;
* \(\varepsilon_{W}(R,T)\) be the joint forward/transpose action defect of an
  order-\(R\) initialized-operator state; and
* \(\varepsilon_{\rm agg}(K,T)\) be the propagated boundary defect of an
  aggregate cutoff.

For staged approximations that remain in common stability regions, the
triangle inequality and comparison estimate (5) give a bound of the form

\[
 \sup_{t\le T}|O_{P,Q,R,K}(t)-O_{\rm dense}(t)|
 \le E_{\rm mem}(P,T)
 +C_T\bigl(\varepsilon_{\rm data}
           +\varepsilon_W+\varepsilon_{\rm agg}\bigr).
 \tag{22}
\]

The constant and defect norms must be specified for each construction; (22)
is a proof architecture, not a claimed numerical rate. In particular, the
operator defect must control both orientations on the actual query family,
and the data defect must control all evolving source integrands. Each term can
be isolated by keeping the other axes exact during its validation.

## 8. Recommended research program

### First: sample-axis compression of the working closure

For circle tasks, implement Fourier--Galerkin coefficients for every
sample-indexed response and history field while retaining the neuron
population and exact \(W_0\). Compare it with the current parent closure at
matched \(P\), width and time. Measure Fourier tails of forward fields,
backward fields, residuals and history moments separately. This directly
tests the regularity premise behind (19).

This route has the strongest structural reason to work: it compresses a smooth
input variable, leaves feature learning intact, provides a whole-circle
predictor automatically, and makes \(W_0\) commute with the projection. It is
not the earlier Fourier readout attached to an inaccurate scalar ODE.

### Second: separate storage from action complexity for \(W_0\)

Use a reproducible shared operator or an exact lazy Gaussian transcript so
that forward and transpose calls remain coupled. Instrument the ranks and
singular tails of the actual query spans generated by the Fourier population
closure. This gives evidence for or against a smaller causal operator state
without changing the parent dynamics.

Only after a stable tail is observed should one truncate the transcript. The
theorem target is a bound on joint action error over the reached query family,
followed by a comparison estimate. A low-rank approximation of the raw matrix
is not the target.

### Third: revisit population-to-scalar compression only with a tail mechanism

Keep exact forest pruning and the clipped hierarchy as the correctness
baseline. Search for a small subsystem only after identifying a reason its
boundary defect decays: an invariant law family, low predictive rank, a
contractive conditional expectation, or a quantified diagram tail. Validate
the source defect and its amplification separately. Small output error on one
trajectory is evidence, not an autonomous restart guarantee.

## 9. Direct answers

* **Can the neuron populations be replaced by scalars?** Approximately at
  each fixed width, yes: the clipped aggregate hierarchy gives finite-horizon
  convergence. An exact smooth scalar state of fixed dimension cannot work
  uniformly in width on an open nonlinear feature-population family, by the
  jet-rank obstruction in section 3. A width-uniform finite-accuracy theorem
  for the response-memory closure remains open.
* **Can \(W_0\) be redrawn each gradient-flow round?** No, not while claiming to
  approximate the same initialized network. It destroys temporal reuse and
  shared transpose correlations and changes plateau behavior.
* **Are \(W_0,W_0^\top\) fundamentally uncompressible?** Their unrestricted
  exact action is not fixed-rank compressible. Their action on the task's
  adaptive query family may admit a smaller shared predictive state. Lazy
  Gaussian conditioning is exact for finite query transcripts; fixed size
  requires a new effective-rank theorem.
* **Can dependence on the number of samples disappear?** Not uniformly for
  arbitrary datasets and labels. It can be replaced by dependence on intrinsic
  task complexity. Fourier input modes on the circle, or a coreset satisfying
  (20), are principled ways to do so.
* **Which avenue is most promising now?** For eliminating sample count \(m\),
  use input-axis compression on top of the existing population closure. For
  eliminating neuron width \(n\) while retaining all \(m\) samples, the
  relevant target is a width-uniform aggregate hierarchy or a moving
  representative population with a shared initialized-operator law.

## Claim status and adversarial checks

Exact in this note: the projectability criterion, fixed-kernel loss hierarchy,
sample-count dimension lower bound, one-sample tanh width/jet-rank
obstruction, moving-atom approximation estimate, Gaussian reuse covariances,
shared-action compatibility conditions, and the coreset comparison
inequality.

Established internally elsewhere in this study: finite-horizon response-memory
bounds and the clipped scalar-hierarchy convergence theorem at fixed finite
problem parameters.

Conditional proposals: Fourier--Galerkin convergence for the complete
response-memory fields and fixed-size causal compression of \(W_0\). Their
listed regularity, containment, response-rank and source-defect obligations
remain to be proved and tested.

The following shortcuts are excluded from a successful result: replaying a
recorded target trajectory; refreshing moments from the parent population;
using independent forward and reverse random operators; hiding the dataset or
matrix in unbounded-precision scalar coefficients; matching only one-time
Gaussian marginals; or proving a small truncation source without controlling
its nonlinear amplification.

This assessment uses the current response-memory construction, its aggregate
hierarchy and scalar-tail analyses, its shared-operator and Gaussian-autonomy
checks, and the maintained Gaussian-reuse and observable-closure material. No
new experiment is claimed.
