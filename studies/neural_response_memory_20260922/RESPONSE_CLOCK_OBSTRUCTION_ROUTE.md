The route initially searched independently for reachable finite-time clock

## 1. Finding and precise scope

No counterexample was established. In particular, a bounded physical path
with infinitely rotating normalized residual is only a kinematic possibility;
it is not a counterexample unless generated by this closure.

There is a complete mechanism excluding this obstruction on each fixed
finite horizon for all sufficiently large orders. On every regular interval,
the reconstructed middle matrix has total variation bounded independently of
both the order and the clock length. If the residual is bounded below, this
bounds the clock length as well. Comparison with the dense trajectory then
prevents loss of the residual lower bound at sufficiently large order.

This proves an eventual-in-order continuation mechanism. It does not prove
global regularity for every fixed small order, or any statement uniform in
the physical horizon.

## 2. Uniform physical bounds on any regular segment

Use exactly the notation and algorithm of the assigned closure. All matrix
norms below are Frobenius norms and all vector norms are Euclidean. Fix a
physical horizon T. For a physical parameter triple use the sum norm
||theta||_* = ||W1||_F + ||W2||_F + ||W3||_2. Put

    Y = (mean_a y_a^2)^(1/2),
    B = (||W3(0)|| + sqrt(n) Y) exp(2T),
    R = B/sqrt(n) + Y,
    A_* = 1 + TR.

On every regular segment contained in [0,T], independently of P and L,

    ||W3|| <= B,   rho <= R,   A <= A_*.

Indeed, ||h2_a||<=sqrt(n) and Cauchy--Schwarz give

    ||W3_dot|| <= 2 sqrt(n) rho <= 2||W3||+2 sqrt(n)Y.

The integral version of this inequality gives the displayed bound. Also

    sum_a ||u_a||^2 <= M B^2,
    sum_a ||h1_a||^2 <= Mn.

These estimates hold on the matching prefix too, since B bounds the initial
outer weights. Weighted orthogonal projection is contractive. Consequently,
writing S_a for its product reconstruction,

    sum_a ||S_a||
      <= [sum_a integral ||u_a||^2 dmu]^(1/2)
         [sum_a integral ||h1_a||^2 dmu]^(1/2)
      <= M sqrt(n) B A.

The same bound without A applies to the sum of prefix corrections. Thus

    ||W_hat|| <= B2 := ||W0|| + 2B(A_*+1)/sqrt(n).

If X=(mean_a ||x_a||^2/d)^(1/2), the prescribed first-layer equation gives

    ||W1_dot|| <= 2 B2 B X rho.

It bounds W1 on [0,T]. All physical weights therefore lie in a common
bounded set independent of P, L and the duration of the regular segment.
These estimates do not require a positive lower bound for rho.

## 3. Endpoint error is an exact energy-production term

For f equal to one of the vector histories h_a or u_a, let F denote its
moment matrix and set

    Z_f(t) = integral ||f(xi)||^2 dmu_t(xi),
    D_f(t) = Z_f(t) - tr(F G^(-1) F^T).

Projection orthogonality identifies D_f with
||f-Pi_mu f||_(L2(mu))^2. In particular D_f>=0. The matching constant prefix
is exactly represented for every P>=1, so D_f(0)=0.

Let fstar=F G^(-1)e. The moment equations imply

    d/dt tr(F G^(-1)F^T)
       = rho(2 f_current dot fstar - ||fstar||^2).

To check the potentially dangerous moving-coordinate terms, insert

    F_dot = rho f_current e^T -(g/L)F T^T,
    (G^(-1))_dot
      = -rho G^(-1)ee^T G^(-1)
        +(g/L)(G^(-1)T+T^T G^(-1)).

The two negative dilation terms from F_dot cancel the two positive terms
from the derivative of G^(-1), before taking the trace. Since a historical
value at its fixed raw coordinate xi never changes after insertion,

    Z_f_dot = rho ||f_current||^2.

Hence the exact identity is

    D_f_dot = rho ||f_current-fstar||^2.                 (4)

There is no derivative of a fitted historical value in Z_f. The fitted
polynomial changes, but the data being projected do not. There is no
unaccounted derivative of the trial space: degree<P polynomials in xi/L
span the same raw-coordinate polynomial space for every L>0. Either the
explicit cancellation above or this fixed-space interpretation establishes
the identity.

## 4. Coarse total variation of the reconstructed middle matrix

Write Delta h_a=h_a-hstar_a and Delta u_a=u_a-ustar_a. The exact velocity
defect is

    E = (2rho/(nM)) sum_a Delta u_a Delta h_a^T.

Integrating, applying Cauchy--Schwarz first in time and then in a, and using
(4), gives

    integral_0^t ||E|| ds
      <= (2/(nM)) sum_a sqrt(D_(u,a)(t) D_(h,a)(t))
      <= (2/(nM)) sqrt(sum_a D_(u,a)(t) sum_a D_(h,a)(t)).

The physical bounds and D_f<=Z_f imply

    sum_a D_(u,a)(t) <= MB^2 A(t),
    sum_a D_(h,a)(t) <= Mn A(t).

Therefore, throughout every regular segment,

    integral_0^t ||E|| ds <= 2BA(t)/sqrt(n).             (5)

The norm of the canonical middle velocity is at most 2B rho/sqrt(n), because
sum_a |r_a|<=M rho. It follows that

    integral_0^t ||W_hat_dot|| ds
      <= 2B(2A(t)-1)/sqrt(n)
      <= V_* := 2B(2A_*-1)/sqrt(n).                    (6)

The constants in (5)--(6) do not depend on P or L. The estimate is deliberately
coarse; it need not vanish at initialization and is used for continuation,
not for the convergence rate. Pointwise endpoint errors may still be large.

## 5. The normalized-residual derivative and clock bound

Let s=r/rho, where r is the M-vector of sample residuals. Then ||s||=sqrt(M)
and, wherever rho>0,

    s_dot = (I-rr^T/||r||^2) r_dot/rho.

In particular ||s_dot||<=||r_dot||/rho. The factor is exactly 1/rho;
there is no missing sqrt(M).

For clarity, let stacked norms include all samples and define

    C_h = 2 sqrt(M) B2 B X^2,
    C_delta = 2 sqrt(Mn) + 2B B2 C_h + 2B sqrt(Mn),
    C_r = 2 sqrt(M) + (B B2 C_h)/n + B sqrt(M/n).

Direct differentiation of the prescribed network gives, with
v=||W_hat_dot||,

    ||h1_dot||_stack <= C_h rho,
    ||delta2_dot||_stack <= C_delta (rho+v),
    ||r_dot|| <= C_r (rho+v).

For example,

    ||h2_dot||_stack <= sqrt(Mn) v + B2 C_h rho;

substitution into the formulas for delta2_dot and f_dot gives the last two
bounds. These constants are finite even for degenerate data; sharpness is
irrelevant.

Since u_a=s_a delta2_a, ||delta2_a||<=B, and |s_a|<=sqrt(M),

    ||u_dot||_stack
      <= sqrt(M)||delta2_dot||_stack + B||s_dot||.

Thus on an interval where rho>=m>0,

    ||Psi_dot|| <= C_Psi (rho+v),
    C_Psi = C_h + sqrt(M) C_delta + B C_r/m.

Using (6) in the actual clock definition yields the order-independent bound

    L(t) <= Lambda(T,m)
      := 1 + (1+C_Psi)(A_*-1) + C_Psi V_*.             (7)

The residual lower bound is essential to this argument. A finite variation
bound for r alone does not control the variation of r/||r|| near zero.

## 6. Continuation and the large-order bootstrap

For a dense trajectory with rho0>0, all physical weights are bounded on a
fixed finite horizon. Its residual obeys r_dot=-K(t)r with a continuous,
bounded matrix K(t), obtained by differentiating the outputs along the
canonical gradient velocities. Consequently there is a finite k_T such that

    rho_dense(t) >= mu := rho0 exp(-k_T T) >0.

This is a finite-horizon lower bound, not a positive limiting training error.
It follows directly by integrating rho_dot>=-k_T rho. Put m=mu/2.

For a closure, consider its maximal regular initial interval on which rho>m.
Equations (2)--(7) give bounds for all physical variables and for L, with
L<=Lambda(T,m), throughout that interval. The same-history discrepancy and
the common bounded-region Lipschitz estimate from the assigned conditional
tracking argument therefore give

    sup_(s<=t) ||theta_hat_P(s)-theta_dense(s)||_*
      <= Lambda^2(Lambda-1) exp(K_T T)/(2n P(P+1)).     (8)

The proof of this comparison is local to each such regular interval: each
outer coordinate obeys its exact integral equation at theta_hat, the middle
coordinate obeys that equation plus the same-history discrepancy R_P, and
the norm of the common physical field difference is bounded by
K_T||theta_hat-theta_dense||_*. Integrating this inequality proves (8).
It assumes neither prior existence up to T nor prior clock control there.

Prediction, hence rho, is Lipschitz as a function of the physical weights
on the common bounded region. Choose P0(T) so that this Lipschitz constant
times the right side of (8) is less than mu/4 for every P>=P0(T). Then

    rho_hat(t) >= 3mu/4 > m

on the entire interval under consideration. A first contact with rho=m is
impossible by continuity.

There is also no other finite endpoint of regular existence before T.
For each fixed such P, prefix positivity gives

    lambda_min(G)
      >= min_(1<=ell<=Lambda, ||c||=1)
          integral_0^1 |c^T p(xi/ell)|^2 dxi
      =: gamma_(P,Lambda) >0.

The minimum is positive because its domain is compact, its integrand is
continuous, and a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an interval.
The dependence of gamma on P is allowed: continuation is performed for each
finite P separately. No uniform-in-P conditioning estimate is required.

The history formulas bound every entry of H, U and G for fixed P: their
basis arguments stay in [0,1], and the basis polynomials are bounded there.
Together with rho>=3mu/4, 1<=L<=Lambda and the physical bounds, these place
the full ODE state in a compact subset of its regular domain. Its locally
Lipschitz vector field is bounded on a neighborhood of that compact set, so
a finite maximal endpoint has a state limit; local existence from that limit
extends the solution. This contradicts maximality. Thus every P>=P0(T)
exists regularly through T and satisfies (7)--(8).

If rho0=0, the prescribed stationary bypass applies. Neither u nor its clock
needs to be constructed. This is a separate permitted branch of the stated
algorithm, not a regularization of the positive-residual dynamics.

### B. Prefix and energy cancellation

The squared projection residual is the raw history energy minus
tr(B_f G^-1 B_f^T). Raw historical values freeze at their raw xi coordinates.
Only the insertion boundary adds energy, with derivative rho||f||^2.
The coordinate-dilation terms in the fitted energy cancel exactly, leaving
rho(2<f,fstar>-||fstar||^2). Thus equation (9) is valid for both h and u.

The initial residual energy vanishes because constants belong to every
degree<P space, including P=1. There is no omitted prefix derivative.
The weighted polynomial space is fixed in raw xi, even though its basis
and the current measure change. The resulting integrated norm estimate
(10) uses Cauchy--Schwarz correctly and does not confuse the norm of an
integral with the integral of a norm.

### E. Sharp projection, defect and tracking rates

The histories are Lipschitz in xi because g>=||Psi_dot|| and g>=rho>0.
Their matching constant prefix is continuous, so their weak derivative
vanishes on an interval of length one. The measure density satisfies
rho/g<=1 on actual history and equals one on the prefix.

Weighted best approximation can therefore be compared with ordinary
unweighted Legendre projection. For k>=P the eigenvalue k(k+1) is at least
P(P+1). Integration by parts in the Legendre equation has no boundary term
because x(1-x) vanishes at both endpoints. Weighted derivative Bessel bounds
the coefficient tail. Rescaling gives the squared-error bound
L^2||fbar'||_2^2/[4P(P+1)], and the derivative bound improves it to
L^2(L-1)/[4P(P+1)]. This includes P=1.

Inserting these two squared errors in (10) produces precisely
L^2(L-1)/[2nP(P+1)]. The signed discrepancy is its time-integrated defect
because both reconstructions have the same initial W0. Equations (22)--(24)
therefore follow. In particular, the proof now controls the integrated norm
of the physical defect, a stronger statement than the earlier signed
same-history error alone.

### F. First exit, compact continuation and quantifiers

The choice of P0 in (25) makes af times the physical error at most mu/4.
The non-strict inequality in the definition of P0 is sufficient: the
resulting residual floor 3mu/4 remains strictly above the stopping floor
mu/2. Every constant in that choice was fixed from data, initialization and
T before selecting P.

On the stopped interval, the state bounds and L<=Lambda hold before any
putative singularity. For each fixed P, the prefix Gram is positive definite
for every l>0. Its smallest eigenvalue has a positive minimum on [1,Lambda].
This minimum may depend strongly on P; no order-independent inverse-Gram
bound is needed for the continuation step. Bounded basis values and history
energies bound each moment and Gram entry at that fixed P.

The full state therefore remains in a closed bounded set with a positive
distance from the rho=0, L=0 and singular-Gram boundaries. Reconstruction
is continuous there. A compact neighborhood contained in the regular ODE
domain has bounded vector field, which makes the state Cauchy at a finite
maximal time. Local existence at its limit extends it, contradicting
maximality. This argument also handles a possible endpoint exactly at T.
Local uniqueness supplies the uniqueness asserted in the theorem.

Thus neither the residual stopping boundary nor another regular-domain
failure can occur before the prescribed horizon. This establishes precisely
the eventual-P theorem, without implying all-small-P global existence or
an all-time clock bound.
