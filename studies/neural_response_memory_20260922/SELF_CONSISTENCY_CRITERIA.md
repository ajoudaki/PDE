# Self-consistency criteria for current-state neural motion laws

2026-09-24. Continuation of the response-memory question. The user now asks
for criteria that any proposed history-free neuronal motion law must obey,
with its population aggregates determined self-consistently. No new toy
closure is selected. The earlier minimum-norm transport model remains one
illustration, not the adopted answer to this question.

Scope: one sample, actual reused initialized Gaussian middle operator and
transpose, canonical nonlinear feature learning, and a specified interface
of current retained coordinates, population statistics and initialized
operator actions. No experiment, external research, or other study is used.

## 1. Canonical setup and exact causal identities

Use normalized inner products <u,v>=u^T v/n. Set chi=||x||^2/d and

    z1=W1 x/sqrt(d), h1=phi(z1), z2=W2 h1, h2=phi(z2),
    f=<W3,h2>, r=f-y,
    delta2=W3 .* phi'(z2), b1=W2^T delta2,
    delta1=phi'(z1) .* b1.

Loss r^2 and physical mobilities (W1,W2,W3)=(n,1,n) give

    z1'=-2r chi delta1, W3'=-2r h2,
    W2'=-2r delta2 h1^T/n.

All statements are finite-width identities on a smooth existence interval.
Population versions use the actual common-space forward/adjoint actions
and require the products and expectations in question to be integrable.

The two nodes immediately receiving a changing middle-matrix action are
the forward preactivation z2 and the ungated reverse response b1. By the
product rule,

    z2'=-2r delta2 <h1,h1> + W2 h1',                  (1)
    b1'=-2r h1 <delta2,delta2> + W2^T delta2'.        (2)

The first term is the explicit W2' contribution; the second transports
the changing input to the matrix. Other nodes propagate these changes:

    h1'=phi'(z1) .* z1',
    h2'=phi'(z2) .* z2',
    delta2'=W3' .* phi'(z2)+W3 .* phi''(z2) .* z2',
    delta1'=phi''(z1) .* z1' .* b1+phi'(z1) .* b1'.

In particular, varying W2 with W1 and W3 held fixed also changes delta2
through z2, then the first-layer reverse response through W2^T delta2.
Calling just the first terms in (1),(2) the direct matrix-node effects
does not discard these indirect paths. First-layer forward activation has
no direct algebraic W2 dependence, but its velocity depends on W2 through
backpropagation, and its acceleration includes W2'.

## 2. Population feedback cannot be prescribed independently

For any current population observable g(m), its average is computed under
the same current population generated by the proposed motion. Provided
the chain rule and differentiation under expectation are justified,

    d E[g(m)]/dt = E[grad g(m) . m'].

For example,

    d E1[h1^2]/dt=2 E1[h1 h1'],
    d E2[delta2^2]/dt=2 E2[delta2 delta2'].

The declared current-state interface excludes importing coefficients from
an external dense trajectory. These identities check the internal feedback
of its population; by themselves they neither exclude externally supplied
correct coefficients nor prove compatibility with canonical network motion.

## 3. Shared-operator and interaction-balance tests

Take any differentiable retained first-layer source q and second-layer
source p, including responses, their derivatives, or nonlinear observables.
Both orientations must use the same W2, hence

    <p,W2 q>=<W2^T p,q>.                              (3)

Source linear relations must also be respected. If sum c_a q_a=0, then
sum c_a W2 q_a=0, and likewise for reverse sources.

Differentiating the mixed contraction and substituting W2' gives the exact
population balance identity

    d <p,W2 q>/dt
      =<p',W2 q>+<W2^T p,q'>
        -2r <p,delta2><h1,q>.                        (4)

The product of two layer averages is forced by the rank-one gradient; it
is not an optional mean-field ansatz. Two stronger separate motion tests
for proposed forward action u=W2 q and reverse action v=W2^T p are

    <p,u'>-<v,q'>=-2r<p,delta2><h1,q>,
    <p',u>-<v',q>=+2r<p,delta2><h1,q>.               (5)

Their sum differentiates (3), whereas either equation also uses the actual
prescribed matrix velocity. Tests must include mixed pairs of all retained
orders, not only q=h1 and p=delta2. The latter pair gives

    <delta2,z2'>-<b1,h1'>
        =-2r<h1,h1><delta2,delta2>.                   (6)

An order-one defect in (3) is a quantitative action-error obstruction:
if proposed u=W2 q+eF and v=W2^T p+eR, then

    |<p,u>-<v,q>| <= ||p||_n ||eF||_n+||q||_n ||eR||_n.

Finite source lists have a precise instantaneous realizability criterion.
Writing their columns as Q,U,P,V, a common matrix W with WQ=U,W^T P=V
exists exactly when

    ker Q subset ker U, ker P subset ker V, P^T U=V^T Q.

For prescribed D=W', apply this criterion to source/output pairs
(Q,Q') -> (U,U'-DQ) and (P,P') -> (V,V'-D^T P). This also tests
instantaneous derivative realizability with the prescribed W'.
The full proof is in SHARED_OPERATOR_CONSISTENCY_CHECK.md. These finite
tests do not prove one consistent matrix path or autonomous state sufficiency.
For limiting Gram data, source-kernel compatibility additionally requires
control of amplification along nearly null sources, for example a uniform
operator bound. It is automatic when the stated bounded common-space actions
already exist, but does not follow from moment convergence alone.

## 4. The exact no-history criterion

Let X be the full canonical state, with the fixed initialization and task
included as fixed parameters. Let P(X) be the proposed complete retained
collective state: both neuron populations, the specified current aggregates,
and precisely the permitted operator information. Let the full vector field
be V(X). Its induced retained velocity is DP(X)V(X).

An exact current-state law exists on the admissible reachable class only
if equal retained states imply equal retained velocities:

    P(X)=P(Y) implies DP(X)V(X)=DP(Y)V(Y).             (7)

This is an algebraic necessary and sufficient condition for defining a
single-valued velocity on the image of P. Regularity, computability and
well-posedness of that velocity remain additional requirements.

If a proposed F obeys

    DP(X)V(X)=F(P(X))                                (8)

on the reachable class, the projected canonical path solves the proposed
ODE. Uniqueness of that ODE proves exact agreement from matching initial
states on their common existence interval. This is the meaningful strong
self-consistency criterion; a finite set of scalar moment matches is weaker.
Require one rule over the admissible family and restarts, not an encoding
of a single observed trajectory or of physical time alone.

For a specified tagged-neuron information interface I, let v be its actual
retained velocity. Any candidate F(I) obeys the exact L2 decomposition

    E|v-F(I)|^2
      =E Var(v|I)+E|E[v|I]-F(I)|^2.                  (9)

Expand the square after adding/subtracting E[v|I]. The mixed term has zero
conditional expectation, proving (9). A positive first term rules out every
deterministic motion rule of that particular information interface. This
does not assume it is positive. The interface must include the current
aggregates and initialized receiver fields actually available to the law;
conditioning only on local coordinates would test a more restrictive model.

Conversely, if I is so rich that it determines the full velocity already,
the variance test is zero and says nothing about efficient evaluation.
The entire initial condition plus unrestricted trajectory reconstruction
cannot be smuggled into I as a compression certificate. Checking a specified
finite expression F remains meaningful even when that unrestricted
measurability test is vacuous.

Conditional-mean matching may give correct weak one-time marginal transport,
but it does not certify tagged paths or their temporal correlations. Those
are relevant here because repeated forward/adjoint actions depend on them.

## 5. Gaussian initialization as a local certificate

For the user's retained state (h1,h1',delta2,delta2'), the two position
equations are identities. The informative test is the pair of acceleration
equations. Under the same Gaussian roots and actual reused W2(0), compute

    E1[|h1''(0)_canonical-h1''(0)_proposed|^2],
    E2[|delta2''(0)_canonical-delta2''(0)_proposed|^2]. (10)

Also compute mixed action defects in (3),(5), all proposed population
coefficients from that same joint initialization, and the initial readouts.
Zero in (10) is equivalent to agreement almost surely under that population
law, assuming square integrability. Equality of average accelerations alone
allows cancellation and is not this certificate.

The canonical accelerations are obtained by differentiating (1),(2) and
the ordinary chain rules. Classical h1'' requires phi in C^2, and delta2''
requires phi in C^3. For example,

    h1''=phi''(z1) .* (z1')^2+phi'(z1) .* z1'',
    z1''=-2 chi [f' delta1+r delta1'].

Every fixed derivative order at initialization is a finite expression in
the initialized weights, matrix products/transposes, phi and its derivatives,
and population contractions. Established Gaussian source/response calculus
therefore provides the joint normal forms for admissible such expressions;
unbounded products or high derivatives require their moment and truncation
justifications. No polynomial approximation of phi and no convergent time
Taylor expansion are assumed.

For finite Gaussian programs the book supplies empirical joint convergence.
Identifying a limit of finite-network derivatives with derivatives of an
infinite-width trajectory additionally requires a justified interchange.
The initial tests can be stated as limits of finite-network jet residuals
without making that stronger claim.

The finite test is useful in either direction: solve for proposed law
coefficients using the Gaussian equations, then verify residuals not used
to choose them; or show a positive least possible residual, falsifying a
specified current-state interface. This is a test program, not an experiment
run or a computed certificate for an as-yet unspecified law.

## 6. How to go beyond an initialization certificate

Matching finitely many initial derivatives is necessary local evidence, not
an all-time theorem. Even all initial derivatives do not determine a general
smooth trajectory: the function exp(-1/t^2), extended by zero at t=0, is
flat at zero and nonzero afterward. This fits the user's warning about
nonanalytic population dynamics rather than being a reason to avoid finite
Gaussian calculations.

The appropriate next proof target is preservation of the consistency
relations. For example, let E(t) be a nonnegative squared defect controlling
all required retained-motion discrepancies. If its evolution can be proved
to satisfy E'(t)<=C(t)E(t), with C locally integrable, then

    E(t)<=E(0) exp(integral_0^t C(s)ds).

This follows by multiplying by exp(-integral C) and differentiating; no
analyticity is used. A Gaussian certificate E(0)=0 then propagates. An
inhomogeneous omitted-response term would need an explicit bound instead.
Here E is locally absolutely continuous and its differential inequality
holds almost everywhere.
The existence of such a closed defect estimate is a substantive proof
obligation, not a consequence of passing the initial tests. The defect set
must determine the intended motion; preserving a few aggregate identities
does not suffice.
