# Independent depth-three/depth-four order-five derivation and audit

## 1. Bottom line and claim levels

This route starts from the finite-width moving-feature flow, builds every
forward/transpose response chronologically, eliminates all Gaussian
innovations by inverse-free Wick--Stein rules, and terminalizes in products of
one-dimensional layer moments. It was developed and frozen before the primary
depth-three/depth-four coefficient maps were inspected.

For each \(H\in\{3,4\}\), the route produces exact deterministic arithmetic
DAGs and fully distributed sparse maps for

\[
A_H=F_H'(0),\qquad B_H=F_H^{(3)}(0),\qquad C_H=F_H^{(5)}(0).
\]

The frozen independent and primary maps agree atom by atom, both with layer
tags and after the unit-Gram quotient. A later exact rational audit also
proves agreement of every explicit \(Q^0\) coefficient. The resulting
large-width statement is theorem-level under the strong fixed-program
regularity hypothesis in Section 11. The following are deliberately not
claimed: positive-time convergence, convergence of the Taylor series,
Stieltjes positivity, a simultaneous \(H=H(n)\) limit, or a depth-uniform
bound on the size of the distributed formula.

The arbitrary-depth deep-linear formulas displayed in Section 9 have a
different status: \(A_H\) is proved, while the proposed closed forms for
\(B_H,C_H\) are exact finite-difference candidates checked on a finite depth
range. No symbolic depth-induction proof was obtained, so those two formulas
are not promoted as arbitrary-\(H\) theorems.

## 2. Model and exact finite-width feature flow

Write

\[
h^\ell=\phi(z^\ell),\qquad
M^\ell={W^\ell\over\sqrt n}\quad(2\leq\ell\leq H),
\]

and use \(\langle u,v\rangle_n=n^{-1}\sum_i u_iv_i\). Thus

\[
z^1={W^1x\over\sqrt{d_0}},\qquad
z^\ell=M^\ell h^{\ell-1},\qquad
f_n=\langle a,h^H\rangle_n.
\]

Define the unnormalized backward vectors

\[
b^H=a\odot\phi'(z^H),\qquad
b^{\ell-1}=\phi'(z^{\ell-1})\odot(M^\ell)^Tb^\ell.
\]

Let a dot denote the parameter flow generated by
\(D_n=n\nabla f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla\). Direct differentiation in the raw
parameters, before taking a width limit, gives

\[
\boxed{
\dot a=h^H,\qquad
\dot M^\ell={1\over n}b^\ell(h^{\ell-1})^T,
\qquad
\dot z^1=Q^0b^1.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The factor \(Q^0=\lVert x\rVert^2/d_0\) in the last equation is the
first-layer parameter metric. It is not an optional forward-variance
normalization.

For an ordinary Taylor coefficient
\(v_{[k]}=\partial_t^kv(0)/k!\), (2.1) implies the exact finite-width rules

\[
M^\ell_{[r]}={1\over rn}
\sum_{p+q=r-1}b^\ell_{[p]}(h^{\ell-1}_{[q]})^T,
\tag{2.2}
\]

\[
z^\ell_{[k]}=M^\ell_0h^{\ell-1}_{[k]}
+\sum_{r=1}^kM^\ell_{[r]}h^{\ell-1}_{[k-r]},
\tag{2.3}
\]

\[
h^\ell_{[k]}=
\sum_{s=0}^k{\phi^{(s)}(z^\ell_0)\over s!}
[t^k]\left(\sum_{r\geq1}z^\ell_{[r]}t^r\right)^s,
\tag{2.4}
\]

\[
a_{[r]}={h^H_{[r-1]}\over r}\quad(r\geq1),
\qquad
f_{n,[k]}=\sum_{p=0}^k\langle a_{[p]},h^H_{[k-p]}\rangle_n.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The reverse coefficients are obtained by the same convolution rule applied
to the displayed definition of \(b^\ell\). Finally,

\[
D_n^kf_n=k!f_{n,[k]}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Equations (2.1)--(2.6) are exact finite-width identities. They do not use a
Gaussian limit or a response ansatz.

For completeness, they were cross-checked against the universal six-family
fifth-derivative identity. In normalized parameter coordinates
\(\vartheta=\theta/\sqrt n\), put
\(p=\nabla_\vartheta f_n\), \(H=\nabla_\vartheta^2f_n\), and let
\(T,U,V\) be the third, fourth, and fifth derivative tensors. Since
\(D_n=p\mathbin\cdot\nabla_\vartheta\), direct product-rule differentiation
gives the exact finite-width identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_n^5f_n={}&2V[p,p,p,p,p]+22U[Hp,p,p,p]
+14T[T[p,p],p,p]\\
&+30T[H^2p,p,p]+36T[Hp,Hp,p]
+16\lVert H^2p\rVert^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus all six contraction families, with coefficients
\((2,22,14,30,36,16)\), are present. The moving-feature Taylor route and
(2.7) are two representations of the same exact finite-width derivative;
the response/equality analysis below is applied only after this identity
level.

## 3. Chronological population response transfer

Fix one initialized internal matrix \(M^\ell_0\), \(2\leq\ell\leq H\).
Introduce the deterministic coordinate covariances

\[
\mathsf H^\ell_{pq}
=\mathbb E[h^{\ell-1}_{[p]}h^{\ell-1}_{[q]}],
\qquad
\mathsf B^\ell_{pq}
=\mathbb E[b^\ell_{[p]}b^\ell_{[q]}].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \((F^\ell_0,\ldots,F^\ell_5)\) be a fresh centered Gaussian family with
covariance \(\mathsf H^\ell\), and let
\((R^\ell_0,\ldots,R^\ell_4)\) be a fresh centered Gaussian family with
covariance \(\mathsf B^\ell\). Distinct matrix layers have distinct fresh
families. The forward and reverse fresh families for one reused matrix are
independent; the response terms below restore the dependence caused by
matrix reuse.

Define the chronological response coefficients

\[
\alpha^\ell_{k,s}
=\mathbb E\!\left[\partial_{R^\ell_s}h^{\ell-1}_{[k]}\right]
\quad(0\leq s<k\leq5),
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\beta^\ell_{k,s}
=\mathbb E\!\left[\partial_{F^\ell_s}b^\ell_{[k]}\right]
\quad(0\leq s\leq k\leq4).
\tag{3.3}
\]

These are formal derivatives in the finite polynomial response grammar; no
covariance inverse or pseudoinverse occurs. The limiting coefficient of the
forward matrix action is

\[
\boxed{
z^\ell_{[k]}=F^\ell_k
+\sum_{s<k}\alpha^\ell_{k,s}b^\ell_{[s]}
+\sum_{r=1}^k{1\over r}
 \sum_{p+q=r-1}
 b^\ell_{[p]}\mathsf H^\ell_{q,k-r}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The corresponding transpose action
\(\rho^\ell_{[k]}=[t^k](M^\ell)^Tb^\ell\) is

\[
\boxed{
\rho^\ell_{[k]}=R^\ell_k
+\sum_{s=0}^k\beta^\ell_{k,s}h^{\ell-1}_{[s]}
+\sum_{r=1}^k{1\over r}
 \sum_{p+q=r-1}
 h^{\ell-1}_{[q]}\mathsf B^\ell_{p,k-r}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
b^{\ell-1}_{[k]}
=\sum_{p=0}^k
[t^p]\phi'(z^{\ell-1}(t))\;\rho^\ell_{[k-p]}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The schedule is important. At order \(k\), first compute the forward sweep
through every matrix and only then compute the reverse sweep. Consequently
the transpose response in (3.3)--(3.5) includes the same-order term \(s=k\),
whereas the forward response in (3.2)--(3.4) has only \(s<k\). Omitting this
same-order transpose response changes the fifth coefficient.

The bottom-layer update closes the sweep:

\[
z^1_{[k+1]}={Q^0\over k+1}b^1_{[k]}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equations (3.1)--(3.7), followed by (2.4)--(2.5), are the independent
arbitrary-fixed-depth transfer used by the compiler. They are a proof
intermediate representation, not the terminal answer.

## 4. Equality partitions, transpose reuse, and closure

Neuron indices at different hidden layers are different types even though
their numerical range is the same. A Wick delta can therefore join only
compatible uses of the same initialized matrix with compatible orientation.
For one matrix, a forward use at order \(k\) has exactly three leading
sources:

1. the all-free forward Gaussian \(F^\ell_k\);
2. one opposite-orientation identification with an earlier reverse use,
   giving the \(k\) response slots \(s<k\);
3. one of the \(k(k+1)/2\) literal rank-one terms from (2.2).

A transpose use at order \(k\leq4\) likewise has:

1. the all-free reverse Gaussian \(R^\ell_k\);
2. one identification with a forward use \(s\leq k\), including the
   chronologically earlier same-order use;
3. one of the \(k(k+1)/2\) transposed rank-one terms.

Any additional equality of a same-layer free index removes one free width
sum without supplying another normalization and is \(O(n^{-1})\). An
equality between different layer types is not a legal Wick delta. Thus the
three sectors above exhaust the leading equality partitions.

Through terminal order five, each initialized internal matrix requires 15
forward responses, 15 transpose responses, 21 forward covariances
\((0\leq s\leq k\leq5)\), and 15 reverse covariances
\((0\leq s\leq k\leq4)\). This is 66 registered scalar states per reused
matrix and \(66(H-1)\) before zero or duplicate expressions are merged. The
state closes at every fixed \(H\). It does not imply a constant-size
flattened formula: distributing the deterministic products grows rapidly
with depth.

The forward fifth coefficient uses at most \(\phi^{(5)}\); the last needed
reverse coefficient is order four, and its \(\phi'\) composition also stops
at \(\phi^{(5)}\). This finite-width observation alone is not sufficient,
because a careless Stein integration can create higher derivatives. The
inverse-free terminal rules and frozen-map scan below supply the separate
derivative-ceiling audit.

## 5. Inverse-free Gaussian elimination

The reverse fresh variables contain no base activation Gaussian and are
removed by ordinary Wick recursion. For reverse multiplicities
\(\rho=(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_4)\), choose \(i\) with \(\rho_i>0\), put
\(\rho'=\rho-e_i\), and set

\[
\mathcal W_{\mathsf B}(\rho)
=\sum_{j:\rho'_j>0}
\rho'_j\,\mathsf B_{ij}\,
\mathcal W_{\mathsf B}(\rho'-e_j),
\qquad
\mathcal W_{\mathsf B}(0)=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the forward family, \(F_0\) is the base preactivation. Let
\(\eta=(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_5)\) count nonbase forward innovations and let
\(\nu=(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_5)\) count activation derivatives at that layer.
Choosing \(i\geq1\) with \(\eta_i>0\), \(\eta'=\eta-e_i\), Gaussian
integration by parts gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_{\mathsf H}(\eta,\nu)
={}&\sum_{j=1}^5\eta'_j\mathsf H_{ij}
       \mathcal S_{\mathsf H}(\eta'-e_j,\nu)\\
&+\mathsf H_{i0}\sum_{r=0}^4\nu_r
       \mathcal S_{\mathsf H}
       (\eta',\nu-e_r+e_{r+1}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Its terminal value is

\[
\mathcal S_{\mathsf H}(0,\nu)
=M^\ell_{\nu_0\ldots\nu_5}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The readout Gaussian is removed by its double factorial. Applying
(5.1)--(5.3) matrix layer by matrix layer leaves only deterministic rational
arithmetic and the one-dimensional atoms

\[
M^\ell_{\nu_0\ldots\nu_5}
=\mathbb E_{G\sim N(0,Q^{\ell-1})}
\prod_{r=0}^5\phi^{(r)}(G)^{\nu_r},
\qquad Q^\ell=M^\ell_{200000}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

No auxiliary multivariate Gaussian, tangent, backward carrier, implicit
Stein derivative, covariance inverse, or empirical random covariance
remains. In the unit-Gram quotient all layer tags are identified and
\(M_{200000}=1\).

The compiler stores deterministic additions and multiplications in a
hash-consed DAG and then applies a separately written distributive
canonicalizer. Thus the DAG is already a finite terminal Gaussian normal
form; the expanded coefficient map is an independent canonicalization audit,
not an instruction to evaluate a response recursion.

## 6. Parity

Let \(S\) negate only the readout \(a\). Then \(f_n(S\theta)=-f_n(\theta)\),
and orthogonality of \(S\) gives

\[
(D_n^kf_n)(S\theta)=(-1)^{k+1}(D_n^kf_n)(\theta).
\]

The initialization is invariant under \(S\). Therefore, whenever the
expectations exist,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E f_n=\mathbb E D_n^2f_n=\mathbb E D_n^4f_n=0}
\tag{6.1}
\]

at every finite width and every depth. The zero parity roots in all four
frozen artifacts are also literal compiler zeros, but (6.1) is the stronger
audit because it does not rely on compiler cancellation.

## 9. Deep-linear arbitrary-depth formula: exact scope

Put \(m=H+1\), the number of independently initialized parameter blocks. At
initialization the \(m\) blockwise gradient energies each converge to one,
so

\[
\boxed{A_H=m}
\tag{9.1}
\]

is proved for every fixed \(H\).

Exact compiler values through \(H=10\) suggest

\[
B_H={2m^2(m^2-1)\over3},
\qquad
C_H={m^2(m^2-1)(17m^3-4m^2-38m-4)\over15}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Equivalently, with base \(B(1)=C(1)=0\), their proposed induction increments
are

\[
B(m)-B(m-1)={4\over3}m(m-1)(2m-1),
\tag{9.3}
\]

\[
C(m)-C(m-1)={m(m-1)\over15}
(119m^4-262m^3+118m^2-7m-26).
\tag{9.4}
\]

All exact values in the tested range satisfy (9.3)--(9.4), including the
separately Wick-enumerated \(H=3,4\) cases. However, the current derivation
does not prove that the deep-linear depth transfer has polynomial degree at
most seven, nor does it derive (9.4) from a closed symbolic state induction.
Consequently:

- (9.1) is a theorem;
- the \(H=3,4\) numerical values are exact controls;
- (9.2)--(9.4) for arbitrary \(H\) are strongly checked conjectures and are
  not used to certify the generic maps.

This classification prevents a finite interpolation from being silently
promoted into an arbitrary-depth proof. The missing obligation is precise:
derive a finite leading-diagram transfer, or a symbolic specialization of
(3.1)--(3.7), for which (9.3)--(9.4) are genuine induction identities.

## 10. Complexity and arbitrary fixed depth

At derivative order five, the chronological registry has a fixed number of
response/covariance slots per initialized internal matrix, and the outer
forward/reverse sweep is \(O(H)\). This proves an executable construction for
every separately fixed depth. It does not prove \(O(H)\) size for the
flattened output. The tagged \(C\) map already grows from 1,045 terms at
\(H=2\) to 27,421 at \(H=3\) and 462,776 at \(H=4\).

Nor is the state literally memoryless in depth: a response coefficient is
layer-local with respect to the reused matrix, but its deterministic value
contains the complete earlier forward/reverse chronology. The hash-consed
arithmetic DAG shares this history exactly; dropping it would change the
normal form.

## 11. Probability bridge and activation hypotheses

The finite-width identities in Section 2 require only enough regularity and
integrability to differentiate five times. A convenient finite-algebra
envelope is

\[
\phi\in C^5,\qquad
|\phi^{(r)}(x)|\leq C_r(1+|x|^{m_r}),
\quad0\leq r\leq5.
\tag{11.1}
\]

This alone does not justify a population limit or interchange that limit
with expectation.

For a direct fixed-program annealed theorem at each separately fixed
\(H\in\{3,4\}\), a sufficient stronger hypothesis is

\[
\phi\in C^\infty,
\qquad
\forall r\geq0\ \exists C_r,m_r<\infty:
|\phi^{(r)}(x)|\leq C_r(1+|x|^{m_r}).
\tag{11.2}
\]

Under (11.2), the order-five construction is a finite polynomial-smooth
tensor program with repeated matrix/transpose uses, empirical moments, and
rank-one Taylor updates. The all-finite-\(L^p\) fixed-program convergence
theorem cited in the project probability audit then yields, for
\(k=1,3,5\),

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb E[D_n^kf_n]
=\mathbb E\!\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}D_n^kf_n\right].
\tag{11.3}
\]

In particular, \(L^{1+\varepsilon}\) convergence supplies uniform
integrability; it is not an extra hidden assumption in this strong tier.
Singular forward Grams need no pseudoinverse in the inverse-free route.

Under a weaker theorem that gives only convergence in probability or almost
surely, the missing annealed bridge must instead be assumed or proved. A
sufficient condition is

\[
\sup_n\mathbb E|D_n^kf_n|^{1+\varepsilon}<\infty,
\qquad k\in\{1,3,5\},
\tag{11.4}
\]

for some \(\varepsilon>0\). Merely citing (11.1) does not establish (11.4).
