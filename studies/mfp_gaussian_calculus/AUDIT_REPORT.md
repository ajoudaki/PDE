## 1. Coordinate and metric audit

Let \(\theta\) denote all raw parameters and write

\[
D_n=n\nabla_\theta f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla_\theta.
\]

There are two equivalent, but not interchangeable, ways to write the cubic
feature derivative.

In raw coordinates, with

\[
p_\theta=\nabla_\theta f_n,\qquad
H_\theta=\nabla_\theta^2f_n,\qquad
T_\theta=\nabla_\theta^3f_n,
\]

direct differentiation gives

\[
D_n f_n=n\|p_\theta\|^2,
\]

\[
D_n^2 f_n=2n^2H_\theta[p_\theta,p_\theta],
\]

and

\[
D_n^3 f_n
=n^3\left(
4\|H_\theta p_\theta\|^2
+2T_\theta[p_\theta,p_\theta,p_\theta]
\right).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Indeed, the derivative of \(H[p,p]\) in the direction \(p\) is

\[
T[p,p,p]+2H[Hp,p]
=T[p,p,p]+2\|Hp\|^2.
\]

To absorb the metric, define the whitened coordinate explicitly by

\[
\vartheta=\frac{\theta}{\sqrt n},\qquad \theta=\sqrt n\,\vartheta.
\]

Then

\[
\nabla_\vartheta f_n=\sqrt n\,\nabla_\theta f_n,
\qquad
D_n=\nabla_\vartheta f_n\mathbin\cdot\nabla_\vartheta.
\]

Consequently, if \(p,H,T\) mean derivatives with respect to \(\vartheta\),
then and only then does (1.1) become

\[
D_n^3 f_n=4\|Hp\|^2+2T[p,p,p].
\tag{1.2}
\]

The initialization law in the whitened coordinate is
\(\vartheta\sim N(0,n^{-1}I)\); whitening the optimizer metric does not
preserve the raw standard-Gaussian initialization law.  Any compiler or proof
must state which coordinate its derivatives use.  Omitting this distinction
creates an erroneous factor \(n^3\).

## 2. Exact readout parity

Let \(S\) flip all readout coordinates \(a_i\) and leave the hidden weights
fixed.  Because the readout is linear and centered,

\[
f_n(S\theta)=-f_n(\theta).
\]

If \(g(S\theta)=\varepsilon g(\theta)\), orthogonality of \(S\) gives

\[
(D_ng)(S\theta)=-\varepsilon(D_ng)(\theta).
\]

It follows inductively that

\[
(D_n^kf_n)(S\theta)=(-1)^{k+1}D_n^kf_n(\theta).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus \(D_nf_n\) and \(D_n^3f_n\) are even, whereas \(f_n\) and
\(D_n^2f_n\) are odd.  Their odd expectations vanish exactly at every width.
This fact does **not** permit deletion of an odd factor before it has been
multiplied by the residual or by another copy.  Products such as
\(f_nD_n^2f_n\) are even and need not vanish.

The same symmetry gives the formal oddness of the annealed feature jet.  If

\[
A=\lim_n\mathbb E[D_nf_n]>0,
\qquad
C=\lim_n\mathbb E[D_n^3f_n],
\]

then

\[
F(s)=As+\frac{C}{6}s^3+O(s^5).
\]

Formal reversion therefore yields

\[
K(y)=F'\!\left(F^{-1}(y)\right)
=A+\frac{C}{2A^2}y^2+O(y^4).
\tag{2.2}
\]

This proves the algebraic identity \(\mu_0=C/(2A^2)\).  It does not prove
\(\mu_0\geq0\), convergence at positive time, or concentration of the random
finite-width feature curve.

## 3. Finite-width loss jet

For label one, put

\[
r_n=1-f_n,\qquad L_n=r_n^2,
\]

and abbreviate the finite-width feature derivatives by

\[
K_n=D_nf_n,\qquad J_n=D_n^2f_n,\qquad C_n=D_n^3f_n.
\]

The physical squared-loss flow is generated by

\[
\mathcal G_n=2\eta r_nD_n.
\]

Applying this derivation before taking any expectation or width limit gives

\[
L_n'(0)=-4\eta r_n^2K_n,
\tag{3.1}
\]

\[
L_n''(0)
=16\eta^2r_n^2K_n^2-8\eta^2r_n^3J_n,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
L_n'''(0)
=-64\eta^3r_n^2K_n^3
+112\eta^3r_n^3K_nJ_n
-16\eta^3r_n^4C_n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For example, differentiating the first term of (3.2) produces

\[
32\eta^3r_nD_n(r_n^2K_n^2)
=-64\eta^3r_n^2K_n^3
+64\eta^3r_n^3K_nJ_n,
\]

while differentiating the second produces the remaining
\(48\eta^3r_n^3K_nJ_n-16\eta^3r_n^4C_n\).  This checks the coefficient
\(112=64+48\).

Equation (3.3) exposes three limit obligations that formal scalar reversion
alone hides:

1. \(\mathbb E[K_n^3]\to A^3\) needs concentration, not merely
   \(\mathbb E K_n\to A\);
2. the term \(r_n^3K_nJ_n\) is not odd because the residual contains odd
   powers of \(f_n\);
3. replacing \(r_n^4C_n\) by \(C_n\) needs uniform integrability.

A convenient sufficient set of assumptions is

\[
f_n\longrightarrow0\quad\text{in }L^8,
\qquad
K_n\longrightarrow A\quad\text{in }L^6,
\tag{3.4}
\]

\[
\sup_n\|J_n\|_{L^3}<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_n\|C_n\|_{L^2}<\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E C_n\longrightarrow C.
\tag{3.5}
\]

These are sufficient rather than claimed minimal.  They are proved for the
fixed program under the polynomially-smooth activation envelope by the
\(L^p\) master theorem cited in `PEELING_AND_PROBABILITY_LEDGER.md`; the
bounded finite-order tier is proved separately in
`PROBABILISTIC_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md`.  To verify the middle term,
expand

\[
r_n^3K_nJ_n
=(1-3f_n+3f_n^2-f_n^3)K_nJ_n.
\]

By (2.1), the terms with \(1\) and \(f_n^2\) have zero expectation exactly.
The \(f_n\) and \(f_n^3\) terms tend to zero by Hölder using (3.4)--(3.5).
The same parity argument removes odd residual powers in the other two terms,
and Hölder removes the remaining factors containing \(f_n^2\) or
\(f_n^4\).  Therefore (3.1)--(3.3) imply

\[
\lim_n\mathbb E L_n'(0)=-4\eta A,
\]

\[
\lim_n\mathbb E L_n''(0)=16\eta^2A^2,
\]

\[
\lim_n\mathbb E L_n'''(0)
=-64\eta^3A^3-16\eta^3C.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Consequently, if
\(\mathcal J_3[h](t)=\sum_{k=0}^3h^{(k)}(0)t^k/k!\), the coefficientwise
expected loss jet is

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal J_3[\mathbb E L_n](t)
=1-4\eta At+8\eta^2A^2t^2
-\left(\frac{32}{3}\eta^3A^3+\frac83\eta^3C\right)t^3
\pmod{t^4}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The frozen-NTK solution is \(e^{-4\eta At}\), whose cubic coefficient is
\(-32\eta^3A^3/3\).  Hence the first feature-dependent loss coefficient is
exactly

\[
-\frac83\eta^3C
=-\frac{16}{3}\eta^3A^2\mu_0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Equations (2.2) and (3.8) are independent algebraic routes to the same
conversion.  They become an accepted neural-width statement only after the
moment conditions above, or a rigorously proved alternative, have been
verified for the model.

The normalization to label one loses no one-sample coefficient information.
For an arbitrary fixed scalar label \(y_*\), replace \(r_n\) by
\(y_*-f_n\).  The same calculation gives, coefficientwise,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal J_3[\mathbb E L_n](t)
={}&y_*^2-4\eta y_*^2At
+8\eta^2y_*^2A^2t^2\\
&-\left(
\frac{32}{3}\eta^3y_*^2A^3
+\frac83\eta^3y_*^4C
\right)t^3\pmod{t^4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus arbitrary one-sample labels require no new Gaussian atoms; the first
feature correction simply carries \(y_*^4\).  This simplification does not
extend unchanged to a nonsymmetric multi-sample label vector.

## 4. Solvable controls

### 4.1 Constant activation and frozen features

For \(\phi\equiv c\),

\[
f_n=\frac cn\sum_i a_i,
\qquad
D_nf_n=c^2,
\qquad
D_n^kf_n=0\quad(k\ge2).
\]

Thus \(A=c^2\) and \(C=0\).  More generally, if only the readout block is
trained, \(f_n\) is affine along feature-ascent time and \(C=0\) for every
frozen hidden feature array.  Any generic formula retaining a nonzero cubic
coefficient in the readout-only metric fails this control.

### 4.2 Linear activation: exact finite-width calculation

For \(\phi(x)=x\), first take \(q_0=1\).  The network is

\[
f_n=n^{-3/2}a^TWu.
\]

Let \(G=a^TWu\), without the normalization, and set
\(p_G=\nabla G\).  Then

\[
p_G=(Wu,W^Ta,au^T),
\]

and

\[
H_Gp_G=\left(
a\|u\|^2+WW^Ta,
u\|a\|^2+W^TWu,
(Wu)u^T+aa^TW
\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because \(G\) is trilinear,

\[
T_G[p_G,p_G,p_G]=6G^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For independent standard-Gaussian \(a,u,W\), direct Gaussian moment
calculation gives

\[
\mathbb E G^2=n^2,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E\|H_Gp_G\|^2=12n^3+12n^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For completeness, either vector block in (4.1) contributes
\(5n^3+3n^2\).  This uses

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(WW^T)^2=2n^3+n^2.
\]

The matrix block contributes \(2n^3+6n^2\); its cross term is
\(2\mathbb EG^2=2n^2\).  These three contributions prove (4.4).

Since each derivative of \(f_n=n^{-3/2}G\) contributes its normalization,
(1.1), (4.2), and (4.4) give the exact finite-width expectation

\[
\mathbb E[D_n^3f_n]
=n^{-3}\left{
4(12n^3+12n^2)+12n^2
\right}
=48+\frac{60}{n}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Similarly, \(\mathbb E[D_nf_n]=3\).  Hence

\[
A=3,\qquad C=48,\qquad \mu_0=\frac83.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For general input norm \(q_0>0\), write \(u=\sqrt{q_0}\,v\).  The metric on
the single input projection becomes Euclidean in \(v\), and
\(f_n=\sqrt{q_0}\,n^{-3/2}a^TWv\).  Therefore

\[
D_{f_n}^kf_n=q_0^{(k+1)/2}D_g^kg,
\]

where \(g=n^{-3/2}a^TWv\) is the \(q_0=1\) model.  Thus the stronger control
is

\[
A=3q_0,\qquad
\mathbb E[D_n^3f_n]=q_0^2\left(48+\frac{60}{n}\right),
\qquad
C=48q_0^2,\qquad
\mu_0=\frac83.
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is an independently solvable nonquadratic regression, including its
finite-width correction.

### 4.3 Quadratic activation

For \(q_0=1\) and \(\phi(x)=x^2\), define

\[
q_1=\mathbb E[\phi(U)^2]=3,
\qquad
d_1=\mathbb E[\phi'(U)^2]=4,
\]

with \(U\sim N(0,1)\).  If \(Z\sim N(0,q_1)\), then

\[
q_2=\mathbb E[\phi(Z)^2]=27,
\qquad
d_2=\mathbb E[\phi'(Z)^2]=12.
\]

The generic NTK expression therefore gives

\[
A=q_2+q_1d_2+q_0d_1d_2=27+36+48=111.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The maintained quadratic compiler and its independent direct pairing
evaluator give

\[
C=1\,685\,184,
\qquad
\mu_0=\frac{C}{2A^2}=\frac{280864}{4107}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

```text
python3 independent_checks.py --max-order 3 --direct-wick-through 3
```

At order three, the transparent exhaustive pairing and the recursive
large-width evaluator both returned `1685184`; at order one they both returned
`111`.  This is an external regression gate for the new generic compiler, not
an independent derivation of the generic-activation normal form.

### 4.4 Atomwise comparison of the normal form

The independently derived analytic normal form uses the following notation:

\[
Q=q_1,\quad d=d_1,\quad r_4=e_1,\quad
a=m_1,\quad b=j_1,\quad c_2=s_1,\quad e=\ell_1,
\]

and, for the outer atoms,

\[
\begin{gathered}
D=d_2,\quad s_0=d_2+b_2,\quad s'_1=s_2+r_2,
\quad p'_1=u_2,\quad p'_2=b_2,\\
p'_3=w_2,\quad p'_4=r_2,\quad r=e_2,\quad
e'_0=p_2,\quad e'_{12}=v_2,\quad e'_2=s_2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Under (4.10), its intermediate contractions are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar c&=Q+q_0d,\\
\nu&=q_0^2r_4D,\\
\rho&=q_0^2Da,\\
\beta&=e'_0+2\bar c p'_1+3\bar c^2e'_{12}+\nu e'_2,\\
\chi&=D+s_0+\bar c s'_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.11}
\]

These agree term for term with

\[
\bar c=c,\qquad \beta=\tau,\qquad
\chi=k=d_2+\{d_2+b_2+c(r_2+s_2)\}
\]

in [`L2_B1_GAUSSIAN_NORMAL_FORM.md`](L2_B1_GAUSSIAN_NORMAL_FORM.md).
In particular, the coefficient of \(r_2+s_2\) is the full response
\(c=q_1+q_0d_1\), not \(q_1\), \(q_0d_1\), or one.  This is the nested
transpose-response factor most vulnerable to a missed equality sector.

The independent straight-line expression is

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_\star={}&c^2u_2+q_0^2e_1d_2b_2
+q_0^2d_2m_1(d_2+b_2)+c^3w_2\\
&+cq_0^2e_1d_2r_2
+cq_0^2d_2m_1(r_2+s_2)
+d_2^2(q_0^2m_1+q_0^3j_1),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.12}
\]

so the document's \(S_\star\) is exactly \(3T_\star\).  Its Hessian
expression is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\star={}&c^2e_2+2q_0^2e_1d_2^2+q_1\tau\\
&+q_0\left(
3q_0^2d_2^2s_1+k^2\ell_1+\tau d_1
+2q_0d_2km_1
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus both derivations give

\[
C=6T_\star+4H_\star=2S_\star+4H_\star.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Every \(q_0\) factor in (4.12)--(4.13) agrees with the independent
derivation: the fresh first-layer tangent variance carries \(q_0^2\), the
\(\phi'''(U)\) response carries \(q_0^3\), the first-weight Hessian norm has

### 4.5 Smooth nonpolynomial stress test and sign counterexample

For the audited formula, take \(q_0=1\) and
\(\phi(x)=\sin x\).  Set

\[
x=e^{-2},\qquad
Q=\frac{1-x}{2},\qquad
y=e^{-2Q}=e^{-(1-e^{-2})},
\]

\[
d=\frac{1+x}{2},\qquad
r_4=\frac{3+4x+x^4}{8}.
\]

Elementary Gaussian Fourier moments reduce the complete normal form to

\[
A=1,
\]

\[
C_{\sin}
=\frac{d(1+y)^2-(1+2y+y^4)}{2}
-r_4(1+y)(2+y)
=-1.88699982730593\ldots .
\tag{4.15}
\]

Equivalently, the independent decomposition gives

\[
T_\star=-1.31182638213145\ldots,
\qquad
H_\star=1.49598961637070\ldots,
\]

and \(C=6T_\star+4H_\star\).  A separate finite-width Taylor-jet oracle,
which performs no Gaussian replacement, gave the following initialization
Monte Carlo means (uncertainties are one standard error):

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
n&\text{seeds}&\mathbb E_{\rm MC}[D_n^3f_n]\\ \hline
16&2500&-2.0032\pm0.1244\\
32&1200&-1.7934\pm0.1024\\
64&400&-2.0122\pm0.1197
\end{array}
\]

This numerical route is a stress test, not a proof of (4.15).  The
theorem-certified probabilistic peel makes the exact negative expression
(4.15) a smooth bounded counterexample to generic Stieltjes positivity:

\[
\mu_0=\frac{C_{\sin}}{2}<0.
\]

Accordingly, for unrestricted smooth activations the audited object should be
called the **first nonlinear feature correction** or the **first Stieltjes
coefficient** (without a positivity implication).  It is not generically a
Stieltjes moment.

## 5. A concrete missing-response trap

Write, at initialization,

\[
b_j=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_iW_{ij}a_i\phi'(z_i).
\]

The first-layer contribution to the top preactivation velocity contains

\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_j
W_{ij}\phi'(u_j)^2b_j.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Substituting the definition of \(b_j\) exposes the equality sector in which
the row inside \(b_j\) equals the external row \(i\).  Its leading response is

\[
d_1a_i\phi'(z_i),
\qquad
d_1=\mathbb E[\phi'(U)^2].
\tag{5.2}
\]

For input metric \(q_0\), it enters the velocity multiplied by \(q_0\).  The
row-distinct part of (5.1) supplies a fresh Gaussian channel.  Treating
\(W_{ij}\) and \(b_j\) as independent deletes (5.2), already loses the
\(q_0d_1d_2\) term in the NTK, and cannot possibly give an audited value of
\(C\).  At cubic order, squared tangent fields also retain cross-copy equality
sectors even when either copy has zero one-copy mean.

Accordingly, every proposed normal form must explicitly account for:

- equality partitions before width counting;
- Wick contractions within each partition;
- Stein/Onsager responses such as (5.2);
- covariance-fluctuation terms before replacing an empirical covariance by
  its deterministic limit;
- cross-copy contractions created by \(\|Hp\|^2\).

A term may be discarded only after its global width degree or exact parity has
been established for the complete contraction.

## 6. Activation regularity

The finite-width differential identity (1.1) requires \(f_n\in C^3\), so
\(\phi\in C^3\) is the minimal classical differentiability assumption for the
unpeeled algebraic skeleton.  It is not automatically sufficient for a chosen
Gaussian integration-by-parts implementation.  If the peel differentiates an
integrand already containing \(\phi^{(3)}\), it can generate higher activation
derivatives.

The safe base contract is therefore:

1. run the complete symbolic peel and record its actual maximum derivative
   order \(r_*\);
2. assume \(\phi\in C^{r_*}\) and polynomial, or otherwise explicitly
   integrable, growth for every \(\phi^{(r)}\), \(0\le r\le r_*\);
3. verify the moment bounds needed for every displayed Gaussian atom and for
   (3.4)--(3.5).

The algebraic formula should state its audited finite \(r_*\), while any
probabilistic theorem must separately state the regularity required by the
limit theorem it invokes.  ReLU is outside this classical calculation:
distributional
derivatives introduce Gaussian boundary terms and cannot be obtained by
silently substituting pointwise derivatives into the smooth formula.

The normal form has recorded \(r_*=3\): all seventeen
atoms use only \(\phi,\phi',\phi'',\phi'''\), and the largest integrand has
four factors.  The algebraic atom audit found no hidden higher derivative.
This \(r_*=3\) statement is the regularity needed to define the algebraic
atoms.  The published \(L^p\) Tensor Program theorem used for the most general
annealed passage imposes the stronger all-orders polynomial-smoothness
hypothesis.  Under only finite-order regularity, the bounded tier is proved in
`PROBABILISTIC_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md`; the unbounded tier retains the explicit tail
condition stated in the peeling ledger.

## 7. Acceptance gates and final status

The base coefficient is audited because the following artifacts now exist and
agree.

1. **Finite-width source expression.**  An automatic or hand derivation of
   every term in (1.1), with raw/whitened metric factors traceable term by term.
2. **Complete peel ledger.**  For each parameter group, a list of every
   equality partition, Wick branch, response derivative, covariance state,
   and its global width degree.  Vanishing branches must carry a stated parity
   or negative-degree certificate.
3. **Literal Gaussian normal form.**  A canonical finite dictionary whose
   entries give each Gaussian dimension, covariance matrix, activation
   derivative multi-index, and exact coefficient.  No hidden tangent field,
   empirical covariance, neuron sum, or unevaluated derivative may remain.
4. **Independent canonicalization.**  A second implementation or derivation,
   not sharing the first implementation's contraction engine, must reduce to
   the same atom dictionary.  Numerical agreement for selected activations is
   useful but is not atomwise proof.
5. **Exact controls.**  Constant/readout-only \(C=0\), the exact linear family
   (4.7), and the quadratic integers (4.8)--(4.9) must all pass.  The linear
   finite-width term \(60q_0^2/n\) is an additional scaling diagnostic.
6. **Probabilistic bridge.**  Leave-one-out covariance replacement, joint
   finite-channel convergence, uniform integrability, and the moment bounds
   required by the loss audit must be proved or named explicitly as
   assumptions.  An annealed expectation calculation is not a concentration
   theorem.
7. **Direct loss check.**  The compiled \(A,C\), inserted into (3.3), must
   reproduce (3.7)--(3.8); no residual-weighted equality sector may survive.
8. **Reproducibility.**  Exact tests, commands, source hashes, canonical atom
   serialization, and failed branches must be retained.

At the date of the final bridge audit, every listed base-case gate passes
under polynomial smoothness: the finite-width skeleton, literal atom
dictionary, independent reduced-algebra comparison, controls, complete
response/negative-width ledger, joint almost-sure limit, and \(L^p\)/uniform-
integrability passage agree with no coefficient or \(q_0\)-scaling mismatch.
Therefore \(C\) is an **audited Gaussian normal form and annealed mean-field
coefficient** in that activation class.  Only the weaker unbounded
finite-order regularity tier remains conditional; batch/depth closure remains
a separate extension problem.
