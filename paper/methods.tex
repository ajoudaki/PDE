\section{Three ways to retain the learning process}
\label{sec:methods}

A hidden matrix has $n^2$ entries, but training does not write those
entries independently. Each update pairs a forward feature with a backward
response. The three constructions exploit different structure in these
pairs: temporal regularity, joint regularity in time and input, and a finite
computation whose random rows can be regenerated. Legendre and Harmonic
track a coupled dense reference; Logarithmic constructs a new predictor
compared with an independent dense run. Their different reference and
execution contracts are part of the result.

\subsection{Legendre: smooth histories generate a large matrix}
\label{sec:legendre-method}

Integrating the hidden-layer flow in \Cref{eq:dense-flow} gives
\begin{equation}
 W^{(\ell)}(t)-W_0^{(\ell)}=-\frac{2}{mn}\sum_{a=1}^m
 \int_0^t r_a(s)\delta_a^{(\ell)}(s)
 h_a^{(\ell-1)}(s)^\top\,\mathrm ds,
 \qquad 2\le\ell\le L.
 \label{eq:methods-history}
\end{equation}
The forward feature specifies which incoming directions are active; the
backward response specifies how changing this layer affects the output.
The residual sets the strength and sign of the write. As these vectors
turn during training, their accumulated outer products can have much
higher rank than any instantaneous update. Legendre compression uses
the regularity of that motion.

For a toy history with constant residual weighting, take
$h(s)=u_0+s u_1$ and $\delta(s)=v_0+s v_1$ on $[-1,1]$, where all four
coefficients are vectors in $\mathbb R^n$. Their average interaction is
$\frac12\int_{-1}^1\delta(s)h(s)^\top\,\mathrm ds
=v_0u_0^\top+v_1u_1^\top/3$.
Four vectors determine a matrix with potentially $n^2$ nonzero entries.
Keeping only the constant modes drops $v_1u_1^\top/3$; the mixed terms
$v_0u_1^\top$ and $v_1u_0^\top$ vanish on integration. Keeping both
polynomial modes recovers this toy interaction exactly. More generally,
orthogonal projection cancels every retained--discarded cross term.
The interaction error pairs the two discarded histories, so improving
both approximations multiplies their gains.

The actual model stores $q$ Legendre moments per sample and hidden link.
Hats denote its own reconstructed network. Its clock obeys
$\dot\tau=\widehat\rho$, with $\tau(0)=1$ and
$\widehat\rho=(m^{-1}\sum_a\widehat r_a^2)^{1/2}$.
This clock measures accumulated learning activity: it slows as residuals
decay, so the infinite physical training interval has finite clock length.
On clock time $\xi$, the paired histories are the forward feature
$\widehat h_a^{(\ell-1)}$ and the weighted backward response
$\widehat r_a\widehat\delta_a^{(\ell)}/\widehat\rho$.
The latter is a historical definition only where the clock advances.
The runtime equations have no residual denominator and stop regularly
when every residual vanishes.

Let $p_j$ be the shifted Legendre polynomial on $[0,1]$, normalized by
$p_j(1)=1$ and $\int_0^1p_ip_j=\mathbf1_{i=j}/(2j+1)$.
The unnormalized integrals of these two histories against
$p_j(\xi/\tau)$ on $[0,\tau]$, for $0\le j<q$, are the stored vectors
$\bar h_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)}$ and $\bar\delta_{a,j}^{(\ell)}$.
On the initial prefix $[0,1]$, the forward history equals its initialized
value and the backward history is zero. Orthogonality gives the
reconstruction
\begin{equation}
 \widehat W^{(\ell)}=W_0^{(\ell)}-
 \frac{2}{mn\tau}\sum_{a=1}^m\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(2j+1)
 \bar\delta_{a,j}^{(\ell)}\bar h_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)\top}.
 \label{eq:methods-legendre-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The first matrix and readout follow the dense update at this reconstructed
state. Differentiating the moment integrals supplies a triangular system
driven by its current features, backward responses and residuals. Thus
the moments evolve without storing a past trajectory or consulting the
dense reference. Initially only the zeroth forward moments are nonzero.

Truncating the histories is the first approximation. The same cancellation
as in the toy example makes the instantaneous defect a product of endpoint
projection errors. Temporal derivative bounds control those errors;
stability then controls their feedback through subsequent training.
The moving state uses $Cn(d+mq)$ real coordinates. The initialized mixers
remain stored, costing another $Cn^2$, and still enter every forward and
backward pass. Detailed moment equations and the all-time argument are in
the \hyperref[int:legendre-runtime]{Legendre construction} and
\hyperref[int:legendre-proofs]{its proofs}.

\subsection{Harmonic: preserve the geometry of a response family}
\label{sec:harmonic-method}

Harmonic exploits regularity in both physical time and input direction.
For a simple picture on the input circle, a response might have the form
$h(t,\theta)\approx u_0(t)+u_c(t)\cos\theta+u_s(t)\sin\theta$.
If these vector coefficients also vary smoothly in time, a short temporal
expansion describes the whole family. In the construction, Chebyshev time
polynomials and real spherical harmonics replace this illustrative
three-mode expansion. Analytic regularity controls their spectral tails.
Their finitely many coefficient vectors span a source space
$E_\ell\subset\mathbb R^n$ at each layer.

These spaces contain approximations to forward features, backward
responses, and the images needed when the initialized mixers act in either
direction. Applying identical scalar coefficient operations to a vector
and its initialized image preserves that pairing exactly. Exact initialized
training features, first-weight columns and constants are also included.
This records the directions needed by the dynamics even when the full
initialized matrix has no useful low-rank approximation.

Select neuron coordinates $I_\ell$, with $|I_\ell|=q_\ell\le q$, and
construct a positive metric $M_\ell$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{u^\top v}{n}=u(I_\ell)^\top M_\ell v(I_\ell),
 \qquad u,v\in E_\ell.
 \label{eq:methods-source-isometry}
\end{equation}
A few measured coordinates now reproduce every inner product inside the
source space. The metric contains the geometry lost by discarding the
other coordinates; it is generally not diagonal. Its comparison with a
positive diagonal metric also bounds coordinatewise nonlinear operations.
Spectral truncation and finite coefficient accuracy determine how well
$E_\ell$ captures the responses; the identity
\eqref{eq:methods-source-isometry} itself is exact.

Selecting actual coordinates matters for nonlinear networks. Restriction
commutes exactly with coordinatewise activation:
$\phi(z)(I_\ell)=\phi(z(I_\ell))$. A general linear projection does not:
for example, $\tanh((z_1+z_2)/2)$ generally differs from
$(\tanh z_1+\tanh z_2)/2$. The selected network therefore keeps the
original activations. Small matrices implement initialized forward and
reverse actions on the paired source spaces; the spectral tails and
stability estimates control the mismatch along its own evolving state.

There is a second essential ingredient: a corrected readout. The model
stores selected weights $W_H^{(1)},\ldots,W_H^{(L)}$, a raw readout
$w_H\in\mathbb R^{q_L}$, and label deficits $c_H\in\mathbb R^m$.
Its forward features $h_H^{(\ell)}$ use the recursion in
\eqref{eq:network} at the selected widths. Write
$\langle u,v\rangle_{M_L}=u^\top M_Lv$,
let $V$ have columns $h_{H,a}^{(L)}/\sqrt m$, and set $Q=V^\top M_LV$.
The effective readout is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat w_H&=w_H+VQ^{-1}
 \left(\frac{y-c_H}{\sqrt m}-V^\top M_Lw_H\right),\\
 f_{\rm Harm}(x)&=\langle\widehat w_H,h_H^{(L)}(x)\rangle_{M_L}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-corrected-readout}
\end{equation}
Multiplying by $V^\top M_L$ shows that
$f_{\rm Harm}(x_a)=y_a-c_{H,a}$ exactly. The correction lies in the
span of the training features and removes precisely the discrepancy
between their current predictions and the retained deficits. The feature
gap keeps the solve well defined.

Initially $w_H=0$, $c_H=y$, and the selected weights reproduce selected
coordinates of the dense initialized training features and their full
empirical Gram matrix. The optimizer evolves
the deficits using its response Gram and the weights using metric
rank-one directions, with $\widehat w_H$ supplying the top backward
signal. It is a specified autonomous optimizer: coordinatewise gates need
not be self-adjoint in the selected metric, so ordinary gradient flow on
an arbitrary smaller network would not give these equations. The readout
correction also cancels the leading coupling between readout and deficit
errors in the stability proof. Complete updates are in the
\hyperref[int:harmonic-runtime-definition]{Harmonic runtime definition}.

The source coefficients are computed during setup. Certified local
expansions advance numerical anchors over the required training horizon;
passive spatial nodes measure responses without contributing to the
gradients. One execution stores the dense initialization. The other
samples its Gaussian actions conditionally as forward and transpose
requests arrive, reproducing the same joint law without materializing the
hidden matrices. The latter constructs a fresh coupled reference; it
does not convert an arbitrary supplied array at the same cost.
Both initialize the final model at physical time zero and discard their
setup trajectories and source arrays. Runtime retains $Cq^2$ real
coordinates, including fixed metrics and solve caches. Its small size
does not pay for the preceding source construction; both setup costs are
given in \Cref{sec:costs} and the
\hyperref[int:harmonic-efficient-initialization]{initialization appendix}.

\subsection{Logarithmic: a short description of a finite computation}
\label{sec:logarithmic-method}

The Logarithmic decoder takes a different view of redundancy. A large
array need not be stored if its entries can be regenerated from a short
description. Here the description includes both random seeds and the
scalar interactions through which training changes the network. Storing
an initialization seed alone would not specify the learned state.

The starting point is a prescribed finite numerical training program,
independent of the target dense run. Each virtual neuron carries a
packet of random inputs from which its forward features, gates and
backward responses are computed. The rows communicate through empirical
scalar contractions. Their rounded values and the program's decisions
form a finite transcript. A proved finite-space generator replaces the
independent packets by a short seed while controlling the distribution of
this transcript. This is a statement about the specified computation;
it does not assume that an arbitrary pseudorandom matrix reproduces
Gaussian training.

Offline setup may inspect the completed source computation. It selects
packets and builds a positive metric that reproduces the empirical pairings
of its named fields. With controlled metric precision, copied scalar
random marks and prescribed rounding, compact training acquires exactly
the same scalar transcript on the certified event. The retained state
includes those packets, the metric, the seeds, training data and the
current scalar coefficients. Full row tables and future answers are
discarded. Future training answers are then acquired causally from this
state, without an unknown trajectory oracle.

An unseen input explains both the compression and its computational price.
Suppose the current hidden increment is represented by old fields
$u_b,v_b\in\mathbb R^n$ and scalar weights $c_b$ as
$D=n^{-1}\sum_b c_bu_bv_b^\top$. Its action on a new feature
$h\in\mathbb R^n$ is
\begin{equation}
 Dh=\sum_b c_bu_b
 \left(\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n v_b(i)h(i)\right).
 \label{eq:methods-streamed-contraction}
\end{equation}
The decoder regenerates the rows needed for each scalar sum, accumulates
it, and releases the row workspace. It evaluates the same finite empirical
interaction as its virtual source. A small training metric cannot simply
replace this sum for a new $h$ outside its recorded source family; nor
can a population expectation replace the empirical mean.

Regeneration also has to preserve the computation's history. An old field
is reevaluated with the scalar arguments from its creation time. Its rows
are reproduced without rerunning scalar training. Initialized matrix
actions use the conditional Gaussian law given both earlier forward and
transpose requests, including its covariance correction. Fresh query
fields remain local to that query and do not alter the training state.
A median over complete source models, together with finite input/time
coding and a fitted-tail estimate, gives the simultaneous query guarantee.

This model counts finite numerical words with a proved bit length. Its
inventory includes the seeds, selected packets, metrics, ensemble members
and live query workspace. At fixed admissible parameters that inventory
is $O(\log^6n\,\log\log n)$ words, but a new input still requires
streaming $n$ virtual rows. With the counted finite activation evaluator,
query work is near-linear in width and complete prescribed training uses
only polylogarithmic word work. Initialization also processes the large
virtual source. General activation evaluators require their own work and
scratch charges. Finite arithmetic and these qualifications are explicit in
\Cref{sec:costs}. The construction and its probability, replay and query
arguments are in the \hyperref[int:decoder-finite-construction]
{finite decoder construction} and
\hyperref[int:logarithmic-decoder-proofs]{decoder proofs}.
