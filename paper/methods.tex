\section{Compressing the responses that carry learning}
\label{sec:methods}

The common object behind the constructions is the history of an interaction.
A forward feature determines which incoming directions a training example
activates; a backward response determines how a change at that layer reaches
the output. The residual controls the strength and sign of their interaction.
The matrix records the accumulated interactions, but its individual entries
need not be the most economical state in which to describe them.

There are two reductions. Legendre compresses the temporal histories while
keeping their original neuron coordinates. The selected-coordinate
construction compresses the neuron spaces needed to realize the interactions.
Harmonic and Taylor use that same selected architecture and optimizer;
they build its source spaces for different query sets. Harmonic covers the
whole sphere through joint time and input regularity. Taylor covers a
finite panel declared before initialization, and uses the increasing temporal
regularity of a fitting trajectory. All three models then evolve from time
zero without consulting their coupled dense reference.

\subsection{The exact memory written into a weight matrix}
\label{sec:response-identity}

For $2\le\ell\le L$, integration of \Cref{eq:dense-flow} gives
\begin{equation}
 W^{(\ell)}(t)-W_0^{(\ell)}=-\frac{2}{mn}\sum_{a=1}^m
 \int_0^t r_a(s)\delta_a^{(\ell)}(s)
 h_a^{(\ell-1)}(s)^\top\,\mathrm ds.
 \label{eq:methods-history}
\end{equation}
Each instantaneous update has rank at most $m$. Its integral can have much
higher rank because both vectors turn during training. Compressibility must
therefore come from control of their entire motion, rather than the rank of
one update.

Write $\langle u,v\rangle_n=u^\top v/n$ for the empirical neuron pairing.
Suppressing the layer superscripts in one hidden link, the two actions of
\eqref{eq:methods-history} are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 W(t)u&=W_0u-\frac2m\sum_a\int_0^t
 r_a(s)\delta_a(s)\langle h_a(s),u\rangle_n\,\mathrm ds,\\
 W(t)^\top v&=W_0^\top v-\frac2m\sum_a\int_0^t
 r_a(s)h_a(s)\langle\delta_a(s),v\rangle_n\,\mathrm ds.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-paired-actions}
\end{equation}
Forward propagation and backward propagation exchange the roles of the two
histories. This identifies what a compression must preserve: their pairings,
the initialized actions in both directions, and the nonlinear operations
that create the next features and responses. Matching the final output alone
does not supply these objects.

The initialized mixer can remain full rank. A smaller model needs its action
on the relevant source vectors, not an accurate approximation to its action
on every vector in $\mathbb R^n$. Conversely, replacing a historical pairing
by a current-current pairing changes the evolution. The constructions below
retain the historical dependence through explicit state or through source
spaces whose selected realization supports autonomous learning.

\subsection{Legendre: evolving moments of the two histories}
\label{sec:legendre-method}

Hats denote the Legendre model's own reconstructed network. Its activity
clock satisfies $\dot\tau=\widehat\rho$, $\tau(0)=1$, where
$\widehat\rho=\|\widehat r\|_2/\sqrt m$. Under the fitting estimates,
$\int_0^\infty\widehat\rho(t)\,\mathrm dt$ is finite. Thus the entire
physical trajectory occupies a finite interval of clock time. The clock
slows when little error remains to drive the weights.

At one hidden link, write $h_a(\xi),b_a(\xi)\in\mathbb R^n$ for the
histories defined, while the clock advances, by
$h_a(\tau(t))=\widehat h_a(t)$ and
$b_a(\tau(t))=\widehat r_a(t)\widehat\delta_a(t)/\widehat\rho(t)$.
On the initial prefix $0\le\xi\le1$, set $h_a(\xi)=h_{0,a}$ and
$b_a(\xi)=0$, where $h_{0,a}$ is the initialized forward feature.
This prefix gives a nonsingular starting interval and preserves
the realized initialization. The quotient defines a history for the proof;
the autonomous equations below contain no division by a residual.

Let $p_j^\tau(\xi)=P_j(2\xi/\tau-1)$, where $P_j$ is the Legendre
polynomial with $P_j(1)=1$. These polynomials have squared norm
$\tau/(2j+1)$ in Lebesgue $L^2([0,\tau])$. Let $\Pi_q^\tau$ denote
orthogonal projection onto polynomials of degree below $q$, applied to each
neuron coordinate. The stored moments at each link are
\[
 \bar h_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)}=\int_0^\tau p_j^\tau(\xi)
 h_a^{(\ell-1)}(\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi,\qquad
 \bar\delta_{a,j}^{(\ell)}=\int_0^\tau p_j^\tau(\xi)
 b_a^{(\ell)}(\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi,\qquad 0\le j<q.
\]
They are unnormalized history integrals. Each is still a vector in
$\mathbb R^n$: the projection removes temporal modes, not neurons.
Orthogonality gives the reconstructed mixers
\begin{equation}
 \widehat W^{(\ell)}=W_0^{(\ell)}-
 \frac{2}{mn\tau}\sum_{a=1}^m\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(2j+1)
 \bar\delta_{a,j}^{(\ell)}\bar h_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)\top}.
 \label{eq:methods-legendre-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
Its correction is exactly the integral of
$(\Pi_q^\tau b_a)(\Pi_q^\tau h_a)^\top$ with the factor $-2/(mn)$.

The current forward pass and backward responses are evaluated using these
matrices. The first matrix and readout follow \Cref{eq:dense-flow} at that
reconstructed state. Differentiating the moving-interval moment integrals
gives the remaining autonomous equations:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot{\bar h}_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)}
 &=\widehat\rho\widehat h_a^{(\ell-1)}
 -\frac{\widehat\rho}{\tau}
 \left(j\bar h_{a,j}^{(\ell-1)}
       +\sum_{i<j}(2i+1)\bar h_{a,i}^{(\ell-1)}\right),\\
 \dot{\bar\delta}_{a,j}^{(\ell)}
 &=\widehat r_a\widehat\delta_a^{(\ell)}
 -\frac{\widehat\rho}{\tau}
 \left(j\bar\delta_{a,j}^{(\ell)}
       +\sum_{i<j}(2i+1)\bar\delta_{a,i}^{(\ell)}\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-legendre-moments}
\end{equation}
Initially $\bar h_{a,0}^{(\ell-1)}=h_{0,a}^{(\ell-1)}$ and every other
moment is zero; the stored first matrix and readout share the dense initial
values. The triangular sums account for rescaling the basis as the
clock interval grows. At zero residual every velocity vanishes, including
the clock velocity. No past trajectory is stored or replayed.

The strength of this reconstruction comes from projecting both histories
with the same orthogonal projector. At a fixed clock endpoint, suppressing
its superscript on $\Pi_q$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\tau bh^\top\,\mathrm d\xi
 -\int_0^\tau(\Pi_qb)(\Pi_qh)^\top\,\mathrm d\xi
 =\int_0^\tau[(I-\Pi_q)b][(I-\Pi_q)h]^\top\,\mathrm d\xi.
 \label{eq:methods-legendre-tail-pairing}
\end{equation}
The retained--discarded cross terms vanish coordinate by coordinate.
For example, the average of $(v_0+\xi v_1)(u_0+\xi u_1)^\top$ on
$[-1,1]$ is $v_0u_0^\top+v_1u_1^\top/3$. Dropping the linear modes
loses their mutual interaction, with no cross term involving a retained
constant mode. The same cancellation holds for arbitrary histories.

There is also an exact dynamical form. If
$e_g=g(\tau)-(\Pi_q^\tau g)(\tau)$ denotes an endpoint projection
error, differentiation of the reconstructed mixer gives
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\widehat W}^{(\ell)}
 =-\frac2{mn}\sum_a\widehat r_a\widehat\delta_a^{(\ell)}
       \widehat h_a^{(\ell-1)\top}+\mathcal E_\ell,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_\ell=\frac{2\widehat\rho}{mn}
       \sum_a e_{b,a}^{(\ell)}e_{h,a}^{(\ell-1)\top}.
 \label{eq:methods-legendre-defect}
\end{equation}
This product of two tails is responsible for the essentially quadratic
decay in order. It does not follow from a pointwise low-rank approximation
of the weights. The moment histories are endogenous: changing the moments
changes the network that writes their next values. The stability argument
below controls that feedback.

The moving state has exactly $n(d+1)+1+2(L-1)mnq$ real coordinates.
The initialized hidden mixers add $(L-1)n^2$ fixed coordinates and still
act during forward and backward propagation. Legendre therefore compresses
the learned history while preserving an original-width nonlinear network.
The leading error is $q^{-2}$ times a coefficient that is subpolynomial
in $n$ at fixed parameters. Comparing with the intrinsic variability scale
$n^{-1/2}$, with its subpolynomial and logarithmic factors, leads to
$q=n^{1/4+o(1)}$. The theorem chooses the overhead to obtain negligible
relative error; substitution into the $mnq$ moment count gives
$n^{5/4+o(1)}$ moving coordinates at fixed $m,d,L$.

\subsection{A selected realization of the response spaces}
\label{sec:selected-response-model}

The second reduction replaces each neuron space by a small set of actual
neuron coordinates and a metric. Its input is a fixed finite-dimensional
source space $E_\ell\subset\mathbb R^n$ for each layer. The spaces
approximate the dense forward features and the initialized images needed
for forward propagation, together with backward responses and initialized
transpose images needed for training. Their construction depends on the
promised query set. Exact initial training features and their forward
images, the constant vector, and the first-weight columns are added.

An initialized image must be paired with its preimage. If a computed
coefficient is $u$, its image coefficient is formed as $W_0u$, or as
$W_0^\top u$ in the reverse direction. Identical scalar approximation
operations commute with these fixed matrices. Consequently the computed
source spaces preserve the image relation exactly, while each member has
its own error bound relative to the corresponding dense source. This is
what lets one small mixer serve both directions of
\eqref{eq:methods-paired-actions}.

At layer $\ell$, selection chooses an index set $I_\ell$ of size
$q_\ell\le9\dim E_\ell$ and a positive metric $M_\ell$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \langle u,v\rangle_n
 =u_{I_\ell}^\top M_\ell v_{I_\ell},
 \qquad u,v\in E_\ell.
 \label{eq:methods-source-isometry}
\end{equation}
The metric is generally a full matrix. A positive diagonal comparison
metric $D_\ell$ satisfies
$D_\ell/4\preceq M_\ell\preceq D_\ell$ and
$\mathbf1^\top D_\ell\mathbf1\le4$.
Exact isometry on $E_\ell$ preserves pairings, while the diagonal
comparison bounds coordinatewise gates and converts coordinate approximation
errors into metric errors. Neither property requires a lower bound on the
smallest selection weight.

Coordinate selection also preserves the original nonlinear operation:
$\phi(z)_{I_\ell}=\phi(z_{I_\ell})$. An arbitrary change of basis has
no such identity. For example, averaging two coordinates before applying
$\tanh$ generally differs from averaging their activations. The selected
model can therefore use the same coordinatewise activations as the dense
network. Its activations need not remain in $E_\ell$; the metric controls
their operations on the entire selected coordinate space, and trajectory
comparison controls their deviation from the approximated dense sources.

Here is the common autonomous model. The subscript $C$ denotes either
selected construction. Its moving state is
$(A_C,B_C^{(2)},\ldots,B_C^{(L)},w_C,c_C)$, where
$A_C\in\mathbb R^{q_1\times d}$,
$B_C^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb R^{q_\ell\times q_{\ell-1}}$,
$w_C\in\mathbb R^{q_L}$, and $c_C\in\mathbb R^m$.
For $v=x/\sqrt d$, compute
$z_C^{(1)}=A_Cv$, $z_C^{(\ell)}=B_C^{(\ell)}h_C^{(\ell-1)}$, and
$h_C^{(\ell)}=\phi_\ell(z_C^{(\ell)})$.
Write $\langle u,v\rangle_{M_\ell}=u^\top M_\ell v$.
Let $V_C$ have columns $h_{C,a}^{(L)}/\sqrt m$, and define
$V_C^*=V_C^\top M_L$ and $Q_C=V_C^*V_C$.
The readout used for predictions and backward propagation is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat w_C&=w_C+V_CQ_C^{-1}
 \left(\frac{y-c_C}{\sqrt m}-V_C^*w_C\right),\\
 f_C(x)&=\langle\widehat w_C,h_C^{(L)}(x)\rangle_{M_L}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-corrected-readout}
\end{equation}
The correction is the vector in the training-feature span that enforces
$f_C(x_a)=y_a-c_{C,a}$ exactly. Thus $c_C$ is the actual label deficit,
with the opposite sign to the residual $r=f-y$. The raw readout retains
its component orthogonal to the current training-feature span. This matters
for passive predictions: fitting the training values does not determine
the full readout. The feature gap keeps $Q_C^{-1}$ well defined.

For a map $B_C^{(\ell+1)}$ between consecutive selected spaces, its metric
adjoint is
$(B_C^{(\ell+1)})^*=M_\ell^{-1}B_C^{(\ell+1)\top}M_{\ell+1}$.
Set $k_{C,a}^{(L)}=\widehat w_C$,
$k_{C,a}^{(\ell)}=(B_C^{(\ell+1)})^*\delta_{C,a}^{(\ell+1)}$, and
$\delta_{C,a}^{(\ell)}=\phi_\ell'(z_{C,a}^{(\ell)})\odot k_{C,a}^{(\ell)}$.
Define the unnormalized sample Gram
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 K_{C,ab}={}&\langle h_{C,a}^{(L)},h_{C,b}^{(L)}\rangle_{M_L}
 +\langle\delta_{C,a}^{(1)},\delta_{C,b}^{(1)}\rangle_{M_1}v_a^\top v_b\\
 &+\sum_{\ell=2}^L
 \langle\delta_{C,a}^{(\ell)},\delta_{C,b}^{(\ell)}\rangle_{M_\ell}
 \langle h_{C,a}^{(\ell-1)},h_{C,b}^{(\ell-1)}\rangle_{M_{\ell-1}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-selected-gram}
\end{equation}
The updates are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dot A_C&=\frac2m\sum_a c_{C,a}\delta_{C,a}^{(1)}v_a^\top,&
 \dot B_C^{(\ell)}&=\frac2m\sum_a c_{C,a}\delta_{C,a}^{(\ell)}
                h_{C,a}^{(\ell-1)\top}M_{\ell-1},\\
 \dot w_C&=\frac2m\sum_a c_{C,a}h_{C,a}^{(L)},&
 \dot c_C&=-\frac2mK_Cc_C.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-selected-flow}
\end{equation}
Only training samples enter these sums. These are specified update
directions. A coordinate gate need not be self-adjoint in $M_\ell$, so
they are not asserted to be the gradients of the corrected predictor.

Initialization is also explicit. Choose a basis matrix $U_\ell$ of
$E_\ell$ with $U_\ell^\top U_\ell/n=I$, and put
$P_\ell=(U_\ell)_{I_\ell}$ and $A_0=W^{(1)}(0)$.
Set $A_C(0)=(A_0)_{I_1}$,
$w_C(0)=0$, $c_C(0)=y$, and
\begin{equation}
 B_C^{(\ell)}(0)=P_\ell
 \frac{U_\ell^\top W_0^{(\ell)}U_{\ell-1}}n
 P_{\ell-1}^\top M_{\ell-1}.
 \label{eq:methods-selected-initialization}
\end{equation}
Since $P_\ell^\top M_\ell P_\ell=I$, this mixer reproduces the
initialized action on every paired source vector. Its metric adjoint
reproduces the corresponding transpose action. The exact additions to
the spaces make the initial training features equal the corresponding dense
coordinates, and their full metric Gram equals the dense empirical Gram.
All width-$n$ bases, source vectors
and dense arrays are then discarded.

This is not equivalent to drawing a fresh small Gaussian network.
The selected coordinates and metric preserve interactions from the
realized width-$n$ reference, including its training feature Gram. A fresh
small network resamples those interactions instead. Nor is the reduction
a low-rank approximation to every initialized mixer: it preserves the
paired actions needed by the response space and allows the selected
features to keep changing nonlinearly.
As a full-retention check, taking $E_\ell=\mathbb R^n$, every coordinate,
and $M_\ell=I/n$ recovers \eqref{eq:dense-flow}: the deficit
then equals $y-f_n$, the readout correction vanishes, and the factor $1/n$
in each hidden update comes from its metric.

For $q=\max_\ell q_\ell$, the moving coordinates number
$dq_1+\sum_{\ell=2}^Lq_\ell q_{\ell-1}+q_L+m$.
Here $q$ is a neuron budget, distinct from the Legendre polynomial order.
The fixed metrics and their inverses add $O(Lq^2)$ coordinates. The
effective readout is computed from the state, not a second independent
learned vector. This quadratic dependence on the selected width explains
why a gain in source dimension is squared in the retained storage.

\subsection{Harmonic: one source for the whole input sphere}
\label{sec:harmonic-method}

Harmonic constructs $E_\ell$ using the four vector families
$h_n^{(\ell)}(t,v)$,
$W_0^{(\ell)}h_n^{(\ell-1)}(t,v)$,
$\delta_n^{(\ell)}(t,v)$, and
$W_0^{(\ell+1)\top}\delta_n^{(\ell+1)}(t,v)$,
omitting nonexistent boundary-layer images. Here
$(t,v)\in[0,T]\times S^{d-1}$, including passive inputs.
For a passive input the backward field is evaluated through the trained
network but supplies no update force. The source spaces are the real spans
of finitely computed coefficient vectors for these families, together with
the exact initialized additions described above.

The expansion separates two kinds of regularity. With
$t=T(1+\cos u)/2$, temporal cosines are Chebyshev polynomials in physical
time. Real spherical harmonics describe input direction. On a circle, the
spatial expansion is an ordinary sine and cosine series; in higher
dimension it is organized by harmonic degree. Holomorphy in a complex
time neighborhood and a complex neighborhood of the sphere makes both
coefficient tails decay exponentially. A weighted cutoff in temporal and
spatial degree retains only the coefficient vectors needed at the chosen
source accuracy.

Bounded activation values are unnecessary here. If $s$ is a common bound
on $|\phi_\ell'|$ in the safe strip, integration from zero gives
$|\phi_\ell(z)|\le|\phi_\ell(0)|+s|z|$ on the safe strip, so operator
and feature RMS bounds propagate through the depth even when individual
activations are large. The normalized response energy controls the bulk
motion. Coordinate maxima still enter when the source argument keeps every
complex preactivation inside its analytic strip; the relevant maxima have
order $\sqrt{\log(en)}$ at fixed parameters. This distinction explains why
the available complex radii shrink with width although the RMS state remains
bounded. Bounds on derivatives control the gates; they do not impose a
bounded-activation hypothesis.

This explains the dimension-dependent logarithmic exponent. At fixed
problem parameters and inverse-polynomial source accuracy, the sufficient
horizon is of order $\log(en)$. The certified time and input radii are of
order $1/\sqrt{\log(en)}$. The temporal degree is therefore
$O(\log(en)^{5/2})$, while spherical degrees up to
$O(\log(en)^{3/2})$ contribute
$O(\log(en)^{3(d-1)/2})$ basis functions. The weighted cutoff refines
this product count, with the same resulting source-dimension exponent:
\[
 \dim E_\ell=O(\log(en)^{3d/2+1}),\qquad
 \text{retained state}=O(\log(en)^{3d+2}).
\]
These counts fix data, labels, depth, activation and confidence. The full
parameter dependence, including the learned-storage factor
$(Ym/\gamma)^4$, is retained in the theorem and appendix. In dimension
one the sphere consists of two points and only temporal expansions are
needed.

The source spaces are computed during initialization, before the compact
model begins its own training. The efficient explicit initializer advances
certified local expansions using stored dense matrices. The implicit
initializer generates their Gaussian forward and transpose actions
conditionally on earlier requests, preserving the joint reference law.
It constructs a fresh coupled reference without materializing every hidden
matrix. Both assemble the same kind of selected model at physical time
zero. Their preprocessing costs are separate from the retained state and
are stated in \Cref{sec:costs}. The time and spherical coefficients are
setup objects; inference applies the selected nonlinear network directly.

\subsection{Taylor: a declared input panel and expanding time disks}
\label{sec:taylor-method}
\label{sec:panel-method}

Suppose the requested queries are the $m$ training inputs and $p$ passive
inputs fixed before initialization. A finite panel permits a different
source builder. Retain the forward feature curves and their initialized
images at all $m+p$ inputs, and the backward response curves and their
transpose images only at the $m$ training inputs. There are at most
$2(2m+p)$ vector curves per layer. No spatial harmonic expansion is
needed, because the guarantee is a maximum over these declared inputs.
The selected network remains evaluable elsewhere, but the panel certificate
does not bound its error there.

Removing spatial approximation alone would leave the temporal cost of a
uniform narrow analytic strip. The additional gain comes from fitting.
As the real residual decays, a complex time excursion starts with less
activity driving its parameter motion. The finite-query source argument
uses this fact to certify larger analytic disks at later real times.
The enlarged domain is proved together with the required source bounds;
small real-time derivatives alone would not imply it.

The useful model is a damped mode $e^{-\kappa t}$, with $\kappa>0$:
an imaginary time increment rotates its phase without changing its
magnitude. In the neural proof, freezing the real Gram matrix gives the
same unitary evolution along imaginary time. Growth comes from variation
of that Gram, not from exponentiating its entire norm as though it were
unstable. That variation is paid for by the remaining residual activity.
Later real anchors start with exponentially less residual, so they permit
larger complex excursions before consuming the same activity budget.
The certified radius first grows approximately exponentially and then
linearly. Controlling the corresponding random responses on this enlarged
domain is the difficult analytic step, not simply changing a plotting clock.
In particular, flattening in real time does not by itself justify analyticity
at the endpoint of an arbitrary compactified clock.

Use the source horizon $T_0=32(m/\gamma)\log(en)$ of the panel certificate.
Let $\varrho(t)>0$ be the certified disk radius at a real time
$t\in[0,T_0]$. Cover this interval by predetermined temporal pieces,
each using an anchor $t_b$ with
$|t-t_b|\le\varrho(t_b)/2$. For each required vector curve $g$, use
the degree-$K$ Taylor polynomial
\[
 g(t)\approx\sum_{j=0}^K
 \frac{\varrho(t_b)^j g^{(j)}(t_b)}{j!}
 \left(\frac{t-t_b}{\varrho(t_b)}\right)^j
 \quad\text{on piece }b.
\]
The vector coefficients from every piece enter one fixed space
$E_\ell$. No switch between temporal pieces occurs in the selected
runtime. Each local polynomial can equally be written in Chebyshev form:
the degree-preserving basis change is invertible, so it leaves the vector
coefficient span and its paired initialized images unchanged. The saving
comes from the larger analytic domain, not from renaming the polynomial basis.
If $J$ is their count and $B=2m+d+1$ bounds the exact initialized
additions, their source-dimension bound is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim E_\ell&\le B+2(2m+p)J(K+1),\\
 J&=O\!\left(\sqrt{\log(en)}+\log\log(e^e n)\right),\qquad
 K+1=O(\log(en)).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:methods-panel-source-count}
\end{equation}
The orders here fix the problem parameters and use the source accuracy
chosen for the theorem's trajectory target. The covering count follows
from an integral of $1/\varrho(t)$: early narrow disks require many short
pieces, whereas the later widening disks cover the fitting tail with
progressively longer pieces. The geometric Taylor tail on each half-radius
piece explains why its degree is only logarithmic.

Squaring this source-dimension bound gives the cubic logarithm in retained
storage. The coefficient of the square-root-logarithm term in $J$ carries
$(Ym/\gamma)^2$; its square retains the fourth label/gap power. The
logarithmic-in-$\log n$ contribution has a separate gap-dependent
coefficient, kept in the full bound. Thus the improvement removes the
spatial degree count and reduces the temporal covering cost. It trades
whole-sphere coverage for a panel specified before initialization; the
finite-query flaring theorem does not itself enlarge Harmonic's joint
complex time/input domain.

There is an optional exact simplification of the input dimension. Let
the columns of $U\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}$ be an orthonormal basis of
the span of all panel inputs, with $k\le\min(d,m+p)$. Use inputs
$U^\top v_i$ and first weights $A_C\in\mathbb R^{q_1\times k}$.
All dense first-layer updates lie in this span, so the coupled dense
network with first matrix $A_0U$ has exactly the same panel trajectory.
The Gram and mobilities are unchanged. Retaining $U$ costs $dk$ scalars;
it replaces $d$ by $k$ in the source overhead and leaves ambient input
storage linear in $d$.

Finally, the finite coefficients can be produced from initial derivatives
and certified continuation, using the same scalar operations for each
base/image pair. Their finite availability is not a cheap-setup or
finite-precision guarantee. All coefficients, anchors, original-width
arrays and continuation state are discarded after selection. The retained
model is precisely \eqref{eq:methods-corrected-readout}--\eqref{eq:methods-selected-flow},
initialized by \eqref{eq:methods-selected-initialization}; it counts real
coordinates. The complete flaring domain, coefficient construction,
parameter dependence and panel norm are given in
\hyperref[sec:panel-proof]{the panel-compression appendix}.

\subsection{Why the approximation survives continued learning}
\label{sec:compression-stability}

The global argument has two tasks: show that each approximate model keeps
fitting, and show that it stays close to its dense reference. Accuracy
does not follow merely from small spectral tails, because future source
vectors depend on the approximate state. The small-label assumptions keep
the feature Gram away from singularity while allowing hidden learning.
The surviving gap damps the residual, making total residual activity finite.
The stability proofs retain this sign information before estimating the
nonlinear remainder.

For the selected models, every term of $K_C$ is a positive semidefinite Gram matrix,
including tensor-product Grams for hidden-layer updates. With
$\rho_C=\|c_C\|_2/\sqrt m$, the optimizer gives the exact identity
\[
 -\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\rho_C^2
 =\frac4{m^2}c_C^\top K_Cc_C,\qquad K_C/m\succeq Q_C.
\]
The same quadratic expression is the squared speed of the raw parameters
in their mobility-compatible metric. Consequently the preserved feature
gap controls both residual decay and total parameter path length. This
energy identity is algebraic; it does not require the optimizer to be
gradient flow of the corrected predictor. The resulting small feature
motion closes the gap estimate and keeps the readout solve nonsingular.

Source isometry then transfers approximation error to the interactions.
If source vectors $u,v$ have coordinate errors at most $\eta$ and dense
RMS bounds $A_u,A_v$, inserting their approximants into both pairings gives
\[
 |\langle u_I,v_I\rangle_M-\langle u,v\rangle_n|
 \le3\eta(A_u+A_v)+9\eta^2.
\]
The initialized paired actions have comparably small defects. Equation
\eqref{eq:methods-paired-actions} integrates the learned action defects
against training residuals, so their total forcing is governed by
accumulated activity. No time derivative of a source approximation error
is needed. This is why the same comparison accepts a global harmonic
expansion or the temporal pieces of the panel construction.

One further cancellation is essential for the selected model. Let
$c_n=y-f_n$ on the training inputs and $w_R=(w_n)_{I_L}$ be the
restricted dense readout. Define
$e=(c_C-c_n)/\sqrt m$ and $T_C=V_CQ_C^{-1}$.
The raw-readout difference has a term $+2V_Ce$ in its velocity. The
deficit difference has the feature-Gram damping term $-2Q_Ce$.
Lifting the latter into feature space and adding the two errors gives
\begin{equation}
 \zeta=w_C-w_R+T_Ce,
 \qquad 2V_Ce-2T_CQ_Ce=0.
 \label{eq:methods-readout-cancellation}
\end{equation}
This uses $T_CQ_C=V_C$, not an inverse on the entire feature space.
The remaining terms involve changing current features, small hidden
response contributions, and the source defects. They are controlled by
the residual activity and readout path length. Only the actual selected
feature map is differentiated. A signed estimate for the lifted deficit
retains the damping needed to absorb the hidden contributions under the
stated label allowance. The correction thus serves both exact training
consistency and stable comparison with the dense readout.
Preserving this cancellation permits the stated explicit gap dependence;
estimating both leading coupling terms separately would discard that
structure and repeatedly pay for the inverse feature gap.

Legendre has a different error mechanism. Its reconstruction is the dense
gradient field plus $\mathcal E$ from
\eqref{eq:methods-legendre-defect}. First, growing-interval projection
energy gives an all-order fitting estimate. For a history $g$,
\[
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dA}
 \int_0^A\|g-\Pi_q^Ag\|^2\,\mathrm d\xi
 =\|g(A)-(\Pi_q^Ag)(A)\|^2.
\]
Together with the defect's product structure, this controls its effect on
the residual and preserves a damped fitting flow. It also turns the
integral of the absolute matrix defect into a product of two history-tail
norms. The forward tail is of order $q^{-1}$. For the backward tail the
proof compares with the dense response expressed in the closure clock,
uses residual decay near the clock endpoint, and absorbs the response
difference created by the closure itself. This yields the
$q^{-2}\sqrt{\log(eq)}$ comparison rate, rather than assuming an
independently regular prescribed backward signal.

For the final comparison, put the parameters in physical coordinates:
\[
 \theta=(W^{(1)}/\sqrt n,W^{(2)},\ldots,W^{(L)},w/\sqrt n).
\]
Subtracting the dense and reconstructed gradient fields produces a
negative squared residual-difference term in
$\tfrac12\mathrm d\|\widehat\theta-\theta_n\|^2/\mathrm dt$.
Once its nonpositive sign is identified, that term is dropped for the
upper bound. The Taylor remainder and Jacobian variation carry residual
factors, leaving growth proportional to $\rho_n+3\widehat\rho$, plus
the forcing $\mathcal E$. The residual-weighted growth coefficient has
finite integral even as physical time tends to
infinity. This signed parameter-energy argument is distinct from the
selected model's readout-deficit cancellation. Both use the training
flow's damping to control feedback, but they close different error variables.

The resulting estimates give finite amplification, not uniform contraction.
At fixed problem parameters the comparison coefficient can still grow as
$\exp(C\sqrt{\log(en)})=n^{o(1)}$. The source tolerance is chosen tightly
enough to absorb this factor. Its contribution to $\log(1/\eta)$ is only
of order $\sqrt{\log(en)}$, so it does not change either selected
construction's leading logarithmic storage exponent.

Finally, finite source construction is compatible with all-time fidelity.
For either selected model, comparison through a horizon $T$ bounds the
error by a source tolerance times a stability coefficient. Beyond $T$,
each model is compared to its own state at $T$ using its convergent fitting
tail. Both continue to evolve. The horizon is chosen so that this tail
fits the requested error budget, and the same argument includes their
limiting predictions. Legendre's projection comparison is uniform in the
physical horizon and also passes to its fitted limit. These mechanisms
control amplification by accumulated learning activity, while their width
dependence and source preparation costs remain explicit in the guarantees.
