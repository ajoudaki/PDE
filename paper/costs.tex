\section{Initialization, training and inference}
\label{sec:costs}

A retained-state bound is not an algorithmic cost bound.
Initialization includes every one-off operation needed to build the model;
its memory is the total peak. A training stage means one full-batch
vector-field evaluation, or a fixed-stage explicit update with its stage
multiplier. A query is one new input at an inference-ready state; its
workspace excludes the loaded model. The finite-word decoder uses a
different contract, stated separately below.

Training data are included in peak memory.

Legendre applies correction factors without materializing learned dense
matrices, and uses cumulative prefixes for the triangular moment updates.
Harmonic includes its feature-Gram solve and effective-readout refresh in
training. Those computations are not hidden in the single-query count.
The table assumes unit-cost scalar activation and Gaussian primitives;
the appendix separates arithmetic from actual primitive calls and scratch.
It does not bound the number or accuracy of subsequent numerical steps.

For example, substitute the variability-negligible choices in
\Cref{cor:compression}. Harmonic's query work and total retained state
then share the bound
\[
 C(m+d)^2+
 \left(\frac Cd\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\frac m\gamma\right)^4[\log(en)]^{3d+2},
\]
and its training-stage work is at most $Cm$ times this expression.
Legendre's query and training-stage bounds become, respectively,
\[
 Cn^2+Cm(1+m/\gamma)^3n^{5/4+o(1)},\qquad
 Cmn^2+Cm^2(1+m/\gamma)^3n^{5/4+o(1)}.
\]
These displays keep the structural factors. Merely knowing that $n$ is
larger than $m$ does not justify discarding the second term when the
other parameters also grow.

The implicit execution samples a fresh coupled dense reference. It cannot
read and convert an arbitrary supplied $n$-by-$n$ array in subquadratic
time. The explicit execution traverses the required physical horizon
with high-order local expansions; it is not claimed that a few early Euler
steps determine the whole trajectory. The bounds do not establish a
practical timing advantage at widths of a few thousand.

\subsection{The finite-word decoder}

Here initialization and query counts are word operations, not unit-cost
real arithmetic; each word has the sufficient bit length from
\Cref{thm:logarithmic}. Using that theorem's explicit resource logarithm
$Z$, sufficient full structural work bounds are
\begin{align}
 \text{initialization:}\quad&
 Cn(d+1)(m+d+2)^5(1+m/\gamma)^5
 [\log(em)]^5Z^{29/2},\nonumber\\
 \text{complete training:}\quad&
 C(d+1)(m+d+2)^4(1+m/\gamma)^4[\log(em)]^4Z^{12}\nonumber\\
 &\quad{}\times
 \log\!\bigl(e+C(m+d+2)(1+m/\gamma)\log(em)Z^{5/2}\bigr),\nonumber\\
 \text{one query:}\quad&
 C(d+1)(m+d+2)^4(1+m/\gamma)^4
 [\log(em)]^4\bigl(nZ^{12}+Z^{14}\bigr).
 \label{eq:decoder-work}
\end{align}
Training and live query memory are bounded by the complete retained-word
inventory in \eqref{eq:decoder-words}. Peak setup memory is that inventory
plus
\[
 Cn(m+d+2)(1+m/\gamma)\log(em)Z^{5/2}
 +C(m+d+2)^3(1+m/\gamma)^3[\log(em)]^3Z^{15/2}.
\]
These are total query-memory bounds, unlike the additional-workspace
column in \Cref{tab:runtime}. The finite tanh evaluator is included;
for a general analytic activation, add its supplied evaluator's actual
word work and scratch, as well as external data/query access and output.
The counted data are the finite training table; additionally retained
external descriptions require their own storage.
Analytic regularity alone does not make an arbitrary function a unit-cost
computable primitive.

At fixed parameters the decoder's word-operation rates are
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
\text{setup:}&O(n[\log(en)]^{29/2}),\\
\text{complete training:}&O([\log(en)]^{12}\log\log(e^e+n)),\\
\text{one unseen query:}&O(n[\log(en)]^{12}+[\log(en)]^{14}).
\end{array}
\]
The training row covers its \emph{complete prescribed} schedule.
Its logarithmic retained state does not remove the width factor in query work.
