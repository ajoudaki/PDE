\subsection{One theorem, with the query domain made explicit}
\label{sec:alltime}

The theorem prescribes the compressed sizes; internal orders have already
been chosen. Its benchmark is the actual dense-law quantile in
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-benchmark}, not a potentially loose upper bound for it.

\begin{theorem}[Compression below dense-run variability]
\label{thm:compression}
Fix the admissible data, activations and depth of \Cref{sec:setting}, with
$m\ge2$, $Y>0$ and $m/\gamma\ge1$. For every sufficiently large individual
width $n$, each construction below satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f_{\mathrm{model}}-f_n}_{\mathcal X}
 \le 3b_n(\mathcal X)
 \qquad\text{with probability at least }99\%.
 \label{eq:common-compression}
\end{equation}
Here $f_n$ is its coupled ordinary dense reference, and $\mathcal X$ is
the query domain specified below. The comparison holds simultaneously
over that domain, all physical training times and the fitted endpoint.

The following are sufficient real-coordinate bounds. Legendre and Harmonic
cover the whole sphere; their learned and total states, respectively, satisfy
the bounds below. Here $C$ depends only on activations and fixed depth.
\begin{align}
 \text{\rm Legendre:}\quad
 &Cm\left(\frac m\gamma\right)^3n^{5/4+o(1)},
 \label{eq:headline-legendre}\\
 \text{\rm Harmonic:}\quad
 &C(m+d)^2+
 \left(\frac Cd\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\frac{Ym}{\gamma}\right)^4[\log(en)]^{3d+2},
 \label{eq:headline-harmonic}
\end{align}
Legendre additionally retains $(L-1)n^2$ fixed mixer coordinates.
For Logarithmic, $\mathcal X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{m+p}\}$, and the total is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 C(m+p)^2\bigg[
 &\left(\frac{Ym}{\gamma}\right)^4[\log(en)]^3\\
 &+\left(\frac m\gamma\right)^2[\log(en)]^2
                  [\log(e+\log(en))]^2\bigg]+C(m+p)d.
\end{split}
\label{eq:headline-panel}
\end{equation}
Harmonic and Logarithmic include the data, selected mixers, metrics,
initial copies and working arrays. The fixed activation evaluator's
description and scratch, when not treated as primitives, are charged
separately for each method.
These are real-coordinate, not bit-complexity, bounds.
The $n^{o(1)}$ in \eqref{eq:headline-legendre} can be taken as
$[\log(en)]^2\exp(\sqrt{\log(en)}/2)$, up to $C$.

In fact, each construction can achieve error negligible in probability
relative to an independent dense pair's discrepancy on its own query
domain. Harmonic and Logarithmic can both attain absolute error $Y/n$
with the displayed storage orders. No dense training trajectory or later
trained weight is an input to their theoretical initialization procedure.
\end{theorem}

Dense itself retains $(L-1)n^2+n(d+1)$ weight coordinates.
Legendre reduces the changing part of this state, not its fixed dense
environment. The two selected constructions remove that environment from
the deployed model as well. Ordinary training-data storage is additional
for Dense and Legendre. The bounds are constructive sufficient sizes,
not claims of storage optimality. In particular, Legendre's proved width
exponent is $5/4$, not $1$.

\paragraph{The qualification is shared, not hidden.}
Sufficiently large means a finite threshold for each fixed admissible
problem and confidence. It covers the source event, stability and tail
bounds, internal-order overheads, and dense lower-bound onset; no
polynomial bound on that threshold is proved. The theorem does not assert
one event over infinitely many independently initialized widths or a
uniform limit with growing data. Its small-label hypothesis is fixed
as $n$ grows. The more general recurrence-defined label allowance and
the exact finite-width statements remain in the appendix.

For the last construction, availability of the passive inputs is
substantive: its state is tailored to their response curves.
It can be evaluated elsewhere, but the theorem makes no accuracy claim
there. The input dimension has not disappeared: it remains in the input
map and data charge, and can affect the qualifying width. The two
different sample/gap coefficients in \eqref{eq:headline-panel} are retained
rather than merging them into a misleading single prefactor.

\subsection{Why comparison with actual variability is possible}
\label{sec:variability}

Independent dense trajectories fluctuate on the $n^{-1/2}$ width scale,
up to logarithmic and subpolynomial factors. The lower-bound witness
already occurs at a training input, so it applies to both query domains.
Its mechanism is elementary but important. Conditional on earlier layers,
new Gaussian rows give a fresh empirical feature-Gram fluctuation. A
positive feature-Gram gap prevents all label-weighted feature products
from being deterministic: that would force their feature vector onto a
single line. The zero initial readout turns this fluctuation into a
fluctuating initial prediction velocity. Analytic-time estimates transfer
it to an actual, positive-time prediction difference. This is not an
endpoint lower bound; fitted trajectories agree on the training labels.

The compressed errors are made smaller than this lower scale without
using its unknown quantile as an algorithmic input. Thus the theorem is
not certified by comparing two upper bounds. The panel lower-bound
transfer and the assembly of all three optimized-size claims are given
in \Cref{sec:panel-headline-proof}; the full dense upper and lower
proofs are in \hyperref[int:dense-proofs]{the dense appendix}.

\subsection{What each reduction buys}
\label{sec:size}

At fixed admissible parameters, the three scales are
$n^{5/4+o(1)}$ moving coordinates, $\log^{3d+2}n$ total real coordinates,
and $\log^3n$ total real coordinates. They form a sequence of explicit
trade-offs, not increasingly accurate approximations to the same small
network: compress history; compress neuron coordinates using uniform
time--input regularity; then restrict the query family and adapt the
temporal resolution to remaining learning.

The last step saves two logarithmic powers over a uniform-resolution
finite-panel construction. It does not improve Harmonic's whole-sphere
exponent: that would require joint complex-input estimates on the enlarged
time domain. Nor does initialization-only information imply inexpensive
initialization. These distinctions are part of the result, not exceptions
to its accuracy contract.
