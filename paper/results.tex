\subsection{The whole learning trajectory at a prescribed size}
\label{sec:alltime}

Use the shared setup, normalization and confidence convention of
\Cref{sec:setting}. The appendix gives unsimplified bounds and retains
the full recurrence-defined label allowance for Harmonic and Logarithmic
compression. That allowance implies $Y\le1$: omitting such explicit
factors only enlarges their bounds and does not reduce sample/gap powers.

\begin{theorem}[Dense variability and compression of a realized trajectory]
\label{thm:alltime}
Fix an admissible problem.  For every sufficiently large individual width
$n\ge d$, the following claims hold jointly with probability at least
$99\%$.  The required width is finite but its stochastic threshold is not
quantified.  The event covers every permitted order below.

Let $f_n,\widetilde f_n$ be independently initialized dense runs.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_*}{Y}
 \le C\left(1+\frac m\gamma\right)^5
 \sqrt{\frac dn}\,\log(en)e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}}.
 \label{eq:dense-upper}
\end{equation}
If $m\ge2$, also
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_*}{Y}
 \ge \frac{c\sqrt\gamma}{\sqrt n\,[\log(en)]^{5/2}}.
 \label{eq:dense-lower}
\end{equation}
Legendre compression uses the realized initialization of $f_n$ and its own
residual-driven moment dynamics.  Simultaneously for every integer $q\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{f_{\mathrm{Leg},n,q}-f_n}_*}{Y}
 \le C\left(1+\frac m\gamma\right)^6
 \frac{\sqrt{\log(en)\log(eq)}}{q^2}e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}}.
 \label{eq:legendre-forward}
\end{equation}
There is no additional accuracy-dependent order condition.

Harmonic compression is an autonomous selected-coordinate model with its
own corrected readout.  For every integer budget
$C(m+d)\le q<n$, it satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \frac{\norm{f_{\mathrm{Harm},n,q}-f_n}_*}{Y}
 &\le C\frac m\gamma\left(1+\sqrt{\frac m\gamma}\right)
 n e^{C\sqrt{\log(en)}}\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\times\exp\!\left[-\frac{\sqrt d}{C}
 \left(\frac{q}{(m/\gamma)^2[\log(en)]^{d/2}}
 \right)^{1/(d+1)}\right].
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:harmonic-forward}
\end{equation}
For $q\ge n$, full retention gives exact agreement, including when $n$
is below the stated overhead.  No input-rank assumption is needed for
any assertion of this theorem.
\end{theorem}

The lower bound concerns an actual prediction at a positive early time,
not just a derivative at initialization.  It does not assert endpoint
variability: at the witnessing training input both fitted runs equal the
label.  The restriction $m\ge2$ is essential; at arbitrary confidence its
coefficient also depends on that confidence.  Its coefficient and proof are
in \hyperref[int:dense-trajectory-lower]{the dense lower-bound appendix}.
The dense upper bound and all-order Legendre comparison follow from
\hyperref[int:dense-proofs]{the dense proofs} and
\hyperref[int:legendre-proofs]{the Legendre proofs}; the arbitrary-budget
construction is in
\hyperref[int:harmonic-variable-budget-theorem]{the Harmonic appendix}.
These are function-fidelity guarantees, not assertions about unknown test labels.

\paragraph{Why dense trajectories genuinely fluctuate.}
The argument conditions on preceding layers before using row independence.
A conditional central limit argument propagates empirical-Gram fluctuations
and adds a Gaussian innovation at each layer. For $m\ge2$, the positive
feature-Gram gap forces a nonzero final-layer innovation in the label
direction; earlier fluctuations cannot cancel its variance. Zero initial
readout converts this into fluctuating prediction velocity. Complex-time
control and a polynomial endpoint inequality then yield a positive-time
prediction discrepancy with logarithmic losses.

\paragraph{What is stored.}
The first three models count exact real coordinates, not finite-precision
bits.  Their per-model storage is bounded by
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Model & Learned state & All retained model state\\
\midrule
Dense & $Cn^2$ & $Cn^2$\\
Legendre & $Cn(d+mq)$ & $C(n^2+mnq)$\\
Harmonic & $Cq^2$ & $Cq^2$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Ordinary retained training data are additional for Dense and Legendre.
Exact coordinate counts and the Harmonic overhead are given in the appendix.
Harmonic's last column already includes data, fixed metrics, initial copies
and solve caches; source arrays and setup histories are discarded.
Legendre compresses learned increments, not the fixed Gaussian mixers.
Harmonic compresses both, but uses its specified optimizer rather than
ordinary gradient flow on an arbitrary smaller network.

\subsection{Accuracy determines order and storage}
\label{sec:size}

The next prescriptions invert the displayed sufficient certificates; they
are not minimax claims.  For fixed structural parameters, with $n$
sufficiently large and polynomial accuracy $\varepsilon=n^{-\Theta(1)}$,
a sufficient Legendre order is
\begin{equation}
 q=\left\lceil C\left(1+\frac m\gamma\right)^3
 \varepsilon^{-1/2}
 [\log(en)\log(en/\varepsilon)]^{1/4}
 e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}/2}\right\rceil.
 \label{eq:legendre-order}
\end{equation}
Keeping both logarithms avoids concealing a coefficient depending on the
chosen polynomial accuracy exponent.  Its learned storage is at most
\begin{equation}
 Cnd+Cmn\left(1+\frac m\gamma\right)^3\varepsilon^{-1/2}
 [\log(en)\log(en/\varepsilon)]^{1/4}e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}/2}.
 \label{eq:legendre-inverse-storage}
\end{equation}
The fixed dense mixers remain additional.

A sufficient Harmonic budget in the same regime is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 q=\min\!\Bigl\{n,\Bigl\lceil C(m+d)
 &+\left(\frac C{\sqrt d}\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\frac m\gamma\right)^2\\
 &\quad\times[\log(en)]^{d/2}[\log(en/\varepsilon)]^{d+1}
 \Bigr\rceil\Bigr\},
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:harmonic-order}
\end{equation}
using exact retention when the minimum selects $n$.  Consequently its
learned and total retained state are both bounded by
\begin{equation}
 C(m+d)^2+\left(\frac Cd\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\frac m\gamma\right)^4
 [\log(en)]^d[\log(en/\varepsilon)]^{2d+2}.
 \label{eq:harmonic-inverse-storage}
\end{equation}
For arbitrary targets, replace the last logarithm in the budget by
$\log(en)+\log\!\bigl(e+C(m/\gamma)(1+\sqrt{m/\gamma})/\varepsilon\bigr)$.
The exact integer inverse also handles targets met by initialization alone;
see \hyperref[int:harmonic-variable-budget-theorem]{the budget theorem}.

For an independent dense comparison, width must instead be chosen before
initialization.  As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, a sufficient eventual choice is
\begin{equation}
 n=\left\lceil Cd\left(1+\frac m\gamma\right)^{10}
 \varepsilon^{-2}\log(e/\varepsilon)^2
 e^{C\sqrt{\log(e/\varepsilon)}}\right\rceil,
 \label{eq:dense-inverse}
\end{equation}
and its storage is at most
$Cd^2(1+m/\gamma)^{20}\varepsilon^{-4}
\log(e/\varepsilon)^4e^{C\sqrt{\log(e/\varepsilon)}}$.
This is not an effective confidence-certified width rule: it must also
exceed the unquantified stochastic threshold in \Cref{thm:alltime}.
For $m\ge2$, the dense lower bound conversely requires
$n[\log(en)]^5\ge c\gamma/\varepsilon^2$ eventually at the stated confidence.
Thus independent canonical dense runs need
$\Omega(\varepsilon^{-4}/\log(1/\varepsilon)^{10})$ stored coordinates
at a fixed problem.  This is not a lower bound on arbitrary representations.

\begin{corollary}[Compression error below actual dense variability]
\label{cor:compression}
Fix an admissible problem with $m\ge2$ and $Y>0$.  Choose
\[
 q_{\mathrm{Leg}}=\left\lceil C(1+m/\gamma)^3
 n^{1/4}[\log(en)]^2e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}/2}\right\rceil
\]
and choose the Harmonic budget in \eqref{eq:harmonic-order} with
$\varepsilon=n^{-1}$.  For either of these matched-reference constructions,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{f_{\mathrm{model}}-f_n}_*}
 {\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_*}\xrightarrow{\Pr}0.
 \label{eq:negligible-compression}
\end{equation}
Legendre's learned storage is
$Cnd+Cm(1+m/\gamma)^3n^{5/4+o(1)}$; Harmonic's total storage is at most
\begin{equation}
 C(m+d)^2+\left(\frac Cd\right)^{d+1}
 \left(\frac m\gamma\right)^4[\log(en)]^{3d+2}.
 \label{eq:harmonic-negligible-storage}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

For the stated Legendre order the normalized error is at most
$C/(\sqrt n\,[\log(en)]^3)$ eventually.  Harmonic's is at most $1/n$.
The arbitrary-confidence lower theorem in the appendix then gives
\eqref{eq:negligible-compression}; a fixed $99\%$ event alone would not.
The ratio compares complete trajectory norms, not pointwise or endpoint
ratios.  When $m\ge d$ the $nd$ term is eventually smaller; when
$m/\gamma\ge1$, the displayed cubic Legendre dependence is equivalently
$(m/\gamma)^3$.  The quartic Harmonic dependence has not been reduced by
spending the small-label assumption.

\subsection{A finite-word decoder with no supplied order}
\label{sec:logarithmic}

The Logarithmic decoder constructs a new compressed model, rather than
compressing a supplied dense realization.  It compares with a fresh
independent dense reference $f_n^{\mathrm{ind}}$.  Initialization may inspect
a completed virtual training computation, but retains no dense array or
future-answer table.  Seeds regenerate source rows for new inputs; queries
use the current retained state and do not replay scalar training.

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic decoder]
\label{thm:logarithmic}
Assume the full common label allowance, $m\ge d$ and spanning training
inputs.  There is a finite construction with no tunable order whenever
\begin{equation}
 n\ge\max\!\left\{Y^{-1},
 \left[C(1+m/\gamma)^4(m+d)^4\right]^{1100}\right\}.
 \label{eq:decoder-width}
\end{equation}
This is explicit, although extremely conservative; it has no additional
existential stochastic-width condition.  Define the local resource logarithm
\[
 Z=\log(en)+\log\!\left(e+(m+d+2)(1+m/\gamma)\right).
\]
All retained state and live query workspace use at most
\begin{equation}
 C(m+d+2)^2(1+m/\gamma)^2\log(em)^2 Z^6
 [d+1+\log(e+(d+1)Z)]
 \label{eq:decoder-words}
\end{equation}
numerical words, with $CZ$ bits per word sufficient.  Seeds, selected
packets, metrics, training data and all ensemble members are included.

With probability at least $99\%$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f_{\mathrm{Log},n}-f_n^{\mathrm{ind}}}_*\le3b_n,
 \label{eq:decoder-error}
\end{equation}
where $b_n$ is the exact dense upper certificate in
\hyperref[int:logarithmic-decoder-detailed]{the decoder appendix}, with
$b_n/Y=n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ at fixed problem parameters.
The structural coefficients of this particular certificate are not further
quantified; they are not included in the activation/depth-only constants
$c,C$.  The event covers all physical times, fitted limits, sphere inputs
and adaptively chosen queries.
\end{theorem}

The complete construction, arbitrary-confidence gate and finite arithmetic
proof are in \hyperref[int:logarithmic-decoder-detailed]{the decoder appendix}.
The proof uses a prescribed fixed-confidence member order of size
$O(\log(em))$ and amplifies whole source models, not individual query
answers.  Those orders are internal instructions, not a new model budget.
At fixed admissible parameters, \eqref{eq:decoder-words} is
$O(\log^6n\,\log\log n)$ words, or
$O(\log^7n\,\log\log n)$ bits.  The sixth power counts words, not bits.
Its error has the independent-dense upper-certificate scale
$b_n/Y=n^{-1/2+o(1)}$; no negligible-error ratio analogous to
\eqref{eq:negligible-compression} is proved for this method.

\paragraph{One accuracy scale, four storage costs.}
At fixed admissible parameters, choose $n=\varepsilon^{-2+o(1)}$
sufficiently large for the relevant width gates and error certificate,
and use the preceding inverse orders.  The sufficient storage bounds are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Model & Learned storage & All retained storage\\
\midrule
Independent dense & $\varepsilon^{-4+o(1)}$ & same\\
Legendre & $\varepsilon^{-5/2+o(1)}$ & $\varepsilon^{-4+o(1)}$\\
Harmonic & $O(\log(1/\varepsilon)^{3d+2})$ & same\\
Logarithmic & bounded by total &
$O(\log(1/\varepsilon)^6\log\log(e^e+1/\varepsilon))$ words\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The decoder uses one additional logarithmic power in bits.  For its inverse
choose a width meeting both \eqref{eq:decoder-width} and $3b_n\le Y\varepsilon$.
The inverse rate is eventual because this certificate has fixed-problem
coefficients.
Against an independent dense run, splitting accuracy and failure budgets
between compression and dense variability preserves these exponents.
