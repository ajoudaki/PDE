\section{Detailed execution costs and supplementary decoder costs}
\label{app:execution-costs}

We cost the prescribed sphere models at dense width $n$: Legendre and
Harmonic use the negligible-relative-error choices in
\Cref{thm:compression}, whereas the supplementary Seeded decoder retains
its separate fixed-quantile certificate. Initialization
includes every one-off operation needed to build a model. For the neural
constructions, a training stage means one full-batch vector-field
evaluation; a numerical update pays for all its stages. Initialization and
training memory are total peaks, including data. Query workspace is
additional to the loaded, inference-ready model. The supplementary seeded decoder, which is not the new finite-panel Taylor model, instead has
a finite-word contract and a bound for its complete training schedule.

 n^{5/4}[\log(en)]^2e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}/2},\\

\subsection{Two ways to initialize Harmonic compression}

For $Y>0$, both certified initializers support the Harmonic model chosen to have
error at most $Y/n$. This needs the exact inverse substitution: the
original choice $T=T_0$, $\eta=1/n$ alone gives only
$n^{-1+o(1)}$ error. Write $\ell=\log(en)$,
$\lambda=\gamma/m$, $T_0=32\ell/\lambda$, and
$\eta_0=\min(1,Y,16Y/\lambda)$. The explicit comparison coefficients
$\mathcal A_n,\mathcal D$ in
\eqref{int:eq-0068-harmonic-exact-error-coefficient} and
\eqref{int:eq-0057-harmonic-tail-coefficient} give the all-time bound
$\mathcal A_n\eta+\mathcal D e^{-\lambda T/4}$. Therefore set
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_1&=\mathcal A_n\eta_0+\mathcal D e^{-8\ell},
 &u&=\max\{0,\log(n\mathcal E_1/Y)\},\nonumber\\
 T&=\frac{32\ell+4u}{\lambda},
 &\eta&=\eta_0e^{-u}.
 \label{eq:harmonic-setup-exact-target}
\end{align}
Then $\mathcal A_n\eta+\mathcal D e^{-\lambda T/4}
=\mathcal E_1e^{-u}\le Y/n$. Use the exact inverse's integer
budget and its full-width fallback when required. For activation strip
$|\operatorname{Im}z|<a$, recall the activation envelope
\[
 \beta=\max\left\{10,1+\max_j|\phi_j(0)|,16/a,
 \max_{j,\,k\in\{1,2\}}\sup_{|\operatorname{Im}z|\le a/2}
 |\phi_j^{(k)}(z)|\right\}.
\]
The numerical envelope
in the \hyperref[int:harmonic-budget-inversion-proof]{inverse proof}
also gives the explicit estimate
\begin{equation}
 u\le\log n+65\sqrt\ell+
 \log\max\!\left\{1,4096e^{44}\beta^{42L}
 \frac m\gamma\left(1+\sqrt{\frac m\gamma}\right)\right\}.
 \label{eq:harmonic-setup-target-overhead}
\end{equation}
Thus $u=O(\ell)$, $T=O(\ell)$, and
$\log(1/\eta)=\log(1/\eta_0)+u=O(\ell)$ when the positive problem
parameters are fixed. No factor involving $m/\gamma$ is omitted from
the displayed substitutions.

For completeness, the source radii are
$r_t=c_t/\sqrt\ell$, $r_q=c_q/\sqrt\ell$, with
$c_t=a\gamma/(1024UmY^2)$ and $c_q=\min\{1/8,a/(8V)\}$;
$U,V$ are the source coefficients in \eqref{int:eq-0199-s-25}.
For $d\ge2$, the inverse's exact mode cutoff obeys
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_T^{-1}&=\frac{4T}{r_t}
 =4096\frac Ua\left(\frac{Ym}{\gamma}\right)^2
       (32\ell+4u)\sqrt\ell,\\
 H(T,\eta)&=H(T_0,\eta_0)+2u+
                2\log\left(1+\frac{u}{8\ell}\right).
\end{align*}
Consequently $\alpha_T^{-1}=O(\ell^{3/2})$ and $H(T,\eta)=O(\ell)$.
Use the setup-local temporal and spherical degrees $p,\ell_*$,
node counts $N_t,N_x$, spatial basis size $H_{\rm sph}$, joint mode
count $N$, source-generator bound $R$, and actual rank $r$ from the
\hyperref[int:harmonic-efficient-supplied-orders]{finite setup inventory}.
Here $p$ is unrelated to the passive-panel size elsewhere in the paper.
The exact mode counts and positive quadratures give
\begin{align*}
 p+1,N_t&=O(\ell^{5/2}),
 &\ell_*&=O(\ell^{3/2}),\\
 N_x,H_{\rm sph}&=O(\ell^{3(d-1)/2}),
 &N,R,r,q&=O(\ell^{3d/2+1}).
\end{align*}
At $d=1$, use the certified two-point convention $N_x=H_{\rm sph}=2$;
there is no spherical degree $\ell_*$. The other displayed orders still hold.
The nodal tolerance has $\log(1/\delta_{\rm node})=O(\ell)$.

\subsection{The finite-word decoder}

The decoder counts word operations. Its explicit width gates bound the
resource logarithm by $C\log(en)$ and its member moment order by
$C\log(em)$, so the retained-word bound in the sphere compression certificates
already displays its dependence on $m,d,\gamma$. Each word has $C\log(en)$
sufficient bits under the same activation/depth convention. With all
problem parameters fixed, the complete operation bounds become
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{rl}
\text{initialization:}&O(n[\log(en)]^{29/2}),\\
\text{complete training:}&O([\log(en)]^{12}\log(e+\log(en))),\\
\text{one unseen query:}&O(n[\log(en)]^{12}+[\log(en)]^{14}).
\end{array}
\label{eq:decoder-work}
\end{equation}
Training and live query memory fit the complete retained inventory,
$O([\log(en)]^6\log(e+\log(en)))$ words at fixed parameters. Initialization
has peak $O(n[\log(en)]^{5/2}+[\log(en)]^{15/2})$ words. The
\hyperref[int:decoder-finite-backends]{full word-operation ledger} keeps the structural
prefactors and the separate temporary arrays. These query-memory counts
are total memory, unlike the additional workspace in \Cref{tab:runtime}.

The finite tanh evaluator is included. For another analytic activation,
add its supplied evaluator's work at every recorded value call and its
scratch, together with external data/query access and output. The finite
training table is counted; other retained external descriptions require
additional words. Analyticity supplies no evaluation-cost bound for an
arbitrary function. The training row covers the complete prescribed
schedule. A query streams the regenerated source rows, retaining the
displayed width factor in its work.
