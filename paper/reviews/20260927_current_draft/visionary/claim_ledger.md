# Claim ledger — visionary assessment

## C1. The learning-speed closure tracks the full fixed-width flow

**Claim/type.** Theorem 6.1, pp. 10, 18–21: for arbitrary finite data, realized finite initialization, width and depth, locally C^{1,1} activations permit an autonomous order-P closure with uniform-on-[0,T] parameter error O_T(P^{-1}); bounded activations and first derivatives permit every P≥1.

**Author evidence.** Equations (3)–(8), projection energy (14), exact perturbed-flow identity (15)–(17), layer recursion (18)–(20), and Gronwall/continuation (21)–(22).

**Plausibility:** Highly Plausible. **Soundness:** Sound for the checked argument after the localized symbol corrections L1. The dense energy estimate makes the compact region explicit. Histories in the proof are those generated by the closure; there is no substitution of a posteriori dense histories. The polynomial endpoint factor is controlled before induction over depth, and the accumulated absolute defect is small. This is exactly the nontrivial feedback issue an online signal-projection result does not solve.

**Novelty.** Non-trivial synthesis and theorem, relative to the targeted sources checked. Online Legendre moments are a known ingredient, correctly attributed to HiPPO. The new argument is the combination of paired forward/backward histories, the learning clock, and self-consistent stability for arbitrary fixed finite neural depth. No claim of exhaustive priority verification is made.

**Citation invocation.** HiPPO attribution valid; the theorem itself is proved directly. **Code/empirical evidence.** The saved shared-time predictions are consistent with the mechanism but do not prove the theorem. Underlying closure engines are outside the packet.

**Dependencies/limits.** Does not require a population limit, successful fitting, Gaussian initialization, or a low-rank hypothesis. Does not prove a width-uniform or all-time bound, practical small P, sample compression, or runtime improvement. Its parameter norm should be explicitly defined as the concatenated Euclidean/Frobenius norm (L1).

## C2. The joint clock upgrades the rate and yields causal low-rank increments

**Claim/type.** Theorem 6.2 and Corollary 6.3, pp. 10–11, 21–25: a residual-plus-response-speed clock with weighted Gram projection yields O_T(P^{-2}) and rank at most m(P+1), assuming the stated additional smoothness and positive initial residual. LS gives rank mP with O_T(P^{-1}).

**Author evidence.** Matching prefixes, Gram ODE/reconstruction (23), dense residual lower bound (24), weighted projection energy (25), coarse variation estimate (26), clock bound (28), sharp error (29)–(32), and Gram continuation (33).

**Plausibility:** Highly Plausible. **Soundness:** Sound for the checked argument after L1 notation repair. The clock is bounded using a coarse defect estimate before invoking its improved regularity; its definition is explicitly implementable because reconstructed physical velocities do not contain g. The nonzero matching backward prefix removes the join discontinuity. The rank m(P+1) accounts for the prefix subtraction.

**Novelty.** Non-trivial mechanism: controlling both error factors improves trajectory order, not merely the reconstruction error of an externally supplied signal. The low-rank corollary is mathematically immediate from the reconstruction but scientifically meaningful because the factors are generated online under a closed dynamics. It is stronger in this sense than an endpoint SVD, without claiming optimal rank.

**Adversarial audit.** Local Gram positivity does not ensure benign numerical conditioning. Residual lower bounds can be tiny and constants large. The statement “at P=1 changing the coordinate alone leaves the closure unchanged” should not obscure that LS and joint constructions also differ in their backward prefixes; a compact two-clock definition table would help (L1). Joint-clock empirical superiority is not established; its nonmonotone and unstable low-order behavior is disclosed.

**Dependencies/limits.** Main conclusions survive without the population conjecture. Compression becomes numerically informative only below the stated rank/state thresholds. No width-uniform constant follows from the fixed-width proof. Appendix E.4 gives a useful conditional route through normalized variation, not the missing theorem.

## C3. Small-order memory preserves the dense predictor better than the tested controls

**Claim/type.** Empirical claims in §7, Figs. 3–5 and 7–13, Table 2: shared-time tracking on one circle task; fitted-function fidelity across circle tasks and binary MNIST; superiority to the specific frozen empirical kernel and Euclidean factor-flow controls on circle endpoint fidelity.

**Author evidence.** Saved predictions, matched initialization, common-time replay at two tolerances, endpoint threshold protocol, kernel spectral solution, matched correction rank and two factor seeds.

**Plausibility:** Highly Plausible for the saved-array comparisons; Plausible but independently unverified for original training provenance and unsupplied sweeps. **Soundness of empirical interpretation:** supported at the declared scope. All supplied discrepancy arrays recompute; 29 resolved factor comparisons and 28 wins exceeding two recompute from supplied metadata. The common-time max RMS drops from 0.07097 to 0.005879 to 0.0001752 at P=1,3,7, though only 6/38 positive P=7 times exceed the coarse/fine sensitivity scale.

**Scientific significance.** The controls distinguish fidelity of the learned function from ability to interpolate labels. Equal rank by itself is insufficient to preserve the function selected by dense training; dynamics inside that low-rank representation matter. The frozen control reinforces that memory reproduces feature-learning behavior beyond the initial kernel. This is substantive positive evidence, not merely a list of caveats.

**Concerns.** L3: provide precise readout law, kernel block traces and fit-time scale; the dramatic NTK extrapolations are from this small-readout parameterization, not a universal kernel failure. L4: state binary digits 3/8 in the main MNIST paragraph, and correct the approximate shallow-order range to 1.5–3.5 orders. L6: distinguish normalized plotted coefficients from raw memory moments.

**Coverage.** No new training, no independently checked raw parameter trajectories, no claim that finite sampled times certify continuous error, no inference of unseen-label performance from circle RMS. Missing engines/records are a packet limitation rather than proof of an absent release. No broad baseline superiority or best-hyperparameter claim is justified or necessary.

## C4. A state–accuracy axis organizes complementary descriptions of learning

**Claim/type.** §4, §6.5–6.7, Table 1, Appendix E: retained history order offers a new resource parameter; fixed-width moving state is O(LmnP), and conditional population allocations scale as epsilon^{-3} or epsilon^{-5/2} under A1/A2.

**Author evidence.** Explicit memory counts, error allocation optimization, mobility-matched tangent hierarchy, and specified streamed DMFT solver.

**Plausibility:** Highly Plausible as a representation-specific, conditional accounting; population hypotheses themselves remain conjectural. **Soundness:** checked arithmetic and matched-NTH recursion are sound. The fixed-width results concern parameters while the common population comparison concerns prediction; Appendix E explicitly supplies the transfer assumptions and common metric.

**Citation audit.** NTH source prerequisites are materially different from the current target; the draft correctly says so, and its copied estimate matches after symbol exchange. DMFT's direct two-time objects support the representation count; the solver convergence hypotheses are not attributed as proved source results. General Tensor Programs and multilayer mean-field citations were not independently checked in this pass.

**Concern L2.** Table 1 itself should identify fixed-width/population/conditional status, rather than requiring readers to reconstruct it from a long caption and Appendix E. Its `n^{-1/2}` description of NTH corrections is imprecise relative to the retrieved source's `O(n^{-1})` kernel-velocity conclusion. Use “width-suppressed.” A conditional root-width rate should be marked in its cell. These are local reporting corrections; Appendix E's careful comparison survives.

**Novelty/significance.** The organizing axis is useful because it separates width, time memory and sample-indexed tensors. It is not a proof that one framework intrinsically dominates another. The full initialized matrices remain; total storage/runtime can be worse. Those disclosures strengthen the conceptual comparison by specifying exactly what is compressed.

## Holistic analysis

The central fixed-width dynamical-memory message stands and is scientifically non-trivial. The proof is about compressing endogenous learned interactions while preserving their feedback on an entire finite-horizon trajectory. It does not require that the learned function be simple or that feature movement vanish. Population closure, uniform-width rates, practical small-order constants and compression of the initialized source remain distinct open issues, mostly stated honestly. The most material present proof concern lies in ancillary theorem claims. The empirical arrays support function fidelity to the declared dense target; independent training reproduction is outside the paper-only packet. No fatal flaw is established.
