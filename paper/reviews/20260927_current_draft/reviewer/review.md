# Review of the current 41-page manuscript

## 1. Summary

The paper constructs autonomous response-memory approximations to a specified deep-network gradient flow. Each learned hidden interaction is reconstructed from paired forward and residual-weighted backward history moments, while the initialized matrices remain fixed. For every fixed width, depth and finite horizon, it proves parameter-trajectory error of order `P^-1`, or `P^-2` with a joint response clock, under stated activation regularity. It also derives online low-rank learned increments, gives conditional population resource comparisons, and illustrates prediction fidelity with circle, binary MNIST and sphere experiments.

**The main finite-width contribution survives this review.** I found a valid core in both response-memory proofs, including their difficult feedback steps. The strongest concerns are that the supplementary population and exact-compression theorems are presented more conclusively than their supplied proofs warrant. Several notation and comparison-table corrections would substantially improve clarity without changing the main results.

## 2. Main Contributions

The strongest contribution is a quantitative, causal approximation of the **specified full training trajectory**, rather than compression of one fitted function or a generic low-rank parameterization. The nontrivial mechanism is the combination of paired projection, an exact endpoint-energy identity, and feedback control. The product of two omitted-history errors is not by itself enough; the paper successfully connects it to an integrated absolute velocity defect and then to trajectory stability.

The second clock is also a substantive extension: it first obtains an order-independent total-variation estimate, uses this to bound its own clock, and only then invokes the sharper joint-history approximation. The retained state has fixed size on the chosen horizon; the proof does not merely assume the clock or reconstructed trajectory stays controlled.

The rank corollary is mathematically straightforward once these results are established, but useful: a small rank is generated by an autonomous continuation rule. The paper does not have to outperform all low-rank algorithms to establish that structural result.

## 3. Strengths

- Theorem hypotheses are broad at fixed width: arbitrary finite data and realized initialization, arbitrary fixed depth, and locally regular activations. Successful fitting and special data geometry are unnecessary.
- I re-derived the central projection-energy, defect, endpoint-amplification cancellation, layer recursion, joint-clock and continuation arguments. The first-layer versus later-layer smoothness distinction is consistent with the construction. I found no material error in these chains.
- The paper usually keeps its evidence levels straight. The fixed-order population limit is a conjecture; population cost exponents require explicitly listed hypotheses; dense initialization costs are retained; runtime and total-memory gains are not claimed automatically.
- The empirical presentation separates shared-time tracking from individually fitted endpoints, and numerical sensitivity from confidence intervals. Recomputed circle, MNIST, sphere and five-task control discrepancies agree with the saved arrays. An independent tiny-network finite-difference check supports the full empirical-NTK formula.
- Appendix E treats NTH and DMFT more carefully than a superficial comparison would. The published NTH guarantee has a different scaling, observable and horizon; the matched hierarchy is analyzed conditionally instead. A streamed DMFT implementation is allowed different memory/work tradeoffs. These distinctions are scientifically appropriate.

## 4. Weaknesses and Claim-Level Concerns

**M1 — The population closure proof is incomplete as supplied.** Appendix C, pp. 25–28 (`main.tex:1563–1743`), invokes a correlated Gaussian initialized operator, density of initialization-observable programs and an exponential tail estimate for the reference backward fields without constructing or proving them. The Osgood step is valid once its differential inequality is established, but boundedness as an L2 operator does not imply the required exponential tails. “Locally Lipschitz” also needs a specified state space and moment argument when the observables are unbounded. Convergence of every quadratic observation needs adequate uniform-integrability control. These are substantive proof obligations, not notation details. Supply the lemmas or state C.1 conditionally. This weakens the claimed established population example; it does **not** undermine Theorems 6.1–6.2.

**M2 — Appendix D does not contain complete proofs of its theorems.** On p. 28 (`main.tex:1752–1799`), D.1's colored-Fock-space construction, trace-class analysis, stochastic width limit and vanishing-step convergence are summarized in a paragraph. The exact initialization law is not specified adequately by “order-one initialization.” D.2's growing-contraction idea is plausible, but the nonzero coefficient, applicable graph-independence statement and extension to a finite-order local polynomial PDE are not established. The displayed finite block derivative is consistent; the stronger analytic/probabilistic conclusions remain unverified. Complete these results, formulate them conditionally, or present them explicitly as sketches. They are auxiliary motivation rather than premises of the response-memory theorem, so these gaps are not fatal to the paper's central claim.

**L1 — Repair notation before further conceptual expansion.** Eq. (13), p. 19, sums to undefined `H` instead of depth `L`. Eqs. (22), (31), (32), pp. 21/24, use undefined `K` where the preceding constant is `Lambda_F`. Appendix D then uses K for a kernel. Appendix A introduces barred h for a history, while Appendix B uses the same symbol for both a history and an n-by-P moment matrix in one displayed definition (`main.tex:1359`). Separate histories from moments, declare dimensions, and state the block Euclidean/Frobenius parameter norm. These are local repairs; they do not require new proofs.

**L2 — Make Table 1 as precise as Appendix E.** The table on p. 6 combines a conditional population root-width rate, NTH's source-scaling training error, numerical DMFT “step size,” and the proved finite-width parameter errors. The caption already gives important qualifications, so I do not read this as an unqualified dominance claim. Still, the row cells should state target observable and proof status directly. Mark root width as conditional; include the NTH horizon restriction; indicate DMFT's sampling/iteration/discretization assumptions. Show retained fixed resources beside moving state. Appendix E's allocation algebra remains valid under its assumptions, and does not establish a universal ranking.

**L3 — Attribute the feedback distinction more narrowly.** Sections 6.2/6.4 suggest that endogenous feedback is what distinguishes the method from HiPPO. HiPPO already discusses memory-dependent inputs and recurrent architectures in Sections 2.5 and 4. The defensible new claim is the paired reconstruction and quantitative feedback-stable approximation of a specified parameter gradient flow. Emphasize that theorem, rather than feedback in the abstract. [HiPPO full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.07669).

**L4 — Explain the two prefix conventions and plotted coordinates.** The speed clock uses zero backward prefix; the joint construction uses the initial backward response and subtracts its prefix product. Both are coherent, but they define different approximations. The P=1 remark on p. 25 is true for a coordinate change with a fixed prefix, not a blanket equality of these two constructions. Also, Figure 2's code plots normalized Legendre coefficients from a zero-origin illustrative history, not the raw unit-prefix moments defined for the algorithm. A short normalization statement and distinct notation would remove both ambiguities.

**L5 — State the experimental readout law and report existing control horizons.** Section 3 calls the readout “small”; the main control code uses Gaussian entries divided by width. This matters for its initial tangent kernel. The strongest frozen-NTK examples reach the matched-loss endpoint at physical times around `7.09e7` and `3.33e9`, unlike the `[0,80]` trajectory panel. Their analytic calculations check numerically, so this is not evidence of an unfair or invalid endpoint comparison. Report the already-saved fit times and conditioning so readers can see exactly which question each panel answers.

The absence of a width-uniform theorem, a runtime improvement, a physical-time clock ablation, or a generalization advantage is an acknowledged limitation, not a manufactured fatal flaw. The requested changes do not require resolving all those open problems.

## 5. Questions for the Authors

1. Can Appendix C provide the exact initialized-operator construction and the uniform reference-tail/density lemmas needed before the Osgood inequality? If not, will C.1 be restated conditional on those properties?
2. Can D.1/D.2 be supplied with complete hypotheses and proofs, including the precise meaning of the PDE clause, or reduced to clearly labeled motivating sketches?
3. Will the main notation distinguish history functions, raw moments, normalized coefficients and endpoint reconstructions, and explicitly reconcile the two prefixes?
4. Can the already-available initialization law, fitted times and numerical-sensitivity information be tabulated alongside the controls? This would improve interpretation without requesting new training.

## 6. Reproducibility and Code

I read all four supplied Python scripts and verified every manifest hash. A bounded check reproduced all 30 shallow/deep circle discrepancies, the five P3 kernel/factor comparisons, the three MNIST and three sphere discrepancies, and the shared-time error/sensitivity formulas. The tiny-network NTK finite-difference error was `2.82e-11`; a separate algebra check of the joint reconstruction derivative agreed within `2.45e-15`.

The 29 resolved factor wins, including 28 by more than a factor of two, agree with saved aggregate metadata. Raw predictions independently support the five-task P3 subset, not every underlying factor run. For P=7, 32 of 38 positive shared-time discrepancies are at or below the saved sensitivity diagnostic; those points should not be interpreted as a measured convergence exponent.

The packet deliberately omits study-owned training engines and some original experiment producers. I did not inspect them, run training, audit the unavailable MNIST preprocessing, or reproduce the joint-clock sweep. This is a verification limit of the review packet, not a claim that the repository or future release lacks those files. `code_audit.md` gives the exact scope and commands.

## 7. Limitations and Ethical Considerations

The proved results are finite-width and finite-horizon, with potentially large width/time constants. Useful compression requires the necessary rank to be below width. The method retains dense initialized matrices and its state grows with sample count. The joint Gram system may be ill-conditioned despite exact positive definiteness. The empirical evidence is illustrative and largely single-initialization; the ReLU sphere example is explicitly outside the smooth theorem. No independent ethical concern arose from the theoretical task and supplied datasets.

## 8. Overall Scientific Assessment

This is a credible and non-trivial finite-width training-dynamics approximation paper. Its strongest scientific statement is that learned interactions can be carried by causal paired response memories, with an explicit finite-horizon parameter-error guarantee despite the feedback between memories and the evolving network. That contribution is more specific and better established than the broader aspiration to a canonical population model of learning.

Prioritize completing or qualifying Appendices C/D, then fix notation and bring the comparison table and novelty wording up to the precision of the strongest proofs. The surviving core is worth centering. I found no demonstrated fatal flaw and no evidence that shared ingredients with HiPPO, NTH or DMFT alone eliminate this contribution.

## 9. Confidence and Verification Coverage

High confidence in the checked central algebra/proof architecture and stored-data arithmetic; moderate confidence in novelty relative to the limited primary-source audit; limited confidence in the auxiliary population/operator theorems until the missing arguments are supplied. Complete manuscript and appendix text was read, with visual checks on 15 selected pages. Primary full text was retrieved for HiPPO, NTH, DMFT and the cited multilayer mean-field comparison; no broad literature sweep or independent full training reproduction was performed. Detailed evidence and severity dependencies are in `evidence_log.md` and `claim_ledger.md`.
