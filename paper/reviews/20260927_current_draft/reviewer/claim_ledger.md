# Claim ledger

Scope, source access and executed checks are recorded in `evidence_log.md` and `code_audit.md`. This ledger separates plausibility of conclusions from soundness of supplied arguments. “Sound” refers only to the checked setting and does not imply an independent solver reproduction.

## C1 — A finite response-memory ODE approximates complete finite-width training

**Claim/type.** Theorems 6.1 and 6.2, pp. 10 and 18–25: fixed finite depth/width, arbitrary finite data/initialization, locally regular activations; `sup_[0,T] ||theta_hat-theta||=O_T(P^-1)` with the residual-speed clock and `O_T(P^-2)` with the joint clock. Bounded activations/first derivatives give speed-clock existence for every P; the general results need sufficiently large P. Joint clock assumes positive initial residual and the stated additional smoothness.

**Evidence.** Complete proofs in Appendices A/B, Legendre tail lemma, projection-energy identity, endpoint product defect, first-exit stability and Gram continuation.

**Plausibility: Highly Plausible. Soundness: Sound after the minor notation repairs L1.** Re-derived energy existence, triangular recurrence, projection-energy identity, defect formula, endpoint-norm cancellation, layer recursion, bounded-activation continuation, joint coarse-variation/clock bound and weighted tail argument. No circular small-error assumption was found. Explicit physical velocities make the apparent implicit clock computable; the smoothness conditions match the needed derivatives. Numerical algebra check supports the weighted reconstruction cancellation.

**Novelty.** Non-trivial as a feedback-stable approximation theorem for the prescribed training trajectory. Online Legendre projection itself is known. The useful step is coupling two endogenous memories through a reconstructed operator while still controlling the accumulated absolute defect. HiPPO recurrent architectures already use feedback, so L3 narrows the novelty sentence, not the theorem.

**Citation invocations.** HiPPO LegS attribution valid; the paper's own Legendre proof is self-contained. No other external source is indispensable to this theorem.

**Impact.** Core message survives. Constants depend on width/horizon and need not certify useful low rank at a practical order. These limitations are expressly stated, not missing hypotheses. No inferred population theorem or arbitrary-long-training certificate.

## C2 — The learned hidden increments admit causal low-rank representations

**Claim/type.** Corollary 6.3 and Sections 6.5–6.6: reconstruction rank at most mP for speed clock and m(P+1) for joint clock; moving hidden state of order LmnP, plus the joint Gram matrix; fixed-width sufficient cost `epsilon^-1` or `epsilon^-1/2` in order.

**Evidence.** Explicit reconstruction and C1 parameter-error bound.

**Plausibility: Highly Plausible. Soundness: Sound.** Finite sums of outer products give the speed-clock rank. Joint prefix subtraction contributes at most m additional directions, explaining the different rank bound. Per-sample factors give the stated moving-array count. Fixed dense W0 is still necessary for forward/adjoint reuse; total storage/runtime do not follow the moving-state improvement.

**Novelty.** Simple corollary once C1 is established, but scientifically useful because it is generated by a closed online dynamics, not by fitting an SVD afterward or just choosing low-rank factors. It is informative only when the needed rank is below width, as the manuscript says.

**Code/empirical support.** Rank-matched factor endpoints support the importance of the evolution law over rank alone. No strongest-possible low-rank algorithm comparison follows or is needed.

**Impact.** Survives independently of supplementary population/exact-operator assertions. L4 clarifies the two prefix conventions; neither convention invalidates the rank statements.

## C3 — Population closure and exact-compression obstacles are established auxiliary results

**Claim/type.** Appendix C Theorem C.1/Proposition C.2: autonomous frozen-span population closure and nontrivial feature learning near a two-point task. Appendix D Theorems D.1/D.2: global trace-class linear operator closure, finite-width/vanishing-step convergence, and bounded-contraction polynomial scalar nonclosure.

**Evidence.** Population construction and Osgood proof sketch; small-time expansion; cyclic-block operator formula and a contraction-graph argument.

**Plausibility: Plausible as carefully formalized results; Questionable as fully established by this submission. Soundness: Major gaps M1/M2.** These are not disproved. The population argument relies on a correlated Gaussian-operator construction, observable density, reference exponential tails and moment stability that are neither constructed nor stated as hypotheses. The displayed Osgood calculation cannot supply its own starting tail inequality. The local Lipschitz and quadratic-observation conclusions need an explicit function space/moment argument. The exact-operator theorem provides a roadmap instead of the announced proof of the stochastic width limit. Its initialization law is underdescribed. Nonclosure needs the actual repeated-derivative coefficient and graph-independence theorem; its PDE addendum is not formulated sufficiently to verify.

**Novelty.** Potentially non-trivial, but not independently adjudicated from the sparse arguments. The finite cyclic-block derivative and small-time population expansion are consistent at the formal algebraic level; that does not prove the analytic/probabilistic claims.

**Citation invocations.** No retrieved external theorem is identified by the submission as supplying these missing steps. Background citations cannot be silently substituted for them.

**Cascade.** M1 weakens the claimed already-established population example; M2 weakens Section 5's exact-compression motivation. Neither is a premise of C1 or C2. No fatal/paper-wide rejection inference is warranted. Complete, condition, or remove these auxiliary theorem claims to align evidence levels.

## C4 — The state–accuracy axis supports meaningful comparisons

**Claim/type.** Table 1 and Appendix E compare moving-state costs of descriptions; under A1–A4 they derive common-population-error costs. Fixed-width response-memory costs are unconditional relative to C1; population costs are conditional.

**Evidence.** Error metric, allocation calculus, own matched NTH estimate and streamed DMFT count; scoped primary-source comparisons.

**Plausibility: Highly Plausible for the conditional derivations; not an established universal ranking. Soundness: Sound in the specified conditional representations, with reporting concern L2.** Checked the allocation minimization, NTH convolution/Gronwall bound, factorial growth reduction, Lambert-W integer order, word-integral formula and one-sample reduction. Checked dimension-free Jackson estimate. DMFT costs are credible upper counts for the specified dense streamed solver; its stability/sampling/order assumptions are not a theorem of the retrieved source.

**Citation invocations.** Published NTH theorem's sample/width conversion and assumptions are accurately stated; its parameterization/horizon/observable are explicitly different. HiPPO attribution needs L3. Nguyen–Pham root-width rate is an extra hypothesis, acknowledged in caption/appendix but misleadingly unmarked inside Table 1. Full assumption transfer for Nguyen–Pham or Tensor Programs was not independently completed.

**Novelty.** The allocation arithmetic is simple; the common contract is useful scientific organization. No lower bound over all representations or optimality of the listed methods is proved. Appendix E correctly exhibits a one-sample NTH scalar state and allows streamed DMFT costs that can have a more favorable memory exponent; these are signs of fair qualification, not contradictions.

**Cascade.** L2 affects readability/interpretation of the summary table. It does not invalidate the conditional algebra or prove another method dominates the core construction.

## C5 — Small orders reproduce dense predictions beyond frozen and direct-factor controls

**Claim/type.** Section 7 and Figures 3–13: one shared-time circle replay; fitted prediction agreement on circle/MNIST/sphere; P3 beats specified frozen empirical kernels and factors on five circle tasks; 29 resolved rank-matched wins.

**Evidence.** Saved predictions, manifest provenance, control scripts, refinement diagnostics, and aggregate metadata.

**Plausibility: Highly Plausible for reproduced packet-level discrepancies; Plausible, not independently reproduced, as claims about their generating training runs.** All raw-array discrepancies checked agree with the manuscript. All supplied P3 kernel/factor comparisons are supported. The 29-count statement matches the metadata but not all underlying factor runs are independently present. Tiny-network kernel formula check succeeds. No internal evidence of omitted kernel blocks, ridge tuning, hidden eigenmode discarding, wrong metric or test-label optimization was found in supplied code.

**Scope.** Same-loss endpoints compare selected functions; only the 39-time panel addresses physical-time tracking, and none of these plots empirically establish the parameter-norm exponent. Many P7 shared-time differences are at the numerical sensitivity scale. The one-seed ReLU sphere result is explicitly outside the smooth theorem. MNIST is binary 3-versus-8, not ten-class classification. Width population scaling and broad optimizer superiority remain untested.

**Novelty/significance.** Convincing illustrative support that a small causal state can preserve a fitted function not selected by those two alternatives, even with moving hidden features. It is not a demonstration of improved generalization over dense training or superiority to all kernels/low-rank algorithms, and the manuscript largely says this correctly.

**Concerns.** L4 corrects the illustrative coefficient convention; L5 requests exact readout law and existing fit-time/conditioning reporting. Omitted training engines and data pipelines are verification limits of this packet, not assigned author-code flaws.

## Holistic analysis

The central finite-width theorem stands on the checked proof chain. Its novelty is more than the presence of Legendre moments and less than a proved population model of learning: two responses are paired so discarded errors multiply, and their endogenous feedback is controlled over a fixed horizon. The population conjecture is honestly marked; the already-labeled population and exact-compression theorems in Appendices C/D need the same evidentiary discipline. The empirical story is useful and largely well qualified. Prioritize completing or downgrading the auxiliary theorems, then repair notation, the table's statuses, the HiPPO distinction and experimental initialization/horizon reporting. These changes improve the paper without requiring a new research campaign or a universal-baseline competition.
