\begin{abstract}
Can a deep network's entire learning trajectory be represented by a much
smaller state? We study Gaussian-initialized nonlinear networks with learned
hidden features, fixed depth, at least two training examples, a positive
initial feature-Gram gap, and small fixed nonzero labels. The common principle
is to compress the forward and backward
responses that generate learning, rather than approximate a fitted weight
matrix. Legendre compression summarizes their temporal histories, using
$n^{5/4+o(1)}$ moving coordinates while retaining fixed dense mixers.
Two realizations of a shared reduced nonlinear dynamics also compress the
neuron coordinate: Harmonic compression uses joint time--input regularity
and retains $O((\log n)^{3d+2})$ real coordinates over the whole input sphere;
Logarithmic compression uses residual-adapted temporal approximation and
retains $O((\log n)^3)$ real coordinates on any fixed panel of inputs declared
at initialization. Passive labels are not used. Each tracks a coupled dense
trajectory, uniformly over all physical times including the fitted limit,
with error negligible in probability relative to independent dense-run
variability. Harmonic and Logarithmic attain absolute error $Y/n$, where
$Y$ is the fixed RMS label scale. The proof retains
Gram dissipation and finite residual activity; a readout correction cancels
the feedback that would otherwise give a poor stability estimate. We expose
the trade between query scope, moving memory and retained state, and separate
initialization-only information from the work needed to process it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Compressing a trained function does not necessarily compress its learning.
Two parameter states can agree on every input while their backward responses
differ, so that the same next update separates their predictions. A
description of learning must retain enough state to continue the dynamics,
not merely reproduce one fitted snapshot.

Our target keeps depth, nonlinear activations and trained hidden
representations. Infinite-width descriptions organize these dynamics through
population variables and responses to reused random matrices
\citep{yang2021tensor,nguyen2023rigorous,bordelon2022dmft}. They motivate a
further question: how much finite state is needed to reproduce the actual
learning trajectory of a finite network? Here both the reference and the
compressed predictor remain finite. Accuracy includes inputs absent from
the updates and all physical time. The query domain is an explicit part of
the guarantee: the whole sphere, or a declared finite panel whose passive
labels are never supplied.

\paragraph{An intrinsic accuracy benchmark.}
Independent initializations already produce different dense trajectories.
Our common compression theorem makes the ratio of compression error to
this actual discrepancy tend to zero in probability. All three methods
therefore track their dense reference more closely than independent dense
runs track one another, in their respective query domains. This compares
to realized variability, not an analytic upper envelope or a fixed-factor
target. No measurement of dense variability is needed to initialize the
compressed model. The dense lower
bound is witnessed during early training; it is not an endpoint lower bound.

\paragraph{One object to compress, two reduction mechanisms.}
A learned matrix increment integrates paired forward and backward responses.
The first reduction, Legendre, compresses the history while retaining every
neuron. The second compresses the response space itself and realizes its
geometry on a small set of neuron coordinates. Harmonic and Logarithmic
are two source builders for this same reduced dynamics. Harmonic represents
responses jointly in time and input; Logarithmic represents only the finitely
many requested input curves and spends fewer temporal pieces as learning
slows. The latter removes the dimension-dependent logarithmic exponent by
giving up a uniform guarantee for inputs first revealed after setup.

\paragraph{Why feedback is the central obstacle.}
These are closed learning systems, not playback of an approximated dense
trajectory. A response error changes the next update, which changes the
responses used thereafter. A black-box Lipschitz estimate treats this loop
as exponential growth over an infinite horizon. The proof instead keeps
the dissipative sign of the training Gram, measures forcing against the
finite amount of remaining learning, and, for the selected models, cancels
the leading readout--residual feedback. The main text explains these
mechanisms before the appendix supplies their quantitative estimates.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The results allow arbitrary fixed depth, compatible finite sphere data,
and analytic activations with unbounded values. Labels are small but fixed
as width grows. The feature-learning mobilities are retained rather than
replaced by a frozen-kernel scaling. This permits hidden learning; it does
not assert nonzero feature motion for every activation and dataset.
The guarantees concern prediction fidelity, not risk against unknown test
labels. Sufficient stochastic widths remain qualitative. The logarithmic
storage counts real coordinates, not fixed-precision words; its finite
initialization-jet construction establishes information provenance, not
a practical setup-time bound.

\section{Setting and reading conventions}
\label{sec:setting}
There are $m$ training pairs $(x_a,y_a)$, with $\norm{x_a}_2=\sqrt d$,
width $n$, and $L\ge2$ hidden layers. We use the paper's layer notation:
\begin{align}
 z_a^{(1)}&=W^{(1)}x_a/\sqrt d,&
 z_a^{(\ell)}&=W^{(\ell)}h_a^{(\ell-1)},&
 h_a^{(\ell)}&=\phi(z_a^{(\ell)}),\nonumber\\
 f_{n,a}&=\frac{w^\top h_a^{(L)}}n,&
 r_a&=f_{n,a}-y_a,&
 \cL&=\frac1m\sum_a r_a^2.
 \label{eq:network}
\end{align}
Here $w=W^{(L+1)}$ is the output weight; the activation may vary by layer.
Backward responses exclude the residual:
\[
 \delta_a^{(L)}=w\od\phi'(z_a^{(L)}),\qquad
 \delta_a^{(\ell)}
 =\phi'(z_a^{(\ell)})\od(W^{(\ell+1)})^\top\delta_a^{(\ell+1)}.
\]
With block mobilities $(n,1,\ldots,1,n)$ the exact gradient flow is
\begin{align}
 \dot W^{(1)}
 &=-\frac2m\sum_a r_a\delta_a^{(1)}x_a^\top/\sqrt d,\nonumber\\
 \dot W^{(\ell)}
 &=-\frac{2}{nm}\sum_a r_a\delta_a^{(\ell)}
                 (h_a^{(\ell-1)})^\top,\quad 2\le\ell\le L,\nonumber\\
 \dot w&=-\frac2m\sum_a r_a h_a^{(L)}.
 \label{eq:dense-flow}
\end{align}
First-layer entries are independent $\mathcal N(0,1)$, hidden entries are
independent $\mathcal N(0,1/n)$, blocks are independent, and $w(0)=0$.
Activations are real on the real axis, analytic in a strip about it, and
have bounded first derivative there. Their values need not be bounded.

\paragraph{Data and labels.}
The population initial feature Gram (an uncentered second moment) is defined by
\[
 Q^{(0)}_{ab}=x_a^\top x_b/d,\qquad
 Q^{(\ell)}_{ab}=\E[\phi(Z_a)\phi(Z_b)],
 \quad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,Q^{(\ell-1)}).
\]
Its gap and the label scale are
\[
 \gamma=\lambda_{\min}(Q^{(L)})>0,\qquad Y=\norm{y}_2/\sqrt m.
\]
The gap is not divided by $m$. The clean main-text bounds use
$0<Y\le c\gamma/m$, for a sufficiently small activation/depth constant $c$.
The appendix retains the larger original recurrence-defined allowance and
its full certificates. $Y$ is fixed, not width-dependent; zero labels give
the exact zero predictor separately. Dense, Legendre and Harmonic require
no orthogonality, centering or input-rank assumption. Neither does the new
Logarithmic construction. For it, fix $p\ge0$ further sphere inputs before
initialization and write the full panel as $x_1,\ldots,x_{m+p}$.
Only the first $m$ inputs have labels or enter the training sums.

\paragraph{One observable and one accuracy benchmark.}
For the promised query set $\mathcal X$, write
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f-g}_{\mathcal X}=
 \sup_{t\in[0,\infty]}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}|f(t,x)-g(t,x)|.
 \label{eq:test-norm}
\end{equation}
Take $\mathcal X$ to be the whole sphere for Legendre and Harmonic, and the
declared panel for Logarithmic. This includes fitted limits. Let
$f_n$ be the compressed model's coupled ordinary dense reference and
$\widetilde f_n$ an independently initialized ordinary dense run.
Their discrepancy $\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_{\mathcal X}$ is the
random benchmark in the theorem. Compression error divided by this
discrepancy tends to zero in probability. Thus every fixed positive
relative-error tolerance is met with probability tending to one.
For this ratio, declare failure (value $+\infty$) if a required fitted
limit does not exist or the denominator vanishes; these exceptional
events have probability tending to zero under the theorem's assumptions.
All errors are absolute, with no implicit division by $Y$.
We do not spend the label cap to reduce powers of
$m/\gamma$: the learned-storage envelopes remain cubic for Legendre and
quartic for Harmonic and in the leading Logarithmic term.
The panel and sphere discrepancies need not agree: both sides of a comparison
always use the same query set. In the detailed sphere proofs,
$\norm{\cdot}_*$ denotes the sphere instance of this norm.

\paragraph{Constants, confidence and limits.}
Main-text $c,C>0$ depend only on activations and fixed depth and can change
between occurrences. They hide no dependence on $m,n,d,\gamma$.
The relative-error conclusion is convergence in probability. Finite-width
absolute-error bounds hold at any fixed confidence $1-\delta$ once the
width is sufficiently large; the appendix retains the dependence on
$\delta$ in the lower coefficient and width gates.
At each sufficiently large individual width the stated comparisons hold,
not on one event over infinitely many independent widths.
The stochastic width thresholds are not quantified.
Expressions with $o(1)$ fix the admissible data, depth, activation and
confidence before $n\to\infty$; they are not uniform growing-data claims.

\paragraph{State and reference.}
Internal memory and approximation orders are optimized inside the
constructions. They appear in the mechanism descriptions and appendix,
not as inputs to the headline theorem. Moving state and complete retained
state are distinguished.
All three track a coupled dense reference. The implicit
Harmonic implementation generates that reference jointly instead of
materializing its hidden matrices. For Logarithmic, initialization jets
determine a finite response-space construction; the resulting small model
then evolves autonomously. Its temporal pieces are preparation devices,
not a growing runtime history or a prescribed prediction trajectory.

\section{Related work and discussion}
\label{sec:related}
The comparison is between representations of a specified learning process,
not a claim that other descriptions lack quantitative guarantees.
Tensor Programs and multilayer mean-field theory provide feature-learning
population frameworks \citep{yang2021tensor,nguyen2023rigorous};
dynamical mean-field descriptions organize temporal correlations and
responses \citep{bordelon2022dmft,bordelon2023finite}. Model assumptions,
observables and solver representations must be matched before comparing
their resource costs. Our certificates concern finite Gaussian networks,
all physical times, with the promised query domain kept explicit.

A frozen neural tangent kernel describes a different limiting regime
\citep{jacot2018ntk,lee2019wide}. Higher-order responses appear in the neural
tangent hierarchy \citep{huang2020nth}, whose truncation theorem has its
own scaling and horizon. Online polynomial memory summarizes an externally
given signal \citep{gu2020hippo}; here the signals are endogenous, generated
by the network reconstructed from those memories. Stabilizing that
feedback is an additional obligation. Our second reduction preserves
coordinatewise nonlinear gates together with response inner products;
it is not ordinary projection of every layer onto an arbitrary linear
subspace. No fresh-noise approximation replaces a reused trained matrix.

\paragraph{The trade-off is not one-dimensional.}
Legendre makes the evolving memory small but retains a quadratic fixed
environment. Harmonic removes that environment from the retained state
and has polylogarithmic query work at fixed dimension, with near-linear
implicit setup. The new Logarithmic model uses the same selected runtime
but specializes its response space to declared input curves. This removes
the dimension-dependent logarithmic exponent, not the cost of preparing
that space or the restriction on which inputs are certified. These are
two different notions of generalization: accuracy on inputs absent from
the updates, and uniform accuracy on inputs absent even from setup.

\paragraph{Open boundaries.}
The dense lower bound is transient and does not establish sharp
sample/gap dependence. Small labels are a sufficient stability regime,
not a sharp necessity or a width-dependent scaling.
The analytic class includes unbounded values but not exact ReLU.
Exact-real setup costs do not establish fixed-machine-precision performance
or superiority at practical widths. A fixed program for an arbitrary
analytic activation also needs a separately specified evaluation cost.
The finite-panel third power does not yet imply a second-power neural
construction or an efficient initialization-only compiler.
Finally, trajectory fidelity alone
does not establish out-of-sample label risk: the usefulness of the
selected predictor remains a separate question.

\paragraph{Earlier empirical illustrations.}
\Cref{app:earlier-illustrations} preserves existing Legendre and Harmonic
illustrations and experiments for an older seeded finite-word decoder.
That decoder is a separate supplementary construction, not the present
finite-panel Logarithmic model. Those measurements are not empirical
validation of the new cubic-logarithmic guarantee.

\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Guide to the detailed statements and proofs}

and proof chain. The main text suppresses activation/depth factors,
uses absolute prediction error, and internalizes the choice of approximation
orders. The appendix restores activation/depth
coefficients explicitly; its numerical $C$ is distinguished from the
activation/depth envelope denoted by $C$ in the main text.

is written separately, and $w$ is the same output weight as above.
Local proof constants, source degrees and numerical orders are not new
runtime model parameters.

The full label, width, confidence, storage and cost interfaces are followed
by numerical certificates and proofs. The shared source construction,
both efficient Harmonic initializers, the new residual-adapted panel proof,
and the supplementary seeded decoder are included in the manuscript itself,
not left as dependencies on research notes. The last is retained as a
distinct result, not an alternative definition of the new Logarithmic model.
