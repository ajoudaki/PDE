\begin{abstract}
We give a modular proof of three trajectory-compression constructions for
Gaussian-initialized deep nonlinear networks with learned hidden features.
Legendre compression retains $n^{5/4+o(1)}$ moving coordinates and fixed dense
mixers. Harmonic compression retains $O((\log n)^{3d+2})$ real coordinates
for the whole input sphere. Taylor compression retains
$O((\log n)^3)$ real coordinates on a fixed panel declared at initialization.
All three have error negligible relative to actual independent dense-run
variability, uniformly over their query domain and the complete training
trajectory, including the fitted limit. The two selected constructions also
attain absolute error $Y/n$. The common ingredients are finite residual
activity, analytic response approximation, and geometry-preserving reduction;
the selected dynamics use an additional readout correction to cancel unstable
feedback. A separate short-time deep-linear benchmark shows that a literal
frozen-top neural tangent hierarchy needs, and suffices with, retained size
$n^{\Theta(\log m)}$, already for a fixed number $m$ of training samples. This
manuscript also gives polynomial sufficient widths for Legendre and Taylor,
and exact-real initialization bounds for both selected constructions:
near-quadratic work from supplied dense matrices or near-linear work for a
fresh Gaussian reference sampled implicitly. The guarantees are exact-real,
not finite-precision. A final figure collection provides earlier numerical
illustrations, separately from the theorems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{cp:introduction}

What does a deep network need to remember in order to keep learning?
Backpropagation made it possible to learn internal representations rather than
prescribe them \cite{rumelhart1986learning}. A dynamical explanation of that
process must identify the information that governs how those representations
will change next. The weights certainly contain such information, but their
number need not measure the complexity of learning itself. Two networks can
make the same predictions now and respond differently to the next update.
Understanding learning therefore calls for more than a compact fitted
function: it calls for a compact state sufficient to determine the learner's
future behavior. This is our motivation for compression. Removing information
while preserving the dynamics is a way to isolate what learning must remember.

Large width offers a particularly sharp test of this idea. Kernel limits
revealed regimes in which deep training simplifies to evolution with a fixed
tangent kernel \cite{jacot2018ntk,lee2019wide}. Mean-field theory, tensor programs,
and dynamical mean-field theory then furnished descriptions that retain
feature learning \cite{nguyen2023rigorous,yang2021tensor,bordelon2022dmft}.
These advances make the question more precise: does width merely make
learning describable by a limiting law, or does it make the learning dynamics
of a finite network representable by a much smaller autonomous state?
An evolving distribution or a two-time response kernel can be a powerful
theory without yet supplying that finite state.

Several lines of work come close from different directions. The neural
tangent hierarchy and finite-width effective theories construct closed
expansions beyond the leading kernel description
\cite{huang2020nth,dyer2020asymptotics,roberts2021principles}.
Projected infinite-width models make a different, structured feature learner
computable \cite{yang2022efficient}. Neural Tangent Transfer explicitly seeks
sparse initializations with matching training trajectories
\cite{liu2020transfer}; low-rank training and analytic moment compression
offer further dynamical reductions
\cite{schotthofer2022lottery,wang2026universal}.
The unresolved step is not the idea of reducing dynamics. It is a guarantee
that a small, fixed-size state shadows the specified dense network throughout
training and through convergence, in a regime with a width-independent
nonlinear feature-learning signal. In the closest results, a key part of
this conjunction is absent:
the reference is changed, the compressible structure is assumed, the error is
controlled only on a finite horizon, or the nonlinear fidelity remains
empirical. \Cref{cp:related} makes these distinctions at the level of the
actual constructions and guarantees.

\paragraph{A small sufficient state for deep learning.}
We establish this conjunction for the fully connected network with Gaussian
hidden weights, zero initial readout, and gradient flow specified below, at
arbitrary fixed depth with at least two hidden layers. The compression is
valid through convergence; under the additional nondegeneracy assumptions
stated below, nonlinear feature learning is certified on an early-time interval.
For each fixed admissible problem, an autonomous
system with polylogarithmically many retained real coordinates
reproduces the complete prediction trajectory more accurately than an
independent dense initialization does. To our knowledge, this is the first
guarantee combining total-state polylogarithmic compression of this dense
feature-learning dynamics, fidelity through the fitted limit, and error
negligible relative to actual independent-run discrepancy.
The advance is a finite sufficient representation of learning, not just a
limit equation or a small record of a trajectory already computed.

There are two total-state constructions. Let $n$ denote dense width, $d$
input dimension, and $Y$ the root-mean-square training-label scale.
At fixed problem parameters,
Harmonic compression uses $O((\log n)^{3d+2})$ coordinates and covers every
input on the sphere. Taylor compression uses $O((\log n)^3)$ coordinates
on a fixed finite panel of inputs declared at setup, including passive inputs
whose labels are never supplied. Both attain complete-trajectory prediction
error $Y/n$. Legendre compression is the first
step in the same framework: it retains every neuron but replaces moving
matrix information by response-history moments, using $n^{5/4+o(1)}$ moving
coordinates and keeping the fixed dense mixers. The main theorem exposes
the data and geometry dependence; the polylogarithmic counts include fixed
coefficients and working arrays, not just trainable coordinates.

Why insist on depth? With one hidden layer and fixed input dimension, each
neuron has a fixed-dimensional parameter vector. Between two wide hidden
layers, learning acts through an $n$-by-$n$ interaction matrix. Replacing
that matrix by an integral operator does not by itself compress its finite
representation. Harmonic and Taylor remove this quadratic retained
environment while preserving the nonlinear feedback that determines future
learning. This is the structural obstacle that a shallow reduction or an
assumed low-rank parameterization does not resolve.

\paragraph{An intrinsic accuracy benchmark.}
The reference is a particular finite dense realization, not an assumed ideal
population predictor. Independent dense seeds disagree; we require the
compressed learner to track its reference below that intrinsic discrepancy.
Proving a small absolute error is not enough if the proposed benchmark is
only an upper bound on dense variability. We instead prove a lower bound on
the discrepancy and make compression error negligible relative to it.
Both errors are measured by a supremum over the same query domain and the
complete training trajectory, including its fitted limit. The conclusion is
a ratio of these two trajectory norms tending to zero in probability, not a
ratio bound at every individual time. The dense lower bound is witnessed
early, not at the endpoint. No measurement of dense variability is required
to choose the compressed state.

\paragraph{One object to compress, two reductions.}
A learned matrix increment is an integral of paired forward and backward
responses. Legendre compression replaces their histories by a small number of
polynomial moments, while retaining every neuron and the initialized dense
mixers. Projecting both histories onto the same orthogonal space is crucial:
mixed retained--omitted terms cancel, leaving a product of the two omitted
tails. Thus the approximation benefits from regularity in both histories,
not just one.

Harmonic and Taylor go further by also reducing the neuron coordinate.
They first approximate the relevant responses in a small function space, then
preserve its inner-product geometry on selected neuron coordinates. Selecting
coordinates, rather than mixing them arbitrarily, preserves the meaning of
coordinatewise nonlinear activation. The two methods share the same reduced
nonlinear dynamics; they differ in which responses their source spaces must
represent. Harmonic uses joint temporal and spherical regularity to cover the
whole input sphere. Taylor represents only the finitely many input curves
declared at setup, using temporal pieces adapted to the slowing of learning.
The narrower query contract removes the dimension-dependent logarithmic
exponent. Passive inputs are supplied without their labels.

\paragraph{Why approximation errors do not accumulate indefinitely.}
The compressed models are closed learning systems, not playback of a saved
dense trajectory. Their current responses determine the next update, which
changes their future responses. A generic Lipschitz bound loses the sign of
this feedback and can suggest exponential amplification. The comparison
instead retains the dissipative training Gram and measures perturbations
against the finite amount of remaining learning. For the selected models,
a readout correction cancels the leading readout--residual feedback.
Infinite physical time is therefore controlled by finite residual activity,
not by multiplying a local error by the duration of training.

\paragraph{Nonlinear learning, not a disguised kernel limit.}
The theorem allows layer-dependent strip-analytic activations with bounded
derivative, including unbounded activation values, a positive initial
population feature-Gram gap, and sufficiently small nonzero labels. The label
cap is fixed independently of width: this is not a limit in which the labels
and feature-learning signal shrink to zero as the network widens.
Under the local additional assumptions of nonaffine activations and no
parallel or antiparallel training inputs, \Cref{thm:nonlinear-learning}
proves width-independent early-time separation from every affine input
predictor and from the dense reference's own frozen initial-kernel predictor.
Every dense hidden layer moves, and the compressed predictions inherit the
two separations. Taylor may add up to four predeclared passive witness inputs.
Thus the reduced state preserves behavior that neither deep linear learning
nor the reference's frozen features reproduce. This certificate is early-time;
the compression guarantee itself extends through convergence.

The foundational conclusion is that, in this regime, deep nonlinear learning
does not require retaining its quadratic microscopic description.
The proofs identify sufficient response memory and show why its closed
evolution remains faithful. Whether those coordinates also reveal simple
interpretable mechanisms is the next question, rather than a prerequisite
for establishing that such a small dynamical description exists.

\section{Setup and main results}
\label{cp:setup}

There are $m\ge2$ training pairs $(x_a,y_a)$ with
$x_a\in\R^d$, $\norm{x_a}_2=\sqrt d$, width $n$, and a fixed number
$L\ge2$ of hidden layers. Write $v=x/\sqrt d$. The first matrix is
$W^{(1)}\in\R^{n\times d}$, the hidden matrices are
$W^{(\ell)}\in\R^{n\times n}$, and the readout is $w\in\R^n$.
The activation may depend on the layer; the superscript is suppressed when
the same equation applies to every layer. When a response is indexed by
the normalized query $v$, its network input is $x=\sqrt d\,v$.
The canonical network is
\begin{align}
 z^{(1)}(x)&=W^{(1)}v,&
 z^{(\ell)}(x)&=W^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)}(x),&
 h^{(\ell)}(x)&=\phi_\ell(z^{(\ell)}(x)),\nonumber\\
 f_n(x)&=\frac{w^\top h^{(L)}(x)}n,&
 r_a&=f_n(x_a)-y_a,&
 \cL&=\frac1m\sum_{a=1}^m r_a^2.
 \label{cp:network}
\end{align}
The residual-free backward responses are
\begin{equation}
 \delta_a^{(L)}=w\od\phi_L'(z_a^{(L)}),\qquad
 \delta_a^{(\ell)}=\phi_\ell'(z_a^{(\ell)})\od
                W^{(\ell+1)\top}\delta_a^{(\ell+1)}.
 \label{cp:backward}
\end{equation}
At a passive input use the same forward and backward recursions, without
supplying a label or adding a training term.
Dots denote physical training time. Gradient flow with block mobilities
$(n,1,\ldots,1,n)$ is exactly
\begin{align}
 \dot W^{(1)}&=-\frac2m\sum_a r_a\delta_a^{(1)}v_a^\top,
 \nonumber\\
 \dot W^{(\ell)}&=-\frac2{nm}\sum_a
          r_a\delta_a^{(\ell)}h_a^{(\ell-1)\top}\quad(2\le\ell\le L),
 &\dot w&=-\frac2m\sum_a r_a h_a^{(L)}.
 \label{cp:flow}
\end{align}
First-layer entries are independent $\mathcal N(0,1)$; hidden entries are
independent $\mathcal N(0,1/n)$; all blocks are independent; and $w(0)=0$.
Nothing freezes the hidden features.

\paragraph{Activations, data, and constants.}
Each $\phi_\ell$ is real on the real axis, holomorphic on
$|\operatorname{Im}z|<a$ for a common $a>0$, and has bounded first derivative
on that strip. Its values need not be bounded. For explicit estimates put
\[
 \beta=\max\left\{10,1+\max_\ell|\phi_\ell(0)|,16/a,
 \max_{\ell,\,j\in\{1,2\}}
 \sup_{|\operatorname{Im}z|\le a/2}|\phi_\ell^{(j)}(z)|\right\}.
\]
The second derivative is bounded on the smaller strip by Cauchy's formula.
Define the uncentered population feature Gram and the label scale by
\begin{gather}
 Q^{(0)}_{ab}=v_a^\top v_b,\qquad
 Q^{(\ell)}_{ab}=\E[\phi_\ell(Z_a)\phi_\ell(Z_b)],\quad
 Z\sim\mathcal N(0,Q^{(\ell-1)}),\nonumber\\
 \gamma=\lambda_{\min}(Q^{(L)})>0,\qquad
 Y=\norm{y}_2/\sqrt m>0.
 \label{cp:data}
\end{gather}
Assume $m/\gamma\ge1$ and the same sufficient small-label condition
\begin{equation}
 0<Y\le\frac\gamma m\beta^{-30L}.
 \label{cp:labels}
\end{equation}
No input-rank, centering, orthogonality, or label-sign hypothesis is imposed.
All data, $m,d,L,\gamma,Y$, and activations are fixed separately as $n$ grows.
Throughout the stated bounds, $C>0$ depends only on activations and depth, never on
$n,m,d,\gamma,Y$ or a passive-panel size. Its value may change between occurrences.
All other constants are defined in the statement or proof using them.
Initialization costs use a separate fixed-parameter big-$O$ convention,
specified in \Cref{cp:initialization}; its constants are not the
activation-and-depth-only storage constant $C$.

\paragraph{Query domains and probability.}
For a query set $\mathcal X$, use
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f-g}_{\mathcal X}
   =\sup_{t\in[0,\infty]}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}|f(t,x)-g(t,x)|.
 \label{cp:norm}
\end{equation}
The endpoint $t=\infty$ means the fitted limit. For Legendre and Harmonic,
$\mathcal X=\{x:\norm{x}_2=\sqrt d\}$. For Taylor, fix $p\ge0$
additional inputs on this same sphere before initialization and take
$\mathcal X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{m+p}\}$. Their labels are never supplied.
Let $\widetilde f_n$ be an independently initialized dense run on the same
training problem.

An eventual assertion at confidence $1-\delta$ means: for every fixed
admissible problem and $0<\delta<1$, there is a finite width threshold such
that the assertion holds at each larger individual width. That threshold
may depend on every fixed problem parameter, including the positive $Y$.
The headline theorem quantifies this threshold polynomially for Legendre and
Taylor; Harmonic retains the eventual qualification. Polynomial sufficient
widths need not be practically small. Each probability bound concerns one
fixed problem at one individual width, not a simultaneous event over all
datasets or infinitely many independently initialized widths. No unrestricted
growing-data limit is asserted. For a ratio below,
missing fitted limits or a zero denominator count as failure.

The compression and nonlinear feature-learning statements below inherit this
setup and these probability conventions; the quantitative width improvement
does not extend to the separate nonlinear feature-learning certificates.
The separate NTH benchmark in
\Cref{cp:nth:storage} states its own assumptions and probability convention.
Every storage count charges a nonprimitive activation evaluator's description
and scratch separately.

\begin{theorem}[Compression of the complete learning trajectory]
\label{cp:headline}
Under the preceding assumptions, each construction can be chosen so that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\norm{f_{\rm model}-f_n}_{\mathcal X}}
      {\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_{\mathcal X}}
       \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
 \label{cp:relative}
\end{equation}
Here $f_n$ is the construction's coupled ordinary dense reference, and both
norms use the same promised query domain and all physical times, including
the fitted endpoint. Sufficient real-coordinate counts are
\begin{align}
 \textnormal{Legendre moving state:}\quad&
 Cm\left(\frac m\gamma\right)^3 n^{5/4}
 [\log(en)]^2e^{\sqrt{\log(en)}/2},
 \label{cp:legendre-size}\\
 \textnormal{Harmonic total state:}\quad&
 C(m+d)^2+\left(\frac Cd\right)^{d+1}
       \left(\frac{Ym}\gamma\right)^4[\log(en)]^{3d+2},
 \label{cp:harmonic-size}
\end{align}
and the Taylor total state is at most
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 C(m+p)^2\bigg[&
       \left(\frac{Ym}\gamma\right)^4[\log(en)]^3
       \\ &+\left(\frac m\gamma\right)^2[\log(en)]^2
                     [\log(e+\log(en))]^2\bigg]
       +C(m+p)d.
 \end{split}
 \label{cp:panel-size}
\end{equation}
Legendre additionally retains $(L-1)n^2$ fixed mixer coordinates.
Harmonic and Taylor include the data, selected mixers, metrics, initial
copies, and working arrays. Their predictions additionally satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \norm{f_{\rm model}-f_n}_{\mathcal X}\le Y/n
 \label{cp:absolute}
\end{equation}
at every fixed confidence for sufficiently large width, with the same
storage orders. One theoretical initializer uses the dense initialization,
training data, and finitely many initialization derivatives; no later trained
weight or dense trajectory is an input. The efficient alternatives in
\Cref{cp:initialization} compute disposable numerical continuations from the
initial data. Subsequent compressed evolution is an autonomous nonlinear
system with the stated retained state in either case.

For Legendre and Taylor, the sufficient width is polynomial: for every
$0<\varepsilon,\delta<1$, the stated sizes and
\[
 \norm{f_{\rm model}-f_n}_{\mathcal X}
 \le\varepsilon\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_{\mathcal X}
\]
hold with probability at least $1-\delta$ whenever
\begin{equation}
 n\ge\left[c\beta^L
 (1+m+d+p+\gamma^{-1}+Y^{-1}+\delta^{-1}+\varepsilon^{-1})\right]^c,
 \label{cp:polynomial-onset}
\end{equation}
for an absolute numerical constant $c>0$, local to this width bound.
For Legendre take $p=0$ and omit
$Y^{-1}$; Taylor also satisfies \eqref{cp:absolute} on this event.
This strengthens only the width qualification: the storage formulas, label
condition, query domains and physical optimizers are unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Efficient initialization]
\label{cp:initialization}
Under \Cref{cp:headline}, Harmonic and Taylor admit the following exact-real
initializers, at every fixed confidence and sufficiently large width.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Method & Dense reference & Setup work & Peak setup memory\\
\midrule
Harmonic & Supplied matrices
 & $O(n^2[\log(en)]^{3d/2+1})$ & $O(n^2)$\\
Taylor & Supplied matrices
 & $O(n^2[\log(en)]^{5/2})$ & $O(n^2)$\\
Harmonic & Fresh implicit Gaussian
 & $O(n[\log(en)]^{9d/2+3})$ & $O(n[\log(en)]^{3d/2+1})$\\
Taylor & Fresh implicit Gaussian
 & $O(n[\log(en)]^5)$ & $O(n[\log(en)]^{5/2})$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All four initializers preserve the corresponding retained-state bound,
the complete-trajectory error $Y/n$, and \eqref{cp:relative}.
The constants in this table may depend on the separately fixed
$m,d,p,L,\beta,\gamma,Y$ and confidence. The implicit version is coupled to a
fresh ordinary Gaussian dense reference; it is not a subquadratic conversion
of supplied matrices or a prescribed entrywise seed.
\end{proposition}

The operation model charges scalar arithmetic, comparisons, square roots,
elementary functions, real activation values and Gaussian samples as
primitives. Nonprimitive evaluators add their actual work, description and
scratch. Activation envelopes and a positive population-gap certificate are
supplied; computing them is not included. No high activation derivatives or
trained-reference values are inputs. These initializers compute through
$T=32(m/\gamma)\log(en)$, not merely a short physical-time prefix, then
discard their working structures. The compressed model starts at time zero.
The original initialization-jet compiler remains an alternative without an
efficient work bound. The proof in \Cref{cp:init:proof} and finite-order
ledgers in \Cref{cp:init:sec:costs} separate all parameter dependencies,
retained storage and setup memory. Taylor uses the polynomial source and
accuracy gates above without an additional qualitative source threshold;
Harmonic retains its eventual source-width qualification.

These are sufficient sizes, not optimality claims. Dense itself has
$(L-1)n^2+n(d+1)$ weight coordinates. Data storage is additional to the
Legendre moving-state count. Coordinate counts alone do not bound precision
or preprocessing time; \Cref{cp:initialization} supplies separate arithmetic
and peak-memory bounds, not a finite-precision guarantee.
The finite-panel theorem makes no accuracy promise on inputs first supplied
after initialization. Zero labels give the stationary zero predictor instead.
The sharp literal-array cost of a frozen-top neural tangent hierarchy is
treated separately in \Cref{cp:nth:storage}, for a short-time deep-linear
model. It is $n^{\Theta(\log m)}$ uniformly over $4\le m\le\sqrt n$; no
growth of $m$ is required, so already a fixed dataset forces a power of $n$
whose exponent grows with $m$.

\paragraph{From population dynamics to a finite retained state.}
Multilayer mean-field theory \cite{nguyen2023rigorous} and Neural Hilbert
Ladders \cite{chen2024ladders} describe evolving functions or measures.
Tensor Programs IV \cite{yang2021tensor} rigorously identifies
feature-learning infinite-width limits for fixed training programs.
DMFT \cite{bordelon2022dmft} gives self-consistent equations for evolving
kernels and causal responses. These are genuine dynamical descriptions.
Their general representations, however, are function-valued or carry
time-indexed response information; writing an operator equation is not yet
exhibiting a small finite state that evaluates it.

Nor is seed dependence absent from prior theory. Pham and Nguyen
\cite{pham2021fluctuations} construct a sampled-neuron-coupled fluctuation
correction and state quantitative finite-horizon estimates; Bordelon and
Pehlevan \cite{bordelon2023finite} develop a dynamical fluctuation expansion.
Our distinction is not priority for coupling to initialization or studying
prediction variance. Even granting the advertised finite-horizon estimates,
these descriptions do not supply a polylogarithmic autonomous representation
with the present all-time approximation guarantee. Taking width to infinity
at each fixed time and then studying large time is different from one error
bound covering every time at a finite width.

Yang, Santacroce, and Hu \cite{yang2022efficient} address computability
directly. Their hidden matrices are built through fixed-dimensional Gaussian
projections, and the optimizer projects updates into the same spaces.
The construction yields a nonlinear feature-learning limit, but of this
structured network rather than the unrestricted Gaussian reference.
Its coefficient arrays append forward and backward directions as examples
are processed; retaining the frozen old rows still incurs storage.
The source proves a fixed-program limit, not a bound showing that this
projected learner shadows the ordinary dense one, nor a fixed-state
compression of the accumulating history. Thus computability of a modified
limit and faithful compression of a finite reference solve different problems.

\paragraph{Closed expansions are not yet a feature-learning compression theorem.}
The NTK \cite{jacot2018ntk} and parameter-linearized dynamics
\cite{lee2019wide} establish a profound simplification in their scaling
regimes. Huang and Yau's NTH \cite{huang2020nth} goes beyond freezing the
kernel: it supplies a hierarchy and finite-order truncation estimates.
Dyer and Gur-Ari \cite{dyer2020asymptotics} and Roberts, Yaida, and Hanin
\cite{roberts2021principles} also derive initialized finite-order closed
systems, including corrections to training and fitted predictions.
These are real precedents for autonomous reduced equations, not merely
ensemble endpoint formulas.

Their relevant fixed-depth expansions are around kernel scaling, where
feature-learning corrections vanish with width at fixed training time.
General deep nonlinear
power counting in Dyer--Gur-Ari is partly conjectural, and a perturbative
formula at the fitted endpoint is not a pathwise remainder estimate uniform
through fitting. The present result instead couples its small system to a
regime with a width-independent feature-learning certificate.
Our separate \Cref{cp:nth:storage} isolates another obstacle: a literal
frozen-top hierarchy in its specified short-time deep-linear family has
storage $n^{\Theta(\log m)}$. This is a representation-specific benchmark,
not a lower bound for Huang--Yau's parameterization, every hierarchy
implementation, or the nonlinear family of the main theorem.

\paragraph{Assumed compressibility versus compressibility derived from training.}
Wang et al.\ \cite{wang2026universal} are a particularly close predecessor:
their goal includes polylogarithmic compression of learning dynamics, not
just trained functions. Their analytic moment argument uses fixed-dimensional
objects and a symmetric analytic observable. The dynamical extension
(Theorem~5) requires the observable composed with training to satisfy the
same structural and regularity assumption. Section~5 explicitly leaves the
compressed-width scaling for deep networks open and questions preservation
of that assumption by training. In an all-wide hidden layer, a neuron's
incoming and outgoing coordinates grow with neighboring widths. A
fixed-object-dimension logarithmic power therefore does not establish a
width-uniform deep-network compression theorem. Likewise, analyticity of
each finite-time map does not bound its analytic radius or amplification
uniformly through convergence. These are precisely obligations discharged
by our response estimates and stability analysis, not consequences of
permutation symmetry alone.

Dynamical low-rank training \cite{schotthofer2022lottery} gives a different
and substantive reduction: evolve matrix factors using projected gradients
and robust low-rank integration. Its approximation analysis assumes a small
component of the full gradient normal to the low-rank manifold
(supplementary assumption~P2). It does not derive that premise from
Gaussian-initialized all-wide deep training. Moreover, the error
accumulation argument is finite-horizon; the printed main statement's
omission of terminal-time dependence cannot be used as an all-time
certificate. In contrast, we neither assume low rank of the trained dense
matrices nor require the compressed flow to inherit stability automatically.
We derive a small response space and separately prove stability of its
closed nonlinear evolution.

\paragraph{Trajectory transfer, low-dimensional training, and learned reductions.}
Neural Tangent Transfer \cite{liu2020transfer} explicitly seeks a sparse
initialization whose later predictions follow a dense teacher, and therefore
anticipates our dynamical objective. Its exact proposition is for a linear
model under perfect initial output and kernel matching; the nonlinear
trajectory claim is empirical. In that proposition's binary-mask model,
exact kernel matching even forces each removed input coordinate to vanish
on the training set: the removed Gram contributions are positive
semidefinite and cannot cancel. The proposition does not establish
nontrivial compression for generic inputs. This restriction does not
invalidate the useful nonlinear heuristic; it explains why the exact
theorem is not already the desired nonlinear compression guarantee.

Intrinsic-dimension experiments \cite{li2018intrinsic}, concentration in
tiny gradient subspaces \cite{gurari2018gradient}, and training in
trajectory-derived subspaces \cite{li2023trajectory} provide evidence
that many search directions are redundant. A small coordinate vector may
nevertheless require a dense decoder, full-size workspace, and evaluation of
the original network. Koopman-based training forecasts
\cite{dogra2020koopman} evolve learned operators fitted to recorded
trajectories, but their displayed implementation retains the full weight
state and supplies no complete-trajectory error theorem.
Successful task prediction, sparse trainability, and fidelity to a specified
dense seed are distinct claims. Our total-state accounting and seed-relative
norm make the latter claim explicit.

\paragraph{Feature-learning theory and the role of memory.}
Deep nonlinear feature learning itself has rigorous precedents.
Nichani, Damian, and Lee \cite{nichani2023nonlinear} analyze a
feature-learning stage followed by readout training; Allen-Zhu and Li
\cite{allenzhu2023backward} prove hierarchical learning for a structured
architecture and staged training procedure with backward feature correction.
Adaptive-kernel theories also characterize learned endpoints under their
equilibrium and regularization qualifications \cite{lauditi2025adaptive}.
These results address representation or sample efficiency, rather than
small-state emulation of the dense reference studied here. Our complementary
feature-learning theorem establishes that the behavior being compressed
has not collapsed to an affine predictor or to its own initial kernel.

The closest physical organizing principle is memory reduction:
Mori--Zwanzig and optimal prediction express eliminated degrees of freedom
through memory and unresolved forcing \cite{chorin2000optimal}.
Online polynomial-memory constructions such as HiPPO
\cite{gu2020hippo} show how histories can be represented by finite auxiliary
coordinates. Here the histories being compressed are generated by the
learner reconstructed from those very coordinates. Controlling that
feedback, while deriving the required response regularity from dense
training, is the additional step that turns memory approximation into
a certified learner.

\section{Discussion}
\label{cp:discussion}

\paragraph{Compression as a route to understanding.}
A parameter vector serves two roles: it specifies a current predictor and
it stores how that predictor can change. The second role is the one at
issue here. For a fixed training problem, a state is sufficient if it can
continue the learning process without access to the discarded dense
coordinates. The theorem shows that most of the quadratic microscopic
description is unnecessary for this purpose, at an accuracy finer than
the reference's own seed variability.

This does not identify a unique or minimal memory, and coordinate size is
not a ranking of the semantic importance of individual neurons. Rather,
the construction isolates one sufficient memory in forward and backward
responses: how inputs are represented, how changes propagate through
layers, and how these responses have accumulated during learning.
Compression is therefore a first step toward a mechanism, not a substitute
for explaining one. It makes a sharper mechanistic question possible:
which interactions among these retained responses govern representation
formation? That question is obscured when every dense weight is treated
as an equally indispensable state variable.

The sufficiency is also conditional on the training protocol and promised
observables. It is not a universal summary for arbitrary future datasets,
losses, or interventions. Taylor makes this dependence explicit by
specializing to a declared input panel; Harmonic pays a larger logarithmic
power to support inputs first queried after setup. This tradeoff is part of
the meaning of dynamical memory, not merely an implementation detail.

\paragraph{Why deep learning can have a small dynamical description.}
The crucial regularity is in the responses through which training acts,
not an assumption that every trained matrix is low rank. A hidden-matrix
update pairs a forward feature with a backward sensitivity and integrates
that pairing over training. Many microscopic entries can therefore be
driven by a much smaller family of response curves.
Legendre compresses their temporal memory while keeping the neuron
coordinates. Harmonic additionally compresses their variation over inputs;
Taylor restricts the input family and adapts temporal pieces to the
slowing of learning. They expose successive reductions of the same
interaction structure.

Analyticity explains why logarithms enter. Exponentially accurate
approximation requires only logarithmically many modes for inverse-width
accuracy. The number of input directions, temporal pieces, and the cost of
preserving their inner-product geometry determine the final powers.
But approximation alone would produce a small description of a dense
trajectory, not a small learner. The latter must generate its own future.
Finite residual activity and the preserved sign of dissipative feedback
prevent small representation errors from accumulating over infinite physical
time; the selected models' readout correction cancels the dangerous
readout--residual coupling. This stability argument is why the result
cannot be obtained simply by fitting polynomials to recorded features.

\paragraph{Intrinsic precision and population interpretations.}
The finite network is the primary object: no population solution is needed
to define its compression or its error benchmark. A lower bound on actual
independent-run discrepancy prevents a loose analytic upper envelope from
making a poor approximation appear successful.
For Harmonic and Taylor, the complete-trajectory upper error is inverse
width, while the dense lower bound is inverse square-root width up to
logarithms. Thus compression is asymptotically finer than the benchmark,
rather than merely within a fixed multiple of it. The comparison remains
between trajectory norms; it does not assert a dense lower bound at each
time or at the fitted endpoint.

If a population description is independently available in the same norm,
the triangle inequality transfers any dense-to-population approximation to
the compressed predictor with only the additional compression error.
Consequently the result can serve either a finite-realization or a
population interpretation without assuming that the population predictor
is the scientifically preferred object.

\paragraph{Scope and the next questions.}
The small-label assumption ensures stability but remains independent of
width. Under the separate nondegeneracy assumptions, the early-time
feature-learning separations remain positive as width grows, so small
labels do not turn the theorem into a lazy limit. Extending the
complete-trajectory result to unrestricted labels or nonsmooth activations
such as exact ReLU remains open. So do optimal state counts, practical
finite-precision guarantees, and a similarly small Taylor construction
supporting arbitrary inputs first supplied after setup. The theorem does
not establish improved risk against unknown labels; the final figures
provide separately qualified empirical illustrations, not certificates of
its asymptotic exponents.

Retained state and the work needed to construct it are distinct.
Initialization derivatives establish that later trained weights need not
be supplied to the compiler. The efficient alternatives in
\Cref{cp:initialization} instead compute and discard numerical
continuations from the initial data. At fixed problem parameters their
work is quadratic in width times a polylogarithm for supplied matrices,
or linear in width times a polylogarithm for a fresh implicitly sampled
Gaussian reference. The latter is not a subquadratic conversion of an
arbitrary supplied dense matrix. These are exact-real arithmetic bounds,
not a claim of automatic practical speedup. They make the representation
constructive without conflating a small learner with free preparation.

\paragraph{Proof roadmap.}
First establish fitting and finite residual activity without a source
approximation. A common probabilistic source lemma then controls forward and
backward responses on the two analytic domains. Legendre approximates their
history integrals. The other two methods share one geometry-preserving
selection and one corrected nonlinear optimizer; only their finite source
bases differ. A last-layer innovation and a deterministic analytic estimate
give a dense lower bound at a training input. \Cref{cp:assembly} combines
these ingredients. A quantitative source argument and a third-order Legendre
history estimate supply the polynomial width refinement. Numerical source
construction and a two-direction Gaussian sampler prove
\Cref{cp:initialization}. Every dependency is supplied below. The older
decoder theory remains outside this manuscript; earlier numerical figures
are collected separately at the end.

\section{Assembly of the headline theorem}
\label{cp:assembly}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{cp:headline}]
The preceding constructions provide the stated retained counts and the
initialization-jet provenance alternative; the efficient alternatives have
their own execution proofs. Here we first verify the original asymptotic
comparison with actual dense variability, not an upper certificate. The
quantitative width refinement uses its separately proved finite dense lower
bound rather than claiming polynomial onset for the sharper coefficient
used in this first argument.
Fix a confidence budget $0<\delta_0<1$. By \Cref{cp:dense-lower}, for
sufficiently large width,
\[
 \Pr\left\{\norm{f_n-\widetilde f_n}_{\mathcal X}
 \ge c_{\phi,L,\delta_0}
       \frac{Y\sqrt\gamma}{\sqrt n[\log(en)]^{5/2}}\right\}
 \ge1-\delta_0.
\]
Its witness is a training input, so the same lower bound applies on either
promised domain. The source and fitting events give all required limits.
The Legendre order of \Cref{cp:legendre} gives error at most
$CY/(\sqrt n[\log(en)]^3)$; its ratio to the displayed lower bound is
at most a fixed-problem, fixed-confidence multiple of
$[\log(en)]^{-1/2}$. For the two selected models,
\Cref{cp:harmonic,cp:panel} give error at most $Y/n$, and the corresponding
ratio is at most
\[
 \frac{[\log(en)]^{5/2}}
 {c_{\phi,L,\delta_0}\sqrt{\gamma n}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Intersecting each approximation event with the lower event costs at most
$2\delta_0$ by the union bound, without any independence requirement.
For every fixed positive ratio tolerance the limiting superior of its failure
probability is therefore at most $2\delta_0$. Letting $\delta_0\downarrow0$
proves \eqref{cp:relative}, including the stated failure convention.
For \eqref{cp:absolute}, use the selected-model event at the requested
confidence. The label assumption stays fixed throughout; no label inequality
is used to reduce a displayed sample/gap power.
For the polynomial sufficient width \eqref{cp:polynomial-onset}, apply
\Cref{cp:poly:width} with the explicit maxima in
\cref{cp:poly:eq:legendre-final-width,cp:poly:eq:taylor-final-width}.
That argument supplies its own quantitative actual-dense lower event and
retains exactly \cref{cp:legendre-size,cp:panel-size}; it does not attach
polynomial onset to the sharper coefficient in the display above.
\end{proof}
